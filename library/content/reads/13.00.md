# A palace for tourism, 1937–1943

On the corner of Corso Libertà and via Virgilio in Bolzano stands an office block of about 1990. Until 1988 the Cinema Corso stood there, and before it was a cinema it was the Palazzo del Turismo, built by the Fascist tourist office from 1938 to the design of Armando Ronca. It is the building on the regime's new avenue that local writers on architecture have called perhaps the most interesting, and it is the one that was pulled down. This is its life, from the commission to the demolition, put together from newspapers of the time, a few photographs and the minutes of the provincial council.

## 1937–1938: the commission

The client was the tourist office of Bolzano, the Azienda autonoma di soggiorno. By [one account](row:13.17) it gave the job to Ronca in 1937: a "polifunzionale" building with a concert hall, a hall for receptions, the office's own rooms and the seat of the Automobile Club.

Ronca was an engineer from Verona, born in 1901. He had come to Bolzano by the regime's own doors. In April 1935 he was among the prize-winners of a competition for housing types chaired by the prefect, and that September the *Alpenzeitung*, the regime's German-language daily, [showed his design](row:13.01) for the state radio's new transmitter on a hill above the Monticolo lake. He opened an office in the city the same year.

The minister of popular culture, Dino Alfieri, approved the tourism palace on a visit on 16 June 1938. Six days later the German Catholic paper *Dolomiten* [printed the design](row:13.02) and described it: one of the most imposing buildings of the "Denkmalzone", the monument zone, "der Palast des Tourismus am Corso 9 Maggio", on a plot of 1,492 square metres with a front of 40.35 metres on the avenue. It was to put every tourism body of the province under one roof.

Work began on 10 August 1938. The weekly *Volksbote* [added](row:13.03): "Es ist interessant zu wissen, daß er der erste Palast dieser Art von ganz Italien ist." It is interesting to know that it is the first palace of its kind in all Italy.

A later account says the building commission mocked the slender columns of the entrance on via Virgilio as "fiammiferi", matchsticks, and that Ronca added two buttresses. No record of the objection from the time has been found.

## 1939–1940: a shell, and an exhibition in it

By November 1939 the walls were nearly up. The *Alpenzeitung* [printed a photograph](row:13.04) of the building in scaffolding and expected it to be occupied in spring.

It was not finished when it was first used. On Sunday 6 October 1940 the minister of corporations, Renato Ricci, opened the ninth art exhibition of the Fascist artists' union of the region [in the new palace](row:13.06). There were some 500 works by 85 artists, with prizes for works on "spirit and events of the age of Mussolini". In the atrium stood a portrait of the king and a colossal head of Drusus by the sculptor Piffrader, who also made the relief on the party's house. A critic in *Dolomiten* [noted](row:13.08) the state of the place without complaint: "Daß die Eingangshalle mit den überschlanken Säulen aus Verde antico noch der Vollendung harrt, daß in Vestibül und Treppenhaus gearbeitet wird und der Boden, anstatt mit Parkettbrettchen, mit feinem weißem Sand bedeckt ist, tut der Wirkung keinen Abbruch." That the entrance hall with its over-slender columns of green marble still awaits completion, that work goes on in vestibule and stairwell, and that the floor is covered with fine white sand in place of parquet, does the effect no harm. The regime's magazine [found](row:13.10) the atrium "ancor avvolto nell'odor di calce", still wrapped in the smell of lime. The show closed on 24 November [with 25,572 visitors](row:13.09).

A photograph of those weeks is in the archive of the Italian touring club. On its back is a typed slip from a press service, dated Bolzano, 20 October 1940. Bolzano, it says, "si appresta e si affretta a riprendere quella veste, quella fisonomia e quel cuore italiano che le sono voluti dalle sue origini", is making ready and hastening to take back the dress, the face and the Italian heart that its origins demand.

## 1940–1943: the tenants

The Automobile Club moved in on 20 December 1940. By October 1941 the tourist office had its entrance hall, with a terracotta relief of the Dolomites fifteen square metres large on its walls.

Then the palace got a tenant nobody had planned for. On 6 November 1941 the *Alpenzeitung* [announced](row:13.12) that the undersecretary of the interior would install "des Kommissärs zur Durchführung der italienisch-deutschen Verträge für das Alto Adige, Exz. Agostino Podestà", the commissioner for carrying out the Italo-German agreements for the Alto Adige. "Zum Amtssitz des Kommissariats wurde der neue Palast des Tourismus im Corso 9 Maggio, neben dem Foro della Vittoria, ausgewählt, dessen Bau und Ausstattung in diesen Tagen vollendet wurden." As seat of the commissariat the new palace of tourism was chosen, whose building and fitting-out have been completed in these days.

The agreements were the Option: the arrangement of 1939 by which German-speaking South Tyroleans chose between leaving for the Reich and staying as Italians. So the building put up to bring visitors in was, in the month it was finished, given to the office that administered the departure of the people who lived there. How long the commissariat stayed is not known.

The art exhibition of 1942 was held elsewhere, in a school. Earlier accounts that place it here are mistaken.

# Stage and screen, 1943–1980

## 1943–1945: the Fremdenverkehrshaus

After September 1943 the German occupiers renamed the building as they renamed the street. It became the "Fremdenverkehrshaus", the avenue the "9.-Mai-Straße". In December 1943 two death notices in the occupation's newspaper [set a baker's funeral farewell](row:13.20) "im Fremdenverkehrshaus (Palazzo del Turismo)", the old name in brackets for those who would not know the new one.

The big hall became the town's stage. In February 1944 the air-raid wardens were [called](row:13.13) to a general muster "im Fremdenverkehrshaus (ex Palazzo Turismo)". In April the symphony orchestra of the Reich's regional theatre at Innsbruck played Mozart, Beethoven, Weber and Wagner there. On 2 June the local Nazi organisation held an evening of folk customs with the district leader. On 20 and 22 June the Innsbruck company [gave a musical comedy](row:13.14). According to one architect's account, after the municipal theatre had been bombed the hall was fitted with the screen, curtains and seats of another cinema.

In January 1945 municipal offices moved in. The address was now "neue Grieser Hauptstraße", the new main street of Gries. The building was hit by bombs that year and quickly repaired.

## 1946–1962: Il Corso

In 1946 Ronca himself joined his concert hall and his reception hall into one room for films: 520 seats in the stalls, 162 above and 42 in seven small boxes at the back, [724 in all](row:13.17). Other accounts give about 800. It opened as "Il Corso", in 1946 or 1947. The street outside had become Corso della Libertà.

For years it was the largest and most modern hall of the region. Besides films it had theatre in Italian and in German, opera and concerts. On 14 December 1948 a company from Innsbruck played there, the first German-language performances in the city after the war. In December 1950 the new municipal theatre company, the Teatro Stabile, [gave its first production](row:13.16) on its stage. In 1962 Ronca refitted the room for the wide screen.

A photograph by the Pedrotti studio shows it in those years, with "IL CORSO" in raised letters and a banner for a film called *L'amante indiana*. The porch has changed. The thin free-standing columns of 1940 are gone, and the front is a row of closed bays between pillars. When that was done, in 1946 or earlier, is not known.

Ronca went on building in Bolzano until his death in 1970: schools, churches, apartment towers, three more cinemas. The Corso is the work of his that later writers single out.

# The demolition, 1980–1990

## 1980: the Province does not buy

The tourist office decided to sell. On 16 September 1980 a Communist member of the provincial council asked whether the Province would buy the building for culture. The president of the Province, Silvius Magnago, [answered](row:13.21): "Der Landesausschuß hat nie beschlossen, das Corso-Kino anzukaufen und hat auch nicht die Absicht das zu tun." The provincial government has never resolved to buy the Corso cinema and has no intention of doing so. To buy it, pull it down and build would cost "Unsummen", vast sums. The councillor replied that a public property was being lost, since the tourist office too was a public body.

## 1983–1985: a permit, annulled and revived

What followed is known because the councillor for planning read it into the minutes on 30 March 1988.

In March 1983 private parties appealed to the Province against a project "Abbruch und zum Wiederaufbau des Corso-Kinos", for demolishing and rebuilding the cinema. On 4 July the Province asked the mayor not to issue the permit. "Trotzdem wurde am 5. Juli 1983 die Baukonzession Nr. 305 erlassen." All the same, on 5 July building permit no. 305 was issued. The Province annulled it that autumn, on the ground that this was no rebuilding but a new building, which would have to keep to the density of the plan: the project had 22,500 cubic metres where 13,146 were allowed.

On 8 July 1985 the Council of State in Rome struck the annulment down. The Province, it found, had acted "nicht aus urbanistischen, sondern aus architektonischen und ästhetischen Gründen", for reasons of architecture and beauty and not of planning, and those were not its to judge. The permit of 1983 was alive again.

That January a local monthly had printed two photographs of the cinema in an essay by the architect Silvano Bassetti. [Its caption](row:13.22) ends: "Tutto ciò rischia di scomparire e sarebbe un peccato!" All this is in danger of disappearing, and that would be a pity. For Bassetti the scandal was not that a private owner wanted to make the most of his property. It was "il fatto che l'ente pubblico che ne deteneva la proprietà l'abbia venduto", that the public body that owned it had sold it. He wrote that he liked the marbles of the Cinema Corso better than the stainless steel of the telephone company's building.

## 1987–1989: the end

On 23 September 1987 the company that had applied, Co.Mo., represented by "Geometer Tossolini Pietro", as the minutes spell the name, asked the Province to issue the permit in place of the Comune. On 15 February 1988 the provincial councillor issued it.

A month later the council [debated](row:13.23) a building that was still standing. Communist and Green members had put questions. The Communists began by stating that the Fascist period had received "un'inappellabile condanna popolare", a popular condemnation without appeal, and went on to say that some buildings of the time had their own value all the same: the Corso and the former house of the Fascist girls' organisation by the Druso bridge. The councillor for planning answered with the chronology above and concluded: "aufgrund der heutigen Rechtslage" the cinema "abgebrochen und ein Neubau an seiner Stelle errichtet werden kann", as the law stands it can be demolished and a new building put in its place.

By 28 August 1989 it was gone. On that date a member of the neo-Fascist party tabled a question that listed it with two other losses: "In tempi recenti Bolzano ha visto la distruzione del ponte Druso, del cinema Corso e ora siamo alla volta dell'ex G.I.L." In recent times Bolzano has seen the destruction of the Druso bridge, of the Cinema Corso, and now it is the turn of the former girls' house. When the question [was debated](row:13.25) in February 1990 he spoke of "l'inerzia da parte dell'ente pubblico", the inertia of the public authority, which had led to the destruction "a fini speculativi", for speculative ends, of a fine work of architecture.

So the cinema was defended in the council by the Communists and the Greens, who condemned Fascism first, and afterwards mourned by the neo-Fascists, who suspected a wish to erase it. The governing party answered both with procedure. No order protecting the building as a monument appears anywhere in the record.

Rent for three floors of the new building, for a court, [was paid](row:13.26) from 1 November 1990.

## Afterwards

The sources that date the demolition to 1980, 1982 or 1987 are wrong; the minutes leave the months between April 1988 and August 1989.

The building is remembered with regret. A local magazine called its loss in 2024 "una ferita aperta", an open wound. The municipal theatre shows a photograph of it in its foyer. An exhibition of 2015 on the architecture of the 1930s in Bolzano found the city of those years nearly complete, "meno il Corso". In 2022 [one of Ronca's buildings](row:13.18), a church of 1970, was placed under protection.

No piece of the Corso is known to survive: not the green columns, not the marble stair, not the letters of its name.
