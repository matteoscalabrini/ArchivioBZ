# The offices, 1917–1961

Fascism called it "colonizzazione interna", internal colonisation: moving Italian families to land inside Italy that the state wanted peopled. The best-known case is the draining of the marshes south of Rome. This research asked what tied that machinery to the making of an Italian South Tyrol, and found the tie in three offices, a handful of laws and one vocabulary. This text follows the offices through time and then looks at the words.

The inquiry began from a brief with six mistakes in it, and correcting them was the first result. The migration office was not founded in 1926 and did not end in 1939. The land agency did not begin in 1919. A Senate bill thought to be about it is about small farms. A phrase believed to stand in a decree does not. And an expression assumed to be the regime's, "bonifica dei cognomi", the reclamation of surnames, was found nowhere. The dates and names below are the corrected ones.

## 1917–1927: veterans and land

The oldest of the three offices is the Opera Nazionale Combattenti, the ONC, [created](row:10.15) on 10 December 1917 to help the men who came back from the war. The second is a land agency for the countryside of the Veneto, the Ente di Rinascita Agraria, set up in 1920 or 1921 to restore farms the war had damaged.

The ONC turned to South Tyrol early. In 1926–27 the regime [planned with it](row:10.21) to expropriate 1,000 hectares from German-speaking tenants between Merano and Lavis and to settle 200 to 600 families from the old provinces there. "Lack of resources, foreign policy concerns, and the opposition of the Tyrolean tenants derailed the project." The state archive in Rome keeps a series of the ONC's papers under the heading "Servizio Agrario, Alto Adige, Coloni", agricultural service, Alto Adige, settlers.

Ettore Tolomei, the senator who had drawn up the programme for making the province Italian, said in 1927 why land mattered. Schools are an excellent thing, he wrote, "but schools bear only an ephemeral fruit if a group of Italian families do not settle in any of the Alto Adige villages, if Italian ownership [of the land] will not be extended". The sentence is known here in English only.

## 1930–1935: a commissariat for moving people

In 1926 a standing committee on internal migration had been formed at the Ministry of Public Works. In 1930 it was replaced by a commissariat directly under Mussolini, the Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione interna, built around the farm-union leader Luigi Razza, who ten years earlier had run the Fascist branch in Trento.

[Its law](row:10.01) of 9 April 1931 is worth reading closely. The office is placed "alle dirette ed esclusive dipendenze del Capo del Governo", under the direct and exclusive authority of the head of government. Prefects must report each month how many farming families in their province are "disposte a trasferirsi in altre Provincie", willing to move to other provinces. On any works paid for by the state the commissariat may order, "a suo giudizio insindacabile", by a judgement not open to appeal, that part of the labour be brought from elsewhere. And article 7: "Lo spostamento di gruppi di lavoratori e di famiglie coloniche da una Provincia per l'impiego in altra Provincia dovrà essere sempre disposto o autorizzato dal Commissariato." Every transfer of groups of workers and of settler families from one province to another must be ordered or authorised by the commissariat. A family settled for good "in zone di colonizzazione", in colonisation zones, could receive a premium of up to 6,000 lire.

The first great use of these powers was the Pontine marshes. Five towns were founded there between 1932 and 1938, and by 1939 the ONC had built 2,953 farms for 29,300 people. The commissariat chose them. [A study of the emigration from the Veneto](https://famigliaperuzzo.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/oscar_gaspari_lemigrazione_veneta_nellag.pdf) quotes the rules from a book of the time: the new settlers had to be country people "fedeli alla terra", faithful to the land, each family "unita tutta attorno al suo Capo", united round its head, from districts of high population pressure, veterans, and "di pura razza italiana". The Veneto fitted best. Of the 2,953 families, 1,748 came from there, counting Friuli, which is 59.2 per cent. Only one family in ten was from the marshes' own province. Most signed a plain lease.

In 1931, too, the land agency was given a new purpose. Renamed for "le Tre Venezie", the three Venetian regions, it was now [to buy land](row:10.21) "from owners belonging to the minority populations of South Tyrol and Venezia Giulia".

Razza became minister of public works in January 1935. On 7 March he countersigned [the decree](row:10.03) that let Bolzano expropriate the land for its industrial zone, at the price of farmland and with nothing for the tenants. He died that August.

## 1936–1938: thin results on the land

For all its powers the land agency moved little. By 30 September 1938, a note prepared for Mussolini reported, 325 farms and 8,933 hectares had passed "to Italian hands", which was "only 0.6 per cent of the productive agricultural surface of the new provinces". Tyrolean farms were intensive, expensive and hard to work for newcomers, and local buyers with German or Austrian money outbid the agency. In August 1938 the manager of an ONC estate asked permission to give some high farms to local German speakers, for want of Italian takers.

The demographic change came through the factories. But there, too, the commissariat was present: its Bolzano office is in the budget from 1936–37, as [another text in this library](row:0.02) sets out.

## 1939–1942: the Option

In 1939 everything tightened at once. [The law against urbanism](row:10.07) of 6 July made the commissariat the authority over any hiring from another province and tied residence in a provincial capital to a stable job. Its article 7 forbade farm workers who left "la terra" without good cause to register for other work.

In the autumn came the Option, the agreement under which German-speaking South Tyroleans chose between emigrating to the Reich and staying as Italians. On 21 December 1939 the commissariat sent a circular to the party's provincial secretaries. The settlers "destined to replace the South Tyroleans who would leave for Germany", it said, in the summary of the historian who [found it](row:10.22), "should come from the 56 northern and central provinces, north of Perugia". Southerners were shut out. The rule repeats, for South Tyrol, the choice made for the marshes: a settler body picked for its origin and reliability.

Three weeks earlier, on 27 November, [a law](row:10.08) had turned the land agency into the Ente nazionale per le Tre Venezie. Its text never mentions Alto Adige, the Option or those who opted. It says that the agency "può chiedere il trasferimento in sua proprietà di immobili, a chiunque appartengano", may demand the transfer into its ownership of real estate, whoever it belongs to. It provides for the "rimborso di differenze di cambio negli eventuali trasferimenti all'estero del valore dei beni rilevati", the refund of exchange differences when the value of property taken over is transferred abroad. The people leaving are present in the law only as money crossing a border.

Actual plans for who should take the farms were drawn up only in the winter of 1940–41. About 50,000 people had left by the end of 1940 and some 25,000 more by 1943, about a third of those who had opted.

The commissariat was put under the Ministry of Corporations from 1 March 1942, "per la durata dell'attuale guerra", for the duration of the present war.

## 1945–1961: what stayed

The offices did not end with the regime. In March 1949 a Senate bill [described](row:4.04) the land agency's position as "particolarissima", most peculiar: it administered the property of those who had opted for German nationality and was supposed to sell it. It still held 52 farms of 12 to 20 hectares in the Adige valley, leased year by year to tenants "provenienti da diverse zone, allettati - come furono - di conseguire la proprietà dei fondi affittati", come from various areas, lured as they had been by the prospect of owning the land they rented. According to [a review](row:10.23) of the one book on the subject, the agency blocked the sale of such property to German speakers until 1954. In 1955 parliament [gave it](row:10.11) five billion lire to settle Italian refugees from the lands lost to Yugoslavia.

The commissariat's law of 1931 and the law against urbanism were repealed together in 1961. [The bill](row:10.12) that first proposed it, in 1958, called them contrary to the Constitution's guarantee of free movement.

# The words

## A word for the others

The regime needed a name for the people already living in the provinces it had annexed, and it used one that avoided saying who they were. "Allogeni" means of another stock. A historian [explains](row:10.22) that the term signalled "their divergence from the national 'norm'" and also served "to avoid explicit mention of the presence of German, Croatian and Slovene populations". It entered the law in January 1926, in the title of a decree on revoking the Italian citizenship of allogeni found politically unworthy. In 1927 the regime's review *Gerarchia* ran an article headed "Il fascismo e gli allogeni". Its author signed himself Cobol. As Giuseppe Cobolli Gigli he was minister of public works from 1935 to 1939 and proposed the enlargement of Bolzano's industrial zone.

Surnames show the same habit. The research had expected to find "bonifica dei cognomi". What the regime said was "restituzione del cognome", the giving back of the surname. Tolomei wrote in his journal in 1935: "La restituzione del cognome, nelle province annesse, è legge dello Stato. Nella Venezia Giulia è quasi compiuta, nell'Alto Adige si viene attuando." The restitution of the surname in the annexed provinces is state law; in Venezia Giulia it is almost complete, in Alto Adige it is under way. He is quoted in [a study](row:10.16) of the language of those years. The word itself makes the claim: the name had been Italian to begin with.

## Two registers

Read together, the regime's words for South Tyrol fall into two registers.

The first is technical and legal: "colonizzazione interna", "famiglie coloniche", "bonifica", "trasformazione fondiaria", "pubblica utilità". In this register an operation in Alto Adige is named exactly as one in the Pontine marshes.

The second is national: "redenzione", "restituzione", "allogeni". Here the locals are Italians who have forgotten what they are. Mussolini told parliament in 1926 that of 180,000 German speakers 80,000 were Germanised Italians, and promised "to redeem them". The following year he spoke of "inhabitants of Alto Adige, whom we consider as Italian citizens who must rediscover themselves".

The second register is what separates South Tyrol from Libya, where the natives were subjects and nobody proposed to recover them. It is also what let settlement and assimilation be described as one policy.

Under both registers ran a cruder language, used in memoranda. In June 1919 a memorandum on "La penetrazione italiana nell'Alto Adige" proposed to create around the "intruding race" a "biological environment such that it must inadvertently yield", an environment "vigorously hyper-saturated with the seed of our race". In 1931 the party in Pola wanted to "inject abundant blood of pure Latin race into the population living along the border". These, too, are quoted here from English translations.

By the end the two registers no longer held. Tolomei, in 1939, called for a total exodus on the model of "the great Kemal", the exchange of populations between Greece and Turkey. The prefect of Bolzano wrote of the "illusion that it would be easy to 'assimilate' the German population of Alto Adige", "people of undoubtedly different origins, race and culture". And the foreign minister, visiting on 1 November 1938, had put the aim in his diary without any register at all: "L'aspetto della città si trasforma da nordico in Mediterraneo … Tra dieci anni o anche meno sarà difficile riconoscere in Bolzano la Bozen di un tempo." The look of the city is changing from Nordic to Mediterranean; in ten years or less it will be hard to recognise in Bolzano the Bozen of old.

## Still open

- Who headed the commissariat after Razza's death in 1935. A newspaper of January 1940 names Giuseppe Lombrassa as commissioner; the page itself has not been read.
- Whether the commissariat's yearly reports for 1936–39 count the transfers into the province of Bolzano. The volumes are not online.
- The full text of the circular of 21 December 1939.
- How much the land agency took over between 1939 and 1943, how many families it settled and from where. The one book that should say could not be read.
- The Italian originals of the sentences quoted here in English.
- Where the phrase "manodopera regnicola e non allogena" comes from. It is not in the decree of 1934.
