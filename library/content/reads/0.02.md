# A quarter for the factories, 1934–1943

South of the Isarco in Bolzano there was once a quarter of small houses, each with a vegetable garden. People called it the Semirurali, the half-rural houses, and from 8 October 1939 its official name was Rione Dux. Dense blocks replaced it from 1959. A few houses survived, the one at via Bari 11 among them.

The regime that built it was, in the same years, settling farming families on drained marsh south of Rome and on new farms in Libya. Bolzano's quarter looks like a relative of those villages: families brought from far away, a house, a plot, a square with a Fascist name. This research asked whether it was made with the same tools and went through them one at a time: the office, the law, the money, the designer, the house, the plot, the choice of settlers, the names, the ceremony, the words.

The answer is in the title. The drawings came from somewhere else. The offices were the same.

## 1934–1936: an institute, two decrees and marshy land

The institute that built the quarter was founded on 10 April 1934 as the "Istituto Case Economiche e Popolari di Bolzano". [Its first programme](row:8.09) was 500 dwellings "da destinare alla manodopera della nuova zona industriale", for the workforce of the new industrial zone. It bought land on the right bank of the Isarco that the land registry marked as marshy "e quindi facilmente acquistabili", and so easy to buy. Carla Giacomozzi, writing on the institute's photographs, [gives the purpose](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf) without decoration: to build in Alto Adige "gli alloggi per i lavoratori dell'industria provenienti da altre province italiane", homes for industrial workers coming from other Italian provinces.

The industrial zone came by decree. [The decree of 28 September 1934](row:10.02) offered state grants to the firms that built there, up to 25 million lire in all. [A second decree](row:10.03), of 7 March 1935, took the land. It paid the "puro valore venale del terreno considerato come fondo rustico", the bare market value of the ground counted as farmland. It ruled out any gain from "la progettata creazione della zona industriale", the planned zone, and gave tenants nothing for their cancelled leases.

Who was to work there is the best-known point of the story and the least documented. According to the historian Carlo Romeo, the commission that awarded the grants in 1936 [wanted labour](https://www.novecento.org/sul-confine-lalto-adige-sudtirol-nella-storia-del-novecento/il-fascismo-al-brennero-un-breve-profilo-per-questioni-7361/) "regnicola e non allogena": from the old kingdom, and not from the people the regime called *allogeni*, "of other stock", its word for the German speakers. This research read the 1934 decree in full, and the phrase is not in it. The decree leaves the rules for the grants to later ministerial decrees, which have not been found. Two other historians [describe the same practice](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf): grants in proportion to the workers hired, with the commission making clear that it meant "in erster Linie an Arbeiter aus Altitalien", first of all workers from old Italy.

They came. Once the plants opened, [writes Stefano Gallo](https://www.asei.eu/it/2017/05/operazioni-migratorie-belliche-transfrontaliere-e-rimpatri-da-una-parte-allaltra-delle-alpi/), "si fece massiccio l'ingresso di famiglie provenienti soprattutto dalle province di Padova, Verona e Rovigo": families arrived in mass, above all from the provinces of Padua, Verona and Rovigo.

## 1937–1938: an engineer from Venice

The only designer any document names is the institute's technical director, the engineer Giulio Garbini, "chiamato da Venezia", called from Venice. His signature is on [the general site plan](row:8.09) and on the drawing of a house type stamped by the institute, over the words "Il Direttore Tecnico". Bolzano's German-language daily, the *Alpenzeitung*, [names him](row:8.04) in December 1938 beside the institute's president, the engineer Rota. No biography of Garbini has been found, and no work of his in the colonies or in the Pontine marshes.

![Garbini's general site plan of the Semirurali: blocks of small houses on their plots.](../../../research_notes/Semirurali%20versus%20Libyan%20villages/files/01_semirurali_bolzano/TB01_p25_fig40_Planimetria_generale_case_semirurali_Garbini.jpg)
Giulio Garbini, *Planimetria generale, Case semirurali, Bolzano*, 1:2000, about 1938–39, as printed in *Turris Babel* 1 (1985), p. 25. Study copy; © Ordine and Fondazione Architetti of the Province of Bolzano. [Row 8.09](row:8.09).

He got his low houses through a gap in the town plan. The plan that Marcello Piacentini had drawn for Bolzano foresaw dense public housing here, 330 inhabitants per hectare on 24 hectares. Garbini [used article 19](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2729_Azzolini_ita.pdf) of the plan's rules, which allowed a "villino economico raggruppato", small low-cost houses in groups, "certo per zone diverse da quelle operaie intensive", certainly for zones other than the intensive workers' ones. On about 70 hectares he drew some thirty blocks, with "600 mq per 4 alloggi", 600 square metres for every four dwellings.

The houses had two storeys and mostly four dwellings, the upper two reached by an outside stair. [A flat](row:4.20) had two or three rooms on 40 to 60 square metres, 74 in the type built "per famiglie numerose", for large families. There was a kitchen niche and a toilet, and no bathroom, cellar or attic. The institute's own publicity of 1936, quoted in a study of 1982, said the outside stair recalled "la piccola casa di Predappio, dove il Duce ebbe i suoi natali", the small house at Predappio where the Duce was born.

No law ever defined a "casa semirurale". The quarter was built under [the ordinary public-housing code](row:8.01) of 1938, with contributions from the Ministry of Public Works. The Comune paid for streets and sewers, [starting with 996,400 lire](row:8.03) in 1938. Rents are known only for the same institute's half-rural houses in Bressanone. There, in June 1938, [three rooms](row:8.02) with "100 Quadratmeter Grund für Gemüseanbau", a hundred square metres of ground for growing vegetables, cost about 125 lire a month, and two rooms about 95. Nothing found suggests that a tenant could ever buy.

The type was not Bolzano's own. In those years housing institutes across Italy were told to build low and spread out, against the growth of the cities. The architect who wrote the quarter's history [quotes the aim](row:8.09), from directives he does not source: quarters arranged "in modo che la vita possa svolgersi senza l'obbligo di fare continuo ricorso alla città vicina, dalla quale si vogliono appunto distaccare le masse lavoratrici", so that life can go on without constant recourse to the nearby city, from which the working masses are precisely to be detached. In Turin the housing institute [built workers' villages](https://www.museotorino.it/resources/pdf/books/149/files/assets/common/downloads/page0370.pdf) of small houses "con annesso un terreno da orto", with a vegetable plot attached. In Milan the architects Bottoni and Pucci [drew](https://www.archiviobottoni.polimi.it/docs/archopere/architettura-urbanistica-e-design-1924-1973/op-178-progetto-di-borgate-semirurali-per-abitazioni-operaie-in-provincia-di-milano-1938-39-con-mario-pucci) "borgate semirurali per abitazioni operaie", half-rural settlements of workers' housing, and little of it was built. The Bolzano institute itself built the type in Merano, Vipiteno, Bressanone and at the Brenner. On this evidence the quarter in Bolzano is a large and complete case of a national type, and the type descends from the campaign against the cities.

## 1939: Rione Dux

The first lot, 92 houses, went up between 27 June 1938 and 22 April 1939. That April the Fascist daily *La Provincia di Bolzano* [announced](https://claudiaugusta-bolzano-scomparsa.provincia.bz.it/1939.html) what was coming: "Duecentoventotto graziose casette contornate di orti e giardini costituiranno il ridente quartiere destinato a ottocentodieci famiglie operaie", two hundred and twenty-eight pretty little houses ringed by vegetable plots and gardens, for eight hundred and ten workers' families.

In July the minister of public works, Giuseppe Cobolli Gigli, opened a new bridge over the Isarco. The newsreel of Luce, the state film institute, [said what it was for](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000021490/2/il-ponte-del-littorio.html): "il ponte unisce la zona industriale con il villaggio delle case popolari", the bridge joins the industrial zone to the village of public housing. On 3 August the *Alpenzeitung* [reported a competition](row:8.05) for the best gardens among "alle Bewohner der halbländlichen Häuser", all the residents of the half-rural houses. They had been given "ein kleines Stück Grund", a small piece of ground, with free seed and a gardening booklet. Garbini sat on the jury.

The inauguration was on 8 October 1939. Achille Starace, the secretary of the Fascist Party, came. There was an arch in the shape of an M, a blessing by the chaplain of the Fascist youth, a visit to one house, and a family of twelve put on show. The quarter took the name "Dux" that day. The *Alpenzeitung* [counted](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043421) 228 buildings "mit insgesamt 808 Wohnungen", with 808 dwellings in all, every building finished and all but 150 dwellings lived in, at a cost of 22 million lire. Luce's newsreel, three days later, [gave the same numbers](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022261/2/superba-realizzazione-del-regime-benessere-lavoratori-nella-provincia-del-brennero-inaugurata-dal-ministro-segretario-del-partito.html) and added eight shops. *La Provincia di Bolzano* [rounded up](row:8.06) to "trecento graziose villette con un complesso di un migliaio di appartamenti", three hundred pretty villas with a thousand flats.

![A page of La Provincia di Bolzano with photographs of the inauguration of the quarter.](../../../research_notes/Semirurali%20versus%20Libyan%20villages/files/01_semirurali_bolzano/LPB_1939-10-10_p3_inaugurazione_rione_Dux.jpg)
*La Provincia di Bolzano*, 10 October 1939, p. 3, on the inauguration of the Rione Dux; photographs by Foto Walsa. Scan from the Biblioteca Provinciale Italiana Claudia Augusta; study copy. [Row 8.06](row:8.06).

The central square was named piazza Pontinia, after one of the new towns of the drained marshes, and the streets after Italian cities: Palermo, Cagliari, Bari. In the neighbouring Littorio quarter a banner carried a motto of Mussolini's, which the *Alpenzeitung* [gives in German](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043496): "Land urbar machen und Städte gründen", to make land arable and found cities. The Italian wording has not been found.

## 1940–1943: the rest of the quarter

A second lot [was handed over](row:8.07) on 28 October 1940, and by May 1941 the *Alpenzeitung* [counted eight lots](row:8.08) in Rione Dux. In December 1941 another 110 buildings with 395 dwellings [were under construction](https://claudiaugusta-bolzano-scomparsa.provincia.bz.it/1941.html).

The services came after the houses: a nursery, a mother-and-child dispensary, public baths, the seat of the Fascist neighbourhood group and of the Dopolavoro, the eight shops of piazza Pontinia in 1940, the school in autumn 1941, the first work on a church that July. The institute's president also listed rooms "destinati alla disinfezione delle masserizie dei nuovi inquilini", for disinfecting the household goods of new tenants. That item reads more like the reception of settlers than like city housing. Nothing more is known about it.

How many houses there were in the end depends on who counts. The city's exhibition booklet gives 327 houses, 1,151 dwellings and about 5,500 inhabitants, but it reaches the total by adding the first lot to the 228 houses that already contain it. The sources of the time support this much: about 808 dwellings in 228 houses at the inauguration, and between 1,100 and 1,150 in about 327 houses once the last lots were finished after 1945. No table of the lots has been found.

German-speaking Bolzano had its own name for the workers' quarters across the river. It called them [Schanghai](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf).

# Farms, villages and a zoned city, 1890–1939

What was the toolkit the quarter might have shared? Italy put it together over fifty years and in several places, and the same few offices turn up in most of them.

## 1890–1914: Eritrea first

Italy's first colony tried the farm before anything else. In 1890 Leopoldo Franchetti was named "commissario per la colonizzazione" in Eritrea. [He set up](row:11.01) three experimental farms at Asmara, Godofelassi and Gura on "circa 4500 Ettari" declared state property. Peasant families "arruolati con atto notarile", enrolled by a notary's deed, were to work them and were promised ownership. Between fifteen and thirty families came. A revolt in December 1894 and the defeat at Adwa in 1896 [ended the scheme](row:11.20).

Asmara then became a city divided by law. Two decrees of the governor Giuseppe Salvago Raggi in 1908 [cut it into four zones](row:11.17). The first was reserved "esclusivamente per le abitazioni degli Italiani e dei Cittadini di Stati stranieri", for the homes of Italians and of foreign citizens only. The third was "riservata esclusivamente per le abitazioni dei sudditi coloniali e degli assimilati ad essi", for colonial subjects and those counted with them. Between the two lay a mixed zone around the market, and the fourth held suburbs and industry. A decree of 1914 repeated the ban on natives living in the first zone. All of this was a generation before Fascism.

## 1917–1931: the veterans' body and the migration office

Two offices of the Italian state did most of the later settling. The Opera Nazionale Combattenti, the ONC, was founded on 10 December 1917 for the veterans of the war. The Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione interna, the commissariat for migrations and internal colonisation, dates from 1930. [Its law of 9 April 1931](row:10.01) gave it the "razionale distribuzione della mano d'opera disponibile", the rational distribution of available labour, across the kingdom "e … anche nelle Colonie", and in the colonies too. No group of workers or of farming families could be moved from one province to another without its permission. Its first commissioner was Luigi Razza, who in 1920 had been secretary of the Fascist branch in Trento.

The ONC reached South Tyrol early. From 1923 it administered and sold the "enemy" properties confiscated under the peace treaty, which [were to go](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf) "ausschließlich an Italiener aus den 'alten Provinzen'", only to Italians from the old provinces. At Sinich near Merano a Montecatini fertiliser plant opened in 1924–25, and the regime favoured "die ausschließliche Anstellung von Italienern", the hiring of Italians only. In 1928 the ONC inaugurated a village there and called it Borgo Vittoria. Its Piazza Vittoria gathered the Dopolavoro, the school, the Carabinieri, the church and the office of the Fascist branch. The houses were built "im Stile von Einfamilienhäusern des Veneto", in the style of the single-family houses of the Veneto. Around them land was brought under the plough "oder Höfe von Südtirolern enteignet", or farms of South Tyroleans were expropriated. The settlers were chosen among war veterans. The streets were named after Cesare Battisti, Damiano Chiesa, Fabio Filzi and Nazario Sauro, the martyrs of Italian irredentism.

## 1932–1939: the marshes and Libya

South of Rome the ONC ran the draining and settling of the Pontine marshes, and the Commissariato chose the families. [A study of the Veneto emigration there](https://famigliaperuzzo.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/oscar_gaspari_lemigrazione_veneta_nellag.pdf) is blunt about it: "La scelta delle famiglie venete per colonizzare l'Agro Pontino, è fatta esclusivamente da un ente governativo, il C.M.C.I.", the choice of the Veneto families is made by one government body alone. By 1939 there were 2,953 ONC farms with 29,300 settlers, in five new towns and seventeen or eighteen villages, and 59.2 per cent of the families came from the Veneto, counted with Udine. Each family had a farm of [about 20 hectares](row:10.15). The rules asked for rural families of veterans, large ones, and "di pura razza italiana", of pure Italian race.

Libya divided the work in three. [A land agency](row:9.07), created for Cyrenaica in 1932 under "la presidenza dello stesso Commissario per le Migrazioni Interne, on. Luigi Razza", the presidency of the same commissioner for internal migrations, divided the land and built the farmhouses. The colonial public-works office built the village centres, with architects under contract: Mario Romano for the first four villages in 1933–34, then Florestano Di Fausto, Umberto Di Segni and Giovanni Pellegrini in 1938, Alfredo Longarini and others in 1939. The Fascist federations and the farm union put families forward. The Commissariato chose among them after a medical examination, the "raccolta delle informazioni politiche, professionali e morali", the gathering of political, professional and moral information, and a visit to the home. The governor, Italo Balbo, put the principle in one line: "Selezione fisica e morale adunque: binomio inscindibile", physical and moral selection, then: a pair that cannot be split.

![The piazza of a new village in Cyrenaica, with arcades and a church.](../../../research_notes/Semirurali%20versus%20Libyan%20villages/files/02_overseas_villages/commons/Villaggio_Battisti_DiFausto.jpg)
The piazza of Villaggio Battisti in Cyrenaica, designed by Florestano Di Fausto and built in 1938. Photographer unknown; public domain according to Wikimedia Commons. [Row 9.01](row:9.01).

A Libyan village was defined in law before anyone lived in it. A decree of 1938 makes the "centro rurale" "un nucleo di edifici destinati ad assicurare servizi pubblici", a core of buildings meant to provide public services: a church with its priest's house, schools, a clinic, the Casa del Fascio, a post office, markets. The farms measured 10 to 50 hectares. A family was paid wages for two years, sharecropped for three, and then began to pay off the farm, with ownership expected "in 19 anni", in nineteen years, or after about thirty. The villages were named after Fascist martyrs, aviators and irredentists: Battisti, Oberdan, Filzi, Sauro.

In 1938 the regime sent twenty thousand settlers at once, the "Ventimila", to 1,800 farms built in six months. They sailed on 28 October. The wave of 1939 carried 1,465 families and 10,907 people, with Padua (203 families), Treviso (193) and Venice (166) at the head.

## 1935–1939: Rhodes, Ethiopia, Asmara

On Rhodes the governor Mario Lago [built Campochiaro](row:9.11) in 1935–36 "per boscaioli delle nostre montagne", for woodcutters from our mountains. The recruiter was Giuseppe Valcanover, a forester from Trentino who had worked in Alto Adige from 1918 to 1932 and kept his family home in Bolzano. German-speaking South Tyrolean families followed the woodcutters of the Fiemme valley there in 1936. Most of them left in 1939, because they had opted for Germany. "Da Rodi noi dovemmo partire per le Opzioni", one of them remembered: from Rhodes we had to leave for the Options.

In Ethiopia three decrees of 6 December 1937 created settlement agencies named after Italian regions: Romagna, Veneto and Puglia d'Etiopia. The Veneto programme, never launched, [meant to recreate](row:9.08) "un ambiente simile a quello lasciato", a setting like the one left behind. The ONC ran two settlements of its own with war veterans, in houses of 60 square metres built to look "familiare ai coloni", familiar to the settlers. The Commissariato found only 56 of 90 families fit.

Asmara did not get its Italians through farms. Before 1935 it held some 3,000 to 4,000 Italians beside 12,000 to 20,000 Eritreans. Then came the war on Ethiopia and a building boom. The Italian touring club's guide of 1938 [prints](row:11.08) "ab. 98 000 (di cui 53 000 Italiani)", 98,000 inhabitants of whom 53,000 Italians. [Another count](https://journals.openedition.org/diacronie/1919) gives 48,000 Italians against 36,000 Africans in early 1939. No census stands behind either figure. As one historian [notes](row:11.12), "No proper census took place after 1931".

These Italians were workers on contract. Women were about a fifth of them, and contracts ran "a base semestrale", by the half year. Between October 1935 and June 1939 [more workers came home](row:9.08) from East Africa, 207,983, than were sent out, 201,440. And they were sent by the office already met in the marshes and in Libya: "L'invio di operai nell'Impero era gestito da Sergio Nannini, commissario per la colonizzazione e le migrazioni interne", the sending of workers to the Empire was run by Sergio Nannini, the commissioner, under a procedure that "sottostava alle stesse regole previste per la colonizzazione interna", followed the same rules as internal colonisation. Nannini set a quota for each province and the prefects split it among the towns. Candidates passed medical, occupational, political and police checks, and party and union officials drew up the lists. The Veneto sent the most: 39,871 of the 189,737 workers [counted up to September 1937](row:11.21). The guide of 1938 lists a delegation of the Commissariato among the offices of Asmara, in largo Camperio.

The planners sharpened the old line. The plan of 1937, by Guido Ferrazza, an architect born in Trentino, [promised](row:11.17) that "verrà accentuato il distacco della Città nazionale dal Quartiere indigeno", the detachment of the national city from the native quarter will be accentuated. For the workers' quarters at the edge it promised that "ogni abitazione avrà un giardinetto ed un orticello", every dwelling will have a small garden and a vegetable plot. Four "villaggi operai", workers' villages, were begun that year. One was named after Luigi Razza, who had died in August 1935 [on a flight from Cairo to Asmara](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000019726/2/i-funerali-luigi-razza-e-compagni-volo-periti-7-agosto-nell-incidente-aereo-cairo-e-l-asmara.html). The plan of 1938–39, by the Roman architect Vittorio Cafiero, kept the market as a buffer and moved a "Zona indigena" beyond hills to the north-west that were to become a "«barriera» verde", a green barrier.

Laws made the line personal. [A decree of 1937](row:11.07) punished a citizen's "relazione d'indole coniugale", a relation of a conjugal kind, with a colonial subject with one to five years in prison. [A law of 1939](row:11.10) on the "prestigio di razza", the prestige of the race, punished the citizen who habitually went to public places reserved for natives. On the street, according to [the file](row:11.18) that later put Asmara on the World Heritage List, "Eritreans were forbidden from using or crossing Viale Mussolini".

By 1939 the toolkit was this: a migration office that picked people by province, health and politics; land agencies; architects under contract for the village centres; farms redeemed over decades; names from the Fascist and irredentist calendar; and, in the colonies, law that sorted people by race.

# What reached Bolzano, 1935–1961

## On the drawing board, nothing

Set beside those villages, the Semirurali differ item by item. The builder was a housing institute and the law was the housing code. The money came from the housing line of the Ministry of Public Works. The designer was a housing engineer with no known colonial work, and the tenants rented. The researchers found no Libyan architect at work in Bolzano and no colonial farmhouse plan reaching the institute. A Libyan family was to redeem a farm of 10 to 50 hectares. A Bolzano family rented a flat with about 150 square metres of ground, the footprint of the house included.

What the two share is a doctrine of the 1930s, which was to fix the family to the soil. In Libya after 1938 the farmhouse [stood alone on its land](row:9.07) "con lo specifico scopo di avvicinare anche psicologicamente il colono al proprio terreno", with the specific aim of bringing the settler closer to his land, psychologically too. In Bolzano the regime's magazine [praised](https://storiaeregione.eu/attachment/get/up_126_16520955686792.pdf) gardens "ove gettare un seme e raccogliere un frutto … per non dimenticare del tutto le origini rurali e attaccarvisi anzi maggiormente", where to sow a seed and gather a fruit, so as not to forget one's rural origins altogether and to hold to them all the more.

If a house type travelled at all, it went the other way. Asmara's workers' villages with their vegetable plots were begun in 1937, the year of Garbini's first proposal, and in 1938 the planning journal *Urbanistica* [argued](row:11.17) that workers' villages in the colonies should follow the European ones of "Italia e Germania". On this evidence the type went from Italy to the colony.

The colonial drawing board did reach South Tyrol, ten years before the Semirurali and at another place. Borgo Vittoria at Sinich has the veterans' body, the piazza with church and Fascist office, the expropriated farms and the street names of the Libyan villages. That is the twin. The Semirurali continue Sinich's other half, the factory that hired Italians.

## The office was in Bolzano

The Commissariato's Bolzano office appears in the state budget for 1936–37 and was at work from April 1937. Its task, in a document [quoted by Stefano Gallo](https://www.asei.eu/it/2017/05/operazioni-migratorie-belliche-transfrontaliere-e-rimpatri-da-una-parte-allaltra-delle-alpi/), was the control of "i rapporti di lavoro degli operai che vengono trasferiti in codesta provincia da altre provincie del regno", the employment of the workers transferred into this province from other provinces of the kingdom, in "armonia con la Federazione dei Fasci di Combattimento", in harmony with the federation of the Fascist party.

In 1939 the law gave it more. [The law against urbanism](row:10.07) of 6 July 1939 made the Commissariato the authority over hiring from other provinces. It also barred anyone from taking up residence in a provincial capital or an industrial town without "una proficua occupazione stabile", a stable and gainful job. For a tenant of the Semirurali, on this research's reading, the job, the residence and the flat were now one chain.

Then came the Option. In 1939 Italy and Germany agreed that South Tyroleans should choose between German citizenship, with emigration to the Reich, and staying as Italians. On 21 December 1939 a circular of the Commissariato to the party's provincial secretaries [ruled](row:10.22) that the settlers "destined to replace the South Tyroleans who would leave for Germany should come from the 56 northern and central provinces, north of Perugia". The English is the historian Andrea Di Michele's. The circular is in the central state archive in Rome, and this research has not seen it. A month later *La Provincia di Bolzano* [reported](https://claudiaugusta-bolzano-scomparsa.provincia.bz.it/1940.html) a meeting chaired by "Giuseppe Lombrassa, Commissario per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione" on "la immigrazione dei lavoratori necessari per la sostituzione degli allogeni e tedeschi trasferentisi in Germania", the immigration of the workers needed to replace the allogeni and Germans moving to Germany.

So the office that chose the families for the marshes and for Libya, and set the quotas for Asmara, also governed who could move to Bolzano to work, and then who was to replace those who left. It did not hand out the flats. That was the housing institute's business. The office outlived the plan: from 1 March 1942 it was moved under the Ministry of Corporations "per la durata dell'attuale guerra", for the duration of the present war, and its law of 1931 was repealed only in 1961.

## The same men

Luigi Razza was the Commissariato's first head, from 1930 to 1935, and from 1932 president of the land agency for Cyrenaica. As minister of public works he countersigned the decree of 1935 that took the land of Bolzano's industrial zone at farmland prices. A village in Libya, a workers' village in Asmara and the pier in Naples from which southern colonists sailed carried his name.

Giuseppe Cobolli Gigli was minister of public works from 5 September 1935 to 31 October 1939. He [proposed the enlargement](row:10.05) of the industrial zone in 1936, and his ministry oversaw and subsidised the housing institutes, so the Semirurali were designed, built and opened under him. In 1937–38 Luce [filmed him](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000024252/2/la-rete-stradale.html) inspecting the roads of the empire in East Africa; in July 1939 he opened the bridge to the Semirurali. Count Volpi, a former governor of Tripolitania, [inaugurated the industrial zone](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000027681/2/il-ministro-benni-e-conte-volpi-inaugurano-nuovo-centro-industriale-bolzano.html) in December 1936. Arturo Gemmiti was Luce's art director both for the newsreel of the Rione Dux and for [the newsreels of the Ventimila](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000030534/2/l-arrivo-20-000-rurali-italiani-africa.html).

On screen the two events look alike: a party leader, a priest's blessing, families on show, kitchen gardens. The caption is what changes, from "benessere dei lavoratori", the workers' welfare, to "colonizzazione demografica".

The families, too, came from the same part of the country. The Pontine settlers were mostly from Treviso, Udine, Padua and Rovigo, the Libyan wave of 1939 from Padua, Treviso and Venice, Bolzano's workers from Padua, Verona and Rovigo. Nothing shows that the three were run as one programme. And people [went the other way](row:9.11) as well: families from Trentino to Cyrenaica in 1938–39, and the South Tyroleans to Rhodes.

## The words

The researchers read the report of *La Provincia di Bolzano* on the inauguration, nine pages of the *Alpenzeitung* from 1938 to 1941, and a digest of the Fascist daily for 1937–43. None of them calls the Semirurali a "colonia" or a "bonifica", a reclamation, or speaks of "colonizzazione". The quarter is a "rione", a "villaggio semirurale", a "città operaia", in German an "Arbeiterstadt". "Colonia operaia Rione Dux" appears only in [a municipal text of 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf).

The reclamation words stood around it all the same: the motto on the banner, the square named after Pontinia. And in South Tyrol the vocabulary of colonisation was used in earnest for the land. The ONC's archive has a series called "Servizio Agrario, Alto Adige, Coloni". [The law of 1939](row:10.08) that turned an older land agency into the one that took over the property of those who opted for Germany speaks of "opere di bonifica, trasformazione e riordinamento della proprietà fondiaria", works of reclamation, transformation and reordering of landed property.

Ettore Tolomei, the senator who had written the programme for making South Tyrol Italian, thought it far too little. In a note [quoted by two historians](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf), undated, he complained: "L'azione statale colonizzatrice sul modello di Roma antica s'è limitata ad una decina di masi dati ai combattenti nel Meranese. Goccia d'acqua in vasta arena … L'Agro Pontino conta interi villaggi di veneti. Non era male per i destini della Patria, se qualche centinaio di famiglie venete s'introduceva nelle valli atesine." The state's colonising action on the model of ancient Rome has stopped at ten or so farms given to veterans near Merano, a drop of water in a vast arena; the Pontine marshes count whole villages of Veneto people; it would have done the fatherland no harm if a few hundred Veneto families had been brought into the valleys of the Adige.

The numbers bear him out. By 30 September 1938 the land agency [had moved](row:10.21) 325 farms and 8,933 hectares, "only 0.6 per cent of the productive agricultural surface of the new provinces".

## Where Asmara differs

Asmara is the control case: a city of the same years, filled by the same office, where the division was written into law. Set against it, Bolzano differs at four points.

The first is the legal person. Eritreans were subjects and South Tyroleans were citizens. Asmara zoned the native. Bolzano, in 1939, tried to move him out through the Option. No decree found barred a German speaker from the new city or an Italian from the old town. The workers' quarters were set apart, two historians [write](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf), "vor allem zur Vermeidung von sozialen Konflikten mit dem Mittelstand in der Città nuova", above all to avoid social conflict with the middle class of the new city, and the separation "verschärfte die ethnische Teilung der Stadt dramatisch", sharpened the ethnic division of the city dramatically. It was social in intent and ethnic in effect.

The second is the settlers: in Asmara men on six-month contracts, more of whom went home than came; in Bolzano families in rented flats, who stayed. The third is scale. Asmara's Italians rose from about 4,000 to about 50,000 in five years. In Bolzano the Italian speakers [went](row:10.21) from 21 per cent in 1921 to 62 per cent in 1939. The fourth is the outcome. Asmara's Italian majority dissolved after 1941, and Bolzano's did not.

## What historians make of it

Whether any of this should be called colonial is argued among historians. Roberta Pergher [holds](row:10.20) that "it was in these territories, and not in Ethiopia, that the signature Fascist policy of extending Italianness through mass population settlement was developed and put into practice". Her witness is Tolomei, who in 1938 praised the "splendid exodus to Libya of twenty thousand settlers" and wanted "at least a few dozen families in every municipality" of Alto Adige too.

Emmanuel Dalle Mulle and Alessandro Ambrosino [answer](row:10.21) that land colonisation "was never meant as a total replacement for the assimilation of the local population, but rather as a catalyst to accelerate this", and that the regime's means were always short of its ambitions. Andrea Di Michele [tests](row:10.22) the comparison with colonial subjects and mostly rejects it: "Severe discriminatory measures … regarding citizenship rights were not in fact taken", and the regime described the South Tyrolean peasant as "a condensate of virtues that largely coincided with those that the regime promised to instil in all Italians".

The objections are strong. The tenants of the Semirurali held no Tyrolean land. The farms mostly stayed with their owners, and the locals kept their citizenship. The Italian majority of Bolzano came through factories and rented flats.

This research's own reading rests on a definition by the historian Patrick Wolfe, for whom settler [colonisation](https://doi.org/10.1080/14623520601056240) "is a structure not an event" and assimilation is one of the forms it takes. Read that way, the critics describe how the absorption of the locals was meant to work; they do not deny that settlement was the instrument. What can be defended is a claim about means: settlers filtered by the state, land taken on punitive terms, hiring that left out the locals, all run by the regime's colonisation office. Replacement was openly planned only in the years of the Option, from 1939 to 1943, and even then about two thirds of those who opted for Germany never left.

## After 1945

Some of the instruments went on working. The land agency [blocked the sale](row:10.23) of optants' property to German speakers until 1954. In 1949 it was [still leasing](https://www.parlamento.it/service/PDF/PDFServer/DF/353447.pdf) 52 farms in the Adige valley to tenants "provenienti da diverse zone", from various areas, on a promise of ownership. In 1957 the central committee of the South Tyrolean People's Party accused Rome of changing "il carattere popolare del nostro paese", the character of our people, and [wrote](row:8.09): "Come mezzo principale … l'Italia si serve dell'edilizia popolare". As its principal means, Italy uses public housing. From 1959 the Semirurali were rebuilt as dense blocks on the land Garbini had laid out.

The people who lived there made the quarter theirs. A family of optants who had come back called the flat in via Bari they were given in 1949 "eine große Errungenschaft", a great achievement. Another resident [remembered](row:4.20): "Siamo diventati tutti bolzanini", we all became people of Bolzano.

Asmara went the other way. Its colonised majority took over the colonisers' city after 1941 and kept it almost whole. UNESCO [listed it](http://web.archive.org/web/20261011124247/https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1550/) on 8 July 2017 and named, as part of what the city shows, "colonial planning, based on functional and racial segregation principles".

## Still open

- Who Giulio Garbini was: his dates, his training, his earlier post in Venice.
- Who the first tenants were and by what rules they got a flat. The housing institute's registers have not been seen.
- The ministerial rules behind "regnicola e non allogena", and the minutes of the commission of 1936.
- The Italian wording of the motto on the banner of 8 October 1939.
- The full text of the circular of 21 December 1939.
- Who designed and paid for the four workers' villages of Asmara.
