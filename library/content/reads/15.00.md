# What the camera was shown, 1926–1940

The Fascist state filmed and photographed Bolzano for nearly twenty years, and it decided what the people of Bolzano could watch. This text takes the two sides in turn. The first part goes through the films and photographs that survive in the archive of Luce, the state film institute, in the order they were made, and asks what is in the frame and what is not. The second follows film and radio as they reached the town and the valleys: the travelling cinema, the censor, the renamed picture house, the transmitter in Gries.

A wider account of how Italians elsewhere met Bolzano through newsreel and radio is [in another text](row:2.00) of this library. This one looks at the pictures more closely.

## 1926: the words before the pictures

The pictures followed a speech. On 6 February 1926 Mussolini [told the Chamber](row:15.02) that the Brenner was "un confine segnato dalla mano infallibile di Dio", a border drawn by the infallible hand of God. "I tedeschi nell'Alto Adige non rappresentano una minoranza nazionale, rappresentano una reliquia etnica." The Germans of the Alto Adige are not a national minority; they are an ethnic relic. Fascist Italy, he said, could carry its flag further if necessary, "abbassarlo mai", lower it never. The record notes that the deputies then sang the party hymn. In the same speech he announced a monument for Bolzano.

No recording of the speech was found. The one state series of recorded speeches that turned up begins in 1935.

That July the king laid the monument's first stone. The censor's register [lists](row:15.01) a film of it, *S.M. il Re a Bolzano*, 250 metres long, approved in August 1926. Luce's own catalogue has a record of the same event under 1927 and no film to go with it.

## 1928: the inauguration

The film of the inauguration on 12 July 1928 [can be watched](row:15.04): four minutes and forty-three seconds, silent. The catalogue lists its shots. One note is unintended comedy. The minister Giuriati gives the official speech, "ma è nascosto dalla feluca del valletto": he is hidden by the cocked hat of a footman.

Luce's photographers were there too. [Eleven photographs](row:15.05) are filed under the title "S.M. il Re inaugura il monumento ai caduti di Bolzano". The institute's own caption calls the Monument to Victory a monument to the fallen. One caption describes local notables posing on top of it, among them "una giovane signora che indossa un elegante abito alla moda", a young lady in an elegant fashionable dress, "tra le enormi colonne che raffigurano dei fasci littori", between the enormous columns in the form of lictors' bundles.

## 1928–1938: the province photographed in Rome

This library holds [a list of 144 Luce photographs](row:15.03) found under the words Bolzano, Brennero and Alto Adige. Sorted by where they were taken, they give a result nobody expected: 40 in Rome and 14 at Ostia, against 21 in Bolzano. Luce photographed the province more often arriving at the centre than at home. There are union delegates from South Tyrol with Mussolini in 1928, the bands of the Brenner and Bressanone in 1929, the province's young Fascists at the Vittoriano in 1931, its girls on the beach at Ostia in 1932, "250 giovani fasciste di Bolzano" in the courtyard of Palazzo Venezia in 1938.

Seventeen of the "Bolzano" photographs show no place at all. They are of the cruiser of that name.

## 1935–1936: the Duce, a prince, a count

In late August 1935 Mussolini came, and Luce made a sound film of fourteen minutes, [*Italia. Bolzano*](row:15.09). He stands in his car returning the salute; "un gigantesco ritratto di Mussolini è appeso ad un palazzo", a giant portrait of him hangs on a building; he pays homage before the busts of the three martyrs inside the monument. The second half of the film is the army's manoeuvres, and the record places them in the Val di Non, which is in the province of Trento. The newsreel title that puts them "nel Bolzanino" stretches the geography.

At the Brenner [the camera watches](row:15.10) as "il Duce arriva sul cippo del confine", the Duce arrives at the boundary stone. In the frame this library holds, a man in the crowd is raising a camera of his own.

In October 1936 the Prince of Piedmont [visits the building sites](row:15.12). The record lists a banner of the housing institute, plans of a new school, and then "strada sventrata con lavori in corso" and "operai intenti alla demolizione di un edificio": a gutted street, workers demolishing a building. It is the only time the films show something being taken away. That December Count Volpi [opens the industrial zone](row:15.13).

## 1938: Hitler's train, a roll call, a rally of thanks

In May 1938 Hitler crossed the Brenner on his way to Rome. Luce's long film of the journey [shows](row:15.15) "la svastica sul muso della locomotiva", the swastika on the nose of the locomotive, and then Bolzano station, where a great crowd cheers the passing train among flags and fasces. A Luce photographer on the train [took fifteen pictures](row:15.16). One caption notes "un fotografo in divisa tedesca ed uno in divisa fascista", a photographer in German uniform and one in Fascist uniform, photographing the same scene.

On 22 May the party secretary read out at the monument the names of the province's dead in Spain and Africa. [The newsreel](row:15.17) also shows 500 cars of the Automobile Club parading through the town. In the frame held here a car carries two horn loudspeakers on its roof.

In September came the one event at which German-speaking South Tyroleans are the subject. War invalids, widows and orphans of the old Austro-Hungarian army were gathered in Piazza della Vittoria to thank the regime for measures in their favour. [The newsreel's record](row:15.19) lists the crowd in the rain under open umbrellas, and then: "un fascista ordina il saluto a Mussolini e la gente lo esegue". A Fascist orders the salute to Mussolini and the people perform it. Luce's photograph of the day [shows](row:15.18) women in traditional dress holding flowers. The archive files it under "Etnografia ed etnologia".

## 1939–1940: a bridge, a quarter, the border

In July 1939 a minister [opens a bridge](row:15.20) that "unisce la zona industriale con il villaggio delle case popolari", joins the industrial zone to the village of public housing. On 11 October a newsreel [presents](row:15.21) that village, the Rione Dux, with its vegetable plots. Ten days later the rules for the Option were signed, under which the German-speaking population was to choose between leaving and staying. Nothing in the film hints at it.

In March 1940 Mussolini and Hitler [meet](row:15.22) at the Brenner station in the snow. They met there again on 4 October. No film of that second meeting was found, but [seven photographs](row:15.23) were.

## Not in the frame

Taken together the films show one thing: the state arriving. A king, a Duce, princes, ministers, a party secretary, Hitler's train. The people of the place are the crowd that receives them. German speakers appear in three roles, as a band in costume in 1928, as grateful widows and orphans in 1938, and as names on a list of the fallen. No film lets one of them speak.

The records list the shots and not the commentary, so what the newsreel voice said over these pictures is still unheard.

Missing altogether are the Option and the departures, the old town as a living German-speaking city, anyone displaced by the new quarters, and a word of German.

## Other cameras

Little from outside could be seen for this research. Austria's official newsreel is telling by its silence. In the years when Vienna depended on Mussolini it reported on Italy often. A search of 154 issues with Italian stories found [one](https://www.europeana.eu/item/08621/13766472067491376647206749) on the region, "In den Dolomiten", in October 1935. British newsreels covered the dictators' meeting at the Brenner in March 1940. A German film catalogue lists [a short documentary](https://www.filmportal.de/en/node/1455585) of 1944 on the resettlement of the South Tyroleans, twelve minutes long. None of these could be watched.

# In the cinema and on the air

## 1927–1934: film comes to the valleys

In the German-speaking villages film first arrived in a van. From 15 June 1927 the veterans' organisation sent a travelling cinema through the region with "landwirtschaftliche und patriotische Films", agricultural and patriotic films, meant also for the small rural communes and mountain villages. The Dopolavoro, the regime's leisure body, had one by 1930.

In the summer of 1934 Luce itself [toured the province](row:15.08). The *Alpenzeitung*, the regime's German-language daily, printed the route: sixty stops, with teaching films for farmers and "die letzten Weltereignisse", the latest world events. On the same page stands a notice about setting up private schools for the teaching of German, which the state schools no longer gave.

The vans kept running. In September 1941 a motor cinema of the Fascist farmers' union gave three "Propagandavorstellungen" near Merano to some 3,000 farmers.

## 1930: no foreign word

A national rule shaped what could be heard. On 22 October 1930 the ministry of the interior [ordered](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7f86023v) that no permit be given to films "che contengano del parlato in lingua straniera sia pure in qualche parte e in misura minima", that contain speech in a foreign language even in some part and in the smallest measure. Between 1929 and 1933, 486 foreign sound films were released in Italy with their voices removed. In which language German films were then heard in Bolzano has not been settled. The cinema advertisements in the German paper give German titles for German and Italian films alike.

## 1935–1937: two films kept from two provinces

The best-known film-maker of the region was Luis Trenker of Val Gardena. His German mountain films were welcome in Italy, and in 1936 one of them won the Mussolini Cup at Venice. The censor's register, which this library [holds as a list](row:15.01), shows what happened to two of them at home.

*Il figliuol prodigo*, the story of a mountaineer who emigrates to New York and returns, was approved "con riserva", with a reservation: "Le proiezioni in pubblico del film … sono vietate nelle Provincie di Bolzano e Trento." Public screenings are forbidden in the provinces of Bolzano and Trento. That was September 1935. In January 1937 the same was done to the film that had won the cup, *L'imperatore della California*: "La proiezione di questa pellicola è vietata nelle Provincie di Trento e Bolzano."

The register gives no reasons. It is the clearest case found of film being managed for the German-speaking audience in particular. Trenker's *Condottieri* of 1937, with its Italian hero, was registered as a national film, which counted towards the screenings that cinemas were obliged to give.

A third film passed. The permit of November 1940 for *Der Feuerteufel* [summarises](row:15.24) the story of a woodcutter who leads a rising against Napoleon "nella Carinzia", in Carinthia, and sets no limit for the two provinces. By then the Option was under way.

## November 1935: a cinema changes its name

On 3 November 1935 the League of Nations voted sanctions against Italy for the invasion of Ethiopia. They were to take effect on the 18th. In Bolzano the *Alpenzeitung* listed a cinema called the Edenkino until 26 November. On 27 November [it is](row:15.11) the "Cinema Luce (ex Edenkino)". A house with an English-sounding name, which was also the name of the British minister who stood for the sanctions, took the name of the regime's film institute. That is how one later account explains it; no document of the time was found.

The same page reports an outing: the pupils of the commercial school had been taken by their teachers to the Central cinema to see a documentary on the war, "Von Adua nach Aksum", from Adwa to Aksum.

## 1928–1943: the transmitter

The state radio's Bolzano station went on air on 12 July 1928, with 200 watts, from a building in Gries. Within weeks listeners far away were writing in. The broadcaster's weekly [printed their letters](row:15.06) that September. From Cagliari: "ho captato l'onda di Bolzano", I picked up Bolzano's wave, a dance tune and the agency news. From Forlì: the tourist board's bulletin from Bolzano, "forte e chiaro in altoparlante", loud and clear on the loudspeaker, but spoiled by a whistle from the station at Berne. From a village near Pisa: "ora udiamo Bolzano … tutte le sere", now we hear Bolzano every evening.

A year later the weekly [admitted](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/20s/1929/RC-1929-29.pdf) that "la piccola stazione di Bolzano" was of all Italian stations the one most damaged by interference, since it shared its wavelength with others. So the station was weak and drowned out by day, and at night it carried the name of Bolzano to Sardinia and Tuscany. In a small way it did for the ear what the monument did for the eye.

A film Luce made with the broadcaster in 1937–38 [counts](row:15.14) "la stazione di Bolzano" among the stations of the network. The frame held here shows a microphone engraved with dates on which the Duce and the queen spoke into it.

Searches of the *Alpenzeitung* for 1939 to 1943 found no programme in German from Bolzano. The paper printed the German news times of other countries' stations.

## Still open

- The spoken commentary of the films. They can be watched, and nobody in this research has yet sat down and written out what is said.
- Why the two Trenker films were banned in the two provinces. The ministry's files would say.
- Whether German-language sound films were shown in Bolzano after 1930, and how.
- Where exactly the transmitter of 1928 stood, and what the station did under the German occupation.
- British, German and amateur film of the province, which needs to be looked for by hand.
