# On screen and on air, 1927–1943

Most Italians of the 1930s never saw Bolzano. They met it all the same: in the newsreel before the film, on the radio, on a cheap excursion train, through a son in barracks there, in a schoolbook. This text follows those channels one after the other and asks what each of them carried.

It starts with the two that left the best record, film and radio. A second part takes the trains, the army and the school.

## The newsreel

Luce, the state film institute, made the newsreels shown in Italian cinemas before the feature. Its catalogue is online, and this research searched it under 47 words: place names from Bolzano to the Stelvio, and themes from "monumento alla vittoria" to "treni popolari". What came back, once every record had been opened, is in this library as two lists: [the newsreel stories](row:2.02) and [the documentaries](row:2.01) that name a place in South Tyrol or the Brenner. There are at least 163 newsreel stories between 1928 and 1943 and about 45 documentaries. The catalogue does not say where or how often any of them was screened.

The first long film is silent and dated 1927: [*Visita di S.M. il Re a Bolzano*](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000085506/1/-1680.html), the king in Bolzano, a speech by Carlo Delcroix, the first stone of the Monument to Victory, a cavalcade of South Tyroleans in costume. The tourist films of the same years are silent too. One of them, [*Bolzano e i suoi dintorni*](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000051159/1/-1218.html), walks through Piazza Walther and the arcades and stops at "un possente masso dove verrà posta una statua dedicata a Cesare Battisti", a mighty boulder where a statue to Cesare Battisti will be placed.

Coverage is thin until 1931. The exception is July 1928, when one reel carried three stories from the province. [The inauguration of the monument](row:15.04) on 12 July runs four minutes and forty-three seconds: priests bless it, the king sits in his stand while the minister Giuriati speaks, Italo Balbo is beside him, a "banda musicale altoatesina" plays, the girls and boys of the Fascist youth march past. [*Visita altoatesina a Roma*](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048647/2/visita-altoatesina-roma.html) shows South Tyroleans in costume climbing the Capitol and meeting Mussolini. In the third the Duchess of Aosta [lays flowers](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048751/2/al-brennero-elena-d-orleans-posa-prima-pietra-del-nuovo-asilo-l-infanzia.html) at the boundary stone on the Brenner.

![A frame from a silent film: a crowd and flags before a white monument.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/15_propaganda_film_radio/images/luce_film_A0137_1928_still.jpg)
*A Bolzano inaugurazione del monumento alla Vittoria d'Italia*, Giornale Luce A0137, July 1928: a still. Archivio Storico Istituto Luce; study copy. [Row 15.04](row:15.04).

Those three stories already hold the pattern of the next fifteen years. Sorted by subject, the newsreels show four things.

The first is an Italian Bolzano on a stage. The monument is the backdrop for the reading of the Duce's message to the Blackshirts in October 1933, for the offering of wedding rings in December 1935, for Mussolini's own visit. Three reels released on 4 September 1935 show him [in the city](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020037/2/la-visita-mussolini-alla-citta-bolzano.html), [at the Brenner](row:15.10), where "il Duce arriva sul cippo del confine", the Duce arrives at the boundary stone, and at the army's manoeuvres. A sound documentary of fourteen minutes, [*Italia. Bolzano*](row:15.09), went with them. From 1936 the stage is also a building site. Count Volpi [opens the industrial zone](row:15.13) in December 1936 and the party walks into "uno stabilimento 'Lancia e C.'". The Prince of Piedmont [visits](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000029846/2/la-visita-del-principe-piemonte.html) blast furnaces and an aluminium works in June 1938. In October 1939 a reel presents [the new workers' quarter](row:15.21), "case a due piani con un piccolo terreno", two-storey houses each with a small plot.

The second is Merano and the Dolomites at play: the motorcycle Six Days, tennis, dog shows, the horse race tied to the national lottery, flower parades, ski championships. Sport is the largest single group, about forty stories.

The third is the Brenner. It begins as a sacred border, with the boundary stone as the object every visitor touches. From 1938 it is the door of the Axis. In March of that year trains of Italian farm workers [cross to Germany](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000033074/2/il-passaggio-treni-rurali-migliaia-agricoltori-italiani-diretti-germania.html). In May Hitler's train [passes through Bolzano station](row:15.15), where "una grande folla accoglie acclamante il passaggio del treno", a great crowd acclaims the passing train. On 18 March 1940 Mussolini and Hitler [meet in the snow](row:15.22) at the Brenner station, and two months later the reels show German coal arriving.

The fourth runs the other way. Groups from the province are filmed arriving in Rome: girls from Bolzano [running to the sea](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000032791/2/giovani-bolzanine-gita.html) at Ostia in 1932, a thousand boys of the "Legione Marco Druso" reviewed by Mussolini in 1935, 350 young women at Palazzo Venezia in 1938.

Two years stand out by number: 25 stories in 1935, 26 in 1938. They are the years of Mussolini's visit, at the time of the mobilisation for Ethiopia, and of the Anschluss, Hitler's journey and the princes' tour. After 1940 the stories fall away, and what is left is mostly the Brenner.

What a viewer in Naples or Bari did not see is as clear. German speakers appear almost only as costume: bands, dances, woodcarvers. They are not shown as people with politics. The one exception found is [a rally of September 1938](row:15.19), where war-disabled men and orphans of the old Austro-Hungarian army were assembled in Piazza della Vittoria to express "riconoscenza", gratitude, to the regime.

And there is nothing on the Option of 1939, the agreement under which South Tyroleans had to choose between Germany and Italy. Searches for "opzione", "optanti" and "rimpatrio" found no story about South Tyrol. "Rimpatrio" does find the German resettlers [from the Baltic](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022891/2/il-rimpatrio-tedeschi-dal-baltico-croce-rossa-tedesca-e-organizzazioni-assistenziali-naziste-hanno-preso-cura-questi-rimpatriati.html) in December 1939 and from Bessarabia a year later, so Luce filmed the same policy elsewhere. A keyword search cannot prove that nothing was shot. It fits a regime that had no wish to advertise the Option.

The city's name also travelled without the city. In September 1932 the newsreels [showed the launch](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000034894/2/il-varo-della-nave-bolzano-31-agosto-1932.html) of the heavy cruiser *Bolzano* at Sestri Ponente.

## The feature film

For Italian audiences the South Tyrolean on screen was one man. Luis Trenker, born at Ortisei in Val Gardena, directed and starred in German mountain films, and the Italian state [paid for a large part](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Trenker) of his *Condottieri* of 1937, in which a mountain man from a German-speaking valley plays an Italian captain of the Renaissance. The film opened in Rome on 30 June 1937 and was shown at Venice that August.

The first notes on this could not find how his German films had fared with the Italian censor. Later research did, in the censorship records this library holds as [a list](row:15.01). Two of his films were approved for Italy and banned from public screens in the provinces of Bolzano and Trento alone: *Il figliuol prodigo* in September 1935 and *L'imperatore della California* in January 1937. The first of them is about a mountaineer who leaves home for New York and comes back. It could be shown in Milan and not in Bolzano.

No Italian feature of these years set in German-speaking Alto Adige could be confirmed. Italian cinema seems to have used the Dolomites as a general Alpine and war landscape. That impression has not been checked against a full filmography.

## The radio

The state broadcaster, EIAR, put a Bolzano station on air on 12 July 1928, the day of the monument, and the ceremony was its first programme. The broadcaster's weekly [told how](row:2.03). The station was built "aderendo prontamente a un desiderio espresso da S. E. il Ministro delle Comunicazioni", promptly meeting a wish of the minister of communications; "I lavori si iniziarono il 10 giugno", work began on 10 June. It stood in Gries, "a circa mezzo chilometro fuori dell'abitato di Bolzano", half a kilometre outside the town, with two lattice masts forty metres high and a power of 200 watts. Telephone lines tied it to the Casa del Fascio, the municipal theatre and the main public places. On the day, three loudspeakers stood in Piazza Walther, and the microphone that caught the bands had to be held up at arm's length by an official.

The company's own report for 1928 [adds](row:2.05) that the station had not been in its contract: "Su richiesta del Governo", at the government's request, it was set up in little more than a month, and "nonostante la sua piccola potenza", in spite of its small power, it was heard well in all of Trentino.

![A page of the radio weekly with a report on reception of the Bolzano station.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/15_propaganda_film_radio/images/radiorario_1928-37_p6.jpg)
*Radiorario*, 9–16 September 1928, no. 37: "Ricezioni della Stazione di Bolzano", two months after the station went on air. Study copy. [Row 15.06](row:15.06).

It spoke Italian. Broadcasts in German [began only in 1945](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_S%C3%BCdtirol). By 1932 the programme pages [rate it](row:2.06) at one kilowatt, on the same pages that list Vienna at fifteen. By 1939 it [stands in the list](row:2.07) of stations relaying the national programme, with Rome, Naples, Bari and Bologna.

No document found gives the station the task of answering Innsbruck or Munich. On the figures it could hardly have done so. Its use was more probably for the Italians who were arriving, and as a sign that the state was there. Radio brought the nation into Bolzano far more than it took Bolzano to the nation, with one exception: the day it was born, when the whole country could listen to a monument being opened.

The countryside got its own service in the 1930s, Radio Rurale, for schools. In March 1939 a newsreel [shows](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000018563/2/la-radio-rurale-insegna-ai-bambini.html) "una scuola elementare tra le montagne dell'Alto Adige", an elementary school in the mountains of the Alto Adige, where the set teaches the children little songs.

# By train, in uniform, at school

## The trains, 1928–1939

The first crowd from outside came for the monument. On 12 July 1928, by the radio weekly's account, the last trains brought people "da ogni regione", from every region, and cars arrived through the night with "frotte di camicie nere da tutta l'Italia", swarms of blackshirts from all over Italy.

From 2 August 1931 the regime ran *treni popolari*, cheap Sunday and holiday trains with fares cut by as much as eighty per cent. They [carried](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treni_popolari) 8,774,451 passengers before they stopped on 3 September 1939. The national accounts name seaside and art-city routes, and the first notes of this research found no such train to Bolzano. The local press, read later, has one from the first season: on 1 September 1931 the *Alpenzeitung*, the regime's German-language daily in Bolzano, [reported](row:3.13) a "Volkszug Torino–Bolzano". Of 997 people who had left Turin, 874 came on to Bolzano.

The newsreels suggest how most people of modest means reached the mountains: with an organisation. The Dopolavoro, the regime's leisure body, held its ski-march championship [at Dobbiaco](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000016571/2/vi-campionato-dopolavoristico-marcia-sci.html) in 1935. The university Fascists held their winter games at Ortisei and Bolzano the same month. The Fascists abroad skied at Dobbiaco in 1938.

## The army, 1934–1942

The channel that probably reached the most families left the least on film. Bolzano was the seat of an army corps and of [an infantry division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Infantry_Division_%22Brennero%22) that took the name "Brennero" in 1934, with a regiment in Bolzano and one in Bressanone. An Alpine division, the "Tridentina", [had its command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Alpine_Division_%22Tridentina%22) in Merano from September 1935, and another, the "Pusteria", in Brunico from that December. Their names went round the country in the war news from Ethiopia, Albania and Russia, wherever the men in them came from. No figure was found for the size of the garrison.

Then came the wall. The Vallo Alpino, the line of bunkers along the northern frontier, was built in South Tyrol mostly between the end of 1939 and the autumn of 1940, in the very months of the Option and on the border with an ally. [One account](https://www.south-tirol.com/blog/south-tyrols-bunker-landscape) gives more than a thousand bunkers planned, 306 completed and 135 left unfinished, on some 3,000 expropriated plots. [A research project](https://www.stol.it/artikel/chronik/die-geheimnisse-der-bunker) at the fortress of Fortezza counts nearly 800 planned and a little over 300 finished in the rough. The contractors were large Italian firms and brought their labour with them. Work stopped in the autumn of 1942 under German pressure.

Each of these men wrote home. The library holds [two postcards](row:2.08) written on the same day, 22 April 1940, by one soldier who signs, as far as it can be read, "Dante". Both go to the village of San Ippolito di Vernio near Florence, one to Signora Maddalena Ricci and one to Signor Ferdinando Ricci. The return address is "Deposito Munizioni – Stilves – Campo di Trens (Bolzano)", a munitions depot south of the Brenner. One card shows the pilgrimage church of Maria Trens, published by Fränzl in Bolzano. The other shows the Brenner.

## The school, 1929–1943

From 1930 every elementary school in Italy used the same state textbook, under [a law of January 1929](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1929/01/12/029U0005/CONSOLIDATED/20091216). What it said about the Brenner and the Alto Adige could not be read for this research: no copy was found online.

What can be documented stands around the school. The date of 12 July ties three things together. On 12 July 1916 Austria executed Cesare Battisti, the deputy from Trento who had fought for Italy. On 12 July 1926 the first stone of the monument in Bolzano was laid, and on 12 July 1928 the monument and the radio station opened. Busts of Battisti and of two other executed irredentists, Fabio Filzi and Damiano Chiesa, stand inside the monument, and the newsreel of Mussolini's visit in 1935 shows him before them. In May 1935 another reel [showed](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000019013/2/traslazione-della-salma-cesare-battisti.html) Battisti's remains carried to a new mausoleum at Trento in the presence of the king and of Mussolini. Children's books told the story: a *Cesare Battisti* [published by Paravia](https://biblioteche.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/1608/46307/file/150anniCesareBattisti.pdf) in Turin in 1929 is one.

On screen the province's schools appear as places where Italy teaches. Luce filmed [a vocational school at Ortisei](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000051252/1/la-scuola-elementare-istruzione-post-elementare-obbligatoria-salute-della-stirpe-l-educazione-guerriera.html) in 1931, the kindergarten of the Dante Alighieri society in Bolzano in 1937, and the woodcarvers of Val Gardena making crucifixes for the churches and schools of the new empire in East Africa.

## What it adds up to

The channels were not equal. The newsreel was the loudest and showed a city of ceremonies and building sites. The radio mostly worked inward. The trains came for events. The army, the least recorded, probably put more names of South Tyrolean places into Italian kitchens than all the others, on postcards like the two from Stilves.

They agreed on one thing. In none of them does a German-speaking South Tyrolean say anything.

## Still open

- Where and how often the Luce stories were shown.
- Whether Luce filmed the Option under a title that does not name it, or the Brenner meeting of 4 October 1940, of which only photographs were found.
- What the state textbooks say about the Brenner.
- The size of the garrison in Bolzano, and the number and home regions of the men who built the Vallo Alpino.
- Whether the Bolzano station was given a German-language slot during the Option years.
