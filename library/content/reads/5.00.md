# Built, 1926–1943

West and south of the river Talvera, Bolzano has a second city. Fascism built it between 1926 and 1943, and most of it still stands and is still used for what it was made for: a tax office, a court, a church, a station, blocks of flats. This text is an inventory. It goes through what was built, then what was removed or renamed, then what has been added since 2001, when the city began to answer its monuments instead of only living beside them.

One object is left to a text of its own: the eagles of the Druso bridge, in [the story this library tells about them](row:5.01).

## 1926–1931: a monument, a station, a bridge

The Monument to Victory was decided in February 1926 and designed by Marcello Piacentini, the regime's leading architect. It took the place of a monument to the Tyrolean imperial riflemen, [begun in 1917](row:5.02) and never finished, which was pulled down. The king [laid the first stone](row:5.03) on 12 July 1926 and inaugurated the monument on 12 July 1928. Its fourteen columns have the form of the lictors' bundle, the fasces. Its Latin inscription says that here, at the border of the fatherland, the standards are set, and that from here we civilised the others in language, laws and arts: "HINC CETEROS EXCOLVIMVS LINGVA LEGIBVS ARTIBVS". An earlier draft had "barbaros" where "ceteros" stands.

The new station buildings, by Angiolo Mazzoni, opened on 24 May 1928. State employees were housed in blocks beside the monument, finished the same year, and a quarter with Venetian Gothic fronts went up from 1927, built by the housing institute of Venice.

The Druso bridge was built in 1930–31 for the state road authority by the engineers Eugenio Miozzi and Gilberto Ricci, and opened on 28 October 1931, the ninth anniversary of the March on Rome. On its four central piers stood sculptures more than eight metres high: fasces carrying globes, and on the globes four Roman eagles by Vittorio Morelli of Ancona. The stadium beside it took the same name. Drusus was the Roman general who conquered the area in 15 BC, and the guide the Comune itself published in 2007 [calls the naming](row:5.10) "uno dei molti esempi di strumentalizzazione fascista della storia romana", one of many examples of the Fascist use of Roman history as an instrument. In March 1931, before the bridge was finished, Miozzi moved to Venice, where he built the road causeway to the mainland.

## 1932–1938: offices, an avenue, a zone

The Lido, an open-air bath for two thousand people, opened in 1932. The insurance institute's buildings in Piazza Domenicani, inside the old town, date from 1932–35. The army corps command on Piazza IV Novembre, by Piacentini, is of 1934–35. The red building of the Fascist girls' organisation by the Druso bridge was commissioned in 1934 from two architects of Padua.

From 1935–36 the avenue that carries the new city was cut from the monument towards Gries under Piacentini's plan, and older buildings came down for it. It was first Corso Littorio, then Corso IX Maggio, after the day in May 1936 when the empire was proclaimed, and it opened to traffic in 1938. Its arcades are, in the guide's words, "molto più grandi" than the medieval ones of the old town, much bigger. The square before the monument was closed on three sides by arcaded blocks of the state insurance and pension institutes. One of them still carries a line of Virgil on ruling peoples by command.

South of the river Isarco the industrial zone was opened on 20 December 1936, and beside it two workers' quarters grew: the Rione Littorio of four- and five-storey blocks, from 1935, and from 1937 or 1938 the low houses with gardens that people called the Semirurali.

In 1938 two gilded wooden figures by Ignaz Gabloner went up on tall columns by the Talvera bridge, the she-wolf of Rome and the lion of Venice, and a column for the province's dead in Africa and Spain [was unveiled](row:17.16) behind the monument.

## 1938–1943: the last buildings

Three buildings on the avenue that runs south from there, today's Corso Italia, were to form a gate to the city: a church, the party's house and a courthouse. The church of Cristo Re [was built](row:5.21) in 1938–40. The Casa Littoria, the party headquarters, went up between 1939 and 1942, with a convex front facing the concave front of the court. Across it runs a relief in travertine by Hans Piffrader, a sculptor from Chiusa in South Tyrol: 57 panels in two rows, 36 metres long, with Mussolini on horseback at the centre and the words "credere, obbedire, combattere", believe, obey, fight. The last panels arrived about two months before the regime fell in July 1943. The courthouse was begun in 1939 and stopped in 1942.

On the avenue itself the tourist office built a Palazzo del Turismo to the design of Armando Ronca of Verona, from 1938. It has [a text of its own](row:13.00) in this library.

The old town was altered too. The city museum lost its battlements and its tower. The savings bank's headquarters, built in 1904–07, was stripped of its ornament and given reliefs by Piffrader.

The people who made all this came from many places, and the list is a map: Piacentini, Mazzoni and the architects of the insurance blocks from Rome; the architects of the girls' house from Padua; Ronca from Verona; Morelli from Ancona; Miozzi on his way to Venice; and from Tyrol Piffrader, Gabloner, and the architect Clemens Holzmeister, who with the film-maker Luis Trenker designed a housing quarter. The travertine of the relief was cut by a carver of Pietrasanta in Tuscany.

# Kept, lost and renamed, 1943–1999

## 1943–1957: change of names, completion of works

From September 1943 the German occupiers used the army corps building as the headquarters of the Gestapo. Two men of the resistance, Manlio Longon and Giannantonio Manci, were killed there, and plaques name them.

After 1945 the names of Fascist leaders came off the streets. Commissions sat in 1946 and again at the end of 1948. Corso IX Maggio became Corso della Libertà, Viale Giulio Cesare became Corso Italia, Piazza Arnaldo Mussolini became Piazza del Tribunale. The line "BEN. MUSSOLINI, ITAL. DUCE A. VI" was taken off the south side of the monument. Many names of the nation and its wars were kept, and are there today: Battisti, Diaz, Cadorna, Piave, Vittorio Veneto, Amba Alagi, Piazza della Vittoria, Piazza IV Novembre. The Druso bridge and the Druso stadium kept theirs.

The buildings went on being used, and some were finished. The party's house served as a "Casa del Popolo" in 1946–47, and has been the state's finance office since. The first trade fair after the war [was held](row:5.07) in the unfinished courthouse in September 1948. The court was completed by the Republic and inaugurated by its president on 15 September 1956, with the inscription on its architrave as designed: "PRO ITALICO IMPERIO VIRTVTE IVSTITIA HIERARCHIA VNGVIBVS ET ROSTRIS", for the Italian empire with virtue, justice, hierarchy, with claws and beaks. Three of the 57 panels of Piffrader's relief were mounted only in 1957. A proposal by the heritage office of Trento to remove the relief had come to nothing.

## The 1970s and 1980s: what went

The eagles came down from the bridge in the 1970s, during a renovation. No decision has been found, and the year is given as 1972, as the early or the middle 1970s, or as about 1970. In 1990 a councillor of the neo-Fascist party [said in the provincial council](row:13.25) that the widening of the road had been the reason given at the time, and that it was a pretext. In the 1990s the bridge was widened and given a new parapet made to resemble the old.

On 1 October 1978 a bomb damaged the monument, and it was fenced.

The Semirurali were replaced by dense blocks, mostly in the 1970s and 1980s; the sources do not agree on the decade. One house was kept, at via Bari 11.

The Palazzo del Turismo, a cinema since 1946, was the one building of quality on the avenue to be demolished. The Province [declined to buy it](row:13.21) in September 1980. The demolition permit was issued on 15 February 1988; the building [was still standing](row:13.23) on 30 March and was gone by August 1989. An exhibition on the architecture of the 1930s held in 2015 could say that the city of those years survived almost whole, "meno il Corso".

Heavy industry shrank. The aluminium works closed in the early 1990s. At the end of 1998 work began to turn the girls' house by the bridge into a research academy.

An inventory of the losses shows what caused them: speculation for the cinema, housing policy for the Semirurali, the end of an industry for the aluminium works. Only at the bridge was a symbol taken down, and even there nobody said so.

# Added, 2001–2026

## 2001–2013: a name, plaques, an appeal

In December 2001 the city government renamed Piazza della Vittoria "Piazza della Pace", Peace Square. In a referendum in October 2002, which most German speakers stayed away from, 61.94 per cent voted to have the old name back. [The sign](row:5.12) has since read "Piazza della Vittoria (già Piazza della Pace)", formerly Peace Square.

[Plaques](row:5.09) in four languages explaining the monument were set up some fifty metres from it. On 21 May 2010 the city archive, under the historian Hannes Obermair, appealed for the Fascist monuments to be given their history, defused and made into museums. In January 2011 the minister of culture in Rome promised the provincial president an intervention at the monument, and in February the Province opened a competition of ideas for the front of the former party house. It drew 486 entries.

On 6 December 2013 a crane lorry took the two best-preserved eagle heads from the municipal nursery, where the sculptures had lain in pieces, to the crypt of the monument. They [are there today](row:17.25).

## 2014–2017: a ring of light and a sentence

The exhibition *BZ '18–'45: one monument, one city, two dictatorships* opened in the rooms under the monument on 21 July 2014. Outside, a ring of light with running text was put round one of the columns. [The designers' own paper](row:5.16) says what it is for: the ring "incide sulla monumentalità e la vanifica", cuts into the monumentality and cancels it. One of them called it a nose ring for Fascist rhetoric. The commission of historians had a clause in its mandate that shielded it from political interference, and English comes first on the signs so that neither local language does.

The ring of light on the Monument to Victory, 2014, by Gruppe Gut with Jeffrey T. Schnapp. Photograph by Bartleby08, 29 July 2014; public domain, Wikimedia Commons. [Row 5.15](row:5.15).

In 2015 more than 24,300 people visited, among them 4,000 school pupils. The European Museum Forum [gave the exhibition](row:5.19) a special commendation in 2016, calling it "a highly courageous and professional initiative". A study of the project [is in this library](row:5.20).

In May 2015 the surviving Semirurali house opened as a small museum.

In November 2017 the second intervention was switched on. Across Piffrader's relief, in lit letters and in three languages, runs a sentence of Hannah Arendt's: "Kein Mensch hat das Recht zu gehorchen – Nessuno ha il diritto di obbedire – Degnu n'a l dërt de ulghè". Nobody has the right to obey. It answers the "obbedire" carved below it. The design, by the Val Gardena artists Arnold Holzknecht and Michele Bernardi, [was one of five](row:5.24) the jury had chosen.

Hannah Arendt's sentence across the relief of the former Casa Littoria. Photograph by Bartleby08, 6 November 2018; CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons. [Row 5.24](row:5.24).

The same year the old aluminium works reopened as a technology park.

## 2018–2026: what has not been answered

The two interventions stand at the two most visible objects. The rest of the ensemble has no word of explanation. The she-wolf and the lion were restored by the Comune in 2018 with no note beside them. The savings bank restored its front with Piffrader's reliefs in 2017–18, and [its announcement](row:5.22) does not mention what they show. The column for the dead of the colonial wars, the line of Virgil on the square, and the inscription on the courthouse are as they were. The city's tourist office still opens its history page with the Roman bridge-station of Drusus as the town's origin.

Buildings went on disappearing for ordinary reasons. The parish cinema Concordia on Piazza Cristo Re, beside Corso Italia, was demolished in August 2018 for a block of flats.

A crack has been open in [the architrave of the monument](row:5.30) since 2019. The exhibition was closed from early 2020 and reopened in the autumn of 2021 after an appeal by historians. In 2025 the heritage office announced a restoration, and the Südtiroler Heimatbund demanded demolition instead.

On 16 July 2026, shortly after six in the morning, the central wing of the courthouse [collapsed](row:5.32) during work on its load-bearing columns. A cleaner was slightly hurt. The Austrian broadcaster's report [noted](https://orf.at/stories/3436356/) that the inscription on the building "has never been contextualized", and quoted the historian Günther Pallaver: the Fascist monuments were never really de-fascistised after 1945, and "das rächt sich jetzt", that is now taking its revenge. Proposals followed for a critical reconstruction and for an institute for research on Fascism in the building.
