# Posters, 1919–1943

Between the two world wars South Tyrol was advertised on paper as never before: on station walls, in travel agencies, in leaflets stamped by agents in Leipzig and Dresden. This text goes through what survives of that advertising in the order it was printed, and asks who paid for it, who drew it and what it showed. A second part does the same for the picture postcard.

Most of what can be seen online belongs to two museums in Merano, the Touriseum and the Palais Mamming, which publish their catalogues on the Province's museum portal. This library keeps [a list of 414 of their records](row:1.03) dated between 1919 and 1943: 77 posters, 191 picture postcards, 84 brochures, 13 leaflets, and maps, guides and programmes. About fifty of the posters were read one by one for this research. The pictures themselves stay under the museums' rights, so each poster below is linked to its record and not shown.

## 1919–1928: the old trade under a new flag

Before the war the valley advertised itself in German. The library holds two examples for comparison: a poster of 1906 for [the electric railway from Lana to Meran](row:1.01), and one of a few years later for [the cable car from Bozen up to Kohlern](row:1.02).

After the annexation the first posters keep that voice, and they come from local presses. A sheet printed by Pötzelberger in Meran between 1919 and 1923 still sells [*Meran, Südtirol / Italien, Größter klimatischer Kurort der Südalpen*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000352), the largest climatic health resort of the southern Alps, with the new country added after a stroke. The first Italian slogan found is of 1921, on a colour linocut printed by Vogelweider in Bozen: [*Visitate Bolzano-Gries centro delle Dolomiti e dei ghiacciai*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140001915), visit Bolzano-Gries, centre of the Dolomites and the glaciers. The same year the trade fair, the Bozner Messe, had [a poster by Otto Zoff](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80031197), in German, and advertised [in the local paper](row:1.04).

In 1923 a new name appears. A lithograph for the Palace Hotel at Colle Isarco is [signed](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140018184) "Originallithographie Lenhart, 23 Meran". Franz Lenhart was born in 1898 at Bad Häring in the Austrian Tyrol, by the Touriseum's record; other catalogues give other years. He studied in Vienna and Florence, [became a drawing teacher](https://inverso.pt/labels/artists/Lenhard/lenhard.htm) at the technical institute in Merano after 1919 and took Italian citizenship. A year later he drew [*Golf Links, Merano Italy*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000492) in English, printed in Florence, with an empty field left for overprinting.

The state's own first sheet in this library is not an advertisement. On 19 May 1925 the prefect's commissioner in Bolzano had [a notice](row:1.05) posted in Italian and German. It welcomed delegations of Italian war invalids and ordered every house on their route to fly the tricolour. It called Bolzano "die Königin des Hochetsch", the queen of the Alto Adige, in the official German made for the Italian name. Its border is decorated with red fruit.

![A printed notice in two columns, Italian and German, with a decorated border.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/01_posters_graphics/1925_Bolzano_public_appeal_bilingual_poster_photo_Commons.jpg)
*Municipio di Bolzano – Cittadini! / Bürger!*, notice of 19 May 1925. Photograph from Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA. [Row 1.05](row:1.05).

In the second half of the 1920s the state tourist board, ENIT, begins to sign posters for the province. They are painted by artists from outside and printed far away: [*Solda*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140019373) by Enrico Grimaldi, printed in Naples, and [*Dobbiaco (Pustertal Italien) Eingang ins Dolomitengebiet*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140019414) by Pio Solero, printed in Rome about 1928, gateway to the Dolomites, in German with the Italian place name. On Lenhart's [*Visitate le Dolomiti*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000486), made for the Alto Adige section of the Italian Alpine Club and Bolzano's tourist office, an Italian flag flies over the mountain hut.

## 1929–1933: the monument on a poster

When these notes were first written, in October 2026, the research had found the Monument to Victory on no poster at all. Three weeks later two turned up, hanging as reproductions in the exhibition under the monument itself. They are a different kind of poster. They advertise events staged in Bolzano for Italians from elsewhere.

The first is for [the Iª Esposizione Nazionale dell'Alto Adige](row:17.01), the first national exhibition of the Alto Adige, held from 10 August to 10 September 1929. A winged Victory salutes with the lictor's bundle in her hand in front of the monument. The copy in the Salce collection in Treviso [was printed](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500662517) by Carlo Perego in Milan and names no illustrator. It carries a line the crypt's smaller edition lacks: "Riduzioni ferroviarie del 50%", half fare on the railways.

![A poster: a winged figure with raised arm before a white monument, lettering below.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/17_monument_visit/images/1929_Esposizione_Nazionale_Alto_Adige_poster_visit.jpg)
The poster of the first national exhibition of the Alto Adige, 1929, in the edition shown as a reproduction under the monument. Own photograph, 14 November 2026, cut to the sheet and set square. [Row 17.01](row:17.01).

The second is for the *Settimana dell'Alto Adige* of October 1933. Its organisers [held a competition](row:17.08) and fixed the size, the colours, the motifs and the Italian wording in advance. [The winner](row:17.10) was Anton Hofer of Bolzano.

The Salce collection holds a run of such Bolzano exhibition posters, for 1922, 1927, 1929, 1930, 1932, 1933, 1935 and 1939. Only the two above have been looked at. So the first finding of these notes stands in a narrower form: the monument is absent from the tourist posters, and present where the state invited Italians to its own events.

## 1930–1940: the state's emblems, the hoteliers' union

From about 1930 the large tourist sheets change hands. They carry the emblem of ENIT under the crown of Savoy and the monogram of the State Railways, a ministry's approval number, and the year counted from the March on Rome. They are printed by the national lithographers, in Milan, Genoa, Florence, Treviso and Naples.

Lenhart's [*Bolzano-Gries Dolomites*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000481) of 1930, a couple in an open car driving towards the town, names ENIT as its client. The catalogue of the Salce collection, which holds the German edition, [adds a note](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500658979): the poster advertises the new avenue between Bolzano and Gries, the Via del Littorio. His [*Merano*](row:1.10) of 1934, a woman's face in a cone of light, was published by ENIT with the railways. His [*Herbst in Merano*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140002076) of the same year, a head with hair of fruit, promises German readers "50–70% Fahrpreis Ermässigung auf den ital. Staatsbahnen", fifty to seventy per cent off on the Italian state railways.

The approvals are printed on the sheets. A summer poster of 1935 [says](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140002075) "Con approvazione Direz. Gen. del Turismo Nr. 141 del 23.4.XIII". A Merano poster of the same year, a blonde woman in a dirndl with a branch of blossom, [carries](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80036119) the authorisation of the tourism directorate and of the police headquarters of Genoa, where it was printed. No catalogue names its designer.

![A poster: a woman's face in profile under palm fronds, a town and a white mountain behind, the word MERANO below.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/01_posters_graphics/1934_Lenhart_ENIT_Merano_poster_HNM_Europeana.png)
Franz Lenhart, *Merano*, 1934, for ENIT and the State Railways. Hungarian National Museum via Europeana, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; the artwork is in copyright. [Row 1.10](row:1.10).

In the second half of the 1930s a Bolzano body stands beside ENIT as publisher: the Unione Turistica Alberghiera, the hoteliers' tourist union, UTA for short. Its small poster [*L'estate nelle Dolomiti – 60 stazioni con 40.000 letti*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140018192) counts sixty resorts and forty thousand beds. It published Lenhart's [*Sommer in den Dolomiten*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140002078) in 1938 and a series of illustrated guides in four languages. On this evidence UTA was the main regional publisher of these years. It has had far less attention than ENIT.

One picture went out in several languages, and the collections show it only when put side by side. Lenhart's summer landscape of 1937–38 is in Merano in German, and in Treviso in Italian as [*L'estate nelle Dolomiti*](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500662577) and in French as [*L'été aux Dolomites*](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500658987). His winter poster of 1933–34 exists as [*Wintersport in den Dolomiten*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140017326) and as [*Sport invernale nelle Dolomiti*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000508). Italians saw the Italian edition. The German one went north: a winter brochure of 1937–38 [bears the stamp](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140006230) of a travel agency in Leipzig.

## What the posters show

Nearly all of it is landscape and leisure. Behind Bolzano stands the Rosengarten. Merano has its Kurhaus, its promenade, palms, blossom and grapes. The people are golfers, tennis players, motorists, skiers, bathers and jockeys. A search of the portal for Lenhart's name returns 462 records, 105 of them posters, and the catalogue's own keywords count the subjects: "Frau", woman, 116 times; "Hotel" 80; "Skifahren", skiing, 51; "Tracht", traditional dress, 31.

Traditional dress is there, then, but chosen: a breeder with his Haflinger horses, [a trumpeter of the Algund band](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000124), the woman in the dirndl. It marks an Alpine type. It never stands for a German-speaking people.

One poster turns the whole province into a threshold. About 1930 UTA's Anglo-American information office in Merano issued [*Kennst du das Land… Merano Dolomiti Gardone Venezia*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140005642), after Goethe's line about the land where the lemons bloom: grapes and the Kurhaus for Merano, lemons for Gardone, a gondola for Venice. Rome is carried the same way. A road map of 1937, in German, [sells the Brenner route](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140009622) as *In Italien auf den römischen Konsularstrassen*, in Italy on the Roman consular roads, by way of Bolzano to Rome.

No tourist poster found shows the name of Drusus, the new quarters across the Talvera or the industrial zone. The collections searched were the Province's portal, Wikimedia Commons and Europeana, and an absence there does not prove that nothing was printed. What the absence suggests is a division of work between the media, and the postcards are the other half of it.

The last sheet of the period is a regulation. In 1940 hotels had to display the [*Disposizioni per il soggiorno degli stranieri*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000252), the rules for the stay of foreigners, in German, Italian, English and French. The copy in the Touriseum comes from the Hotel Minerva in Merano.

# Postcards and the people who made them

## The publishers

A handful of local firms made Bolzano's postcards between the wars. The largest was Lorenz Fränzl, a photographer born in Munich in 1879 who died in Bozen in 1951. His cards are signed "Ed. Stab. Art. Fot. Lor. Fränzl – Bolzano" and numbered in series; his negatives later passed to the publisher Schlern-Verlag. Johann F. Amonn was the other Bolzano house. In Merano the names are Leo Bährendt and Rudolf Stricker.

The Pedrotti brothers, often named in this connection, were a Trento studio. Enrico Pedrotti moved to Bolzano only in 1937, and what the provincial archive holds from him for these years is aerial photography of the new quarters: [the Rione Littorio](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120016156), [the Semirurali among the orchards](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120016154). No postcard series of his on the new Bolzano was found.

## What the cards show

The postcard carried what the poster left out. Fränzl published [*Bolzano – Monumento alla Vittoria*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140021403) in the 1930s; the copy in the Touriseum was mailed to a hotel in Curon. In the archive of the Schlern-Verlag are cards of [the new Druso bridge](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072449), of [one of its eagles](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072451), of [the new station](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072427), of [the industrial zone](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072419), of [the Drusus stadium](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072513) and of [the Lido](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072506). The museum's dates for this holding are loose: it files an industrial zone decreed in the mid-1930s under 1925–30.

![A postcard: a wide view over a town towards mountains.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/01_posters_graphics/1940_Bolzano_panorama_verso_Dolomiti_postcard_Commons.jpg)
*700-166. Bolzano: Panorama verso le Dolomiti*, postcard, 1940, with the new quarters of Gries in the foreground. The number matches Fränzl's series, so the card is very probably his. Wikimedia Commons, public domain. [Row 1.13](row:1.13).

The cards also changed the names. A tower in Gries that German speakers call the Gscheibter Turm appears on a card as [the "Torre Druso"](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072443). On a card of the Karersee hotel from the 1920s the German caption is [stamped over](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140004432), and the back is stamped "Alto Adige, Italia". On [a card of 1933](row:1.09) the German name of the suburb Oltrisarco is struck out with black bars. And a Fränzl view of Malles in Val Venosta [catches by accident](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140023304) a slogan painted on a house front: "Nel segno del Littorio abbiamo vinto, nel segno del Littorio vinceremo". Under the sign of the lictor we have won, under the sign of the lictor we shall win.

Not every new place has a card. For Piazza della Vittoria, the avenue then called Corso Littorio, the Semirurali and the Casa del Fascio, the portal returned almost nothing. Dealers' sites and the city archive's photographs were not searched.

## Local hands

Two things about the makers stand out. The first is that the province's image was made at home. The famous poster artists of Italy hardly touched it: the portal has no record at all for Gino Boccasile, and one brochure by Marcello Dudovich, for a place in the Cadore. The work was done by people who lived in Merano and Bolzano, and it was made national afterwards, by emblems, approval stamps and printers in the big cities.

The second is who those people were. Lenhart, trained in Vienna, drew the province for the Italian state in Italian, German, English and French, and in the same years drew advertisements for the "Meraner Trachten" of a Merano outfitter. Fränzl, born in Munich, printed the Italian captions of the Monument to Victory and the "Torre Druso". The regime's Italian picture of the province came in good part from its German-speaking trade.

Lenhart lived until 1992. He designed nearly a hundred mountain posters between about 1930 and 1955, by [one account](https://inverso.pt/labels/artists/Lenhard/lenhard.htm), and in 2017 Merano [put up a sculpture](https://merano-suedtirol.secure.consisto.net/en/merano/city-culture/people-traditions/meran-away-from-the-beaten-paths/tour-public-art-map/humanshapes/mimmo-paladino-homage-to-franz-j-lenhart-2017.html) by Mimmo Paladino for him on the Passirio promenade.

## Where to see them, and what is missing

The Province's portal gives each object [a page of its own](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/search/simple) with a picture. The Collezione Salce in Treviso, the national poster museum, is catalogued in the state's general catalogue, which refuses scripts; three of its records were opened by hand for this research. Openly licensed pictures are few, and this library holds them: [Mario Borgoni's *Merano*](row:1.11) and [Erwin Merlet's *Ortisei – Val Gardena*](row:1.12), both of 1935, and Lenhart's *Merano* through a museum in Budapest.

No study of these posters as a body exists. The one book on Lenhart is a bilingual catalogue of 2001. The history of tourism in South Tyrol and the history of Fascism there have each been written, and they have not yet been joined at the level of the picture. The nearest attempt is a doctoral project in Lucerne by Catrina Klee, [which argues](row:6.04) that the tourism projects of the 1920s to the 1940s "acted as accelerators of the Italianization".

## Still open

- Who drew the 1929 exhibition poster. The signature on the smaller edition is unread.
- The other Bolzano exhibition posters in the Salce collection: 1922, 1927, 1930, 1932, 1935, 1939.
- How ENIT and UTA commissioned Lenhart: contracts, fees, briefs. The 2001 catalogue was not consulted.
- Whether the provincial archive's "tourism advertising" photographs, which include the aerial views of the new quarters, were gathered by a tourist office at the time or filed that way later.
- Any poster made for workers and migrants bound for the industrial zone. None was found in the tourism collections.
