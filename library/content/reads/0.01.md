# On paper and on screen, 1919–1943

An Italian of the 1930s who never went north of Verona could still know Bolzano. There were two of them to know.

The first hung in railway stations and travel agencies. It was called Alto Adige, the name Italy gave South Tyrol after annexing it at the end of the First World War, and it was made of Dolomites, palm trees, grapes and skiers. The second came with the newsreel before the film, over the radio and on the front page: a king before a white marble arch, blackshirts by the trainload, ministers walking into a new factory. The first Bolzano had no Fascism in it. The second was built out of it.

This is the story of how the two were made, told in the order it happened, from the annexation to the war. Two shorter texts follow it: what became of the Fascist city in stone, and what Bolzano sells its visitors today.

## 1919–1927: a health resort changes country

In 1910 Italian speakers were [3 per cent](https://www.asei.eu/it/2006/11/limmigrazione-italiana-in-alto-adige-approcci-e-questioni/) of the people of what is now the province. What Italy took over in 1919 was a German-speaking land with a tourist trade already built, and for some years the advertising went on in the old voice. A poster printed between 1919 and 1923 still sells [*Meran, Südtirol / Italien, Größter klimatischer Kurort der Südalpen*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000352), the largest climatic health resort of the southern Alps, with the new country added after a stroke.

The trade itself had shrunk. In March 1922 the Touring Club's monthly, *Le Vie d'Italia*, [printed Bolzano's visitor figures](https://archive.org/details/2023-02-27-02-08?q=Bolzano): 77,143 in 1921, against 123,191 in 1913.

The state first spoke to the town as an authority. A [notice from the town hall](row:1.05) of 19 May 1925, in Italian and German, signed by the prefect's commissioner, ordered every house on the route of a visit by Italian war invalids to fly the tricolour. It called Bolzano "die Königin des Hochetsch", the queen of the Alto Adige, in the official German made for the Italian name.

Names changed on paper before much else did. On a postcard of the Karersee hotel from the 1920s the German caption is [stamped over](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140004432) with "Alto Adige, Italia".

Then came the decision that gave the second Bolzano its centre. On 6 February 1926 Mussolini told the Chamber that Italy would build a monument in Bolzano "sulle stesse fondamenta sulle quali doveva sorgere il monumento della vittoria tedesca", on the same foundations on which the monument of the German victory was to rise. Four days later a Bolzano paper [boxed the sentence on its front page](row:17.21) and opened a subscription among "tutti i cittadini di lingua italiana". The first stone [was laid](row:5.03) on 12 July 1926.

## 1928: the monument, on every channel

The Monument to Victory was inaugurated on 12 July 1928, and the day was made for an audience that was not there.

Luce, the state film institute, made the newsreels that ran in every cinema before the feature. [*A Bolzano inaugurazione del monumento alla Vittoria d'Italia*](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048984/2/a-bolzano-inaugurazione-del-monumento-alla-vittoria-d-italia.html) runs four minutes and forty-three seconds: priests bless the monument, the minister Giuriati speaks before the king, Italo Balbo stands by, a band from the province plays, the boys of the Balilla march past. The same reel carried [*Visita altoatesina a Roma*](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048647/2/visita-altoatesina-roma.html), South Tyroleans in costume climbing the Capitol to meet Mussolini.

Radio arrived for the same ceremony. EIAR, the state broadcaster, put a Bolzano station on air that day, and the ceremony was its first programme. The broadcaster's weekly [told the story with pride](https://worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/20s/1928/RC-1928-30.pdf). The station was built at the wish of the minister of communications. Work began on 10 June, and it was "installata di sana pianta in poco più di un mese", set up from nothing in little more than a month. It stood in Gries, ran at 200 watts and fed three loudspeakers in Piazza Walther. The same issue describes the special trains that brought "frotte di camicie nere da tutta l'Italia", swarms of blackshirts from all over Italy.

The station was small and spoke only Italian; broadcasts in German began [in 1945](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_S%C3%BCdtirol). For German-speaking listeners it could hardly compete with Vienna, which the broadcaster's own [programme pages](https://worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/30s/1932/RC-1932-42.pdf) list at fifteen kilowatts. Its use was more probably for the Italians who were arriving, and as a sign that the state was present. Radio carried the nation into Bolzano more than it carried Bolzano to the nation.

The papers did the rest. In Turin *La Stampa* headed its front page of 12 July ["Tutta l'Italia oggi in Bolzano italiana"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0027_01_1928_0165_0001_16774629/), all Italy today in Italian Bolzano, and the next day's ["La consacrazione eroica di Bolzano italiana"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0027_01_1928_0166_0001_24358966/). In Bolzano the Fascist daily [wrote](row:3.08) of "il grandioso rito di ieri in Bolzano fascista", yesterday's grand rite in Fascist Bolzano.

![The front page of La Provincia di Bolzano with a headline across its whole width.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/03_press_print/LPB_1928-07-13_p1.jpg)
*La Provincia di Bolzano*, 13 July 1928: "Il grandioso rito di ieri in Bolzano fascista". Biblioteca Provinciale Italiana Claudia Augusta; out of copyright, study copy. [Row 3.08](row:3.08).

From that day the newsreels kept coming. Luce's [catalogue](row:2.02) holds at least 163 stories on South Tyrol and the Brenner between 1928 and 1943. In them German speakers appear almost only in costume.

## 1929–1933: come and see it

Having made the image, the regime began to bring people to it.

In August 1929 Bolzano held the "first national exhibition of the Alto Adige" under Mussolini's patronage. On [its poster](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500662517) a winged Victory comes down the monument's steps with the fasces under her arm, and the copy kept in Treviso prints the offer: rail fares cut by half. The organisers [counted](row:17.03) more than 800 firms from the old provinces and over 100,000 visits.

![A poster on a red ground: a dark winged figure with a fasces under her arm in front of a pale triumphal arch; below, the title of the exhibition and the name Benito Mussolini.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/17_monument_visit/images/1929_Esposizione_Nazionale_Alto_Adige_poster_visit.jpg)
The poster of the first national exhibition of the Alto Adige, Bolzano, August and September 1929. Illustrator unknown. The edition shown as a reproduction under the monument; own photograph, 14 November 2026, cut to the sheet and set square. [Row 17.01](row:17.01).

In 1931 came the *treni popolari*, the regime's cheap excursion trains, and Bolzano was a destination from the first season. On 1 September the regime's German-language paper, the *Alpenzeitung*, [reported](row:3.13) a "Volkszug Torino–Bolzano": 997 people had left Turin and 874 of them came on to Bolzano. More were [announced](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046705) from the old provinces for that autumn's grape festival. *La Stampa*, between 1919 and 1943, has 139 articles that name Bolzano together with a "treno popolare".

On 28 October 1931, the ninth anniversary of the March on Rome, the city [opened a bundle of works](row:3.14) in one morning: municipal baths, a health-insurance building, a new radio transmitter, a tram line, and a bridge named after a Roman general, with four eagles on it. The bridge is in the next text.

The name travelled without the place, too. In August 1932 cinemas all over Italy [showed the launch](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000034894/2/il-varo-della-nave-bolzano-31-agosto-1932.html) of the heavy cruiser *Bolzano* at Sestri Ponente. The same year the regime's glossy monthly, the *Rivista Illustrata del Popolo d'Italia*, was blunt about who was in the way: "questi tedeschi che popolano abusivamente il territorio italiano", it [wrote](https://archive.org/details/larivista-1932-vol2?q=Bolzano), these Germans who unlawfully populate Italian territory.

In October 1933 came the *Settimana dell'Alto Adige*, a week of agricultural and craft shows. Its organisers [held a competition](row:17.08) for the poster and fixed its size, its number of colours and its words, in Italian only. The Bolzano designer Anton Hofer won with [a sheet](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500658946) that sets the monument as a white block among grapes, a hammer and a fox. It too prints a discount: seventy per cent off the fare.

## The 1930s on the station wall

While all this was staged for Italians, another Bolzano was being printed for anyone who might come on holiday.

The largest body of tourist advertising from these years that can be looked at online belongs to the Touriseum in Merano and the Palais Mamming museum, and is published on the Province's museum portal. This library holds [a list of 414 of its records](row:1.03). A search of the portal for one name, Franz Lenhart, returns 462 records, 105 of them posters. [Lenhart](https://inverso.pt/labels/artists/Lenhard/lenhard.htm) was a Tyrolean, trained in Austria, who became an Italian citizen and lived in Merano. He drew the province for the Italian state in four languages, and for Merano's German-speaking shopkeepers he drew advertisements for traditional costume.

The posters show who was paying. From about 1930 the large sheets carry the marks of ENIT, the state tourist board, and of the State Railways under the crown of Savoy, a ministry's approval number and the year counted from the March on Rome. They were printed in Milan, Genoa, Florence, Treviso and Naples. Lenhart's [*Bolzano-Gries Dolomites*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000481) of 1930, a couple in an open car driving towards the city, names ENIT as its client. From 1936 a Bolzano body appears beside it as publisher, the Unione Turistica Alberghiera.

One picture went out in several languages. Lenhart's summer poster of 1937–38 exists in Italian as [*L'estate nelle Dolomiti*](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500662577), in French as [*L'été aux Dolomites*](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500658987) and in German as [*Sommer in den Dolomiten*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140002078). The objects themselves say where they went. A winter-sports brochure of 1937–38 carries [the stamp of a travel agency in Leipzig](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140006230), a tourist map of 1938 [one from Dresden](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140006398). Merano's spa board printed a leaflet [in Polish](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80025594) in 1934, and another advertised [the crossing from New York](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80023859) on the liners *Saturnia* and *Vulcania*.

What all of this sold was landscape and leisure. Behind Bolzano stands the Rosengarten. Merano has its Kurhaus, its palms, its blossom and its grapes. The people are golfers, motorists, skiers, bathers and jockeys. Traditional dress does appear, usually on a woman and as local colour. The blonde in a dirndl on a [Merano poster of 1935](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80036119) reads as an Alpine type. Nothing on the sheet suggests a German-speaking people living under Italian rule.

The regime is there in small print. Besides the crowned emblems and the approval formulas, two cases stand out. On Lenhart's [*Visitate le Dolomiti*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000486), made for the Alto Adige section of the Italian Alpine Club, an Italian flag flies over the mountain hut. And a road map of 1937, in German, sells the Brenner route as a Roman road: [*In Italien auf den römischen Konsularstrassen*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140009622), from the passes by way of Bolzano to Rome.

What is missing is the city the regime was building. In the collections searched for this research (the Province's portal, Wikimedia Commons and Europeana), no tourist poster shows the Monument to Victory, the name of Drusus or the new quarters across the Talvera. An absence in a searchable collection does not prove that no such poster was ever printed. Across some fifty posters studied closely and 414 dated records, it is at least a pattern. The nearest thing to an exception is that couple in the open car: the catalogue of the Salce collection in Treviso [notes](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500658979) that the poster advertises the new avenue between Bolzano and Gries, the Via del Littorio. The regime's city got onto a tourist poster as a pleasant road.

So there were two kinds of poster. The exhibition and the *Settimana* invited Italians, in Italian, to the monument. The tourist posters invited anyone, in four languages, to the mountains.

The new city went out by post instead. The Bolzano studio of Lorenz Fränzl issued photographic cards of the [Monumento alla Vittoria](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140021403). The publisher Schlern-Verlag had [*Bolzano: Ponte nuovo Druso verso le Dolomiti*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072449), a card of [one of the bridge's eagles](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072451), and cards of the new station, the industrial zone and the stadium. A medieval tower, the Gscheibter Turm, went out under a new name, [*Torre Druso*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072443). And on a [card of Oltrisarco from 1933](row:1.09) the German place name is covered with a black bar and the Italian one printed beside it.

## 1934–1939: "la più grande Bolzano"

A city on paper needed people in it, and from 1934 the state arranged for those too.

It began as a law. A [royal decree of 28 September 1934](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1934-09-28;1621) gave plants founded in Bolzano exemption from customs duties, ten years free of the tax on business income and up to five years of operating grants. On 21 February 1935 the Fascist daily, *La Provincia di Bolzano*, [led with](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001-1935-1935_02_21_Nr_45.html) "Il Duce per la più grande Bolzano", the Duce for a greater Bolzano. The *Alpenzeitung* [put it into German](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046469) word for word: "Mussolini für die Schaffung von Groß-Bolzano".

![The front page of a newspaper, La Provincia di Bolzano, with the headline "Il Duce per la più grande Bolzano".](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/03_press_print/LPB_1935-02-21_p1.jpg)
*La Provincia di Bolzano*, 21 February 1935: Mussolini orders the industrial zone. The communiqué names the industrialists: Falck, Donegani, Lancia, Rivetti, Goisis. Biblioteca Provinciale Italiana Claudia Augusta; out of copyright, study copy. [Row 3.18](row:3.18).

The historian Carlo Romeo [has shown](https://www.novecento.org/sul-confine-lalto-adige-sudtirol-nella-storia-del-novecento/il-fascismo-al-brennero-un-breve-profilo-per-questioni-7361/) how the grants were tied to where the workers came from. They were calculated partly on the number of employees from outside the province: "manodopera regnicola e non allogena", in the words of the commission that awarded them in 1936. *Regnicoli* were people of the old kingdom. *Allogeni*, "of other stock", was the regime's word for the German speakers. Read that way, a subsidy for industry was also a payment for changing the population.

In August 1935 Mussolini came in person, during the army manoeuvres that went with the mobilisation for Ethiopia. Luce filmed him [honouring the marble busts](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020037/2/la-visita-mussolini-alla-citta-bolzano.html) of Battisti, Filzi and Chiesa inside the monument and [at the boundary stone](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020066/2/mussolini-visita-confine-italo-austriaco-presso-passo-del-brennero.html) on the Brenner, and Italy's illustrated weekly [put him on its cover](row:17.14), climbing the monument's steps. That year Luce made 25 stories on the province, the first of two peaks.

In December 1936 ministers [opened the industrial zone](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000027681/2/il-ministro-benni-e-conte-volpi-inaugurano-nuovo-centro-industriale-bolzano.html) and walked into a Lancia plant. *La Stampa* wrote ["Bolzano industriale"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,4/articleid,1136_01_1936_0298_0004_24917548/), and in February 1937 *Le Vie d'Italia*, which fifteen years earlier had printed visitor figures, ran ["Opere del Regime – Bolzano e la nuova Zona Industriale"](https://archive.org/details/2022-12-06-02-43?q=%22zona+industriale%22). Turin had a reason to follow. Lancia was a Turin firm, and the zone's skilled workers came largely from the parent factories in Piedmont and Lombardy. When Vincenzo Lancia died that February the paper counted [forty thousand people](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,7/articleid,1132_01_1937_0041_0007_24920881/) behind the coffin. *La Stampa* was describing Bolzano to a city whose workers were moving there.

According to Romeo, the labourers came above all from the Veneto and Trentino. From 1936–37 the state's Commissariat for migration and internal colonisation had an office in Bolzano that [supervised the transfers](https://www.asei.eu/it/2017/05/operazioni-migratorie-belliche-transfrontaliere-e-rimpatri-da-una-parte-allaltra-delle-alpi/) together with the Fascist party. Once the plants opened, families arrived in numbers, above all from the provinces of Padua, Verona and Rovigo. Romeo gives the arrivals year by year: 7,655 in 1937, 4,203 in 1938, 5,773 in 1939 and 6,425 in 1940. That is 24,056 people in four years, into a city that had counted [45,505 inhabitants](row:4.01) at the census of 1936 and 32,679 in 1921.

1938 was Luce's second peak, with 26 stories: the year of the Anschluss, of [Hitler's train](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000084593/1/dal-brennero-roma-arrivo-alla-stazione-ostiense-prima-giornata-romana-piazza-siena-manovra-guerra-aerea-furbara-esercitazione.html) cheered through Bolzano station on its way to Rome, and of the princes' tour of the new factories. In June *La Stampa* wrote ["Bolzano nuova"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,5/articleid,1130_01_1938_0140_0005_24928361/).

Most of the newcomers were housed by the [provincial public-housing institute](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf), founded in 1934. Its largest project was the Rione Dux, known as the Semirurali, the "half-rural" houses: two-storey houses, each with a vegetable plot meant, as the city's [own account](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf) puts it, to supplement devalued wages "secondo l'ideologia ruralista del fascismo". The first 92 buildings with 342 dwellings were inaugurated on 8 October 1939, and Luce [was there](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022261/2/superba-realizzazione-del-regime-benessere-lavoratori-nella-provincia-del-brennero-inaugurata-dal-ministro-segretario-del-partito.html). In the end there were 327 houses with 1,151 dwellings and about 5,500 people; other counts give slightly fewer dwellings. Propaganda compared them to "la piccola casa di Predappio", the house where Mussolini was born. People in the blocks nearby called the quarter "Shanghai". The rest of Italy read about it the next morning, when *La Stampa* [reported](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0030_01_1939_0239A_0001_16426902/) "l'inaugurazione di un rione di 300 casette rurali". By the end of that month the industrial zone [employed 3,004 workers](https://www.provinz.bz.it/cultura/manifestazioni_old/archivio/mostra_foto/volumi/miori_index.htm) in twenty firms: 900 at the aluminium works, 650 at the steelworks, 600 at Lancia.

Those who lived in the Semirurali, recorded decades later for the museum in the last surviving house, tell it from the inside. "Mio padre è venuto in bicicletta dal Cadore", one says. My father came by bicycle from the Cadore, found work at Lancia and soon had the flat as well. And another: "Siamo diventati tutti bolzanini … il nostro dialetto non era un dialetto veneto o un dialetto calabrese ma un dialetto bolzanino." We all became people of Bolzano; our dialect was not Venetian or Calabrian, it was Bolzano's.

In December 1939 *La Stampa* could write ["La grande Bolzano"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,3/articleid,0030_01_1939_0298_0003_24256021/).

## 1939–1943: the other direction

In the same months, the people who had been there before were asked to choose whether to stay.

The Option had no treaty behind it. It came out of a conference in Berlin on 23 June 1939, as the Tyrolean state archive [sets out](https://www.tirol.gv.at/kunst-kultur/landesarchiv/archivglossar/archivglossar-o/). German-speaking South Tyroleans were to declare for German citizenship, which meant resettlement in the Reich, or for Italian. Italians elsewhere followed it in headlines. *La Stampa*, 13 July: ["Tutti gli stranieri lasceranno l'Alto Adige"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,2/articleid,1128_01_1939_0165_0002_24253200/), all foreigners will leave the Alto Adige.

The rules came in October, and the vocabulary split by language. *La Provincia di Bolzano* presented them as rules for ["il rimpatrio dei cittadini germanici e … l'emigrazione di allogeni tedeschi"](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001-1939-1939_10_26_Nr_254.html): repatriation, and emigration. The German-language papers kept to the legal term, ["Option der Staatsbürgerschaft"](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043498). *Dolomiten* printed [the prefect's warning](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115205581) that after twenty years nobody had the right any longer "am Fenster zu stehen", to stand at the window and look on. In November a new state body, the Ente nazionale per le Tre Venezie, was [set up by law](https://www.unibz.it/assets/Documents/Competence-Centres/unibz-storiaregionale-DiMichele-Conquista-del-suolo.pdf) to take over the property of those who left and to resell it. On 22 December *La Stampa* called it ["Libera e spontanea scelta della cittadinanza"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,2/articleid,0030_01_1939_0303_0002_24256161/), a free and spontaneous choice of citizenship. Declarations closed on the 31st.

On 11 January 1940 *La Provincia di Bolzano* [printed the official count](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001-1940-1940_01_11_Nr_9.html) on its front page: 166,488 for Germany, 27,712 for Italy, and 35,300 who had signed nothing and so remained Italian citizens. The [headline in Turin](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,1125_01_1940_0010_0001_16081399/) is word for word the same, which points to a single agency text sent to both.

The percentage usually quoted depends on what is counted. The official result was [69.4 per cent](https://www.tirol.gv.at/kunst-kultur/landesarchiv/archivglossar/archivglossar-o/) for Germany, among some 267,000 people entitled to choose. The familiar 86 per cent is a later recalculation for the territory of today's province.

On film there was nothing. Luce's catalogue has no item on the Option under any of the 47 search terms tried, although in December 1939 it showed [the "rimpatrio" of Germans from the Baltic](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022891/2/il-rimpatrio-tedeschi-dal-baltico-croce-rossa-tedesca-e-organizzazioni-assistenziali-naziste-hanno-preso-cura-questi-rimpatriati.html), and in March 1940 the [meeting of Mussolini and Hitler](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000024850/2/l-incontro-duce-e-fuehrer.html) on the Brenner. A search by keyword cannot prove that nothing was shot. It can say that nothing is catalogued.

Soldiers were on the move as well. Bolzano was the seat of [an infantry division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Infantry_Division_%22Brennero%22) and Merano of an Alpine one, and in 1939–40 the frontier was fortified in a hurry: by [one account](https://www.south-tirol.com/blog/south-tyrols-bunker-landscape) more than a thousand bunkers were planned and 306 finished. No figure for the garrison has been found. What survives is the kind of thing a garrison produces. On 22 April 1940 a soldier at the munitions depot of Stilves, on the Brenner line, wrote [two postcards](row:2.08) to a family at Sant'Ippolito di Vernio, near Florence. "Cordiali saluti", says one. Both cards were printed by Fränzl of Bolzano, and one shows the Madonna of Maria Trens. They were kept by the library of Vernio. Soldiers' post was probably the largest channel outside the media through which ordinary Italian families came to hold a picture of this place in their hands. That is an estimate; nobody has counted.

![The back of a postcard with a red stamp, addressed by hand to Signor Ricci Ferdinando, S. Ippolito di Vernio (Firenze), and dated Stilves 22-4-40-XVIII.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/02_newsreels_radio_mobilization/IA_Cartolina_del_Brennero_1940-04-22_back.jpg)
A postcard written at Stilves on 22 April 1940, year XVIII of the Fascist era, by a soldier of the munitions depot. Biblioteca Petrarca, Vernio, via Internet Archive, Public Domain Mark. [Row 2.08](row:2.08).

In November 1941 the commissariat that carried out the Option [moved into](row:13.12) a building just finished on the new Corso: the Palazzo del Turismo, the palace of tourism.

About 75,000 people [had left by 1943](https://www.uibk.ac.at/de/zeitgeschichte/regionalgeschichte/remigra/projekt/); the archive in Innsbruck [counts 81,500](https://www.tirol.gv.at/kunst-kultur/landesarchiv/archivglossar/archivglossar-u/) by including the men drafted into German service. No figure has been found for the city of Bolzano alone. The two movements mirror each other: four to eight thousand Italians arriving each year, some 75,000 South Tyroleans gone in four. By the municipal register the city had 64,494 inhabitants at the start of 1943, nearly twice the number of 1921. In the province as a whole, Italian speakers were 24.7 per cent of the population in 1939 and 35.9 per cent in 1943.

## 1944–1949: names

In 1944 and 1945 a Nazi transit camp stood next to the streets of the Semirurali. Its prisoners are the one population of 1940s Bolzano that can be read name by name online. Dario Venegoni's [book](https://deportati.it/static/pdf/libri/venegoni_sec.pdf) lists 7,982 people with birthplace, trade, arrest, transport and fate. One entry reads: "Trevisan Romeo. Nato a Padova (PD) il 17/6/1915, meccanico. Arrestato a Bolzano … Deceduto a Gusen il 29/3/1945." The note beneath adds that he worked at Lancia and led the resistance cell in the factory under the name "Trevi". A mechanic from Padua at a Turin firm's plant in Bolzano, dead at Gusen at twenty-nine.

Of the 41 people killed in Bolzano on 3 May 1945, whose origins [the record](https://www.straginazifasciste.it/wp-content/uploads/schede/Bolzano,%203-05-1945.pdf) takes from the city's register, this research counts five from the province of Padua, five from Verona, three from Rovigo and six from Trentino.

After the war some of those who had opted came back: about 9,000 by the legal route between 1949 and 1955, and by [other estimates](https://journals.univie.ac.at/index.php/oezg/article/download/5441/5369/11321) 15,000 to 25,000 in all. One family is among the voices recorded at the Semirurali museum: "1949, im Oktober, sind wir von Graz als Rückoptanten nach Bozen gekommen. Es war eine große Errungenschaft die Wohnung in der via Bari." In October 1949 we came from Graz to Bolzano as returning optants, and the flat in via Bari was a great thing to have got. So one quarter of the city holds all three movements: the workers who came, the optants who left and returned, and the prisoners of the camp next door.

For ordinary families the paper trail is thinner, and it runs backwards. Civil registration in South Tyrol began only in 1924 and stays with the municipalities. The state's genealogy portal [has nothing for Bolzano](https://antenati.cultura.gov.it/archivio/archivio-di-stato-di-bolzano/), and for Rovigo, where many of the families came from, [almost only registers from Napoleon's time](https://antenati.cultura.gov.it/archivio/archivio-di-stato-di-rovigo/). A family that reached the Semirurali from the Polesine in 1938 has to be looked for first in Bolzano, through the city's [historical certificates of residence](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Servizi/Chiedere-un-certificato-anagrafico), and only then in the Veneto, where [FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/1482864) holds the civil registers of Rovigo from 1871 to 1937. For those who left there is a better tool: the University of Innsbruck's [OptionDigital](https://explore-research.uibk.ac.at/p/optiondigital/) has put the resettlement card index online, card by card.

By 1943 both Bolzanos existed, and one of them was built. What happened to it is the next text.

# In stone, 1926–2026

The Fascist city in Bolzano was announced in 1926, was still being finished in 1957 and lost part of its courthouse in 2026. In a hundred years very little of it has been pulled down on purpose. This is what was built, what went, and what has been added.

## 1926–1928: an arch on another monument's foundations

When Italy took Bolzano there was a half-built monument on the far bank of the Talvera: the Austrian [memorial to the Kaiserjäger](row:5.02), the imperial riflemen, begun in 1917 and never finished. On 6 February 1926 Mussolini told the Chamber that an Italian monument would rise on the same foundations, and a local paper [printed the sentence](row:17.21) four days later. It was first meant for Cesare Battisti, the Trentino socialist hanged by Austria in 1916. His widow refused the dedication, and it became a monument to victory. The first stone [was laid](row:5.03) on 12 July 1926. The Austrian drum was taken down, from the spring of 1927 by the account of the exhibition now inside the monument, and Marcello Piacentini's arch, its columns shaped as fasces, was inaugurated by the king two years to the day after the first stone.

Across the top it says, in Latin, HIC PATRIAE FINES SISTE SIGNA. HINC CETEROS EXCOLVIMVS LINGVA LEGIBVS ARTIBVS. Roughly: here are the borders of the fatherland, set down the standards; from here we educated the others in language, laws and arts.

On the day of the inauguration a newspaper in Innsbruck [printed two drawings](row:17.24), one above the other: the Austrian memorial, "das bereits fast vollendet war, von den Italienern aber zerstört wurde", almost finished but destroyed by the Italians, and under it "das 'Sieges'-Denkmal" that stood on its foundations. The quotation marks round "victory" are the paper's. They are an early case of answering the monument without touching it, by punctuation.

![A newspaper page in Fraktur type with two drawings one above the other: a round stone memorial, and an arch with columns.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/17_monument_visit/images/1928-07-12_Innsbrucker_Nachrichten_p2_visit.jpg)
*Innsbrucker Nachrichten*, 12 July 1928: the destroyed memorial above, the "Sieges"-Denkmal below. The reproduction shown under the monument; own photograph, 14 November 2026, cut to the page and set square. [Row 17.24](row:17.24).

## 1931: a bridge named for a Roman

Ponte Druso opened on 28 October 1931, the ninth anniversary of the March on Rome: [three arches](row:5.10) of reinforced concrete faced in porphyry, named after the Roman general who took the Alps in 15 BC, so that an Italian Bolzano would look like a return. On tall stone pylons stood four eagles, [carved by Vittorio Morelli](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-nel-museo-del-monumento-1.508168) of Ancona.

Drusus was meant to go further. Ettore Tolomei, who had drawn up the Italian place names of the province, wanted the statue of the medieval poet Walther von der Vogelweide, on the main square of the old town, replaced by one of Drusus. Mussolini [refused](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf), counting the cost abroad. In 1935 the statue was moved to a park all the same, and the Touring Club's monthly [told Italy](row:14.09) that Bolzano had removed it "per sostituirvi quella di Druso, il fondatore della città", to put in its place that of Drusus, the founder of the city. No Drusus was ever put up.

## 1935–1943: a new centre, left unfinished

Across the river the new city took shape along one avenue, the Corso, opened in 1938 and named first for the Lictor and then for the day the Empire was proclaimed. Behind the monument a column given by the city of Rome [was unveiled](row:17.26) on 5 June 1938, to the men of the province killed in East Africa, Libya and Spain.

On the Corso the engineer Armando Ronca, born in Verona, [designed in 1938](row:13.02) a Palazzo del Turismo for the city's tourist office, with a concert hall. The building commission, the city's [guide to its Fascist architecture](row:5.10) says, laughed at its slender columns as "fiammiferi", matchsticks. It was finished in November 1941 and at once [given another use](row:13.12): it became the seat of the commissariat that carried out the Option. The palace of tourism housed the office of resettlement.

Further south, two buildings were set face to face on one square. The Casa del Fascio went up between 1939 and 1942. Across its front runs [a relief by Hans Piffrader](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/bassorilievo.html), a sculptor from Chiusa: 57 panels of travertine, 36 metres long, with Mussolini on horseback at the centre and the motto "credere, obbedire, combattere", believe, obey, fight. The last panels arrived about two months before the regime fell on 25 July 1943. Opposite, the courthouse was begun in 1939 and stopped in 1942.

## After 1945: finished by the Republic

Very little was undone. Street names were cleared of Fascist leaders (Corso Littorio became Corso della Libertà, Piazza Arnaldo Mussolini became Piazza del Tribunale), but the names of the nation and the war stayed. Mussolini's name was taken off the side of the monument. The heritage office in Trento proposed taking the relief down, without success, and [three of its 57 panels](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/bassorilievo.html) were mounted only in 1957.

The courthouse was taken up again in 1951 and, [according to the German Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justizpalast_(Bozen)), inaugurated by the President of the Republic in 1956. Across its front it carries the words chosen under the regime: PRO ITALICO IMPERIO VIRTVTE IVSTITIA HIERARCHIA VNGVIBVS ET ROSTRIS. Roughly: for the Italic empire, by valour, justice and hierarchy, with claw and beak.

The Palazzo del Turismo became a cinema in 1946, [Il Corso](row:13.15), with roughly 800 seats. The Casa del Fascio became the tax office. The Fascist "new Bolzano" survived by going on being used: court, tax office, church, station, schools, housing.

## The 1970s and 1980s: what went

The eagles did not come down in 1945. A [postcard of the 1960s](row:5.08) shows them in place. They went during a renovation of the bridge in the 1970s, in a year the sources do not agree on, and they were not put back. "Troppo grandi, troppo fasciste e quindi ingombranti", the daily *Alto Adige* [explained in 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/ponte-druso-40-anni-senza-aquile-1.300394): too big, too fascist, and so in the way. No decision ordering their removal has been found. The whole story has a row of its own in this library, [*The eagles of Ponte Druso*](row:5.01).

![A colour postcard looking along a bridge: small cars on the roadway, and on both sides eagles on tall pylons of reddish stone.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/05_landmarks_heritage/images/01_pontedruso__Postcard_-_bolzano_-_ponte_druso_-_anni_60_53851921130_.jpg)
Ponte Druso in the 1960s, some twenty years after the regime, with the eagles still on their pylons. Postcard; public domain, Wikimedia Commons. [Row 5.08](row:5.08).

In January 1985 a local monthly [printed the bridge twice](row:5.33), with its balustrade and eagles and with its new iron railing, under the caption "prima e dopo la cura: non servono commenti", before and after the cure, no comment needed.

The Semirurali went in the same decades, to housing renewal. [One house](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Vivere-il-Comune/Luoghi/Casa-Semirurale) was kept.

Then the cinema. In 1980 the Province [declined to buy](row:13.21) the Corso from the tourist office. A development company obtained the demolition permit on 15 February 1988, and the building was still standing when the provincial council [heard its legal history](row:13.23) on 30 March. By August 1989 a councillor of the post-fascist MSI could [list it](row:13.25) with the bridge in one series: "In tempi recenti Bolzano ha visto la distruzione del ponte Druso, del cinema Corso e ora siamo alla volta dell'ex G.I.L." Lately Bolzano has seen the destruction of Ponte Druso and of the Cinema Corso, and now it is the turn of the former building of the Fascist youth.

None of these went because of what they stood for, or not openly. A renovation took the eagles, renewal took the houses, a developer took the cinema.

## 2001–2017: adding

At the end of 2001 the city renamed the monument's square Piazza della Pace, Peace Square. In October 2002 a referendum [gave it its name back](row:5.12), Piazza della Vittoria, with 61.94 per cent. The street sign now carries both, the failed name in brackets.

After that the method changed, from renaming to adding. In 2004 the city [set up plaques](row:5.09) across the road from the monument, in four languages, saying what it was. In January 2011 the culture minister in Rome [promised the Province](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/2011-un-concorso-di-idee.html) that the monument would be put in context, and a week later the Province opened a competition of ideas for the relief on the Casa del Fascio. It drew 486 proposals.

In December 2013 the two best-preserved heads of the bridge's eagles, which had lain for years in a municipal nursery, were [carried into the crypt](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-in-volo-fino-alla-cripta-del-monumento-alla-vittoria-1.372917) of the monument.

Two of the four eagles today, in the crypt of the Monument to Victory. Own photograph, 14 November 2026. [Row 17.25](row:17.25).

On 21 July 2014 a documentation centre, BZ '18–'45, opened in the rooms under the monument. Outside, a ring of red running text was clamped round one of the fasces columns. Jeffrey Schnapp, who worked on it, [called it](https://karolinum.cz/data/clanek/6122/ST_17_2_0011.pdf) a "nose ring for Fascist rhetoric". More than 24,300 people [visited](https://www.monumentoallavittoria.com/it/info/stampa.html) in 2015.

And since November 2017 a sentence of Hannah Arendt's runs in lit letters across Piffrader's relief, in German, Italian and Ladin: ["Nessuno ha il diritto di obbedire"](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/arendt.html), nobody has the right to obey. It answers the relief's motto with its own middle word.

## 2018–2026: what is still unexplained

Much has had no such answer. The reliefs by Piffrader on the savings bank's headquarters were restored in 2017–18, and [the announcement of the work](row:5.22) says nothing of their history. The [column of 1938](row:17.19) behind the monument and the lines of Virgil on the insurance building beside it are, in the words of a [university excursion report](https://www.geschichtsdidaktik.phil.fau.de/files/2026/06/Exkursionsbericht_gesamt.pdf) of 2026, "nicht erklärt", not explained; others have answered the column with paint, a mourning drape and a wreath for the victims of Italian colonialism. The bridge and the stadium still carry the name of Drusus. And in August 2018 a parish cinema near the Corso, the Concordia, [was demolished](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/demolito-l-ex-cinema-concordia-1.1713147) for a block of flats; the press recorded no debate.

On 16 July 2026 the courthouse lost most of its central wing, which [collapsed](https://orf.at/stories/3436356/) during work on its columns. The historian Günther Pallaver said that the Fascist monuments had never really been de-fascistised after 1945, and "das rächt sich jetzt": that is taking its revenge now. Above the rubble the inscription of the empire is whole. It has never been explained on the spot.

# On sale, 1950–2025

The posters of the 1930s sold the Alto Adige without its Fascist city. This last text follows the selling after the war, up to the year in which Bolzano counted more than a million overnight stays.

## 1950–1961: the public changes

In 1950 Italians accounted for [85.1 per cent](row:6.13) of the province's overnight stays. It was the public of the 1930s, carried over. Foreign arrivals overtook Italian ones in 1954, and by 1957 the Italian share of nights had fallen to 45.8 per cent. In 1961, the year of the separatist bombings, Italian arrivals fell by a quarter.

The city of Bolzano kept its Italian guests longer. They were still more than half in 1990. They are under a third now.

## 1981–2005: the old square, and a brand

The statue of Walther von der Vogelweide, moved to a park by the regime in 1935, [came back](row:14.04) to its square in 1981. Since [the early 1990s](https://www.italia.it/it/alto-adige/bolzano/cosa-fare/mercatini-di-natale-di-bolzano) the Christmas market has filled that square, in the old town.

![A square with a monument on a stepped base and a fountain basin; a woman and a boy feed pigeons.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/14_city_architecture/images/1933_Gabinio_116B5_Piazza_Walther_monument.jpg)
The Walther monument on its square in 1933, two years before the regime moved it. Photograph by Mario Gabinio; CC BY 3.0 IT, Fondazione Torino Musei via Wikimedia Commons. [Row 14.04](row:14.04).

In 2005 the province gave itself a brand, and with it a motto: ["die kontrastreiche Symbiose aus alpin und mediterran"](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/fuenf-jahre-dachmarke-suedtirol-neue-massstaebe), a symbiosis of the Alpine and the Mediterranean, rich in contrasts. It sells the position on the border itself.

## 2010–2025: a million nights

In 2025 Bolzano passed a million overnight stays for the first time. The count was [1,042,903](row:6.13), 81 per cent more than in 2010 and 45 per cent more than in 2019, while the province as a whole grew by 34 and 13.5 per cent.

| Bolzano | 2010 | 2019 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrivals | 258,727 | 343,777 | 457,548 |
| Overnight stays | 574,849 | 720,463 | 1,042,903 |
| Nights spent by foreign guests | 56.2% | 61.8% | 68.3% |
| Nights spent outside hotels | 13.2% | 24.9% | 32.8% |
| Guest beds | 3,614 | 3,912 | 5,537 |

It is a city people pass through. In 2023 the average stay was 2.24 nights, and [no municipality in the province](row:6.06) had more arrivals. Measured per inhabitant the load is light, about 8 nights against 67 for the province. It falls on a few places and on housing. The cable car to the Renon [carried more than a million passengers](https://www.stol.it/artikel/chronik/rittner-seilbahn-knackt-rekord-ueber-eine-million-fahrgaeste-seit-jahresbeginn) in the first ten months of 2025. A third of the nights are now spent outside hotels. The city's tourism councillor [said in 2024](https://www.stol.it/artikel/politik/urteil-zur-touristischen-vermietung-was-bedeutet-das-in-bozen) that private holiday lets had added 800 beds in four years, in flats taken from the housing market. A petition for a stop on new beds, under the slogan "Tourismus in Maßen statt in Massen", in moderation and not in masses, [gathered about 11,000 signatures](https://www.change.org/p/bettenstopp-jetzt-f%C3%BCr-s%C3%BCdtirol-basta-con-l-aumento-dei-posti-letto-in-alto-adige?lang=de-DE).

The old figures give the scale. The 77,143 visitors of 1921 were not counted the way arrivals are today, but 2025's 457,548 arrivals are about six times as many. A poster of about 1936 advertised capacity itself: [*L'estate nelle Dolomiti – 60 stazioni con 40.000 letti*](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140018192), sixty resorts with forty thousand beds, for the whole of the Dolomites. Bolzano alone now has 5,537.

## Today: the two Bolzanos again

Both Bolzanos are still there, and they sit side by side in the city's own tourist communication.

The information pages are frank. The Province's tourism portal [calls the Monument to Victory](https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/experiences-and-events/plp-experiences/experiences-south-tyrol/pdp-experience.smgpoiarchapp97.victory-monument.bolzano-centro-bozen-zentrum) an "apologia for the conquest-hungry fascists" and "a grave insult to German-speaking South Tyroleans". The tourist office's [list of sights](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/en/tourist-attractions.htm) has the documentation centre, the Semirurali house and a full itinerary of the camp.

The sales copy is the poster again. The tourist office's history page gives Fascism [a single sentence](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/bolzano-storia-cultura.htm), on the town plan of 1935. Another page [still says](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/storia-e-cultura) that the city "nasce probabilmente come statio militare romana nel 15 a.C. sotto il nome di 'Pons Drusi'", which is the founding story the regime wrote onto its bridge. None of the [twelve official audioguides](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/audioguide/) covers a site of the Fascist years. The one regular tour of the interwar architecture [is sold as](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/visite-guidate-bolzano.htm) "Bolzano, armonia di stili e sapori: storia, architettura e gelato". It ends at the Monument to Victory, and then at an ice-cream parlour.

The posters of the 1930s left the Fascist city out. Today's copy folds it in, as one contrast more, under "armonia".

Even the corrections can turn into attractions. Two scholars [compared](row:6.01) the red ring on the monument to "the 'open' sign of a cheap restaurant", which leaves the monument "once again 'open for business'".

## What the split leaves

The regime's media divided the work between them, and the present has kept the division. Posters made the Alto Adige desirable by leaving the Fascist city out. Newsreels, newspapers and magazines made Bolzano Italian by putting that city at the centre. Workers, soldiers and optants made both real.

The evidence lies along the same routes. Bolzano's image from those years is kept in Merano and in Treviso, in Budapest (the Hungarian National Museum has a Lenhart *Merano*), in Turin (*La Stampa*), in Rome (Luce) and in Innsbruck (the Option cards). A village library in Tuscany kept a soldier's postcards. The map of the archives is the map of the movements.

One address holds the workers who came, the optants who left and returned, and the prisoners of the camp next door. It is the last Semirurali house, via Bari 11. It [opens on Saturday afternoons](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Vivere-il-Comune/Luoghi/Casa-Semirurale).

The studio this research belongs to works on translocality: on how a place is made by what moves through it. Among the closest existing work is Catrina Klee's doctoral project in Lucerne, [which argues](https://www.unilu.ch/fileadmin/fakultaeten/ksf/institute/gsl/doktorierende/Abstract_PhD_Catrina_Klee.pdf) that the tourism projects of the 1920s to the 1940s "acted as accelerators of the Italianization". As far as this research could find, nobody has yet described Bolzano itself in those terms.
