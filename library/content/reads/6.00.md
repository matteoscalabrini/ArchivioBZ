# The market turns north, 1950–2016

In 1950 the visitors to South Tyrol were Italians. In 2025 the city of Bolzano counted more than a million overnight stays for the first time, and the largest group of its guests came neither from Italy nor from Germany. This text follows the province's tourism from the one date to the other: who came, under which name the place was sold, what the numbers are now, and what a visitor to Bolzano is told today about the city Fascism built.

## 1950–1961: Italians first, then Germans

The provincial statistics office publishes [a series from 1950](row:6.13), commune by commune. It shows something the histories of the "Südtirol" brand do not spell out. In 1950 Italian guests accounted for 85.1 per cent of the province's 1,826,166 overnight stays. That was the tourism of the years between the wars carried on: Italians going to the Alto Adige and the Dolomites the posters had sold them.

Then the guests changed. Foreign arrivals passed Italian ones in 1954. By 1957 Italians had 45.8 per cent of the nights, and by 1960 37.4. In 1961, the year of the bombing campaign by South Tyrolean separatists, Italian arrivals fell from 217,274 to 163,493, a quarter in one year, and the Italian share of nights dropped to 25.4 per cent. Foreign arrivals hardly moved. A review of a recent history of those years [puts it](https://www.falter.at/zeitung/20260407/fallbeispiel-suedtirol) in one sentence: "Die Bomben verschreckten die südlichen Besucher, während jene aus dem Norden vermehrt buchten." The bombs scared off the visitors from the south, while those from the north booked all the more.

So the Tyrolean image of the province, the costumes and farmhouses and onion domes, became the commercial rule for a market that had already turned north. That reading is this research's own, and it would have to be tested against the posters in the museums.

Bolzano itself did not follow. Italians kept about half of the city's nights from 1955 to 1990, and the total barely grew for fifty years: 290,832 nights in 1950, 441,110 in 1960, 512,697 in 1980, 496,423 in 2000. The Italian-speaking capital stayed outside the rural picture the province was selling.

## 1961–2005: a brand grows

Between 1960 and 1980 the province's overnight stays [rose](https://www.trauttmansdorff.it/en/Magazin/Tourismus-in-Suedtirol-1961-bis-1983) from 3.7 million to 17 million. The tourism museum in Merano, which studied those years through some ninety interviews, describes a hinge region where the German and Austrian boom met the Italian one, and a place that "became a brand" through souvenirs, music and food.

How much of this rested on what the regime had built is the subject of a doctoral project in Lucerne. Catrina Klee [argues](row:6.04) that the Fascist state's roads, cable cars and hotels were the basis for a region of car and sports tourism, that the tourism projects of the 1920s to the 1940s "acted as accelerators of the Italianization", and that even the unbuilt ones shaped what came after 1950.

The brand got its form in 2005. The umbrella mark "Südtirol" replaced, by one account, about ninety tourist logos, and its creed [was](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/fuenf-jahre-dachmarke-suedtirol-neue-massstaebe) "die kontrastreiche Symbiose aus alpin und mediterran, Spontaneität und Verlässlichkeit, Natur und Kultur": the symbiosis, rich in contrast, of Alpine and Mediterranean, spontaneity and reliability, nature and culture. In 2016 the marketing company was merged into a new agency of the Province and the Chamber of Commerce, IDM, whose stated mission [is](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/idm-suedtirol-zum-begehrtesten-lebensraum-europas-machen) to make South Tyrol "zum begehrtesten Lebensraum Europas", the most desirable place to live in Europe.

The two names live on inside the one brand. The logo says "Südtirol". The Italian manuals [call it](row:6.08) the "marchio Alto Adige". The official tourism portal runs under suedtirol.info in every language.

In 2009 the Dolomites were put on the World Heritage List. A study by the Bank of Italy [finds](row:6.16) that the listed communes had more visitors afterwards, Italians above all, with no more beds.

# A million nights, 2010–2026

## The count

In 2010 Bolzano had 258,727 arrivals and 574,849 overnight stays. In 2019 it had 720,463 nights; in 2025, [1,042,903](row:6.15), with 457,548 arrivals. Over the fifteen years the city's nights grew by 81 per cent and the province's by 34. Since 2019 the city has grown by 45 per cent, more than three times as fast as the province.

Three things changed with the number. The guests are more international: foreigners accounted for 56.2 per cent of the city's nights in 2010 and 68.3 in 2025, and guests from countries other than Italy and Germany, with 42.7 per cent, are now the largest group. They stay briefly, 2.3 nights on average. And they sleep less and less in hotels: nights outside hotels were 13.2 per cent of the total in 2010 and 32.8 in 2025.

Measured against the people who live there, Bolzano is still lightly visited. In 2023 it had about eight tourist nights per inhabitant, against 67 for the province as a whole. The same year it had more arrivals than any other commune in South Tyrol, ahead of Merano. It is a gate and a hub, where the valleys are resorts.

## Beds and flats

In July 2022 the provincial parliament [voted a ceiling](https://www.stol.it/artikel/politik/bettenstopp-verordnung-was-jetzt-gilt) on guest beds, fixed at the stock of 2019 plus rights acquired since; [the decree](row:6.05) followed in September. The beds went on growing all the same, from 233,847 in 2022 to 262,869 in 2025, through late registrations, older building rights and an advance of 7,000 beds to the communes. The councillor responsible [said](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/in-einem-jahr-10000-betten-mehr-in-suedtirol-trotz-bettenobergrenze) in 2025: "Die Bettenobergrenze greift", the ceiling is working. A petition for a real stop, "Tourismus in Maßen statt in Massen", tourism in moderation and not in masses, [gathered](https://www.change.org/p/bettenstopp-jetzt-f%C3%BCr-s%C3%BCdtirol-basta-con-l-aumento-dei-posti-letto-in-alto-adige?lang=de-DE) about 11,000 signatures in 2026.

In Bolzano the growth is in private flats. The city's beds outside hotels rose from 659 in 2010 to 1,326 in 2019 and 2,321 in 2025. When the city had 229 new beds to hand out under the ceiling, it gave 209 to businesses, 20 to farms and none to private landlords. Its tourism councillor [explained](https://www.stol.it/artikel/politik/urteil-zur-touristischen-vermietung-was-bedeutet-das-in-bozen): "Bei der Vermietung von Privatwohnungen gab es in Bozen einen extremen Boom … in den vergangenen 4 Jahren kamen 800 Betten dazu." In the letting of private flats there has been an extreme boom in Bolzano; 800 beds were added in the past four years. [A decree of April 2026](row:6.11) is the first provincial rule aimed at this: no one may be given beds that would take them above two rental flats.

No public count of the short-term flats exists, and the open database that maps them for other cities [does not cover](row:6.20) South Tyrol. What is published are asking prices. A property portal [put Bolzano](https://www.ildolomiti.it/societa/2025/carocasa-a-bolzano-si-sfiorano-i-5000-euro-al-metro-quadro-e-la-seconda-citta-piu-cara-ditalia-dopo-milano) at about 4,930 euros a square metre in 2025, "la seconda città più cara d'Italia dopo Milano", the second most expensive city in Italy after Milan. The figure comes from a sponsored article and could not be checked at its source.

## Where it presses

The pressure sits at a few points.

The archaeology museum with the Iceman had more than 300,000 visitors in 2024, by a press release this research could read only in a search engine's copy. The Province has [chosen a site](https://www.stol.it/artikel/politik/neues-archaeologiemuseum-enteignungsverfahren-gestartet) for a new museum costing about 98 million euros, on the edge of the 1930s city west of the Talvera.

The Christmas market has stood on Piazza Walther since 1991. In the pandemic winter of 2021–22 the organisers [handed out](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/30-bozner-christkindlmarkt-100-aussteller-315000-besucher) about 315,000 wristbands. For 2022–23 they [estimated](https://www.stol.it/artikel/chronik/weihnachtsmaerkte-schliessen-wir-sind-sehr-zufrieden) 800,000 visitors, and for 2025–26 [a million](https://www.ildolomiti.it/societa/2026/mercatino-di-natale-di-bolzano-un-successo-da-un-milione-di-visitatori). On the opening weekend of 2025 the city police counted 232 coaches. All of these are the organisers' estimates.

The cable car to the Renon plateau [carried](https://www.stol.it/artikel/chronik/rittner-seilbahn-knackt-rekord-ueber-eine-million-fahrgaeste-seit-jahresbeginn) 1,073,000 passengers in the first ten months of 2025. Since December 2024 residents with a season ticket have a queue of their own. It is the only measure found that was made expressly for the people who live here.

And the city is the base for day trips into mountains that have their own crowds: about 4,000 visitors a day [at the Seceda ridge](https://stol.it/artikel/chronik/wenn-der-schnell-tourismus-viel-schaden-anrichtet) and as many at the Karersee, the lake of the old hotel postcards; booking by car [made compulsory](https://www.stol.it/artikel/chronik/pragser-wildsee-zufahrtsbeschraenkung-wird-ausgeweitet) at the Lago di Braies in summer.

No tourism plan of the Comune was found. Three of the four pressure points are being enlarged: a new museum, a market now spread across the city and, by reports not checked here, more cabins on the cable car.

# What the visitor is told

## Two registers

The official channels do not hide the Fascist city. They speak about it in two voices.

The information pages are frank. The city tourist office's [list of sights](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/en/tourist-attractions.htm) has full entries on the exhibition under the Monument to Victory, on the Semirurali house, on the monument's fasces columns and its Latin inscription, and a whole itinerary of the Nazi transit camp of 1944–45. The provincial portal [says](https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/experiences-and-events/plp-experiences/experiences-south-tyrol/pdp-experience.smgpoiarchapp97.victory-monument.bolzano-centro-bozen-zentrum) of the monument that it was built "as apologia for the conquest-hungry fascists" and "was seen as a grave insult to German-speaking South Tyroleans". Its page on architecture [speaks](https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/experiences-and-events/culture-and-attractions/architecture) of "the fascist architecture in Bolzano/Bozen, which forcibly changed the townscape in the 1930s in the course of Italianisation".

What is sold sounds different. The one regular tour of the 1930s city is called "Bolzano, armonia di stili e sapori: storia, architettura e gelato", harmony of styles and flavours: history, architecture and ice cream. In its description the Tyrolean Middle Ages "si fonde con l'eleganza barocca e l'architettura italiana degli anni '20 e '30", merge with Baroque elegance and the Italian architecture of the 1920s and 1930s. The walk ends at an ice-cream parlour. The tourist office's short history of the city [gives the period](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/bolzano-storia-cultura.htm) one sentence and passes to the tourist boom of the 1960s. A search of the sights page for "Piffrader", "Casa del Fascio" and "Mussolini" found nothing.

The pattern is the brand's own. A creed of "contrasts" can take in a Fascist avenue as one more contrast.

Scholars have looked at the exhibition under the monument with the same suspicion. [One reading](row:6.01) notes that its ring of red light resembles "the 'open' sign of a cheap restaurant", so that the monument is "once again 'open for business', but business of a different order". [Another](row:6.03) compares Bolzano with Trieste, whose brochure on its rationalist architecture mentions Fascism three times.

## A word for it

The studio this research belongs to works under the heading of translocality: the idea, from the anthropologist Arjun Appadurai and the geographers who took it up, that a place is produced by what passes through it, and that people and things can be situated in several places at once. Border zones, tourist resorts and migrant quarters are the textbook cases. Bolzano is all three.

The research looked for anyone who had used the term for Bolzano or South Tyrol and found no one. The neighbouring work exists: histories of the border, studies of the monument and of the memory of the Option, research on migrants in the valleys, Klee's thesis on tourism. As far as could be found, they have not been joined under this word.

## Still open

- Whether postwar tourist posters echo or avoid those of the 1930s. No study compares them; the collections are in Merano and Treviso.
- How many day visitors Bolzano has. The statistics count only those who sleep.
- How many flats in the city are let to tourists.
- Visitor numbers for the exhibition under the monument and for the Semirurali house in recent years.
- What the printed guidebooks say about the Fascist city. None could be read for this research.
