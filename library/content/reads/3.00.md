# What the papers printed, 1919–1943

A reader in Turin and a reader in a farmhouse above Bolzano could open their newspapers on the same morning and find the same event. They did not find the same story. This text reads the press of the Fascist years side by side: the Fascist party's Italian daily in Bolzano, its German-language sister, the Catholic German paper that was allowed to go on, and *La Stampa* of Turin. A second part says where all of them can be read today.

## Four papers

*La Provincia di Bolzano* was the daily of the Fascist party in the province. Its first issue is dated 21 April 1927 in the two libraries that hold it, 22 April in the literature, and it ran until 1943. It wrote for Italians.

The *Alpenzeitung* was the regime's paper in German, from 1926 to 1943. By one account, which this research could not check, it was made largely of translations from the Italian daily and sold about 7,000 copies in 1937. Its headlines support the first half of that: when the Italian paper writes "la più grande Bolzano", the German one writes "Groß-Bolzano".

*Dolomiten* belonged to a Catholic publisher, came out three times a week and, [according to a recent study](https://historia-scribere-journal.uibk.ac.at/historia_scribere/article/download/4192/3052/9361), printed about 30,000 copies around 1927 under heavy censorship. It was the paper German speakers actually read.

*La Stampa* stands here for the national press because its archive is open. Turin was also the home of Lancia, which built a plant in Bolzano, so its readers had a reason to follow.

## 1919–1927: before the regime had a paper

In the first years Bolzano reaches Italian readers as a problem. *La Stampa* reports [the demands for autonomy](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,2/articleid,1178_01_1919_0345_0002_24858749/) in December 1919. On 26 April 1921 its front page [carries](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,1173_01_1921_0099_0001_17124196/) "Manifestazione tedesca a Bolzano conchiusasi tragicamente per l'intervento fascista", a German demonstration in Bolzano ended tragically by Fascist intervention: bombs and pistol shots at a costume parade, "1 morto e 8 feriti gravi", one dead and eight gravely wounded. In October 1922, three weeks before the March on Rome, it reports the Fascists in Bolzano and [the governor handing his powers](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0026_01_1922_0237_0001_17169364/) to the army after they occupied the provincial palace.

Then the measures. On 17 October 1923 the paper [explains](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,2/articleid,1171_01_1923_0247_0002_24867919/) "Nomi tedeschi e nomi italiani", German names and Italian names, and three weeks later [how](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,3/articleid,1171_01_1923_0266_0003_24867999/) "insegneremo l'italiano nelle scuole dell'Alto Adige", we shall teach Italian in the schools of the Alto Adige.

The Touring Club's monthly, *Le Vie d'Italia*, kept to tourism and its economics in these years. In March 1922 it [printed](https://archive.org/details/2023-02-27-02-08?q=Bolzano) Bolzano's visitor numbers: 77,143 in 1921, against 123,191 in 1913.

On 12 July 1926 the king laid the first stone of the Monument to Victory. The *Alpenzeitung*, then in its first year, [gave it the front page](row:3.04): the people of the mountains gather round their king, and the stone is sprinkled with water of the Piave. *La Stampa* [headed](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,1163_01_1926_0165_0001_17291562/) its report "L'Alto Adige acclama al Re", the Alto Adige acclaims the king, with Austrian veterans "in fraterna comunione", in brotherly communion, with Italian ones.

## 1928: one inauguration, four reports

The monument was inaugurated on 12 July 1928, and the four papers can be laid next to each other.

*La Provincia di Bolzano* [filled the width of its front page](row:3.08): "Il grandioso rito di ieri in Bolzano fascista. Il Sovrano consacra nel Monumento alla Vittoria il segno antico e nuovo del diritto immortale d'Italia." Yesterday's grand rite in Fascist Bolzano; the sovereign consecrates in the monument the ancient and new sign of Italy's immortal right.

![The front page of La Provincia di Bolzano with a headline across its whole width.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/03_press_print/LPB_1928-07-13_p1.jpg)
*La Provincia di Bolzano*, 13 July 1928, page 1. Biblioteca Provinciale Italiana Claudia Augusta; out of copyright, study copy. [Row 3.08](row:3.08).

*La Stampa* [called it](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0027_01_1928_0166_0001_24358966/) "La consacrazione eroica di Bolzano italiana", the heroic consecration of Italian Bolzano, after announcing the day before ["Tutta l'Italia oggi in Bolzano italiana"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0027_01_1928_0165_0001_16774629/), all Italy today in Italian Bolzano.

The *Alpenzeitung* [reported](row:3.07) the delegations from all over Italy, down to a message from the podestà of Bari. Two days later it [turned to Germany](row:3.10): how the radio station at Nauen had announced the unveiling, beside a polemic about "Südtirol" in German politics.

*Dolomiten* printed the least and one sentence that stands out. [Its report](row:3.09) of 14 July carries a wish: "Angesichts der glanzvollen Feier am Siegesdenkmal möge man auswärts endlich auf die hartnäckige Anfeindung der faschistischen Regierung verzichten." In view of the brilliant ceremony at the monument, may people abroad at last give up their stubborn hostility to the Fascist government. A censored paper is speaking, and it is not possible to say from the page whose sentence this is.

That summer *La Stampa* sent a correspondent, who wrote a series under the title ["Il rinnovamento italiano dell'Alto Adige"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0027_01_1928_0179_0001_16776122/), the Italian renewal of the Alto Adige. It is the first of several such series. The later titles tell the story by themselves: "Bolzano di domani" in 1935, "Bolzano industriale" in 1936, "Bolzano nuova" in 1938, "La grande Bolzano" in 1939.

## 1929–1934: exhibitions, trains, a bridge

The city now staged events for visitors, and the papers announced the reductions on the fare. *La Stampa* [reported](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,5/articleid,1155_01_1929_0172_0005_24884796/) the "fervore di preparativi", the fervour of preparations, for the first national exhibition of the Alto Adige in 1929. On 11 April 1930 the *Alpenzeitung* [described](row:3.11) an exhibition of plans for the city's growth: "Das Stadtbild von Neu-Bolzano", the townscape of New Bolzano.

In 1931 came the cheap excursion trains. The *Alpenzeitung* [announced](row:3.12) several "Volkszüge aus den alten Provinzen", people's trains from the old provinces, for the grape festival, and on 1 September [reported](row:3.13) the one from Turin. The traffic went both ways: readers were glad, the paper wrote that August, that a cheap train from Bolzano to Venice had been granted.

On 28 October 1931 the Druso bridge opened with other works of the regime. [The *Alpenzeitung*'s page](row:3.14) is in this library with [the Italian daily's](row:3.15). Two weeks later the German paper [admired](row:3.16) the lighting and "der geradezu zyklopische Neubau der Drususbrücke und deren einwandfreier Stil", the downright cyclopean new bridge and its impeccable style. In July 1932 *Le Vie d'Italia* [printed its photograph](https://archive.org/details/2021-11-03-22-15?q=Druso).

The glossy monthly of Mussolini's own paper, the *Rivista Illustrata del Popolo d'Italia*, was blunter than any daily. In 1932 it [wrote](https://archive.org/details/larivista-1932-vol2?q=Bolzano) of "questi tedeschi che popolano abusivamente il territorio italiano", these Germans who unlawfully populate Italian territory.

## 1935–1938: the industrial zone

On 20 February 1935 Mussolini chaired the meeting that decided the industrial zone. The next morning three papers ran the story, each under its own headline.

In Bolzano, in Italian: ["Le direttive del Capo per l'immediata realizzazione della zona industriale. Il Duce per la più grande Bolzano"](row:3.18). In Bolzano, in German: ["Mussolini für die Schaffung von Groß-Bolzano"](row:3.17), Mussolini for the creation of Greater Bolzano. In Turin: ["Una zona industriale a Bolzano sorgerà per volontà del Duce"](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,2/articleid,1137_01_1935_0045_0002_24911363/), an industrial zone will rise in Bolzano by the will of the Duce. The Italian daily's page names the firms: Falck, Donegani, Lancia, Rivetti, Goisis.

The German-language paper went on to cover the migration as a story of its own, which the Italian front pages did not. In September 1935 it explained the growth of the population by the arrival of the army corps command and by the zone. In July 1936 it counted "die viertausend Arbeiter, die gegenwärtig in unserer Stadt beschäftigt sind", the four thousand workers at present employed in our city.

The zone was inaugurated on 20 December 1936. The *Alpenzeitung* [wrote that morning](row:3.21): "Der Aufstieg Bolzanos wurde an dem Tage entschieden, als es im Jahre 1927 zur Provinzhauptstadt erhoben wurde." Bolzano's rise was decided on the day it was made a provincial capital in 1927. Its report of the tour [counts](row:3.22) "7000 Tonnen Aluminium" and "700 Arbeiter" at the Montecatini works and follows the guests "zu der Arbeiterstadt am rechten Ufer des Isarco", to the workers' town on the right bank of the Isarco. *La Stampa* [had it](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,1136_01_1936_0303A_0001_16693854/) on page one. The Italian daily's front page of 22 December is [all Ethiopia](row:3.23); the inauguration is inside, on pages this research has not read.

Two months later the Touring Club showed the zone to its members. *Le Vie d'Italia* of February 1937 [ran](https://archive.org/details/2022-12-06-02-43?q=%22zona+industriale%22) "Opere del Regime – Bolzano e la nuova Zona Industriale" by Emilio Emmer, with photographs by the Giacomelli studio of Venice. The prints are in the club's archive as a series of 1936, from [the view over the site](https://www.digitouring.it/it/cerca/33298-veduta-della-zona-industriale-di-bolzano-da-castel-flavone) to the first shed of the steelworks. Text and pictures were one campaign.

On 17 February 1937 *La Stampa* [reported](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,7/articleid,1132_01_1937_0041_0007_24920881/) that forty thousand people had followed the coffin of Vincenzo Lancia through Turin. In Bolzano that May the party secretary came, and the leaders of the province [gathered](row:3.24) "in der Halle des Lanciawerkes", in the hall of the Lancia works.

By 1938 the new city had a name in German print. A travelling opera stage [gave *Aida*](row:3.25) on "die Piazza dell'Impero, der schöne, weitgreifende Platz … im aufstrebenden Neu-Bolzano", the fine, wide square in rising New Bolzano.

## 1939–1941: the Option, in two vocabularies

In October 1939 the prefect and the German consul general signed the rules under which South Tyroleans were to choose between the two states. All the local papers printed the same text. The headlines differ, and the difference is the finding.

*La Provincia di Bolzano* [put it on page one](row:3.30) as "Le norme per il rimpatrio dei cittadini germanici e per l'emigrazione di allogeni tedeschi dall'Alto Adige in Germania": the rules for the repatriation of German citizens and the emigration of German *allogeni*, people of other stock, from the Alto Adige to Germany. The *Alpenzeitung* [printed it](row:3.29) on page two and *Dolomiten* [on page five](row:3.31) as "Durchführungsbestimmungen für die Option der Staatsbürgerschaft", implementing rules for the option of citizenship. Italians read about people leaving. German speakers read about a legal choice.

The pressure is in the German pages. *Dolomiten* [printed the prefect's speech](row:3.32). The option, he said, "ist nicht nur eine elementare Gewissenspflicht, sondern für alle ein kategorischer Imperativ", is not only an elementary duty of conscience but a categorical imperative for all, and "nach zwanzig Jahren hat niemand mehr das Recht, am Fenster zu stehen", after twenty years nobody has the right to stand at the window any more. The *Alpenzeitung* [explained](row:3.33) that "es gibt keine Volksabstimmung", there is no plebiscite, only white forms for those who wish to stay Italian citizens and yellowish-red ones for the others. From 23 November it ran [a box](row:3.34), nine times before Christmas, headed "Die Option der Atesiner": whoever decides to keep Italian citizenship, "und dadurch beweist, daß er sich als Italiener fühlt", and so proves that he feels Italian, "wird in seiner Heimat bleiben können", will be able to stay in his homeland.

The results came on 11 January 1940, and here Bolzano and Turin [print](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,1125_01_1940_0010_0001_16081399/) the same headline word for word: "I risultati ufficiali delle opzioni in Alto Adige comunicati al Duce e al Führer". On this evidence one text from the state news agency had gone to both. For the province of Bolzano [it gives](row:3.37) 166,488 for German citizenship, 27,712 for Italian, and 35,300 who signed nothing and so stayed Italian.

![The front page of La Provincia di Bolzano of 11 January 1940.](../../../research_notes/Fascist%20Bolzano%20media%20and%20migration/files/03_press_print/LPB_1940-01-11_p1.jpg)
*La Provincia di Bolzano*, 11 January 1940, page 1: the official results of the options. Biblioteca Provinciale Italiana Claudia Augusta; out of copyright, study copy. [Row 3.37](row:3.37).

That evening Turin's *Stampa Sera* [added](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,4/articleid,1614_02_1940_0010_0004_22260863/) a line of its own: "Sei campioni altoatesini hanno optato per la Germania", six South Tyrolean champions have opted for Germany.

In the same months the *Alpenzeitung* went on describing the city that was being built for those who came: the gardens of the Rione Dux "in der schönsten Üppigkeit", in their finest abundance, in August 1940; in March 1941 [two workers' quarters](row:3.39) "aus dem Nichts entstanden", risen from nothing; in July 1941 [a cinema](row:3.41) opening in the Rione Dux.

# Where to read them

## Online today

Nearly all of this can be read from a desk, in four places.

The German-language press is in the provincial library's digital collection, Teßmann digital: the *Alpenzeitung*, *Dolomiten*, the weekly *Volksbote* and the papers that end in the mid-1920s, with searchable text. The site refuses scripts and opens in an ordinary browser. Europeana carries a copy made in 2019 under the Public Domain Mark, and the pages in this library come from there. That copy has 4,830 issues of the *Alpenzeitung*, with gaps in 1938–40 and in 1942–43.

*La Provincia di Bolzano* is not there. The Italian provincial library, the Biblioteca Provinciale Italiana Claudia Augusta, shows it as [a gallery of page images](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001.html), one album per issue, from 21 April 1927 to 27 April 1941, with 1930 missing and no searchable text. The same gallery holds the earlier Italian paper *Il Piccolo Posto*, the regime's monthly *Atesia Augusta*, and the first years of Ettore Tolomei's *Archivio per l'Alto Adige*. Many of the daily's pages carry the stamp of the municipal library of Trento.

*La Stampa* is open at its own archive, article by article. Between 1919 and 1943 it has 115 articles that name Bolzano with the "monumento alla Vittoria", 18 on the "zona industriale di Bolzano", and 139 that name Bolzano with a "treno popolare".

The Internet Archive has nearly the whole run of *Le Vie d'Italia* for these years and all of the *Rivista Illustrata del Popolo d'Italia*, 1923 to 1943. It also has two books that presented the new province to Italians right after the annexation, both in this library: [a geographer's handbook of 1919](row:3.01) and Giuseppe Antonio Borgese's [*L'Alto Adige contro l'Italia*](row:3.02) of 1921, four letters from Bolzano.

## How to search

Since only the German papers can be searched, the practical way into the Italian daily runs through them: find the date in the *Alpenzeitung*, then open the same date in the gallery.

The search has to be made in the *Alpenzeitung*'s own German. The paper translated the regime's terms and did not borrow them. "Semirurali" returns nothing; "halbländlichen", half-rural, returns 50 issues. "Treno popolare" returns nothing; "Volkszug" returns 297. "Zona industriale" finds 20 issues, mostly small advertisements, and "Industriezone" 173. The scanned text also reads ü as ii and I as J, so "Jndustriezone" is worth trying.

## Not online

*Il Popolo d'Italia*, Mussolini's daily, was not found anywhere in a free digital run. The *Corriere della Sera* is behind a paywall. *L'Illustrazione Italiana* is online only up to 1923. The celebratory volumes a Fascist city usually printed about itself, a "Bolzano fascista" or a ten-year book, were not found online at all; the library catalogues were not searched.

## Still open

- The inside pages of *La Provincia di Bolzano*. Only front pages were read: the Italian daily's own account of the Druso bridge, of the industrial zone's inauguration and of the Rione Dux is still unread.
- Whose words the conciliatory sentence in *Dolomiten* of 14 July 1928 was.
- How large the readership of each paper was. The two circulation figures come from the literature and were not checked.
- Whether the *Alpenzeitung* really was a translation of the Italian daily, article by article. A comparison of a week of both would show it.
