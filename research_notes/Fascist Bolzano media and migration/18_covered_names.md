# Covered names: German words covered over with Italian on the same surface, 1918–1945

Research date: 24 November 2026. One question: where else, besides the Oltrisarco postcard (row 1.09) and the San Leonardo letterhead (row 16.08), was an existing object's German (or Ladin) name covered over with the Italian one on the same surface, so that the old object was reused and the covering shows. A new print that simply carries a new name is not the method; such cases are listed once, under "Related, not the method". Downloads and their rights are in `files/18_covered_names/manifest.md`; proposed records, not yet in the library, are in `files/18_covered_names/entries.draft.json` (rows 18.01–18.23).

**Tags.** [V] read by me on the cited page or in the cited file (for images: read off the image). [S] secondary source read in full or in the passage cited. [W] Wikipedia. [SS] search-result snippet only. [INF] my inference. Snippet-only and inferred statements are leads, not facts.

**Abbreviations.** OMA = the provincial portal Objekte Museen Archive (`objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it`). BU = *Bollettino Ufficiale della Prefettura di Bolzano*. Europeana = its 2019 copy of the Teßmann newspapers, read through the newspapers API and the per-page full text (method as in notes 03 §1B).

Commons, philatelic references and the web were used for stamps. No browser automation; Teßmann digital and the Catalogo generale were not touched.

## 1. The rule: what the prefects ordered, and that they did not want the crossing-out to show

### Takeaway
The legal basis for covering private lettering is not R.D. 800 of 1923 (row 16.03, which covers State signs) but a decree of the Prefect of the Venezia Tridentina, 28 October 1923, n. 14718. Its text is printed in the Bolzano prefecture's own bulletin of 16 October 1927, inside Prefect Umberto Ricci's circular n. 6480 of 11 October 1927, which extended Italian-only lettering to every commune except six. The circular is the key document of this thread. It orders German lettering "cancellate con raschiatura e coloritura radicali" (scraped and painted out) and forbids the podestà to accept "la semplice cancellatura della parte redatta in lingua tedesca, senza preoccuparsi dello squilibrio, anche estetico" where signs were bilingual: such signs "dovranno essere totalmente rinnovate". The State did not want a visible crossing-out. The struck-through postcards, brochures and labels of sections 2–3 are therefore what private owners and publishers did with stock they had, not the form the regime asked for on shop signs. A follow-up circular of 16 November 1927 answers the gravestone question left open in notes 16: funeral inscriptions had to be Italian only if later than 30 September 1927, and older ones were not to be cancelled. A circular of April 1928 extends the work to stone: kerbstones marked "K. K." and railway boundary stones marked "S. B. G." were to be removed "o per lo meno" have their initials cancelled.

### Inferences
- [INF] The circular of 1927 names the aesthetic of this thread and rejects it. A bar across the German half of a bilingual shop sign was exactly what the prefect did not want: it left the German legible as an absence and spoiled the "insieme". On paper that circulated briefly (postcards, brochures, labels) the cheap visible covering was tolerated; on the fixed face of the town, only a clean Italian sign would do.
- [INF] The decree of 1923 was a Trentino decree applied by a Bolzano prefect after the province of Bolzano was created in 1927. The method was the same act across the old Venezia Tridentina, which fits the brief's scope note.
- [INF] The circular of 16 November 1927 corrects the common claim that German gravestones were banned outright: new ones after 30 September 1927 had to be Italian; old ones stayed. The 1935 Gruber stone (row 16.13) falls under the rule.

### Gaps
- The decree of 28 October 1923 itself (Foglio Annunzi Legali or Bollettino of the Prefettura di Trento, late 1923) was not read in its first printing, only as quoted in 1927; its arts. 3–8 are unread. The Europeana copy of the *Bollettino Ufficiale della Prefettura di Trento* starts later than 1923 in the hits I saw.
- The "Präfekturdekret vom Dezember 1923" on German place names in official use was not found.
- The Guadagnini decree of 23 August 1923 on "Tirol" is known here only from the Touriseum's note.

## 2. Postcards: the Amonn, Fränzl and Bährendt stock overprinted, stamped and blacked out

### Takeaway
OMA holds 54 postcards whose catalogue descriptions say a German title was overprinted, overstamped, struck through, blacked out, painted over or erased (one, *Collalbo sul Renon*, only "überdruckt", so possibly a new print): 27 in the Touriseum, 25 in the Palais Mamming Museum (Meran), 2 in the Stadtarchiv Brixen (all in `oma_covered_names_index.csv`). Most are Johann F. Amonn cards of 1900–1914 whose red German title was reprinted in black with an Italian one ending "Alto Adige, Italia", so that the German still shows through, faint and red, under the Italian. Others are blacked with a bar and left without Italian (Karerseehotel), have only "Südtirol" or "Tirol" blacked and a violet "Alto Adige-Italia" stamped beside (St. Vigil in Enneberg, 1924), or carry a printed row of bars over the German with the Italian above (Bährendt's Schloss Tirol portal). Nine are held as rows here, three of them about Bolzano: the Batzenhäusl card (row 18.02), the Rittnerbahn "presso Bolzano" (18.03), the Mendola road "verso Bolzano" (18.04).

### Inferences
- [INF] Amonn's cards had a German title printed in red over the coloured picture. Overprinting the Italian in black let the publisher sell the stock without a new plate. The red German stays readable under the black, so these cards show the method at its plainest: the old name is not gone, it is outranked. That is art. 3 of R.D. 800 (row 16.03), Italian first and more prominent, done with one more pass through the press.
- [INF] Several overprints are bad Italian or loose translation: "Romanisches Portal" (Romanesque) becomes "Porta Romana" (Roman gate) on the Bährendt card; "Val Solda" for Sulden; "Longomos". The covering was done fast and by people without a list to hand.
- [INF] On the St. Vigil card only "Südtirol" is blacked; the German name of the Ladin village stays. The target in 1924 was the region's name, as the Touriseum's note on the "Tirol" ban says, not yet the village's.

### Gaps
- No record dates the overprinting itself. A posted card gives a latest date (the Banda card 1928, St. Vigil 1924, Plosehütte 1925); none gives an earliest.
- The Karerseehotel back ("Alto Adige, Italia" stamp) is not served online.
- OMA's descriptions are uneven: many more cards in its 414-record 1919–1943 set (notes 01) may be covered without the description saying so.

## 4. Station signs and street signs

### Takeaway
The railway station signs were renamed in the first months of occupation, and a Meran photographer, Albert Ellmenreich, went round photographing them between May and August 1919. The Touriseum describes the Untermais sign as the Italian name put up "über die deutsche". In Brixen, on Sunday 13 August 1922, nearly every street sign was pasted over overnight with white strips bearing Italian names; the local paper called it a prank and the town's men cleaned them off.

- Toblach: "Am Stationsgebäude der Ampezzanerbahn in Toblach prangt seit einiger Zeit die Aufschrift: 'Calalzo-Dobbiaco'" (*Südtiroler Landeszeitung*, 7 Oct 1920) [SS] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115210601).
- Railway carriages: "diese Wagen deutscher und österreichischer Herkunft, wie aus den teilweise mit italienischen Überschriften übermalten Aufschriften hervorgeht" (*Meraner Zeitung*, 10 June 1920) [SS] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059005015).

### Inferences
- [INF] Brixen 1922 is a paste-over without authority, two months before the March on Rome, by unknown hands; the paper's "Ulk" may be relief or prudence. It belongs with the squadrist overpainting at Salurn (§5) as the private, nocturnal form of the act.

### Gaps
- No photograph shows the German station name still legible beneath an Italian one; OMA's small scans do not resolve the signs at Sigmundskron (Bolzano's own station of Ponte Adige) or Siebeneich.

## 5. Inns, shop signs, town halls and house names: the press as witness

### Takeaway
German-language papers, above all the exile paper printed in Innsbruck (*Südtirol*, later *Der Südtiroler*, filed on Europeana as "Suedtiroler Heimat"), reported the covering of lettering on buildings, a stove and tableware. These are partisan witnesses and some reports are second-hand; none comes with a picture. They describe the method on surfaces no archive has kept.

### Cited Findings
- Salurn, *Südtiroler Landeszeitung*, 11 May 1922, p. 6, "Salurn. (Nächtliches Gewerbe.)": "In unserem Orte machten sich in der letzten Sonntagsnacht die Faschisten wieder einmal bemerkbar. Diesmal stach ihnen die deutsche Aufschrift auf dem Gemeindehause in die Augen, die sie überstrichen und in aller Eile durch die italienische ersetzten. Freilich waren es keine Künstler…" — done "bis 3 Uhr früh" in "lautlose 'Patschen'" [V image] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115210847), canvas p6.
- Brenner, *Südtirol* (Innsbruck), Folge 20, 15 Oct 1925, p. 4, "Südtirol": "Dem Bahnhof Brenner gegenüber ist ein stattliches Gebäude, früher 'Gasthof zur Post'; nun ist diese Aufschrift übertüncht, darüber steht aufdringlich Albergo; doch der Gasthof leuchtet mit Tiroler Zähigkeit noch unter der Tünche hervor." "In ganz Südtirol sind alle Glaser, Schuster, Spengler usw. unter der Tünche verschwunden, darüber erschien der vetraio, calzolaio und lattoniere! Auch der elendste Karren muß italienische Aufschrift tragen." [V image] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115186152), canvas p4.
- Klausen, *Bozner Nachrichten*, 26 Nov 1924, p. 1, quoting a *Frankfurter Zeitung* visitor of October 1924, second-hand ("wird mir erzählt"): in an old inn "der ehrwürdige, künstlerisch wertvolle Ofen … trug eine deutsche Inschrift. Die mußte also überklebt werden. Eine vierhundert Jahre alte Urkunde … war ganz zu entfernen"; and every shop sign "zuerst italienisch sein muß und bestenfalls darunter deutsch lauten darf" [V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000058958548), canvas p1. The same text ran in *Volksblatt* and *Der Burggräfler*, 29 Nov 1924 [SS].
- Fassa (Ladin, Trentino), *Der Tiroler*, 19 Sept 1920: "im Fassatale alle deutschen Aufschriften übertüncht, oder durch italienische ersetzt wurden, und soll dies … über Zwangsauftrag von Trient erfolgt sein" [SS] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000058964096); also *Bozner Nachrichten*, 18 Sept 1920 [SS].
- 1929: "Kaum ein Haus begegnet einem ohne übertünchte Inschriften. Viele Handwerker malen jetzt einfach ihr Handwerkszeug auf ihre Schilder, um sie nicht italienisch abfassen zu müssen" (*Südtirol*, 1 Nov 1929) [SS] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115185994).
- Meran, *Der Südtiroler* (Innsbruck), Folge 23, 1 Dec 1934, p. 6, "Meran ist deutsch", reporting a complaint in Tolomei's *Archivio per l'Alto Adige*: "Ja nicht einmal die Verfügung über die Aufschriften, die nur italienische zugelassen sind, werde in Meran streng eingehalten. Mitunter ist sogar die italienische Aufschrift durch die Witterung abgewaschen worden und die darunter befindliche deutsche Aufschrift allein sichtbar." The Forst brewery is told to call itself "Foresta" [V image] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115186159), canvas p6. The same page carries L. Steinberger's open letter to Tolomei (Steinberger's 1932 pamphlet is in thread 17) [V image].
- Meran 1935: a house sign at "Ansitz Weiher … behördlich übermalt"; memorial plaques to Andreas Hofer in Meran and to Friedrich Pacher and Ferdinand von Gilm in Bruneck removed (*Südtirol*, 1 Nov 1935) [SS] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115186104).
- Bolzano: the Walther monument "trägt eine Reihe von Mussoliniköpfen und die Aufschrift 'evviva il Duce'" (*Südtirol*, 1 Apr 1934) [SS] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115186161). Graffiti on an object, not a covered name; noted for the Walther thread.

### Inferences
- [INF] The Brenner inn and Tolomei's own complaint of 1934 describe the same thing from the two sides: whitewash over German lettering wears thin, and the old name comes back through it. The covering was never final; it had to be kept up. This is the strongest single image for the project's "defusing by form" in reverse.

### Gaps
- No photograph of a whitewashed or overpainted inn or shop sign in Bolzano itself was found. Candidates: the Province's photo archive (AFM, Pedrotti), the Comune's archive, OMA searches by street name.

## 6. Stone: kerbstones, boundary stones, war memorials and graves

### Takeaway
The prefecture ordered the "K. K." and "S. B. G." initials off kerbstones and railway boundary stones in 1928 (§1). Reports of 1927 say a podestà in the Bassa Atesina ordered German war-memorial and grave inscriptions removed and a dean in Kastelruth announced from the pulpit that grave inscriptions had to be replaced by 30 November; the prefect's circular of 16 November 1927 says older funeral inscriptions were not to be touched. No surviving stone with chiselled-off initials or a recut inscription was found online.

### Cited Findings
- Circular 5 April 1928 n. 3715 on "paracarri con le sigle tedesche" and boundary stones [V image] — see §1.
- *Südtirol* (Innsbruck), 15 Nov 1927, p. 1: "Der Amtsbürgermeister (!) von Margreid, Kurtinig und Fennberg … gab sogar den Auftrag, die deutschen Kriegerdenkmal- und Grabinschriften zu entfernen"; p. 6: in Kastelruth the dean "über Auftrag des Amtsbürgermeisters Vanzo am 30. Oktober von der Kanzel herab verkündet, daß … alle Inschriften nunmehr italienisch zu sein hätten. Auch die Grabinschriften müßten bis zum 30. November durch italienische ersetzt werden" [V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115186128).
- BU, 16 Nov 1927, circular n. 7622: earlier funeral inscriptions "non debbono … essere cancellate o sostituite" [V image] — see §1.

### Inferences
- [INF] The Kastelruth announcement and the circular of 16 November are a fortnight apart and contradict each other; the circular reads as the prefecture reining in over-zealous podestà after the October order. Settle by reading the Kastelruth and Margreid parish or communal records, or later reports on whether stones were recut.

### Gaps
- No image of a kerbstone or boundary stone with cancelled "K. K." or "S. B. G." found on Commons or OMA. Railway heritage groups and the Südbahn stones still standing along the Brenner line are the next place to look.

## 7. Stamps, postmarks and state paper

### Takeaway
The stamp lead is half right. Austrian stamps were overprinted "Regno d'Italia / Trentino / 3 nov. 1918" (eighteen values, issued 11 November 1918 according to one reference); that is the method, though it was a Trento issue and covers the Austrian design rather than a German name. The second overprint, "Venezia Tridentina" (6 and 18 December 1918), was printed on Italian stamps, not on Austrian ones, so it is not the method. No openly licensed image of the Trentino overprint was found. Held instead: an Austrian playing-card tax stamp ("ÖSTERR. KARTENSTEMPEL") struck with a violet Italian "BOLZANO" handstamp, 1919–22, on Commons.

### Cited Findings
- Section "1918 Stamps of Austria, overprinted 'Regno d'Italia Trentino 3. nov. 1918'": values 3 h to 10 Kr; "The stamps 10 h, 15 h and 20 h exist with an error: '8 nov' instead of a '3 nov' (very rare)"; section "1919 Stamps of Italy overprinted 'Venezia-Tridentina'", with "5 h on 5 c green, 10 h on 10 c red, 20 h on 20 c orange" [V page] — [Klaseboer catalogue](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol2/html/italytir.htm).
- "The first issue on 11th November 1918 consisted of overprinted Austrian stamps. Forged examples are known"; 6 Dec 1918 "a new issue of Italian stamps overprinted Venezia Tridentina"; 18 Dec 1918 Italian stamps overprinted with heller values, used "in Trentino until early January 1919 and in Alto Adige until 19 April of the same year"; local postage-due provisionals at Bolzano 1 and "Bolzano 3" (February 1919), and a "5c di c on 5c" at Tirolo and Lana withdrawn after 14 days "because of the use of German" [V page, via a fetch summary] — [italianstamps.co.uk](https://italianstamps.co.uk/po/occaust/trentino/index.html). The two references disagree on the year heading of the Venezia Tridentina issue (1918 issue dates against Klaseboer's "1919" heading); a catalogue such as Sassone would settle it.
- Commons: "Photo: Italian provisional overprint Bolzano (on Austrian card tax stamp, 1919–1922)", source Peter Endebrock's page on overprints on Austrian tax stamps; licence {{PD-art|PD-old-assumed}} [V page] — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SPKW-Stempel-Endebrock-Ueberdruck-Bozen-1919.jpg). Seen in the image: a red ring "ÖSTERR. KARTENSTEMPEL" around a blue double eagle on a playing card's ornament, a red heart, and a violet circular handstamp reading "…OLZANO" across them.
- Endebrock himself hedges: "Der Überdruck könnte ein italienischer Datumsstempel sein … Als Ort ist hier 'BOLZANO' ('Bozen') angegeben"; and on another card: "ein provisorischer italienischer Stempel, der nach dem ersten Weltkrieg 1919 benutzt wurde, um in Südtirol den früheren österreichischen Stempel ungültig zu machen. Der Text ist 'R. Ufficio delle Imposte' … und 'Merano'" [V page] — [endebrock.de](http://www.endebrock.de/stamps-de/st-a0.html). He also writes that Bozen "bis zum Herbst 1919 österreichisch war"; Italian troops held it from November 1918 and annexation came in October 1920 (the annexation feast of 10 Oct 1920 in *Der Tiroler*, 12 Oct 1920, p. 3 [V OCR]), so his date is loose.
- An envelope from Innsbruck to "Meran-Untermais, Deutsch-Südtirol": "Letzteres Wort war von einem italienischen Chauvinisten auf dem Postamte Innsbruck mit einem Papierstreifen überklebt worden", the Innsbruck postmark struck across the strip (*Meraner Zeitung*, 8 Nov 1919, p. 2) [V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059007260), canvas p2.
- A parallel on the other side of the border: an Austrian passport of 1919 where "Im Namen seiner Majestät Karl, Kaisers von Österreich …" is overstamped "Im Namen des Staates Deutschösterreich", issued to a Meran schoolgirl [V record] — [OMA 140015752](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140015752). Same method, different regime; not Italian.

### Inferences
- [INF] The Lana and Tirolo provisional withdrawn "because of the use of German" is the mirror of the cards: an Italian overprint that itself used German was too much.

### Gaps
- No reproducible image of the Trentino 1918 overprint with clear rights; Commons has none in "Stamps of occupied Italian territories". The Museo Storico in Trento or the Istituto di Studi Storici Postali (Prato) may hold scans.
- Austrian postmark cancellers re-cut with Italian names (e.g. "BOZEN 1" into "BOLZANO") were not found illustrated with an open licence.

## 8. Letterheads, forms and seals

### Takeaway
Nothing new beyond row 16.08 was found as an image with the covering visible. The Elisabethgarten card's back (a photographer's "K. u. K. Hofphotograph" with "K. u. K. H" blacked) is the only other struck-out imperial title found, and it is a private act.

### Gaps
- OMA "Briefkopf" (31 hits) and "Formular" (60 hits) gave no record describing a struck "k.k." or a stamped Italian heading. Communal archives (letterheads of 1919–1926 with "Gemeindevorstehung" struck and "Municipio" stamped) are the place to look; the Südtiroler Landesarchiv's Gemeindearchive inventories may name them.

## 9. Maps, school books and banknotes

### Gaps
- Maps: no evidence found that the IGM overprinted Italian names on Austrian 1:75,000 sheets; Tolomei worked with De Agostini on new maps in 1916–17 [SS] — [Wikipedia, Ettore Tolomei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Tolomei). The only covered map found is post-war: a Touriseum map "Dolomiti – Venezia Tridentina – Italia", 1960–80, "deutsche Bezeichnung ist überklebt" (OMA 140012538).
- School books: none found with Italian pasted over German.
- Banknotes: no evidence found that Austrian crown notes were stamped by the Italian administration.

## 10. Covering without replacing: censorship by paste-over

### Cited Findings
- Meran (Obermais), *Der Tiroler*, 12 Oct 1920, pp. 2–3: a poster with Andreas Hofer's picture invited to a service of mourning on 9 October ("Abmarsch halb 8 Uhr vom Rathause Obermais"); Carabinieri arrived and "das Wort Abmarsch mußte, während sie das Hoferbild nur so anschielten, überklebt werden" [V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000058964143).
- St. Leonhard in Passeier, *Südtirol* (Innsbruck), 15 Nov 1925, p. 5: a 72-year-old greengrocer had in her shop a picture of St. Anthony "mit der natürlichen deutschen Schrift: 'Hl. Antonius, bitte für uns!' Da diese deutschen Worte nicht überklebt waren, erschienen eines Abends vier Karabinieri, verhafteten die Greisin" [V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115186130). Same commune as the 1926 letter of row 16.08.
- 1927: Carabinieri removed holy pictures and holy-water stoups "die deutsche Aufschriften trugen" from shops, and an earthenware stoup inscribed "Gelobt sei Jesus Christus" had to go (*Südtirol*, 1 Sept and 15 Nov 1927) [SS/V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115186121).

### Inferences
- [INF] By 1925 an uncovered German word on a devotional picture could be prosecuted: the paste-over had become the expected state of a German inscription in public view.

## 11. The reverse: German over Italian

### Cited Findings
- Tramin, *Der Tiroler*, 28 March 1920, p. 4: the purely Italian inscriptions on the town hall and the signposts annoyed the population "und über Nacht waren sämtliche übertüncht"; the commune then paid, at the Carabinieri commander's demand, to renew the inscription at the post office, and "Municipio di Termeno" was painted above "Rathaus" [V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000058963975), canvas p4.
- 1945: an Italian paper claimed "In allen Gemeinden Südtirols werden die Aufschriften in italienischer Sprache heruntergerissen, herausgemeißelt, ausgelöscht und in Eile durch deutsche ersetzt"; *Dolomiten* answered that "nach dem 4. Mai 1945 fast alle deutschen Aufschriften durch italienische ersetzt worden waren" (25–26 Aug 1945, p. 3) [V OCR] — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115203922). It implies German lettering went up in 1943–45 and came down in May 1945; no object of 1943–45 was found.
- After 1945: the Touriseum map of 1960–80 with its German name pasted over (§9).

### Gaps
- No image of German pasted or painted over Italian in 1943–45. The *Bozner Tagblatt* (1943–45) on Teßmann and the Operationszone's ordinances are the place to look.
