# Propaganda in print, picture, object, school and ritual, 1916–1948, and the counter-propaganda it met

Research date: 12 November 2026. This thread extends notes 01 (tourist posters), 03 (press, propaganda volumes, photographic archives) and the school section of 02. It adds the printed apparatus of Italianisation (place-name lists, decrees, surname decrees, lettering on stones, plates and mastheads), the regime's own provincial magazine, school material, ceremony print, and the German, Austrian and Nazi print that answered it. Downloads and their rights are in `files/16_propaganda_print/manifest.md`; library rows 16.00–16.23 are in `files/16_propaganda_print/entries.json`.

**Tags.** [V] read by me on the cited page or in the cited file (for images: read off the scan). [S] from a secondary source read in full or in the passage cited. [W] Wikipedia text. [SS] search-result snippet only, page not opened. [INF] my inference. Snippet-only and inferred statements are leads, not facts.

**Abbreviations.** BPI = Biblioteca Provinciale Italiana Claudia Augusta, web gallery `webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it`. GU = *Gazzetta Ufficiale del Regno d'Italia*. AZ = *Alpenzeitung*. OMA = the provincial portal Objekte Museen Archive Südtirol.

**Method.** Scripts only, with a generic client name: the BPI gallery, gazzettaufficiale.it, the Wikimedia Commons API (the upload server refused full-size originals for a while, so some files are Commons' standard thumbnails, marked in the manifest), the Internet Archive and Europeana APIs (including the Europeana newspapers full-text endpoint described in notes 03 §1B), INDIRE's FotoEdu catalogue and web search. No browser automation; Teßmann and the Catalogo generale were not touched.

## 1. The Italianisation apparatus as printed matter: the place-name list, the decrees, and their lettering

### Takeaway
The 1916 *Prontuario dei nomi locali dell'Alto Adige* is online in full, not only as a private PDF: it fills most of *Archivio per l'Alto Adige* XI (1916) in the BPI gallery (contrary to notes 03 §4). Its own introduction states the programme of this thread in one sentence: Italy must make the new provinces' belonging evident "con la nomenclatura dei luoghi, con tutto ciò ch'è scritto, stampato, pubblico ed apparente in pubblico". Seven years later R.D. 29 March 1923 n. 800 made the lists official and regulated lettering directly: where a second (German) name was allowed, it had to stand second, in brackets, and "non può essere scritto con caratteri più appariscenti di quelli del nome italiano". The effects can be held as pictures: a 1935 gravestone that names a German farmer-mayor "Luigi Gruber ex Sindaco di Vanga", a German journal printed in Fraktur as *Der Sciliar*, a newspaper renamed from *Der Tiroler* to *Der Landsmann*, an enamel plate "26 SOPRABOLZANO", and twelve pages of the 1935 *Gazzetta* filled with Bolzano surname decrees ("Egger" into "Mancini").

### Inferences
- [INF] Read together, the 1916 introduction and art. 3 of the 1923 decree turn Italianisation into a rule of typography: the Italian name first, the other name second, bracketed and never larger. That is exactly the project's second direction (defusing or charging by a change of form) run in reverse, by the State.
- [INF] The gravestone shows the decree's reach beyond State lettering: a private stone of 1935 for a German-speaking former mayor is cut entirely in Italian, with the first name and the commune Italianised. Whether a rule compelled it is not proven (see Gaps); it shows at least what had become normal.
- [INF] The Bolzano surname decrees are printed as requests "on demand", one after another, with the old and new name side by side in guillemets. As a picture, a page of them is the bureaucratic counterpart of the struck-through postcard (row 1.09).

### Gaps
- No primary text found for a ban on German inscriptions on gravestones. It appears only in popular articles (e.g. *Junge Freiheit*: "deutsche Inschriften auf Grabsteinen wurden verboten", from 1926) [SS] — [jungefreiheit.de](https://jungefreiheit.de/wissen/geschichte/2003/erfolgreiche-realisierung-eines-groessenwahns/). The BAS list of the 32 *Provvedimenti* does not mention gravestones [S]. Gruber 1974 is the next check.
- No printed text of the *Provvedimenti* themselves in Italian was reached; the BPI gallery's *Archivio* stops at 1919, and the 1923 volume, where Tolomei printed them, is not online that I found.
- The prefectural decree on shop signs ("insegne") was not found; art. 2 of R.D. 800 covers State signs only.
- No street photograph of a repainted shop sign or re-cut stone in Bolzano itself was found with an open licence.
- The later editions of the Prontuario (1923? 1935?) were not located (as notes 03).

## 2. The regime's own magazine and books about the province

### Takeaway
*Atesia Augusta. Rassegna mensile dell'Alto Adige* began with anno I n. 1 in March 1939, under the prefect Mastromattei as president of the editorial committee, published by the "Ente Cooperativo Provinciale della Stampa Fascista" and printed by Ferrari Auer in Bolzano. Its first issue opens with a portrait of Mussolini dedicated in his own hand "Alla Rivista 'Atesia Augusta' che ha nel nome il programma", and closes with the Victory Monument printed on the back cover. Its title is set in Roman capitals with V for U ("AVGVSTA", "LIRE QVATTRO"). It is the regime's own picture book of the province: Tolomei on "Terra d'Italia", "Romanità dell'Alto Adige", the toponymic dictionary, the industrial zone, the rione Littorio from the air (photos by Pedrotti), and "Vita del Partito" in every issue.

### Inferences
- [INF] *Atesia Augusta* was the prefecture's magazine rather than the party's: the prefect presides, the party's provincial press cooperative publishes, and the first word comes from Mussolini's own hand. The title itself ("Augusta", Roman capitals with V) and the back-cover monument make the magazine an object of the same Romanising lettering as the monument's inscriptions.
- [INF] The 1939 run sits exactly across the Option: n. 1 in March presents the province as Roman and Italian; n. 9 in November explains the "accordi italo-germanici". The missing nos. 2–9 in the BPI gallery are the issues to see for how the regime narrated the Option to Italians.

### Gaps
- BPI holds only nos. 1 and 10 of 1939, no. 12 of 1940 and an incomplete run for 1941–43 (as notes 03 §1). Nos. 2–9 of 1939 must be sought in the Biblioteca Civica Bolzano or the Biblioteca Comunale di Trento.
- Provincial and party yearbooks, Dopolavoro and GIL papers, and illustrated volumes on the new city or the industrial zone were not found online in this pass.
- Guido Pelizzari (cover) was not identified further.

## 3. School and childhood

### Takeaway
The legal hinge is art. 17 of R.D. 1 October 1923 n. 2185 (the Gentile reform): from 1923–24 the first classes of the "scuole elementari alloglotte" were taught in Italian, one more class each year. The German side answered on the front page of *Der Landsmann* three days later with an appeal by the four German deputies, among them Eduard Reut-Nicolussi. What followed can be held as paper: a podestà's letter of August 1926 expelling a woman from her village for running "una scuola clandestina per l'insegnamento della lingua tedesca", on letterhead where "IL SINDACO" is struck out and "IL PODESTÀ" stamped above; a 1928 postcard of the former Kaiserin-Elisabeth-Schule in Bolzano captioned "Scuole Elementari Regina Elena"; and three INDIRE photographs of the "Regia Scuola Tecnica Commerciale 'Duca di Pistoia'" with its Italian name lettered over a Habsburg-era doorway. The ONAIR kindergartens have a recent study but no pictures found.

- [INF] The Ladurner letter is a small typographic record of the 1926 change from elected mayor to appointed podestà: the State had not reprinted its paper yet, so the new office is stamped over the old.

## 4. Ceremony, exhibition and object

### Takeaway
Ceremony print is thinner online than expected. What can be held: the 1921 *Domenica del Corriere* cover of the King's oath at the Brenner (§2), the back cover of *Atesia Augusta* that turns the Victory Monument into an emblem (§2), and the historical guide of the Mostra della Rivoluzione Fascista, which names the occupation of Bolzano in October 1922 as the "typical" episode of the insurrection. No medal, special postmark, stamp or printed programme for the 1926 first stone or the 1928 inauguration was found in an open collection.

### Cited Findings
- *Mostra della Rivoluzione Fascista. Guida storica*, ed. Dino Alfieri and Luigi Freddi, PNF, "I° decennale della marcia su Roma": cover with Mussolini's head over "DU-CE" repeated; on p. 31, quoting Mussolini: "Gli episodi salienti di questa insurrezione sono noti. Tipica l'occupazione di Bolzano e la occupazione — squisitamente rivoluzionaria — di Trento" [V] — [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/aa.-vv.-mostra-della-rivoluzione-fascista-ocr-1933). The scan has 33 pages only, so it is incomplete; the OCR text names Terragni "per la sala del 1922" [V].
- *Atesia Augusta* 1939 n. 4 (June) ran "Pagine illustrate: Bolzano ai Caduti per l'Impero" [V] — [index, BPI](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/Atesia-Augusta-1939_10.html). Whether this is the "column to the fallen of the Empire" named in the brief is not established.
- Victory Monument print already held: the Perdomi photograph of the first stone (row 5.03); AZ and Dolomiten pages on the 1926 and 1928 ceremonies (rows in section 3); the 1928 *Alpenzeitung* front page explaining the inscriptions (section 5) [V, existing entries].
- Medal, postmark and stamp searches (web search for "medaglia … Monumento alla Vittoria Bolzano 1928", "annullo speciale Bolzano 1928"; Commons categories "1928 postcards of Italy", "Monumento alla Vittoria (Bolzano)"; Europeana) found nothing for Bolzano [V, null results].

### Inferences
- [INF] The 1921 cover predates fascism in power: the Brenner was already staged as a sacred border by the liberal monarchy and the bourgeois press. The fascist print of the 1930s inherited the oath and set it in stone.

### Gaps
- What the "sacro rito del Brennero" of October 1921 was (date, place on the pass, monument) is not established; La Stampa's archive for 15–25 October 1921 is the check.
- Medals and postmarks: Sassone/Unificato catalogues, the Museo Storico della Comunicazione, Numista and Italian auction houses (Bolaffi, Varesi, Nomisma) by hand.

## 6. The answer from the other side: German, Austrian, Nazi and anti-Nazi print

### Takeaway
The counter-print runs from the deputies' appeal of 1923 (§3), through Reut-Nicolussi's *Tirol unterm Beil* (1928), whose cover turns the fascist axe into the image of oppression, and Hitler's 1926 pamphlet on the "Südtiroler Frage" that gave South Tyrol up for an alliance with Italy, to the 1939 Option. For the Option there are now three kinds of paper held: a typewritten anonymous Dableiber leaflet "Landsleute!" that ridicules "Galizien" as the promised land and names the "sizilianische Gespenst"; two hand-painted "Heim ins Reich!" posters in Fraktur; and the Italian bureaucracy's own orange index card of "allogeni tedeschi che hanno optato per la Germania". Albert Stolz's "Heimat und Front 1941–1942" postcard for the optants' organisation closes the series.

### Inferences
- [INF] The Dableiber leaflet answers the Nazi promise with its own tourism slogan inverted: "Kennt Ihr das Land?" is Goethe's line that the UTA put on Lenhart's poster for Merano (notes 01, Q1 #9, Q4). Here it asks whether the land offered is Galicia.
- [INF] The two posters are hand-made, not printed: the "Heim ins Reich" campaign in the province partly ran on single painted sheets, probably for VKS or AdO rooms, which fits an organisation that was illegal until January 1940 (notes 04). Their date and maker need an archive.
- [INF] On the Italian card the same people are "allogeni tedeschi", the word that also heads the 1923 school decree ("scuole alloglotte"): the classifying vocabulary is constant from 1923 to 1948.

### Gaps
- No original VKS leaflet (e.g. with the "Sicily" rumour in print) and no Italian or church-side Dableiber leaflet signed by a group were reached; the Südtiroler Landesarchiv's Option holdings and the exhibition catalogue held as row 4.06 are the places to look.
- The holding of both "Heim ins Reich" posters is unknown; the Commons uploader names none.
