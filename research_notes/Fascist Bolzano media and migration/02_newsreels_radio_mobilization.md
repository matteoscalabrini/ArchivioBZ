# How Italians outside South Tyrol encountered Bolzano / Alto Adige through mass media and mass mobilization, 1924–1943 (Luce, film, radio, OND trains, military service, school)

Conventions used throughout:
- **[V]** = verified directly by me in a primary record (a Luce catalogue page, a Radiocorriere scan, an Internet Archive item image).
- **[S]** = taken from a secondary source and not checked against the primary.
- **[I]** = my inference.
- Luce codes: `IL5000…` are newsreel stories (archive section "2", cinematografico/cinegiornali) and `IL3000…` are documentaries (section "1"). "Clip code" is the catalogue's *codice filmato*. Giornale Luce A items (1927–32) are dated to the month only; Giornale Luce B/C items carry the reel's release date.
- Supporting files: `files/02_newsreels_radio_mobilization/` (see `manifest.md` there). The two CSVs hold the full catalogue with sequence summaries. The tables below are the citable index.

## 1. Istituto Luce: which newsreels and documentaries showed Bolzano, Merano, Alto Adige, the Brenner and the Dolomites (1927–1943)?

### Takeaway
The Luce online catalogue holds at least **163 newsreel stories (1928–1943)** and **about 45 period documentaries** about South Tyrol and the Brenner. I list every one below with its catalogue code and URL; 152 of the newsreels also have a YouTube link. Coverage is thin until 1931, peaks in **1935 (25 stories) and 1938 (26)**, and falls away after 1940. It falls into four kinds of picture: a staged "Italian" Bolzano (the Monument, the Duce's visits, royal tours, the industrial zone, the Rione Dux); a cosmopolitan, folkloric Merano and Dolomites (horse racing, flower parades, costumes, skiing); the Brenner as a sacred border and, after 1938, an Axis gateway (Hitler's trains, the Duce–Führer meetings, German coal); and a flow of South Tyrolean youth groups brought to Rome. Keyword searches in the catalogue found **no Luce item on the 1939 Option**, although Luce did cover the "return home" of Baltic and Bessarabian Germans.

### Cited Findings

**Method [V].** I ran the public catalogue search (https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/search/result.html) for 47 terms: place names (bolzano, merano, alto adige, brennero, dolomiti, bressanone, vipiteno, gardena, ortisei, san candido, dobbiaco, pusteria, carezza, stelvio, collalbo, gries and others) and themes (monumento alla vittoria, vallo alpino, semirurali, druso, lancia, acciaierie, alluminio, zona industriale, trenker, opzioni/optanti, Hitler/Führer, giro d'italia, alpini adunata, cesare battisti, treni popolari, dopolavoro). I kept film items dated 1924–1945, opened every candidate's detail page and kept only items whose catalogue "luoghi" or title name a South Tyrolean place or the Brenner. The catalogue's own facet for "bolzano" alone shows 119 Cinegiornali and 27 Documentari across all years (2026-10-22). The series ranges are as the catalogue labels them: Giornale Luce A (1927–1932), Giornale Luce B (1931–1940), Giornale Luce C (1940–1945). Every record's text is saved in `files/…/luce_catalogue_records_fulltext.txt`.

**Priority items, verified from the catalogue records [V]:**

- **Inauguration of the Monumento alla Vittoria, 12 July 1928.** *A Bolzano inaugurazione del monumento alla Vittoria d'Italia*, Giornale Luce A / A0137, code IL5000048984, clip A013701, catalogue date 07/1928, 4'43", silent, artistic direction Mario Craveri. Sequences: the monument's columns, busts and bronze statue; three priests bless it; the king on the royal stand while Giuriati gives the official speech; combatants and Blackshirts process with flags; Balbo beside the king; a "banda musicale altoatesina"; the procession crosses the Talvera bridge; women, veterans and Garibaldini in the crowd; Giovani Italiane and Balilla march past — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048984/2/a-bolzano-inaugurazione-del-monumento-alla-vittoria-d-italia.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bMqh-q3eUJI). The same week's reel also carried *Visita altoatesina a Roma* (A0129, IL5000048647, 2'25"): South Tyroleans in traditional dress climb the Campidoglio, a South Tyrolean band plays in Piazza Navona, the delegation meets Delcroix and Mussolini, and ends in Piazza San Pietro — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048647/2/visita-altoatesina-roma.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NljUAYhu-5g). It also carried *Al Brennero Elena d'Orleans posa la prima pietra del nuovo asilo per l'infanzia* (A0126, IL5000048751), in which the duchess takes the bouquet to the Italy–Austria boundary stone — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048751/2/al-brennero-elena-d-orleans-posa-prima-pietra-del-nuovo-asilo-l-infanzia.html).
- **The monument before and after.** *Visita di S.M. il Re a Bolzano* (documentary IL3000085506, catalogue date 1927, silent): a demonstration in Piazza Vittorio Emanuele, a speech by Carlo Delcroix, "la posa della prima pietra del monumento alla Vittoria", and a cavalcade of South Tyroleans in costume — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000085506/1/-1680.html). *Adunata dei bersaglieri a Bolzano alla presenza di Emanuele Filiberto duca d'Aosta* (IL3000052097, M009908, [1924–31], 13'31") shows the procession passing "il monumento alla Vittoria d'Italia, ancora in costruzione", then Merano, then a train and an "adunata al Brennero" of veterans at the border — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000052097/1/alla-presenza-emanuele-filiberto-duca-d-aosta.html). The monument then serves as the stage for later rituals: the reading of the Duce's message to the Blackshirts on 28 Oct 1933 (B0360, IL5000011160); the closing of the Littoriali della neve in Feb 1935 (B0627, IL5000016094); the *Offerta delle Fedi Nuziali* led by the Duchess of Pistoia at the "Ara della Vittoria" (B0803, 24/12/1935, IL5000020640); Starace's homage to "Altoatesini caduti" in Spain and Africa (B1312, 01/06/1938, IL5000029437, which also shows "la colonnina del confine del Brennero" and 500 cars of an RACI rally); and Delcroix's rally of "riconoscenza" by war-disabled and orphans of the former Austro-Hungarian army in Piazza della Vittoria (B1368, 08/09/1938, IL5000028666). URLs are in the table below.
- **Mussolini in Bolzano and at the Brenner, late August 1935** (all reels released 04/09/1935): *La visita di Mussolini alla città di Bolzano* (B0741, IL5000020037, 2'58"): crowds, flowers on the car, homage inside the monument with "i busti marmorei dedicati a Battisti, Filzi e Chiesa", a visit to the ONB — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020037/2/la-visita-mussolini-alla-citta-bolzano.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kAqW9weT7w). *Mussolini visita il confine italo-austriaco presso il Passo del Brennero* (B0742, IL5000020066, 1'41"): "il Duce arriva sul cippo del confine" — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020066/2/mussolini-visita-confine-italo-austriaco-presso-passo-del-brennero.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SaCbGlqlUUo). *La conclusione delle grandi manovre militari nel Bolzanino* (B0744, IL5000020134, 6'06"; actually filmed in the Val di Non) — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020134/2/la-conclusione-grandi-manovre-militari-nel-bolzanino-alla-presenza-del-re-e-mussolini.html). A long sound documentary on the same visit: *Italia. Bolzano* (IL3000090303, D046109, 1935, 14'37") — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000090303/1/-689.html).
- **Industrial zone, Lancia, aluminium, steel, Montecatini.** *Il ministro Benni e il conte Volpi inaugurano il nuovo centro industriale di Bolzano* (B1014, 23/12/1936, IL5000027681, 1'11"): the Montecatini aluminium exhibition building, fascist formations, the authorities entering "uno stabilimento 'Lancia e C.'" — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000027681/2/il-ministro-benni-e-conte-volpi-inaugurano-nuovo-centro-industriale-bolzano.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iI1ESWrFFis). *La visita del principe di Piemonte* (B1321, 15/06/1938, IL5000029846, 2'49"): "la zona industriale di Bolzano", workers at the blast furnaces, "una fabbrica di alluminio", and the opening of the Feltrinelli masonite plant (autarky themes) — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000029846/2/la-visita-del-principe-piemonte.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fhNMu8TVQ1U). Earlier: *Fabbricazione della carta in Alto Adige* (A0230, 12/1928); *La centrale elettrica di Castelrotto* (A0868, 10/1931); *Centrale idroelettrica di Ponte Gardena* "realizzata in soli 20 mesi" (B1436, 04/01/1939). Montecatini's sponsored autarky film *Azoto* (I documentari dell'autarchia / 1, IL3000089422, D039501, [1935–40]) names the Merano plant among its locations — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000089422/1/-3065.html).
- **Housing and urbanism ("Semirurali" / Rione Dux, the new city).** *Il principe di Piemonte visita i nuovi lavori urbanistici e architettonici in corso a Bolzano* (B0967, 07/10/1936, IL5000025643): banner "Istituto case popolari di Bolzano. IV e V lotto", plans of the new technical institute and the employees' housing, demolitions — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000025643/2/il-principe-piemonte-visita-i-nuovi-lavori-urbanistici-e-architettonici-corso-bolzano.html). *"Una superba realizzazione del Regime per il benessere dei lavoratori nella provincia del Brennero…"* (B1599, 11/10/1939, IL5000022261, 1'30"): "il nuovo Rione Popolare Dux, case a due piani con un piccolo terreno", its blessing and inauguration, the vegetable plots — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022261/2/superba-realizzazione-del-regime-benessere-lavoratori-nella-provincia-del-brennero-inaugurata-dal-ministro-segretario-del-partito.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PVaJ8LGvYJs). Also *La Casa di riposo per gli anziani del lavoro … a Gries* (B0508, 07/1934) and its inauguration (B0572, 11/1934), and the *nuovo palazzo reale di Bolzano* (B0573, 11/1934).
- **Ponte Druso / Stadio Druso.** No item titled for either was found. The closest is *Il ponte del Littorio* (B1540, 05/07/1939, IL5000021490): Cobolli Gigli and prefect Mastromattei open the Littorio bridge over the Isarco, which "unisce la zona industriale con il villaggio delle case popolari e la via Roma", and the minister fires the mine that starts the Virgolo tunnel for the Brenner road — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000021490/2/il-ponte-del-littorio.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oWN2oILRN3s). The "Stadio di Bolzano" place-tag in the catalogue appears only on postwar items.
- **Fiera di Bolzano.** No pre-1945 item was found. The "Palazzo Mercantile a Bolzano" tag appears only on postwar INCOM items. The nearest period items are exhibitions: *Mostra della moda* (B1338, 13/07/1938) and *La mostra dei prodotti dell'impero* (B1349, 03/08/1938, organised by the Banco di Roma).
- **Sport.** Sport is the largest category (about 40 stories). Examples: the Merano motorcycle *Sei giorni* in 1931 and 1932, with full-length documentaries IL3000052490 and IL3000052494; the 1932 documentary record gives the route (Merano–Vipiteno–Ortisei–Passo Gardena–Giovo, Pordoi, Costalunga, Mendola…) and 128 riders from 7 nations. Others: the Merano tennis and dog shows; the Merano Gran Premio ippico / "corsa dei milioni", with about 10 stories 1934–1942 tied to the national lottery; the *Coppa del Duce* cross-country skiing at the Brennero (B0832 1936, B1061 1937, B1262 1938); the Littoriali della neve e del ghiaccio at Ortisei and Bolzano (Feb 1935, 5 stories); the *VI campionato dopolavoristico di marcia su sci* at Dobbiaco (B0635, 02/1935); the ski championships of the *Fasci all'Estero* at Dobbiaco (B1243, 26/01/1938); the *Coppa Guido Presel* Bolzano–Mendola hill-climb, "24 km in salita" (B1336, 13/07/1938); and the Stelvio hill-climbs (1933, 1935, 1936, 1938). **Giro d'Italia: no period Luce story shows a stage in Bolzano or the Dolomites.** The pre-war Giro stories in the catalogue are Milan–Turin, Rome–Naples, Florence–Bologna and similar. The Bolzano/Dolomites Giro material in the catalogue is postwar (Coppi, Bartali, Koblet era).
- **Hitler's train, 3–9 May 1938.** The feature-length documentary *Il viaggio del Führer in Italia* (IL3000084593, D031201, 1938, 1:49:17, sound) opens with "Dal Brennero a Roma" (VHS/D312, 8'25"). Hitler is received at the Brenner station by the Duke of Pistoia, Starace and the German ambassador; then come "la stazione di Bolzano – una grande folla accoglie acclamante il passaggio del treno … bandiere, fasci littori inneggiano al Führer" and the station at Trento — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000084593/1/dal-brennero-roma-arrivo-alla-stazione-ostiense-prima-giornata-romana-piazza-siena-manovra-guerra-aerea-furbara-esercitazione.html). In the newsreel *La visita del Fuhrer. La conclusione del viaggio* (B1306, 18/05/1938, IL5000028761), "alla frontiera del Brennero Hitler viene salutato dal duca di Pistoia e da Starace" — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000028761/2/la-visita-del-fuhrer-conclusione-del-viaggio.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6MUQ-nEn3f0).
- **Brenner meetings of Mussolini and Hitler.** 18 March 1940: *"L'incontro fra il Duce e il Fuehrer."* (B1692, released 22/03/1940, IL5000024850, 1'39"): snow at the Brenner station, Hitler's train arrives, review of troops, Ciano and Ribbentrop — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000024850/2/l-incontro-duce-e-fuehrer.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AEkuuw0DHM0). 2 June 1941: *"L'incontro del Duce con il Fueher"* (C0150, released 07/06/1941, IL5000016076, 1'57"): a station meeting in a saloon car, with Ciano, Cavallero, Alfieri and Ribbentrop named in the keywords — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000016076/2/l-incontro-del-duce-fueher.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nIEhgGNDtn4). **4 October 1940: no Luce story was found.** Searches for brennero, incontro, Hitler, Fuehrer and convegno over 1940 return only the March meeting and the Florence meeting of 28 Oct 1940 (*L'incontro del Duce col Fueher*, C0088, 01/11/1940, IL5000013486, set in Palazzo Vecchio) — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000013486/2/l-incontro-del-duce-col-fueher.html).
- **The Brenner as an Axis corridor.** *Il passaggio dei treni rurali con migliaia di agricoltori italiani diretti in Germania* (B1275, 23/03/1938, IL5000033074): labour-migrant trains at the border station, with flags and slogans to the Duce — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000033074/2/il-passaggio-treni-rurali-migliaia-agricoltori-italiani-diretti-germania.html). Then: *Il comandante Russo parte alla volta della Germania* (B1340, 20/07/1938); *Giovani fascisti e hitleriani insieme in bicicletta…*, where the cyclists meet the Hitlerjugend and von Schirach at the Brenner (B1563, 09/08/1939); *"Arrivo del carbone tedesco in Italia"*, electric coal trains at the Brenner (C0036, 23/05/1940); MAS boats trucked through Bolzano, with houses demolished to let the lorries pass, on their way to Lake Ladoga (C0279, 11/09/1942); and the Allied *Le fortezze volanti bombardano il Brennero* (Combat film RW007, 1943).
- **The 1939 Option: absent in what I could search.** Queries for opzione, opzioni, optanti and rimpatrio returned no item on South Tyrol. "Rimpatrio" does return *Il rimpatrio dei tedeschi dal Baltico* (B1635, 13/12/1939, IL5000022891) and *Il rimpatrio dei cittadini tedeschi dimoranti nella Bessarabia* (C0104, 27/12/1940, IL5000013912) — [Baltic](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022891/2/il-rimpatrio-tedeschi-dal-baltico-croce-rossa-tedesca-e-organizzazioni-assistenziali-naziste-hanno-preso-cura-questi-rimpatriati.html), [Bessarabia](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000013912/2/rimpatrio-cittadini-tedeschi-dimoranti-nella-bessarabia.html).
- **Alpini and military.** *A Vipiteno manovre del 6° reggimento Alpini* (A0416, 08/1929, with French general Gouraud and Gazzera); *Il 232° fanteria* (A0954, 05/1932: the Duke of Pistoia takes command at Bolzano station); *A Trafoi, presso Bolzano, sorge la colonna montana della M.V.S.N.* (B0334, 1933); the return of the 5th Alpine Division "Pusteria" from East Africa to Rome (B1080, 22/04/1937); the "606ª Brigata Pusteria" field hospital at Ambò (B1279, 30/03/1938); the Divisione "Tridentina" back from Albania (C0161, 15/07/1941); the documentary *Il battaglione sciatori della guardia di finanza alla rivista di Bolzano* (IL3000083592, D033301, late 1930s–early 1940s: Umberto and Maria José review the parade, and the Guardia di Finanza skiers "marciano ma non con il passo romano"); and *Verso il Brennero* (IL3000051842, [1924–31]: "fortificazioni militari in prossimità del confine"). **Vallo Alpino: zero hits** for "vallo alpino" in the catalogue.
- **Royal visits.** Principally the June 1938 tour of Umberto and Maria José: B1316 (Bolzano and Merano, including the Valsecchi Alpino monument in Merano, a gift of the city of Milan), B1319, B1321 (industrial zone) and B1320 (Val Pusteria/Brunico). Also the Queen at Millan/Bressanone (B0759, 1935), the king at the Merano races (B1386, 05/10/1938), and the Duke and Duchess of Pistoia, who were resident royals in Bolzano (several stories).
- **Youth groups shown travelling to Rome.** *Giovani bolzanine in gita* (A0944, 04/1932): a group of Giovani Italiane from Bolzano get off the train at Ostia and run to the sea — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000032791/2/giovani-bolzanine-gita.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mJroAppYWL4). *Giunti da Bolzano mille avanguardisti* and *Il Duce passa in rivista i mille avanguardisti della Legione Marco Druso di Bolzano* (B0648 and B0650, 27/03/1935). *Giovani Fascisti di Bolzano … rendono omaggio al Milite Ignoto* (B0703, 26/06/1935). *350 giovani fasciste e giovani italiane della GIL di Bolzano … Palazzo Venezia* (B1426, 14/12/1938). *A Roma rappresentanze altoatesine di Bressanone* (A0253, 01/1929).
- **Tourism and folklore documentaries, silent, [1924–31].** *Bolzano e i suoi dintorni* (IL3000051159, M000502, 7'54"): Piazza Walther, the Portici, Castel Mareccio, "un possente masso dove verrà posta una statua dedicata a Cesare Battisti". *Nel regno delle Dolomiti* (IL3000051331, M021601, 53'56"): Bolzano, the CIT office with tourist cars, "l'Arco della Vittoria, il busto di Cesare Battisti", Gries, the Virgolo funicular, the Mendola, Castelrotto, Val Gardena, the Renon and Collalbo. *Dalla Val Pusteria a Merano* (IL3000051193, M003105). *L'Ampezzano e la Val Gardena* (IL3000051260). *In Val d'Ega e in Val di Fassa* (IL3000051524). Later sound documentaries: *Festa internazionale in costume a Merano* ([1932], D040807); *Merano canti e danze* ([1932–37], D064808: regional folk groups from Gorizia, Trento, Lombardy, Lazio and the Marche parading in Merano); *Scalatori del C.A.I. alle prese colle Dolomiti* (anni Trenta, D048701); *Rocciatori ed aquile* (1942, D007203: the GIL "Raggruppamento Rocciatori" at the Lago di Carezza); *Val Gardena* (1943, D030005).
- **Postwar items, not in period.** *Bolzano. La città dell'enrosadira* (IL3000083386, D032901) is dated "[anni trenta/quaranta]" in the catalogue, but its own record gives "Anno di produzione: 1949" (dir. Adriano Zancanella). The INCOM colour films *Arcobaleno italico* no. 9 *Brennero Roma* and no. 14 *Itinerario turistico Brennero Cortina Venezia* are undated INCOM colour productions and are probably postwar.

### Inferences
- [I] For a viewer in Naples or Bari, Bolzano appeared mainly as a stage for national ritual (the Monument, the Duce, the princes, Starace, Delcroix) and as a modern industrial-urban project (Lancia, aluminium, the Rione Dux, the Littorio bridge). The German-speaking population appears mainly as *costume*: bands, flower parades, folk dances, woodcarvers. Luce almost never shows South Tyroleans as political subjects. The one exception is the 1938 rally of former Austro-Hungarian veterans, framed as "riconoscenza" toward the regime.
- [I] Merano and the Dolomites are a separate register from Bolzano: cosmopolitan leisure (the lottery-linked horse races, tennis, dog shows, skiing championships). This fits the regime's tourist promotion of the area through ENIT/CIT. The *Nel regno delle Dolomiti* documentary, for instance, opens on a CIT office with tourist cars.
- [I] The 1935 and 1938 peaks match the regime's two pushes on the area: Mussolini's August 1935 visit and manoeuvres at the time of the Ethiopian mobilisation, and the 1938 Anschluss, Hitler's trip and the princes' tour, which coincide with the build-up of the industrial zone. After 1939 the Brenner footage is about the Axis (meetings, coal, Hitler Youth) rather than about the province itself.
- [I] The apparent silence on the Option is consistent with the regime's known reluctance to publicise it, but a keyword search cannot prove absence. A story could exist without "opzione" in its title or keywords.
- [I] The heavy cruiser *Bolzano*, launched at Sestri Ponente on 31 Aug 1932 (A1003, B0135), carried the city's name into naval newsreels nationwide. It shows the name working as a national symbol detached from the place.

## 2. Feature films: which films shot in or about South Tyrol reached Italian audiences (Trenker, Bergfilme, Italian productions)?

### Takeaway
Luis Trenker is the decisive figure. He was a Gardena-born (Ortisei) director-star whose Tyrolean-patriotic Bergfilme were valued in Fascist Italy, who made the regime-financed *Condottieri* (ENIC, 1937), and who opted for Germany in 1940. Italian release data (titles, censorship visas, dates) for his German films could not be verified online for most titles. I found no Italian fiction feature of 1924–43 that I could verify was set in South Tyrol.

### Cited Findings
- *Berge in Flammen* / Italian title *Montagne in fiamme* (1931, dir. Luis Trenker and Karl Hartl, 93'): a Franco-German production (producers Charles Delac [spelled "Delca" on the page] and Marcel Vandal), with a French version *Les Monts en flammes* and a US version *Doomed Battalion* (Universal, 1932). The original was shot near Innsbruck. The plot follows Florian Dimai, a Tyrolean guide serving with the Kaiserjäger on the Dolomite front, while Italian Alpini under Capt. Arturo Franchini occupy his village and mine the Austrian position; the film ends in 1931 with the two men as friends revisiting the battlefield — [it.wikipedia, Montagne in fiamme](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montagne_in_fiamme) [S]. Its Italian release date and censorship history were not found [gap].
- *Der Rebell* (1932, dir. Curtis Bernhardt, Edwin H. Knopf and Luis Trenker): a Tyrolean mountaineer leads a revolt against French-Bavarian occupation in 1809, modelled on Andreas Hofer, and the film was meant to reflect German resistance to Versailles — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rebel_(1932_film)) [S]. The Deutsche Biographie says Fascist Italy valued Trenker's German-language patriotic nature films, *Der Rebell* among them — [Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz113619.html) [S]. I found no source for an Italian ban.
- *Der verlorene Sohn* (1934, Deutsche Universal-Film, German release 6 Sept 1934; 102'): a South Tyrolean mountaineer emigrates to New York and returns home — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prodigal_Son_(1934_film)) [S]. Its Italian title is *Il figliuol prodigo* — [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_figliuol_prodigo_(film_1934)) [S]. Italian Wikipedia's synopsis instead places the home village in Bavaria, so the sources conflict on the setting. Museo Nazionale della Montagna restored "l'edizione italiana di 'Il Figliol prodigo'", which implies an Italian-language edition existed — [planetmountain](https://www.planetmountain.com/it/notizie/eventi/premio-alpcervino-2a-giornata.html) [S, snippet only].
- *Condottieri* (1937): produced by "Consorzio Condottieri, E.N.I.C.", with ENIC also distributing. Shot 3 Aug 1936 – Feb 1937; interiors at Cines (Rome) and Tobis (Berlin); exteriors at Torrechiara, Gradara, the Dolomites, Lake Garda (Arco, Riva), Verona, San Gimignano, Florence, Venice and Padua. Rome premiere **30 June 1937**. Shown at the 5th Venice Mostra on 15 Aug 1937, where it won the Direzione Generale cup — [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condottieri_(film)) [S]. It received 9.6 million lire of Italian government funding and drew parallels between Mussolini and Giovanni de' Medici as unifiers of Italy — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condottieri_(film)) [S]. Trenker himself wrote that "die italienische Regierung finanzierte auch zum großen Teil die Arbeiten dazu"; 60 men of the Leibstandarte SS were used in the mass scenes; he received the Mussolini Cup at Venice in 1936 for *Der Kaiser von Kalifornien* and an officer's cross of the Order of the Crown of Italy (1937); he "optierte … im März 1940 … nach langem Zögern für das Deutsche Reich"; and Goebbels wrote on 5 March 1940 "Dieses Schweinestück hat in Südtirol nicht für uns optiert" — [de.wikipedia, Luis Trenker](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Trenker) [S]. (The two Wikipedia statements about his Option are in tension. Goebbels's diary implies he had not opted, or had delayed.)
- *Der Berg ruft!* (1938; German release 6 Jan 1938). Its Italian version is *La grande conquista*. A Val Gardena museum programme gives "Der Berg ruft (La grande conquista)" from Carl Haensel's novel *Kampf ums Matterhorn* and dates the Italian edition 1937 — [Museum Gherdëina PDF](https://www.museumgherdeina.it/la/download/extkQcIzu.pdf); [it.wikipedia disambiguation](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_grande_conquista) [S]. Its setting is the Matterhorn, not South Tyrol.
- *Der Feuerteufel* (1940) is set in the 1809 Tyrolean rising, premiered at the Ufa-Palast am Zoo on 5 March 1940, and reportedly displeased Hitler — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fire_Devil) [S].
- Trenker backed a planned film city in Bolzano that was never built, and shot his Italian films in German — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Trenker) [S].
- Multiple-language versions: Trenker's *Der Sohn der weißen Berge / Les Chevaliers de la Montagne* (1930) was a German–French multiple version. The article's bibliography cites a 1930 Italian trade item, *Cine-Sorriso Illustrato*, "Avant-Première dei Cavalieri della Montagna", which suggests an Italian release — [Adorno & Fuchs 2023, CC BY](https://riviste.unimi.it/index.php/cinemaetcie/article/view/17960) (PDF saved in `files/`) [S].
- Luce documentaries close to fiction: *Vertigine bianca* (1941, D032303, filmed at the Cortina world ski championships); *La folla e il fiume* (INCOM, 1941, D004904, with a manifesto for the Merano lottery); and *Destino d'amore* (1942, credited to "Dolomiti Film") — see the documentary table above [V].

### Inferences
- [I] For Italian audiences the "South Tyrolean" screen persona was Trenker himself: a German-speaking Gardenese who on Italian screens played an Italian condottiere. *Condottieri* turned Alpine physicality into Italian national myth. The 1940 Option exposed the ambiguity.
- [I] Italian fiction cinema of the period seems to have used the Dolomites as a generic Alpine/WWI landscape (Cadore, Cortina) rather than staging stories in German-speaking Alto Adige. This needs checking against a filmography (Chiti/Poppi, *Dizionario del cinema italiano: i film 1930–1944*).

### Gaps
- Italian nulla osta numbers and release dates for *Montagne in fiamme*, *Il figliuol prodigo*, *Il ribelle* (if released) and *La grande conquista* were not found. The Italia Taglia censorship database and Chiti/Poppi would answer this.
- I could verify no Italian fiction film of 1930–43 set in Alto Adige. One may exist (for example, mountain comedies of 1939–42), but none was confirmed.
- Reception in the Italian press (*Cinema*, *Lo Schermo*, *La Stampa*) was not checked.

## 3. Radio: the EIAR station in Bolzano, and Radio Rurale

### Takeaway
EIAR's Bolzano station began regular service on **12 July 1928**, the same day as the Monument inauguration. The government ordered it and it was built in about a month. It stood in **Gries**, with **200 W** in the aerial and a wavelength of about **400 m** (listed in 1929 as "1 BZ", 445.9 m, 0.2 kW), using a **Standard Elettrica Italiana** transmitter. Its first broadcast was the inauguration itself, relayed to loudspeakers in Piazza Walther. It broadcast in Italian; German-language radio in the province began only under RAI in 1945. By 1932 it was rated at 1 kW, and by 1939 it relayed the national programmes. No source examined documents an explicit "counter-propaganda against Innsbruck/Munich" mandate. I infer that purpose from context but could not verify it.

### Cited Findings
- *Radiorario* (Radiocorriere) 1928 no. 30, "La Sagra di Bolzano – L'Inaugurazione della Stazione Radiofonica dell'E.I.A.R." [V; saved as `Radiocorriere_1928-30.pdf`]: the article frames 12 July as "veramente storica per tutto il popolo italiano". It says the music of the bands was picked up "dal microfono collegato alla stazione di Bolzano, microfono che … dovè trovare l'unica possibile posizione sul braccio, teso in alto, di un funzionario dell'E.I.A.R.". EIAR installed "in piazza Walter … tre potenti altoparlanti" and also loudspeakers mounted on cars. Giuriati's speech "viene nitidamente trasmesso dalla nuova stazione radiofonica di Bolzano", and the transmission "ha costituito … il battesimo di una nuova stazione dell'E.I.A.R., installata di sana pianta in poco più di un mese". The station was built "aderendo prontamente a un desiderio espresso da S. E. il Ministro delle Comunicazioni" (Costanzo Ciano); "I lavori si iniziarono il 10 giugno". Technical details: "La stazione sorge in località Gries a circa mezzo chilometro fuori dell'abitato di Bolzano"; a single-storey building with an auditorium; two 40 m lattice towers about 80 m apart; an inverted-L bifilar aerial; "lunghezza d'onda di circa 400 m"; "piccola potenza della stazione (200 Watt-antenna)"; "Il complesso trasmettitore-modulatore, fornito dalla Standard Elettrica Italiana". Power came from the Azienda Elettrica Consorziale di Bolzano. Telephone lines linked the station "alla Casa del Fascio, al Teatro Civico, e ai più importanti luoghi di ritrovo pubblico". The programme box reads: "Stazione di Bolzano – Lunghezza d'onda: m. 400 – Potenza: Kw. 0,2 – antenna; Tutti i giorni: 12,30–12,45 Notizie Stefani; 17–17,15 Notizie Stefani…". The same article notes that "alla stazione gli ultimi treni portano da ogni regione altro contributo di folla" and that cars arrived overnight carrying "frotte di camicie nere da tutta l'Italia".
- EIAR's report on 1928 (*Radiocorriere* 1929 no. 15) [V; `Radiocorriere_1929-15.pdf`]: "Su richiesta del Governo abbiamo nel brevissimo spazio di tempo di poco più di un mese impiantata la stazione di Bolzano, che ha cominciato a funzionare il 12 luglio … La stazione di Bolzano non era prevista nel nostro capitolato d'oneri … Nonostante la sua piccola potenza tale stazione è sentita bene in tutto il Trentino e, quantunque la sua onda sia in comune con quella di altre stazioni estere, essa è talvolta captata anche a [distanza]". EIAR also planned to link Genova and Bolzano to Milan by line for programme centralisation. The listing reads "Bolzano (1 BZ) – m. 445,9 – Kw. 0,2", and a programme item reads "16–17,30: Dal Casino Mun. di Bolzano".
- *Radiocorriere* 1932 no. 42 (15–22 Oct 1932) [V; `Radiocorriere_1932-42.pdf`]: the programme block "BOLZANO … kw. 1" (frequency and wavelength are garbled in the OCR). Its schedule includes "Comunicazioni del Dopolavoro – Radio-giornale dell'Enit – Notiziario agricolo – Comunicato della Reale Società Geografica – Giornale radio", though the column layout makes the attribution to Bolzano slightly uncertain.
- *Radiocorriere* 1939 no. 10 [V; `Radiocorriere_1939-10.pdf`]: Bolzano appears in the network groupings "ROMA I – NAPOLI I – BARI I – BOLOGNA – BOLZANO …", i.e. it relayed the national programme.
- Luce documentary *Radio italiana anno XVI [EIAR]* (IL3000083387, D032902, 1937–38, 15'40", made with EIAR) [V]: a map animation adds EIAR stations year by year from 1926, and "la stazione di Bolzano" is shown among Turin, Milan, Trieste, Genoa, Florence, Bologna and Rome — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000083387/1/eiar.html).
- Rai Südtirol's lineage: the station's roots are in an EIAR station "whose inaugural broadcast aired on 12 July 1928, then in the Italian language, as part of the propaganda of the Mussolini government" — [en.wikipedia, Rai Südtirol (radio)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_S%C3%BCdtirol_(radio_station)) [S]. "Erste Radiosendungen in deutscher Sprache wurden von der RAI … 1945 … ausgestrahlt" — [de.wikipedia, Rai Südtirol](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_S%C3%BCdtirol) [S].
- Radio Rurale: the Ente Radio Rurale was founded in 1933 and began school broadcasts in 1934, "based on fascist culture and propaganda" — [Bravi, Annali online Unife](https://annali.unife.it/adfd/article/view/2330/2164) [S]. It was reorganised by Legge 29 maggio 1939 n. 881 — [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1939/06/30/039U0881/CONSOLIDATED/20091216) [S]. The newsreel *La radio rurale insegna ai bambini* (B1476, 15/03/1939, IL5000018563) shows "una scuola elementare tra le montagne dell'Alto Adige", where "una radio rurale insegna loro delle canzoncine" — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000018563/2/la-radio-rurale-insegna-ai-bambini.html) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oXYlKMvsZxM) [V].

### Inferences
- [I] The station was created as part of the monument event. It was ordered by Ciano, built in about a month and launched on the monument's inauguration day, so its first "programme" was the national ritual. This makes Bolzano 1928 a joint radio-plus-monument media event, heard through relays as far as Trentino.
- [I] Counter-propaganda: a 200 W, then 1 kW, Italian-only local station sharing a wavelength with foreign stations could not have competed with the big German-language transmitters, such as Vienna ("kc. 580 – m. 517,2 – kw. 15" in the same 1932 programme pages), for German-speaking listeners. Its function was more plausibly to serve the incoming Italian population and to stage presence. This is an inference, not a documented mandate.
- [I] Radio carried the place to the nation in only a limited way. The station's main traffic ran the other way, bringing national programmes into Bolzano. National listeners would have heard Bolzano mainly through relayed events (1928, possibly the 1935 visit).

### Gaps
- I did not check, in later Radiocorriere issues (1935–43), whether national programmes about Alto Adige were broadcast, or whether the station gained a German-language slot during the Option period (1939–43).
- Uncertainty: the wavelength figures differ between sources (about 400 m in July 1928, 445.9 m in 1929, and an unreadable OCR value in 1932). This is consistent with the 1929 Prague wavelength plan changes, but that is not verified.
- No figures were found for radio licences or Radio Rurale sets in the province.

## 4. Opera Nazionale Dopolavoro: Treni Popolari, excursions, the Brenner as pilgrimage, OND publications

### Takeaway
The Treni popolari ("Treni speciali celeri per servizi festivi popolari") ran from **2 Aug 1931 to 3 Sept 1939** and carried **8,774,451** passengers nationally, with discounts of up to 80% in third class. National sources name seaside and art-city routes, not Bolzano or the Brenner. I found no verified Treno popolare to Bolzano, Merano or the Brennero. The evidence I did find shows mobilization running both ways: national crowds brought to Bolzano for regime rituals (1928, the 1938 Alpini adunata in Trento, sports championships) and South Tyrolean youth groups taken to Rome.

### Cited Findings
- Treni popolari: inaugurated 2 Aug 1931 on the initiative of the Ministry of Communications (Costanzo Ciano); the official name was "Treni speciali celeri per servizi festivi popolari"; discounts reached 80% of the ordinary third-class fare; 459,887 passengers in 1931 and 1,226,261 in 1938; launch routes Rome–Nettuno/Gaeta, Milan–Venice/Savona, Turin–Albenga/Aosta, Bologna–Pesaro/Pracchia, Taranto–Bari and Caserta–Naples; ended 3 Sept 1939 — [Il Post, 15 Aug 2024](https://www.ilpost.it/2024/08/15/ferragosto-treni-popolari-fascismo/) [S].
- Passenger totals by year: 1932 833,943; 1933 864,297; 1934 1,030,388; 1935 1,064,262; 1936 1,195,571; 1937 1,261,267; 1938 1,226,261; 1939 838,625; total 8,774,451. Day-one fares (2 Aug 1931): Roma–Nettuno 6 lire, Milano–Venezia 18, Torino–Aosta 12. The OND coordinated the operation with local fascist organisations. Earlier concessions (40% Sunday reductions) had been used by domestic tourists travelling to "Alto Adige" or "Cadore". Sources cited: Giuntini 2002; Ministero delle Comunicazioni, *Relazione 1931–32*; FS annual reports — [it.wikipedia, Treni popolari](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treni_popolari) [S].
- Scholarly article: Stefano Cecini, "Trains for Everybody. Italians on a Trip by Popular Trains. 1931–1939", *Dimensioni e problemi della ricerca storica* 2/2014, pp. 113–139, DOI 10.7376/80245 — [rivisteweb](https://www.rivisteweb.it/doi/10.7376/80245) [S].
- Luce shows the trains as an object of propaganda: *Impressioni di vita N°1 / Ritmi di stazione* (1933, D062002) includes "l'insegna dei treni popolari" and "un cartello indica la direzione del treno popolare", and *Credere, obbedire, combattere* (1930–39, D039301) includes "i treni popolari" with a timetable poster for a train to Naples [V] (see the documentary table).
- OND and Dopolavoro in South Tyrol, as seen in Luce [V]: *VI campionato dopolavoristico di marcia su sci* at Dobbiaco (B0635, 02/1935, IL5000016571) — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000016571/2/vi-campionato-dopolavoristico-marcia-sci.html); *Merano canti e danze* (regional Dopolavoro-style folk groups from Gorizia, Trento, Lombardy, Lazio and the Marche in Merano, [1932–37]); *OND – Per i nostri soldati* (1943, D033702: OND "Carri di Tespi" trains touring for troops, with German soldiers in the audience).
- National crowds brought to Bolzano for the 12 July 1928 inauguration: special trains and overnight cars "da tutta l'Italia", 47 gold-medal holders, 10 decorated communes' banners and their podestà, Garibaldini, the mothers of Filzi and Chiesa — *Radiorario* 1928 no. 30 [V].
- Alpini adunate as mass travel: the 19th Adunata nazionale was held in **Trento, 23–24 April 1938**. None was held in Bolzano before 1949, which was the 23rd — [it.wikipedia, Adunata nazionale degli alpini](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adunata_nazionale_degli_alpini) [S]. The Luce story on the Trento adunata says "20000 alpini sono giunti a Trento" and shows homage at the Battisti mausoleum on the Verruca (B1297, 04/05/1938, IL5000028443) — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000028443/2/l-adunata-nazionale-degli-alpini.html) [V]. Earlier Alpini national gatherings in the region: the 3rd convegno in Trento (1922) and the 7th at Rifugio Contrin (1926); the 2nd (1921) was at Cortina — [same source](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adunata_nazionale_degli_alpini) [S].
- The Brenner as "confine sacro" in Luce imagery [V]: the boundary stone (cippo) appears as a ritual object in A0126 (1928: the Duchess of Aosta lays flowers at "il cippo di confine Italia-Austria"), in B0742 (1935: "il Duce arriva sul cippo del confine"), and in B1312 (1938: "la colonnina del confine del Brennero" in a commemoration). The documentary *DUCE (Duce 1923/1926)* (IL3000089725) shows a marble cippus with a Latin inscription marking the Saint-Germain border, a laurel wreath, and a sign commemorating the Italian dead "firmata dal 'Governo Fascista'" — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000089725/1/duce-1923-1926.html).
- Tourist and OND-adjacent print, on Internet Archive (rights unclear, so recorded rather than downloaded): Touring Club Italiano, *Le stazioni della Venezia Tridentina, del Cadore e della Carnia* (1935) — [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/StazioniVeneziaTridentina) [V metadata]; *Questioni e vita dell'Alto Adige. Notiziario, giugno 1942 – marzo 1943* — [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/questioni-vita-dell-alto-adige) [V metadata].

### Inferences
- [I] In the newsreels the Brenner appears less as a holiday destination than as a ritual end-point (the cippo). Visits by the Duce, royals and youth groups re-enacted the 1919 frontier. Ordinary dopolavoristi probably met the Dolomites through sports championships (Dopolavoro ski marches, GIL and GUF championships, Fasci all'Estero meets at Dobbiaco and San Candido) rather than through cheap day trains. The distances from most of Italy exceed the day-trip radius of the published Treni popolari routes.
- [I] Mobilization ran in both directions: South Tyrolean (mostly Italian-speaking or Italianised) youth formations were shown travelling to Rome (1932, 1935 twice, 1938), presenting the province as loyal to the nation's centre.

### Gaps
- No specific Treno popolare or OND excursion to Bolzano, Merano, the Brennero or the Dolomites (dates, prices, participant numbers) was verified. The Teßmann digital newspaper archive (*La Provincia di Bolzano*, *Alpenzeitung*) refused automated access (HTTP 403, bot shield), and *Il Brennero* (the Trento fascist daily) is on Internet Archive for one issue only (12 May 1935, CC BY). These would be the main source for train arrivals.
- De Grazia's *The Culture of Consent* (1981) and Cecini (2014) were not read in full. Their tables on excursion volumes by destination are unchecked.
- No OND publication specifically promoting Alto Adige was identified.

## 5. Military service, the Vallo Alpino and the Alpini: the army as a carrier of knowledge about the place

### Takeaway
Conscription and garrison duty probably carried the place most directly into ordinary Italian families. Bolzano was HQ of an army corps (the "Bolzano Corps" in 1938–39; usually identified as the IV Corps, which I did not verify) and of the **11th Infantry Division "Brennero"** (231st Infantry at Bressanone; 232nd Infantry and 9th Artillery at Bolzano). Merano was HQ of the **2nd Alpine Division "Tridentina"** (from 10 Sept 1935) and Brunico of the **5th "Pusteria"** (from 31 Dec 1935). The **Vallo Alpino** in South Tyrol was built mostly between late 1939 and autumn 1940 and stopped in autumn 1942. About 800–1,000+ works were planned and about 300 completed, built by large Italian contractors with workforces brought from outside the province. Exact workforce figures were not found. An April 1940 postcard sent from a munitions depot at Stilves to a family near Florence shows directly how this circulation worked.

### Cited Findings
- 11th Infantry Division "Brennero": HQ Bolzano. Regiments in 1940: 231st "Brennero" at Bressanone (Brixen), 232nd "Brennero" at Bolzano, 9th Artillery "Brennero" at Bolzano. The division was renamed "Brennero" in 1934 and its regiments renamed in 1939. It moved to Piedmont on 4 Jan 1940 and attacked at Mont Cenis on 19–24 June 1940 — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Infantry_Division_%22Brennero%22) [S]. The Luce newsreel *Il 232° fanteria* (A0954, 05/1932) shows the Duke of Pistoia taking command of the regiment at Bolzano station [V].
- The Bolzano Army Corps existed in 1938–39 (a general's career entry: "Commanding Officer Artillery, Bolzano Corps", 1938–39) — [generals.dk](https://generals.dk/general/Merzari/Fabio/Italy.html) [S].
- 2nd Alpine Division "Tridentina": formed 10 Sept 1935 with the 5th and 6th Alpini and the 2nd Alpine Artillery; garrison/HQ Meran — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Alpine_Division_%22Tridentina%22) [S]. 5th Alpine Division "Pusteria": HQ Bruneck, active 31 Dec 1935 – 8 Sept 1943; the 11th Alpini was formed on 22 Dec 1935 for it — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Alpine_Division_%22Pusteria%22); [11th Alpini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Alpini_Regiment) [S]. In Luce, the Pusteria is shown back from East Africa in Rome (B1080, 1937) and with a field hospital at Ambò (B1279, 1938), and the Tridentina back from Albania (C0161, 1941) [V].
- Garrison size: no figure was found for the number of soldiers in Bolzano.
- Vallo Alpino (South Tyrol): building ran "from the end of 1939 to the autumn of 1940 … at breakneck speed"; about 3,000 plots were expropriated; "more than 1,000 bunkers were planned. 306 were completed, and 135 remained unfinished"; over 1.68 million m³ of concrete in one year; only Italian construction companies were commissioned, with no forced labour — [south-tirol.com (IDM)](https://www.south-tirol.com/blog/south-tyrols-bunker-landscape) [S]. A Festung Franzensfeste research project (scientific lead Heimo Prünster; period 1931–1942) gives "fast 800 Bunkern" planned, "etwas mehr als 300 im Rohbau fertiggestellt, weitere 150 blieben als Baustellen" — [stol.it](https://www.stol.it/artikel/chronik/die-geheimnisse-der-bunker) [S]. It also says "Durchgeführt wurden die Bauarbeiten von großen italienischen Baufirmen – mit guten Kontakten zu Rom. Einige Südtiroler Tagelöhner halfen auch mit. Zwangsarbeit gab es nicht. Im Herbst 1942 wurden die Bauarbeiten eingestellt – infolge des politischen Drucks durch Deutschland"; standard wall thickness was 3.50 m — [stol.it](https://www.stol.it/artikel/chronik/warum-mussolini-in-suedtirol-heimlich-1000-bunker-bauen-wollte) [S]. Circolare n. 15.000 of 1939 set the criteria; the Genio Militare under Gen. Edoardo Monti directed the work; the first sites opened in late autumn 1939 — [Panorama](https://www.panorama.it/news/vallo-del-littorio-bozza) [S]. A local source says the contractors' workforces came "mainly from southern Italy" and that local firms were excluded — [all4shooters, Gampen bunker](https://www.all4shooters.com/it/tiro/cultura/gampen-bunker-fortificazione-in-alto-adige/) [S, single source]. Nationally, Ambrosio's report of 3 Oct 1942 counted 1,475 bunkers completed and 450 under construction, and the Guardia alla Frontiera, created in 1937, had about 21,000 personnel — [en.wikipedia, Alpine Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Wall) [S]. Further literature: Bernasconi & Muran, *Le fortificazioni del Vallo Alpino Littorio in Alto Adige* (1999); *Il testimone di cemento* (2009); Urthaler, Niederkofler & Pozza, *Bunker* (2006) [S].
- **Item-level trace of a soldier's correspondence [V].** Two postcards, both dated "Stilves 22-4-40-XVIII", from one sender signing "Dante" (my reading). The first, *Maria Trens m. 993* (publisher L. Fränzl, Bolzano), is postmarked CAMPO DI TRENS (BOLZANO) and addressed to "Signora Ricci Maddalena, S. Ippolito di Vernio (Firenze)". The second, of the Brennero, is addressed to "Signor Ricci Ferdinando" at the same address, with the return address "Deposito Munizioni – Stilves – Campo di Trens (Bolzano)" — [archive.org 006_20240119…](https://archive.org/details/006_20240119_20240119_1643); [archive.org 110_20231218](https://archive.org/details/110_20231218). Both are Public Domain Mark items from the Biblioteca Petrarca, Vernio, and are saved in `files/`.
- Alpini adunate in the region: Trento 1938 (19th), see section 4. None was held in Bolzano before 1949 [S].
- Military films: *Alpini sciatori* (IL3000051309, 1929, 42'34"): a five-man Alpini ski patrol crossing "3000 km" of the Alpine arc. *Verso il Brennero* ([1924–31]): "fortificazioni militari in prossimità del confine". *Il battaglione sciatori della guardia di finanza alla rivista di Bolzano* (D033301) [V].

### Inferences
- [I] From 1939 to 1942, three military flows converged on the province: the garrison (conscripts of the Brennero, Tridentina and Pusteria divisions), the Guardia alla Frontiera, and the Vallo workforce (contractors' labourers from other regions). Each produced letters, postcards and leave stories sent home, such as the Stilves cards to Vernio. This is probably the largest non-media channel through which ordinary families "knew" Bolzano, and it coincides with the Option.
- [I] The Alpini divisions with regional names (Tridentina, Pusteria) spread the place names through national war reporting (Ethiopia 1936–37, Albania 1940–41, Russia 1942–43), whatever the soldiers' own origins.

### Gaps
- Garrison strength in Bolzano (1928–43), and the Vallo Alpino workforce size and regional origins, were not found. The likely sources are the Festung Franzensfeste project publication (Prünster 2023, valloalpino.info, which needs JavaScript and could not be scraped), AUSSME, and the 1931 and 1936 census tables for military population.
- The Teßmann newspapers were inaccessible for local coverage of troop movements.
- Statistics on conscripts' regional origin for units in the province were not found.

## 6. School: Venezia Tridentina, Alto Adige and the Brenner in the Libro di Stato and geography teaching; Cesare Battisti as martyr

### Takeaway
The single state textbook (Legge 7 gennaio 1929, n. 5) standardised elementary teaching nationally from 1930. Its specific wording on the Brenner and Alto Adige could not be retrieved online in this session. What is documented is how Battisti and the Brenner circulated through school-adjacent media: Luce films of school life that include Alto Adige, the Battisti busts in the Bolzano monument, the Battisti mausoleum at Trento (1935), youth-organisation films, and the Paravia children's biography *Cesare Battisti* (1929).

### Inferences
- [I] For schoolchildren nationwide, "Alto Adige" was most likely learnt as the end-point of the Risorgimento (the Brenner as natural border, the Roman Drusus) and through the Battisti martyr story (Trento, 12 July 1916). The 12 July date links Battisti's execution, the 1928 inauguration of the Bolzano monument and the radio station's opening. The repeated busts of Battisti, Filzi and Chiesa in Bolzano tie the city to that national pedagogy.
- [I] Luce's school films show South Tyrol as a place to be educated (vocational schools, the Dante Alighieri kindergarten, Radio Rurale) rather than as a subject of the curriculum.

### Gaps
- No digitised page of a Libro di Stato with the Brenner or Alto Adige was found. Catalogue access to GEI and EDISCO (Edisco/Macerata, GEI-Digital) was blocked to scripts. INDIRE and the Museo della scuola "Paolo e Ornella Ricca" (Macerata) are the next places to look.
- Ascenzi and Sani's work on the Libro di Stato, and Andrea Di Michele's *L'italianizzazione imperfetta*, were not consulted.
- I found no geography-textbook passage quoting the "confine sacro / naturale" for the 1930s.
