## 1. The plan as a drawn and published thing: the 1929 competition, the municipal plan of 1933, Piacentini's plan of 1934–41

### Takeaway
The plan went through four stages. (1) A national competition was announced in July 1929, judged in 1930 by a jury that Piacentini sat on, and gave three equal winners: Libera and Pollini, Muzio, and Sottsass. It produced no plan. (2) In 1933 the municipal chief engineer, Guido Ferrari, was told to make a plan from the best entries. (3) Piacentini was commissioned in 1934. The monumental zone around the Victory Monument had already been declared of public utility by decree n. 809 of 9 June 1934. The Comune adopted the plan, dated November 1935, by the podestà's act of 7 December 1935. The Soprintendenza objected in 1936, the Consiglio Superiore dei Lavori Pubblici approved it with conditions in 1937 (its stamp on the sheets is dated 15 November 1937), and supplementary sheets for the "Zona della Vittoria" are dated July 1938. (4) It became Law 23 January 1941, n. 173. The Comune still holds the signed sheets and serves them online. The old-town sheet of November 1935 marks large demolitions along Via dei Portici. A 1937 review in a national architecture journal called them "eccessive, o per meglio dire totali". The July 1938 old-town sheet is far more modest.

### Cited Findings
**The 1929–30 competition**
- The bando appeared in July 1929, "redatto con la consulenza di S.E. Alberto Calza Bini". In March 1930 the entries of "9 partecipanti" were shown at the Teatro Civico. The bando required leaving "intatto, o quasi, il vecchio centro a Nord di piazza Vittorio Emanuele" and placed "il nucleo più popoloso, più civile e più italiano" in the plain of Gries. The jury was Piacentini, Bazzani and Duilio Torres, with the podestà and the chief engineers of the Genio Civile, the provincial technical office and the Comune. It "dichiara vincitori ex aequo gli architetti Libera e Pollini, G. Muzio ed E. Sottsass", with encouragement prizes to "ingegneri Chiodi e Merlo, e N. Galimberti". [S] — Canali 2016, pp. 33–34, quoting *La Provincia di Bolzano* (6 Jan 1929, 25 Jul 1929) and E. Tolomei, "L'edilizia", *Archivio per l'Alto Adige* XXV (1930), pp. 305–310 (row 14.20, [PDF](https://flore.unifi.it/handle/2158/1074525))
- Pollini (from Rovereto) entered with the Trentino architect Adalberto Libera and the Bolzano surveyor Elvio Laitempergher. Their plan was radial and zoned, modelled on the recent plan for Amsterdam, with a third bridge over the Talvera. Muzio printed his own entry ("Concorso per il Piano regolatore di Bolzano", Milan, January 1930, ACS, Min. LL.PP., Div. XXIII, b. 185). [S] — Canali 2016, nn. 50–51 (row 14.20)
- Published nationally in Plinio Marconi, "Concorsi per il Piano Regolatore di Bolzano e di Arezzo", *Architettura e Arti Decorative* II, 12 (1929–30), pp. 340–365. [S] — Canali 2016, n. 46 (row 14.20). I found no openly served scan of that volume (see Gaps).
- **Conflict on the date and the winners.** Vincenzo Civico in *Urbanistica* 5 (1933), p. 164 wrote of the "Concorso nazionale 1930, vinto dagli architetti Libera e Pollini" (a single winner). [S] — quoted in Canali 2016, n. 55. Verdorfer has "Nel 1929 fu indetto un concorso nazionale", with Piacentini as "presidente della commissione giudicatrice". [V] — row 5.10. Dunajtschik & Steinacher: competition of 1929, Piacentini chairman of the jury, "Nachdem keines der eingereichten Projekte den Zuschlag erhalten hatte". [S] — row 5.11, p. 118 f. The likeliest reading is: announced 1929, judged 1930, three equal winners. The jury report in *La Provincia di Bolzano* (spring 1930) or Marconi's article would settle it.
- **Conflict on Chiodi and Merlo.** Their project is described as "vincitore del secondo premio ex aequo" in the Politecnico di Milano archive record (fondo Cesare Chiodi). [SS] — [archimistaweb.polimi.it/fonds/260/units/5081](http://www.archimistaweb.polimi.it/fonds/260/units/5081) (the host did not resolve when I tried it). Canali's newspaper quotation gives them only an encouragement prize. [S]
- In 1932 Nino Gallimberti, writing on the Verona competition, called Verona "la seconda città delle Venezie, dopo Bolzano" to hold a national plan competition: "Da Bolzano a Verona si è fatto passi da gigante". [V] — [arcover.dicea.unipd.it, 207_PRG_inItalia (PDF)](https://arcover.dicea.unipd.it/biblioteca/207_PRG_inItalia/207_PRG_inItalia_300.pdf), last page (not held)

**From the municipal plan (1933) to Piacentini (1934–35)**
- In 1933 the municipal chief engineer Guido Ferrari was to draw a plan from the best entries. It gave the Victory Monument no urban role, "forse fu anche per questo motivo per cui il Piano di Ferrari ebbe molto poca fortuna". In 1934 Piacentini took Ferrari's plan as a base and added "uno spazioso e grandioso sistema di strade e di piazze", making the monument the new centre. [S] — Canali 2016, pp. 40–41, quoting O. Zoeggeler in Ippolito & Zoeggeler 1992, pp. 34–35, and noting "Il testo non presenta purtroppo alcuna fonte documentaria"; same account in row 5.11, p. 119
- The commission to Piacentini was made public in "Deliberazioni del Commissario al Comune", *La Provincia di Bolzano*, 30 November 1934. The monumental zone "dal Monumento alla Vittoria fino alla piazza Druso" had already been declared "zona di pubblica utilità" by "Decreto n.809 del 9 giugno 1934". [S] — Canali 2016, nn. 81–82, citing the Genio Civile report of 22 April 1936 and the plan's report of November 1935 (ACS, Min. LL.PP., Div. XXIII, b. 185)
- **Conflict on the year of the commission.** Zoeggeler 1992, p. 22: "l'incarico … nel 1935 fu assegnato a Piacentini". [S] — quoted in Canali 2016, n. 79. The Comune's own page has 1934. [V] — [Comune di Bolzano, Piani urbanistici](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Vivere-il-Comune/Approfondimenti/Piani-urbanistici). Dunajtschik & Steinacher also have 1934. [S] The newspaper of 30 Nov 1934 supports 1934.
- Canali adds a figure the other sources leave out. Gustavo Giovannoni supervised the general plan "più volte", and this was "sconosciuto" before. [S] — Canali 2016, abstract. Canali also quotes the ministries in 1936–37: "vanno considerate le ragioni d'ordine politico" (Ministry of National Education to the prefect, 29 Apr 1937) and "Il Piano di Bolzano è stato oggetto della particolare attenzione di S.E. il Capo del Governo" (Ministry of Public Works, 21 June 1937). [S] — Canali 2016, p. 21 epigraphs
- A detail of how the plan worked on single plots: when the old slaughterhouse site was sold to the INA in 1934, the INA had to build under "un vincolo che deve consentire la visuale delle Dolomiti da Piazza della Vittoria". [S] — Canali 2016, n. 49, quoting *La Provincia di Bolzano*, 7 March 1934

**The sheets themselves (rows 14.10, 14.14, 14.12)**
- The Comune's page gives the chronology: Piacentini commissioned 1934; plan approved by the Comune 1935; "parzialmente respinto" by the Soprintendenza alle Belle Arti per la Venezia Tridentina 1936; approved with reservations by the Consiglio Superiore dei Lavori Pubblici 1937; final approval 1939; law 1941. The Archivio Storico holds some sheets, viewable as zoomable images (19 sheets for this plan). [V] — [Comune di Bolzano](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Vivere-il-Comune/Approfondimenti/Piani-urbanistici)
- Read on the November 1935 sheets: "Bolzano Novembre 1935-XIV, deliberato con atto podestarile 7 Dic. 1935-XIV N. 967.13425", with the signatures of the Ingegnere Capo and the Podestà; a stamp of the Consiglio Superiore dei LL.PP., Assemblea generale, "15 Nov. 1937 Anno XVI"; and the royal visa "Visto, d'ordine di Sua Maestà il Re d'Italia e di Albania Imperatore d'Etiopia, come da decreto in data … 1941 Anno XIX, il Ministro dei Lavori Pubblici f.to Gorla". [V] — row 14.10. The July 1938 sheets carry the same visa with the date legible as "23 Gen 1941", the date of the law. [V] — row 14.14
- Tav. 4 "Zone" sets out the new city's zones (nucleo urbano, "centro civico di Gries", extensive, semi-intensive and intensive zones with "case operaie", industrial and military zones, the airfield) and gives "Area della nuova città … Popolazione … abitanti 100.000". [V] — row 14.10. The target of 100,000 matches the 1929 competition's brief: "Eine massive Vergrößerung der Stadt von 35.000 auf 100.000 Einwohner war die Zielvorgabe." [S] — row 5.11, p. 118
- Tav. 4 "Espropriazioni" (1:2880) marks the "zona dichiarata di pubblica utilità con decreto 809 del 9 giugno 1934-XII". The sheet thus confirms Canali's decree. [V] — row 14.10
- Tav. 2 "Sistemazione del nucleo cittadino", 1:1440, legend "Monumenti, Nuove costruzioni, Demolizioni, Giardini pubblici, Giardini privati". Yellow demolitions and new orange fronts with arcade hatching run along the north side of Via dei Portici, around Piazza del Grano and towards Via dei Conciapelle. A new "Mercato coperto" is drawn by the Talvera. The square is still "Piazza Vittorio Emanuele"; a "Via del Littorio" runs east of it. [V] — row 14.10 (detail images)
- The July 1938 sheets: Tav. 7 extends Corso IX Maggio to a "Piazza Tiberio" around the Gries abbey church ("sistemazione della visuale terminale"); Tav. 8 joins the Merano–Mendola road to the "Foro della Vittoria" via "Viale Cesare Augusto", "Viale Giulio Cesare" and "Piazza dell'Impero". The July 1938 Tav. 5 "Sistemazione del nucleo urbano" shows a much smaller set of interventions in the old town than the 1935 Tav. 2. [V] — row 14.14; the comparison is my reading [INF]
- No name or signature of Piacentini appears on the title blocks I read (Tav. 2 and Tav. 4 of 1935, Tav. 7 of 1938). The sheets are the municipal set signed by the Comune's engineer and podestà. [V] — rows 14.10, 14.14
- An untitled sheet served with the plan ("tavola senza nome 02") draws the western expansion over cadastral parcels. It runs from Gries to the Isarco, with "Corso Stelvio", a hemicycle at San Quirino and, at the south-east, the block grid of "Via Bari", "Via Alessandria", "Via Piacenza" and "Piazza Pontinia", the centre of the Semirurali. [V] — row 14.12. It is undated; the square's name dates it to 1936 or later [INF].

**The law**
- Legge 23 gennaio 1941, n. 173 approves "il piano regolatore generale di massima edilizio e di ampliamento della città di Bolzano, comprendente il piano particolareggiato per la zona di Gries" (art. 1). Owners of new buildings that must have arcades leave them as public way without compensation, "come pubbliche vie" (art. 9). Compensation excludes any rise in value due to the plan (art. 5). The plan has no time limit and the Gries detailed plan 15 years (art. 12). Signed by Vittorio Emanuele, Mussolini, Gorla, Grandi and Bottai; GU n. 81, 4 April 1941. [V] — row 14.17, [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/atto/caricaDettaglioAtto?atto.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=1941-04-04&atto.codiceRedazionale=041U0173&tipoDettaglio=originario)

### Inferences
- The arcades of the new city were a legal duty as well as a style. Art. 9 turns the Laubengasse type into compulsory public space that comes free to the Comune. The 1935 plan drew the same arcade on the old Via dei Portici by demolishing its north side. The plan carried one form across the Talvera in both directions: the new street copied the old arcade, and the old street was to be rebuilt to the new measure.
- The 1935 sheet is the strongest single document of "charging by change of form" in the base. It shows, in a legend colour, what was meant to go. Comparing it with the 1938 sheet shows what the Soprintendenza's 1936 objection and the 1937 conditions removed.
- Three of the 1929 winners (Libera, Pollini, Sottsass) came from Trentino. The competition that made the "Italian" Bolzano was largely won by architects from the next valley south. Ettore Sottsass then built the Lido (§4) and became one of the city's builders without the plan ever being his [INF].

## 2. Period views and city maps: before and after

### Takeaway
Three maps now frame the change. The 1858 Austrian cadastre shows the Talvera plain as vineyards and meadows (row 14.01). Baedeker's 1927 plan shows German street names and no new city yet (row 14.06). A British War Office town plan of 1942, "copied from an Italian plan dated 1936", shows the Foro della Vittoria, Piazza IV Novembre and the Corpo d'Armata in bird's-eye view (row 14.19). Openly licensed period photographs of the new city itself are scarce. Gabinio's Bolzano set (1933, Commons) has the old town, the Walther monument and panoramas of the station quarter, not the new city. Two TCI archive photographs (the Lido, 1932; the Garage centrale, c. 1940), a Vorarlberg stereo plate of the Victory Monument (27 July 1938) and a 1936 Luce newsreel of the building sites add the period views that could be held.

### Inferences
- The Allied town plan is a copy of an Italian tourist bird's-eye plan of 1936. The same drawing served the regime's visitors in 1936 and the bomber staffs in 1942–43.
- The scarcity of open photographs of the new city is itself telling. The images that reached national circulation (Luce, TCI, the journals) are under copyright. The openly licensed ones (Gabinio, the Swedish slides, the Vorarlberg plates) are tourists' views of the old town and the monument.

## 3. What was removed, moved or disguised

### Takeaway
The regime worked on the Tyrolean town by subtraction and renaming as much as by building. In order: Gries was absorbed by royal decree on 2 November 1925. The Austrian station of 1865 was rebuilt by Angiolo Mazzoni and reopened on 24 May 1928. The Laurin fountain was wrecked on the night of 4–5 June 1933 and ended up in the war museum at Rovereto. The Walther statue left its square in 1935; a national magazine announced that Drusus would replace it, and he never did. The Museo Civico lost its tower and battlements from 1936, was renamed "Museo dell'Alto Adige" and got a Roman milestone of Claudius at its door. The Sparkasse front was flattened in 1938 (row 5.11; notes 05). Houses were demolished for the Corso (1936), and the 1935 plan meant to rebuild the north side of Via dei Portici; that was not done. After 1945 the symbols came back one by one: Walther in 1981, the museum tower in 1990, Laurin in 1993–96. The station, the Corso and the 1925 merger stayed.

### Cited Findings
**Gries absorbed (1925)**

It cites the Bolzano prefectural commissioner's request of 7 February 1925 and the Gries council's deliberation of 6 February 1925. [V] — row 14.05
- it.wikipedia gives "Regio decreto, n. 2299, del 2 novembre 1925" in its text and "n. 2027" in its footnote. [W] — [Gries-San Quirino](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gries-San_Quirino). **Settled by the Gazzetta:** 2027 is the decree number, 2299 the publication number. The article also says Gries' last elected mayor, Josef Mumelter-Möckl, was removed with the council. [W]

**The station (1928)**
- "I nuovi edifici della Stazione furono inaugurati il 24 maggio 1928. **The Laurin fountain (1933)**
- By Andreas Kompatscher and Arthur Winder, set up in May 1907 on the Wassermauer promenade to sell Bozen as the "Laurin-Stadt"; inscription "Der Bürger Wohl zu mehren, dem fremden Gast zu Ehren". Fascists wrecked it "in der Nacht vom 4. auf den 5. Juni 1933" (source given there: *Südtiroler Heimat*, 1 Aug 1937, p. 6). It went to the Stadtmuseum, then to the Kriegsmuseum in Rovereto, back to Bozen in 1993 (through culture councillors Anton Zelger and Bruno Hosp), and stands on Silvius-Magnago-Platz since 1996. [W] — [de.wikipedia: Laurinbrunnen](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurinbrunnen). Before picture: row 14.07. After picture identified on Commons but not fetched (manifest).
- Dunajtschik & Steinacher also have it back in the city in the 1990s. [S] — row 5.11, p. 134. No order or court record of 1933 was found.

**The Walther monument (1935)**
- Tolomei's 32 "Provvedimenti" asked only one thing about monuments: removal of the Walther statue (point 17). In 1925–26 Mussolini refused to replace it with a Drusus because of the cost abroad, and the square was only renamed "Piazza Vittorio Emanuele III" (1926). The statue was removed in 1935, "neun Jahre nach dem … Veto". [S] — row 5.11, pp. 105–107, 121
- Moved in 1935 by "die faschistischen Behörden" to the "weniger zentral gelegenen Roseggerpark"; back on its square since 1981 after a citizens' committee campaigned from 1976. [W] — [de.wikipedia: Walther-Denkmal (Bozen)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther-Denkmal_(Bozen)), citing Johler 1995, p. 186, and *Dolomiten* 31 Oct 2001
- *Le Vie d'Italia*, July 1935: "Bolzano, oltre la rimozione della statua di Walther von der Vogelweider dalla piazza Vittorio Emanuele III, per sostituirvi quella di Druso, il fondatore della città, ha compiuto il restauro della chiesa di S. Domenico". [V] — row 14.09. The Drusus statue "unterblieb". [W]. So the magazine announced a replacement that was never made.
- Before pictures: Curman 1909 and Gabinio 1933 (row 14.04). The Baedeker of 1927 names the monument as "the chief rallying-point of visitors". [V] — row 14.06

**The Museo Civico (from 1936)**
- Built 1902–04 by the Comune; the exterior "nel gusto della tipica architettura italianizzante d'Oltradige" was designed by Carlo (Karl) Delug, the works directed by Guglielmo (Wilhelm) Kürschner; opened 16 April 1905. [V] — row 14.18, p. 5. Before picture: row 14.02.
- From 1936 its whole exterior was remade in the manner of the new city. The core of the change was removing "des markanten neugotischen Dachturms mit seinen hervorstechenden 'deutschen' Zinnen", replaced by a lower "italienisches" flat roof. Tolomei had called the museum a stronghold of Germandom. [S] — row 5.11, p. 122. "Al Museo furono asportate merlature e torre, quest'ultima ricostruita nel 1990." [V] — row 5.10
- Arslan, its director 1933–39, describes the transfer to the Comune (talks from 1930), the reorganisation from July 1935 and the reopening on 31 October 1937: "L'istituto assume in quel tempo la denominazione di Museo dell'Alto Adige". Before the entrance stands "il Miliario eretto nel 46 d. C. da Claudio al confine della X Regione Italica". He says nothing about the tower. [V] — row 14.18, pp. 6–7

**Other subtractions and corrections**
- 1932–36: Piazza Domenicani was remade as the only reshaped square of the old town, to take the central role from the Waltherplatz. 1938: the Sparkasse building (Wilhelm Kürschner, 1904) was "den Häusern der Città nuova angeglichen". [S] — row 5.11, pp. 121–122; INA building there 1932–33 and INFPS 1933–35, both by Paolo Rossi de' Paoli [V] — row 5.10
- Via Cassa di Risparmio (1898) had façades meant to express "il carattere tedesco" of the town; "Gli urbanisti fascisti si affrettarono, quindi, a correggere, almeno parzialmente, l'aspetto della strada." [V] — row 5.10
- The demolitions for the Corso (Kuendlhof, Bräuhaus Schwarz, Hotel Badl) are in notes 05 [W]. The Luce newsreel of 7 Oct 1936 films "strada sventrata" and "demolizione di un edificio" during the works. [V] — row 14.11
- A customs house ("Zollhäuschen") was "abgetragen 1935 und in Rentsch wiederaufgebaut", per a Commons file description. [V of the description only; the claim is unsourced, so a lead]
- Talvera bridge as "Ponte Claudio" in 1942. [V] — row 14.18. Piacentini's large replacement bridge was never built; only the left of the two semicircular INA buildings went up (1936). [V] — row 5.10

### Inferences
- The pattern is consistent. Things that marked Bozen as German were moved, cut down or wrecked: Walther, Laurin, the museum's tower and battlements, the Sparkasse's front. Things that could be read as Roman were added in their place or beside them: Drusus announced, Claudius's milestone, "Ponte Claudio", "Piazza Tiberio", Viale Giulio Cesare and Cesare Augusto. This is change of form in both directions at once: defusing the Tyrolean sign and charging the Italian one, often in the same spot.
- The postwar reversals are almost all on the German side's symbols (Walther 1981, museum tower 1990, Laurin 1993–96). The Italian additions mostly stayed or simply went out of use, and the 1925 merger was never in question.

### Gaps
- No primary order was found for the 1935 removal of Walther, the 1936 museum works or the 1933 attack on Laurin. Leads: the Comune's council and podestà acts for 1933–36 (Archivio Storico), *Alpenzeitung* and *Dolomiten* June 1933 and 1935 (Teßmann).
- No "after" photograph of the museum without its tower, or of Walther in the Roseggerpark, was found openly licensed.
- Where Claudius's milestone stands today was not checked.

## 4. The other modern architecture of the same years

### Takeaway
While the state built in Bolzano, local and German-speaking offices kept working and some built in a modern idiom of their own. Amonn & Fingerle (1921–40) built the La Rinascente department store with a cinema (1928) and a "Zentralgarage" (1929) that came close to rationalism. Marius Amonn also worked against the destruction of the old town and on solutions for it that would fit the regime. Clemens Holzmeister and Luis Trenker designed the first state employees' houses (Rione Battisti, 1925). The Lido (1932) is by Ettore Sottsass senior, one of the 1929 competition winners, with the Bolzano architect Willy Weyhenmeyer. The line between "regime" and "local" architecture is not clean: the same local architects worked for the state.

### Cited Findings
- **Amonn & Fingerle**, a partnership of Marius Amonn, August Fingerle and Hedwig Amonn-Fröhner, 1921–1940. Bozen works of the years 1922–40 include the 1928 "La Rinascente" department store with the "Cine Centrale" cinema, the 1929 "Zentralgarage", Haus Endrizzi (1934), Haus Dr. von Walther in Gries (1935), the upper floor of the Franciscan convent (1938) and Villa Rössler (1939). The archive holds a partial estate. Literature: M. Just & C. Hölz, *Architekten Amonn & Fingerle* (Innsbruck 2023); exhibition catalogue, Bozen 2019. [V] — [Universität Innsbruck, Archiv für Baukunst](https://www.uibk.ac.at/de/archiv-baukunstgeschichte/sammlung/unsere-bestande/ammon-fingerle/)
- Christoph Mayr Fingerle and Albert Mascotti (*Turris Babel* 2, 1985, pp. 41–45, held as row 8.10) write that the office came close to "der rationalistischen Architektur … (Zentralgarage)". "Amonn hat seit Beginn der faschistischen Ära gegen die Zerstörung des alten Stadtbildes gekämpft. So hat er sich lange Zeit mit Lösungen für die Bozner Altstadt beschäftigt, die mit den faschistischen Ideen in Einklang zu bringen waren." The article prints a photograph "La Rinascente mit Eingang zum Kino – Bozen". [V] — row 8.10, pp. 44–45
- **Conflict on Amonn's birthplace:** Innsbruck's archive has "born 22 August 1879 in Trieste" [V]. *Turris Babel* 2 says he was born on 22 August 1879 "in Bozen", son of Arnold Amonn [V]. A baptism register (Bozen parish, 1879) would settle it.
- A TCI photograph of about 1940, "Garage centrale e concessionaria FIAT a Bolzano" (row 14.16), very probably shows that Zentralgarage [INF: same name; neither source gives an address].
- **Holzmeister and Trenker.** The "Rione Battisti" in today's Via Diaz, state employees' houses of 1925, was designed by Clemens Holzmeister (North Tyrol) and Luis Trenker (South Tyrol), who had just opened an office in Bozen after studying in Vienna. [S] — row 5.11, p. 117, citing Verdorfer in *Bozen Innsbruck* (2000). Verdorfer counts Rione Battisti, with the Lido and the GIL, among works with "l'impronta netta di un moderno razionalismo". [V] — row 5.10. Canali cites a 1934 *Casabella* article "Villa a Bolzano" under "Hans Holzmeister" (see §1). [S]
- **The Lido**, by Ettore Sottsass and Willy Weyhenmeyer, "considerato uno dei migliori esempi del razionalismo italiano", flat roof, L-shaped plan, opened 1932, room for 2,000. [V] — row 5.10; period photograph row 14.08. Sottsass senior was one of the three equal winners of the 1929–30 plan competition. [S] — Canali 2016
- **Erich Pattis** published "Tre ville a Bolzano" in *Architettura* 1937. [S] — Canali n. 83. After the war he co-signed the 1945–53 reconstruction plan with Guido Tancredi Pelizzari. [V] — Comune di Bolzano, Piani urbanistici
- **Guido Pellizzari**, "architetto bolzanino", built the semicircular Istituto magistrale "Pascoli" (1938) in a rationalist manner. [V] — row 5.10. He also designed the Casa Littoria and Cristo Re with others (notes 05).

### Inferences
- Amonn's case is the strongest for the project: a local architect who defended the old town against the regime's plan while looking for forms "in Einklang" with it. It is the other side of Piacentini's Tav. 2 (row 14.10).
- "Rationalism" in Bolzano was not only the regime's style. It came from the Lido, the Zentralgarage and Holzmeister/Trenker as much as from the state offices. That complicates any reading of flat roofs as Italian and pitched roofs as German, a reading that Dunajtschik & Steinacher also make for the Corso.

### Gaps
- No openly licensed photograph of the Rione Battisti, the La Rinascente building or a Holzmeister–Trenker work was held. The Province's monument browser and the 2019 Amonn & Fingerle exhibition catalogue are the next places to look.
- Weyhenmeyer's biography was not found.

## 5. People and translocal links

### Takeaway
The architects of the main buildings came from Rome (Piacentini, Rossi de' Paoli, Calza Bini, Mazzoni, Ricci, Busiri Vici), Padua (Mansutti, Miozzo), Milan (Muzio, Colombo, Chiodi) and Trentino (Libera, Pollini, Sottsass senior); a few were local (Pellizzari, Weyhenmeyer, Plattner, Amonn & Fingerle). The clients were state bodies, not the town: the INA, INFPS, INCIS, the army, the State Railways, the party. Where the stone and the workers came from is documented only for a few works.
