## 1. The Zona Industriale di Bolzano: laws, firms, workforce, hiring policy, the Commissariato, and the 1939 anti-urbanism law

### Takeaway
The legal backbone is the **R.D.L. 28 settembre 1934 n. 1621**, which gave tax and customs privileges and state grants to firms founded in Bolzano by 30 June 1944 (later extended to 1945). It was followed by the **R.D.L. 7 marzo 1935 n. 234** (expropriation of about 300 ha between the Isarco and the Brennero road) and the 1936/37 extension decree. The big plants opened in 1937–1938: Montecatini/INA aluminium, Acciaierie (Falck), Lancia, Magnesio, Feltrinelli-Masonite and SAFFA. The workforce was about 3,000 at the end of October 1939 and about 6,500–7,000 by 1942. Grants were explicitly tied to hiring "manodopera regnicola e non allogena", meaning workers from the "old" provinces and not German-speaking South Tyroleans. From 1937 an office of the Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione interna policed the transfers of these workers.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The grant formula in the 1934 decree, applied by a commission that included the fascist industrial-workers' confederation and favoured "regnicola e non allogena" labour, made the ethnic composition of the workforce a condition of subsidy. That is the mechanism by which industrial policy became population policy. A studio project can show this directly by pairing the decree with the ASCBZ "Zona industriale" files on plot allocation.
- [INFERENCE] The two INA figures (100 vs. "più di 900" at start-up) probably measure different moments in 1937. The 900 matches Miori's October 1939 count, so the Comune 2004 phrasing may compress the timeline.

### Gaps
- No zone-wide workforce totals were found for 1937, 1940 or 1943, and there is no per-company split after 1939.
- No document was found showing the fascist unions' uffici di collocamento explicitly excluding German speakers. Exclusion is documented only through the grant criterion and secondary statements.
- No source was found for a Bolzano-specific exemption or special status under L. 1092/1939. The law's text contains none.
- The 1926 precursor body (often cited as the Comitato permanente per le migrazioni interne) was not verified. Only the 1931 law was checked.

## 2. Where the immigrants came from, and Bolzano's population by census and language group

### Takeaway
No study found gives province-of-origin **percentages** for the 1930s influx. The qualitative consensus is that unskilled workers came mainly from **Veneto (Padova, Verona, Rovigo named explicitly) and Trentino**, while technicians and skilled workers came from Lombardy and Piedmont. For all Italians in the province over the whole interwar period, **Trentino ranks first, then Veneto, then Lombardy**. In census terms, Bolzano city grew from 32,679 (1921) to 37,351 (1931), 45,505 (1936), 70,898 (1951) and 88,799 (1961). Municipal register data add 56,812 for 1 January 1939 and 64,494 for 1 January 1943. Arrivals from Italian provinces into the comune were 7,655 (1937), 4,203 (1938), 5,773 (1939) and 6,425 (1940).

## 3. The Semirurali ("Rione Dux"): IFACP programme, numbers, ideology, demolition, museum, oral history

### Takeaway
The Semirurali, officially the **Rione Dux**, were built by the **IFACP della Provincia di Bolzano** (founded 1934) from spring 1938. The phases were:
- a first lot of 92 buildings and 342 dwellings, inaugurated on 8 October 1939;
- 228 houses and 808 flats added in summer 1940;
- a 1941 lot finished only after the war.

The total was **327 houses with 1,151 dwellings and about 5,500 inhabitants** (Comune 2015; other counts range from 1,094 to 1,147 dwellings). Each two-storey house had a vegetable plot, following fascist ruralist ideology ("piccola città giardino"; "la piccola casa di Predappio"). The quarter was demolished in phases from the late 1970s and 1980s. The **Casa Semirurale, via Bari 11**, is the only surviving house; its exhibition opened on 16 May 2015 with recorded testimonies from 18 named former residents.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The best headline figure is "327 houses / about 1,150 dwellings / about 5,500 people", from the Comune's own 2015 synthesis, which includes the post-war completion of the 1941 lot. For the fascist years alone, 342 + 808 = 1,150 dwellings by 1940 (booklet) matches Mezzalira's 1,094 "by 1944" only roughly. A studio text should say "about 1,100 dwellings by 1940–44".
- [INFERENCE] The museum's Rückoptanten quote and the 1945 Lager next door make the Semirurali a single site where all three flows of the brief meet: state-organised Italian worker migration, the Option and return migration, and deportation.

## 4. The Option of 1939, ADERSt/AdO, the "Sicilian legend", ENTV land purchases and planned Italian resettlement, and where the Option files are

### Takeaway
**The Option**
- The Option was not a signed treaty. It came out of a Berlin conference on **23 June 1939**, with implementing "Richtlinien" dated **21 October 1939**, and declarations were due by **31 December 1939**.
- The official 1940 count gave **69.4% for Germany** of about 267,000 eligible people.
- The famous **86%** is a modern recalculation for today's province, about 246,000 eligible.
- The usual "about 166,000 / about 213,000" figures are the same result counted two ways: heads of household, or including dependants.
- About **75,000** people left by 1943. The Tiroler Landesarchiv puts the official total at 81,500 including men drafted into the Wehrmacht, and civilians at 60,000–70,000.
- Returns after the **D.Lgs. 2 febbraio 1948 n. 23** are estimated very differently, from about 15,000 to 25,000; the Tiroler Landesarchiv puts legal re-options at about 9,000 for 1949–1955.

**The ENTV**
- The ERA (founded **1921**) became the ENTV by **L. 27 novembre 1939 n. 1780**, with the job of taking over and reselling optants' property.
- Totals bought by 1943 and the origin of the planned Italian settlers were **not found** in accessible sources. Di Michele's *Terra italiana* (2023) is the place to look.
- In 1949 the ENTV still administered, among other holdings, 52 farm units of 12–20 ha in the Val d'Adige.

**Files**
- There is now an **online name database: OptionDigital** (Universität Innsbruck). It holds 113,379 Tiroler Landesarchiv resettlement cards and 24,936 re-option cards from the Archivio di Stato di Bolzano, with scans.
- The paper files are at the **Tiroler Landesarchiv** (DUS personal files, DUT property files) and the **Archivio di Stato di Bolzano** ("Domande di opzione", Wertfestsetzung commissions 1939–43, ADERSt fragments).

- **ADERSt.** It ran resettlement "einschließlich des damit verbundenen Vermögenstransfers … mit ihrem Hauptamt in Bozen und Nebenstellen in Bozen, Meran, Brixen, Bruneck und Sterzing", under Himmler as Reichskommissar für die Festigung deutschen Volkstums. **What the cards and files tell a family historian** [VERIFIED-PORTAL from the sample record; SECONDARY from the legend as summarised by the assistant]

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For the studio's "translocal" framing, the Option and the Italian influx are mirror images in the same years. In 1937–1940 about 4,000–7,700 Italians a year arrived in Bolzano comune (Section 2). Meanwhile about 75,000 South Tyroleans left in 1940–43, and the ENTV was set up to hand their property to Italian buyers.
- [INFERENCE] OptionDigital's "Ort" filter can produce a list of Bozen-addressed optant cards. Combined with the Comune's anagrafe, this gives a name-level view of who left the city while the Semirurali filled. That is a feasible studio exercise within the database's teaching-use terms.
- [INFERENCE] The South Tyrolean DUS personal files at the TLA are the functional equivalent of the eastern "EWZ" files. No source confirms that they include photos or ancestry proofs.

## 5. Genealogy portals, tested: Antenati, FamilySearch, the Bolzano archives, military records

### Takeaway
- **Antenati has nothing for Bolzano.** The Archivio di Stato di Bolzano never held stato civile. Civil registration in South Tyrol began only on 1 January 1924 and stays with the Comuni. The Diocese of Bozen-Brixen parish registers to 1923 are online on the Südtiroler Landesarchiv's own portal.
- **Antenati coverage of the origin provinces is very uneven** (registry facets counted on 2026-10-22):
  - **Verona** has 35,290 registers, all Italian stato civile from 1870 to 1946.
  - **Mantova** has 32,970, continuous from 1866 to 1945.
  - **Vicenza** has 17,102, dense from 1871 to 1909 and thin afterwards.
  - **Udine** has 49,184, dense from 1871 to 1900.
  - **Rovigo, Treviso, Venezia and Belluno** are on Antenati almost only for the Napoleonic period, 1806–1815. Rovigo has 690 registers, only 30 of them Italian-period.
  - **Padova** has only Napoleonic and Restoration registers plus Padova city family sheets for 1869–1936.
- **FamilySearch fills the Veneto gap** with "Civil Registration (Tribunale)" image collections: Rovigo 1871–1937, Padova 1871–1929, Venezia 1871–1930 and Treviso 1871–1941. The Archivio di Stato di Rovigo itself points to FamilySearch for 1871–1937.
- **For people *in* Bolzano 1934–1945**, the tools are:
  - the Comune di Bolzano anagrafe's historical certificates;
  - the Comune's stato civile (from 1924);
  - the Archivio di Stato di Bolzano's military fonds (liste di leva classes 1910–1946; ruoli matricolari classes 1877–1950), Questura, Prefettura and labour-office fonds, none online;
  - a handful of digitized name lists.
- **The one genuinely name-searchable online database for 1939–1950s Bolzano residents is OptionDigital**, for German-speaking Optanten and re-optants (Section 4).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Most 1930s immigrants were born roughly 1880–1925. For Veneto births the decisive sources are therefore FamilySearch Tribunale images (Rovigo, Padova, Venezia, Treviso) and Antenati only for Verona, Mantova and partly Vicenza. Telling students "use Antenati" without this caveat will fail for the Polesine.
- [INFERENCE] Liste di leva are compiled by the comune where the conscript's family is resident in his 18th–20th year. Sons of 1937–1940 immigrants born about 1918–1926 are therefore likely in the **Bolzano** liste di leva (ASBZ, classes 1910–1946), not in their birthplace's. Their fathers are in the leva and matricular records of the province of birth.
- [INFERENCE] The Comune di Bolzano historical certificates are the most direct document of the move itself (date of "iscrizione anagrafica" and household composition). They are designed for living persons' administrative needs, so requests for long-dead individuals may need a justified interest.

### Gaps
- **FamilySearch could not be tested directly.** Image access conditions (free login; some restricted images) and exact per-comune coverage for Rovigo are unverified beyond the AS Rovigo statement.
- **Antenati name indexes** ("Cerca i nomi") were not tested. Leva and matricolari for the 1930s are not online for any origin province checked.
- **Bolzano Comune 1930s population registers** (fogli di famiglia, "pratiche migratorie") are not listed among the Archivio Storico's fonds. Their location (still in the Servizi demografici?) is unverified.
- **ENTV fonds.** No fonds of the Ente Nazionale per le Tre Venezie, IACP/IPES or the Commissariato per le migrazioni appears on the ASBZ site.
- **Commissariato records in Rome.** The ACS fonds of the Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione was not located online; Gallo's work implies it exists.

## 6. The second translocal flow: the Polizeiliches Durchgangslager Bozen (1944–45) as a model of name-level tracing

### Takeaway
The Bolzano Lager is the one 1940s Bolzano population that can be traced name by name online:
- the **Comune di Bolzano list, updated April 2025**: 8,294 names, CC BY 4.0;
- **Dario Venegoni's book**: 7,982 entries in the 2nd edition, 2005, free PDF on deportati.it, with birthplace, date, occupation, arrest, matricola, block, transport and fate;
- **Arolsen Archives**: the Bozen prisoner register donated by ANED Milano, 146 documents;
- video testimonies.

Estimates of total prisoners differ: about 9,500 in the Venegoni/Fondazione Memoria della Deportazione figure, about 11,000 in the Comune's.

### Cited Findings
- **Comune di Bolzano list.** "Elenco deportat* nel Lager di Bolzano 1944-45 – Aggiornamento aprile 2025". ### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Venegoni's entries carry birthplace, so filtering for "Arrestato a Bolzano" plus a Veneto birthplace would give a reproducible list of industrial-zone immigrants caught in the 1944 repression. This is a ready-made studio exercise on the translocal biographies of the zone.
- [INFERENCE] The gap between Venegoni's 7,982, the Comune's 8,294 and the totals of 9,500 or 11,000 is itself a finding: name-level reconstruction still leaves an unidentified remainder.

### Gaps
- The Arolsen register images were not reviewed page by page. Whether they carry birth data is unverified.
- The deportati.it site has no live searchable database; only the PDFs were found.

## 7. Deliverable: how to trace a family that came to the Bolzano Semirurali from the Polesine in 1938, plus named individual stories

### Takeaway
The trace has to run **backwards from Bolzano**, because almost nothing about 1938 Bolzano residents is online:
1. Comune di Bolzano historical certificates.
2. Bolzano-based name lists: Questura, Lager, 3 May 1945, IMI/CAR, war dead.
3. Military records: father in Rovigo; sons coming of age in Bolzano at ASBZ.
4. Polesine births and marriages 1871–1937 on **FamilySearch (Rovigo Tribunale)**, not Antenati.
5. 1816–1871 in parish and diocesan (Adria-Rovigo) records.
6. 1806–1815 on Antenati.

Published oral histories already name several Polesine-to-Semirurali migrants: Dall'Aglio (Castelguglielmo), Chieregato (Fratta Polesine) and Bolognini (Giacciano con Baruchella). Two Polesine men from Bolzano were killed on 3 May 1945: Saudo (Badia Polesine) and Giuriola (Costa di Rovigo).

### Cited Findings

**Step-by-step guide.** Each step names the portal and its verified status.

1. **Fix the family in Bolzano.**
   - Request a *certificato storico di residenza* and a *certificato storico di stato di famiglia* from the Comune di Bolzano, Servizi Demografici: form 1.4, 15 days, €16 stamp duty, phone reservation for the office. [VERIFIED-PORTAL] — [Comune di Bolzano](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Servizi/Chiedere-un-certificato-anagrafico)
   - Then request *estratti per riassunto* of any Bolzano births, marriages or deaths (from 1924). [VERIFIED-PORTAL] — [stato civile page](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Servizi/Richiedere-certificati-di-nascita-matrimonio-unione-civile-e-morte)
   - [INFERENCE] The historical certificate should give the date of "iscrizione" and the comune of provenance. That is the anchor for every later step.
2. **Look for the street.** The Semirurali streets were vie Palermo, Milano, Cagliari, Brescia, Genova, Vercelli, Belluno, Mantova, Alessandria and Udine. [VERIFIED-DOC] — [Giacomozzi](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf)
   - Aerial and construction photos are in the Archivio Storico photo fonds and the IFACP album. [VERIFIED-PORTAL]
   - [INFERENCE] The house assignment (IFACP) will be in the IPES/IACP archive, if it survives. Not verified.
3. **Search the Bolzano-side name lists, free online.**
   - The ASBZ Questura "sovversivi" register: 1920s–30s, mostly railwaymen. [VERIFIED-DOC]
   - The Comune's Lager list and Venegoni's book. [VERIFIED-DOC]
   - The Atlante delle stragi scheda for 3 May 1945. [VERIFIED-DOC]
   - The ASBZ IMI/CAR lists, if a son was a soldier interned after 8 September 1943. [VERIFIED-PORTAL]
   - The Difesa war-dead database. [VERIFIED-PORTAL]
4. **Find the oral history.** Check the Biblioteca "Claudia Augusta" oral archive: the summaries are in Urru 2022, pp. with "Riassunto del contenuto". [VERIFIED-DOC] Check the Casa Semirurale narrators list. [VERIFIED-DOC]
5. **Military records of the men.** [VERIFIED-PORTAL, Antenati descriptions]
   - **Fathers** (born about 1880–1915, called up in the Polesine):
     - liste di leva of the Ufficio di leva di Rovigo are at AS Padova (classes 1855–1910 per the Rovigo page, 1855–1944 per the Padova page; check with the archive);
     - Rovigo ruoli matricolari: classes 1851–1900/1909 at AS Padova; ruoli classes 1910–1935 and fogli 1860–1943 at AS Rovigo;
     - the AS Padova online leva database (1846–1902) is under maintenance.
   - **Sons** (born about 1918–1926): [INFERENCE] probably in the **Bolzano** liste di leva (ASBZ, classes 1910–1946) and the Bolzano district ruoli (classes 1877–1950). Not online; reading room only.
6. **Polesine births and marriages, 1871–1937.**
   - Open FamilySearch "Italy, Rovigo, Rovigo, Civil Registration (Tribunale), 1871-1937" (collection 1482864) and browse by comune, register type and year. [SECONDARY for the portal; VERIFIED-DOC via the AS Rovigo guide]
   - For missing years, write to the **Comune of birth**, which keeps the other copy, as AS Rovigo advises. [VERIFIED-DOC] — [AS Rovigo guide](https://archiviodistatorovigo.cultura.gov.it/fileadmin/risorse/Moduli_e_risorse/Risorse/00_NoteRicercaGenealogica.pdf)
   - Do **not** expect Antenati to have these. Rovigo on Antenati is 1806–1815 plus 30 scattered Italian-period registers. [VERIFIED-PORTAL]
7. **Emigration paperwork at the origin.**
   - AS Rovigo holds, but has not put online, for the Comune di Rovigo: "Rubriche delle iscrizioni anagrafiche (1930 ca.-1956)", "Indice decennale migrazione e immigrazione (1901-1929)" and "Censimenti della popolazione (1901-1921)". [VERIFIED-PORTAL, Antenati description]
   - Antenati has online only two "Documenti di emigrazione" registers: Boara Polesine 1887–1896 and Grignano Polesine 1918–1929. [VERIFIED-PORTAL]
   - [INFERENCE] For other Polesine comuni, the 1930s "cancellazioni per emigrazione" are in each comune's own archivio storico.
8. **Before 1871.**
   - 1816 to 31 August 1871: parish registers and the **Archivio della Diocesi di Adria-Rovigo**; certificates from the parish priest or the Curia.
   - 1806–1815: Napoleonic stato civile, free on Antenati (AS Rovigo).
   [VERIFIED-DOC, AS Rovigo guide; VERIFIED-PORTAL, Antenati]
9. **If the family were railwaymen or politically suspect.** Check the ACS Casellario Politico Centrale database (front end only verified) and the ASBZ Questura fonds.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The Cressotti surname appears both among the 3 May 1945 dead (Bortolo, from Brenzone VR) and among the museum narrators (Gemma Cressotti Ottoni). A possible family link is a good test case for the guide, but it is **not** established.
- [INFERENCE] For a Polesine family the most efficient order is: Comune di Bolzano certificate, then FamilySearch Rovigo Tribunale, then the Comune of birth. Military records add biographical texture (occupation, height, literacy, campaigns) once the birth date is known.

### Gaps
- None of the steps were run end to end on a real family, because that would require submitting a certificate request. The certificate's exact content for a 1938 immigration is therefore unverified.
- FamilySearch coverage for specific Polesine comuni (for example Badia Polesine or Castelguglielmo) was not checked image by image.
