# Propaganda in moving image and sound, 1924–1945: Bolzano and Alto Adige in Luce films and photographs, foreign and German-language eyes, feature films and their censorship, the EIAR station, and the cinemas of the city

Research date: 12 November 2026. This thread extends `02_newsreels_radio_mobilization.md` (cited as notes 02), which holds the Luce catalogue tables; it does not repeat them. Downloads, manifest and records: `files/15_propaganda_film_radio/` (rows 15.00–15.25).

Tags: **[V]** read by me on the cited page or in the cited file. **[S]** from a secondary source read in full. **[W]** Wikipedia text. **[SS]** a search-result snippet only, page not opened. **[INF]** my inference. Snippet-only and inferred statements are leads, not facts.

Notes on method: the Luce catalogue pages embed each newsreel as a video of the "Archivio Luce Cinecittà" YouTube channel and name its thumbnail as their preview image; documentaries play in the portal's own player, which serves a poster frame. I held one such frame per film and the full catalogue record as text; no stream was downloaded. Teßmann newspapers were read through the Europeana newspaper API and IIIF images (Public Domain Mark), never through digital.tessmann.it.

## 1. Luce films that can be watched: what they show, what they say, what they leave out

### Takeaway
Twelve Luce films are now held as a frame plus a full record (rows 15.04, 15.09, 15.10, 15.12–15.15, 15.17, 15.19–15.22), each with the address where it plays. Mussolini was at the 1935 manoeuvres, but the record places them in the Val di Non, in the province of Trento. The catalogue records list shots, not the spoken commentary, so what the speakers said is still unheard. Across all of them the German-speaking population appears as crowd, costume and gratitude, never as voice. The Option is absent: the Rione Dux film came out ten days before the Option's implementing rules, and nothing in it hints at them.

### Inferences
- [INF] What is filmed in Bolzano is the arrival of the state: a king, a Duce, princes, a party secretary, ministers, Hitler's train. The camera's subjects are those who come; the residents are the crowd that receives them.
- [INF] The German-speaking population appears three ways only: as a band or costume (1928), as grateful widows and orphans (1938), and as names read out among "Altoatesini caduti" for Spain and Africa (1938). None of these films lets one speak.
- [INF] Conspicuously not shown: the Option and departures (1939–43), the old town as a living German-speaking city, the displaced or the price of the new quarters (the Semirurali appear as gardens and two-storey houses), the camp of 1944–45, and any spoken German.
- [INF] "The 1938 column" of the task is most likely the "colonnina del confine del Brennero" of B1312, the only column of 1938 in the records; if another was meant, it was not found. **Superseded, 14 November 2026:** the column is the Colonna Romana unveiled in the park behind the Monument to Victory on 5 June 1938 (notes 17 §7; its film is B1319). B1312 shows Starace's roll call of the province's dead at the monument on Sunday 22 May 1938 (Dolomiten, 23 May 1938, p. 1); 1 June 1938 is the reel's catalogue date, not the day of the event.

### Gaps
- No catalogue record transcribes spoken commentary, so I could quote no speaker's line; titles in quotation marks (B1599, B1692) are the closest. Watching B1014, B1312, B1368, B1599 and D046109 with notes of the commentary is the next step.
- Which present-day bridge was the "ponte del Littorio" (B1540) and which building the frame shows was not established.
- No Luce film of the 4 October 1940 Brenner meeting was found; Luce photographed it (§2).

## 2. The Luce photographic archive: what the Foto Attualità fund holds

### Takeaway
The 73 "Foto Attualità" hits for "bolzano", with 43 for "brennero" and 30 for "alto adige", come to 144 records in 28 photo stories (row 15.03). Luce photographed the province more often in Rome and Ostia (54 photos) than in Bolzano (21). Its photos of the 4 October 1940 Brenner meeting fill a gap the film catalogue left. Five individual stories are held (rows 15.05, 15.07, 15.16, 15.18, 15.23, 15.25).

### Cited Findings
- Method: catalogue search with the fund filter "Foto Attualità (1927–1956)" (archive name `luceFondoLuceCronologico`), then every record page read for date, code, caption, photographer, place, keywords and the 720-pixel image path — [search](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/search/result.html) [V]. The CSV is `files/15_propaganda_film_radio/luce_foto_attualita_bolzano_brennero_altoadige_index.csv`.
- By place of shot: Roma 40, Ostia 14, Bolzano 21, the Brenner 28 under three spellings ("Stazione del Brennero", "Brennero", "Passo del Brennero"), Firenze 4, Napoli 3 [V, measured]. Seventeen "bolzano" hits are the cruiser *Bolzano* (Naples 1936, Sardinia 1938, with Mussolini aboard after inaugurating Carbonia) [V].
- *S.M. il Re inaugura il monumento ai caduti di Bolzano*, 11 photos, 13.07.1928, author "Istituto Nazionale Luce": Luce calls the Monument to Victory a "monumento ai caduti"; one caption reads "un gruppo di autorità locali (?), tra cui una giovane signora che indossa un elegante abito alla moda, posa in cima al monumento ai caduti, tra le enormi colonne che raffigurano dei fasci littori" — e.g. [A00004247](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL0000026357/12/inquadratura-del-monumento-ai-caduti-e-della-piazza-antistante-dove-sono-schierati-i-soldati.html), row 15.05 [V]
- *Bolzano – Saggio ginnico di Giovani fascisti*, 08.06.1931: "Balilla schierati nello stadio di Bolzano per un saggio ginnico" — [A00030295](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000031194/12/balilla-schierati-nello-stadio-bolzano-saggio-ginnico.html), row 15.07 [V]

- *Brennero – L'incontro del Duce con il Fuerher alla presenza dei ministri degli esteri Conte Ciano e von Ribbentrop*, 04.10.1940, 7 photos in two stories — [A00127044](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL0000014657/12/hitler-e-mussolini-passano-rassegna-truppe.html), row 15.23 [V]. ### Inferences
- [INF] Luce's photography confirms the newsreel pattern of notes 02 and adds a sharper one: the province is more often photographed arriving in Rome than at home. The image of Alto Adige sent to the nation was of its loyalty displayed at the centre.
- [INF] The caption date 13.07.1928 for the inauguration of 12 July is probably a filing date; the same one-day lag may hold for other Luce photo dates.

### Gaps
- The "merano" query gives 63 further photo hits, not crawled.
- Which ground was "lo stadio di Bolzano" in 1931 was not established.
- No photo of the Option, the Semirurali, the Lager or the EIAR station was found under these three words.

## 3. Other eyes: foreign newsreels, German and Austrian film, amateur film, later reuse

### Takeaway
Little could be held. British Pathé and Learning on Screen refused scripts, the Bundesarchiv film portal did not answer, Commons rate-limited, and the provincial OMA portal's search needs a browser. What could be read shows two things: the Austrian newsreel of the Ständestaat years looked at Italy often and at South Tyrol almost never, and German film about the region exists as a resettlement documentary of 1944.

### Cited Findings
- British newsreels of the March 1940 Brenner meeting: *Dictators Meet at Brenner Pass* (Pathé Gazette 40/26, 28 March 1940, 64 feet, held by British Pathé), *First Pictures: Axis Leaders Meet* (British Paramount News, 28 March 1940) and a British Movietone item *What Happened at Brenner Pass* — [Learning on Screen record 97842](https://learningonscreen.ac.uk/newsonscreen/search/index.php/story/97842) [SS]; the page redirects to a login and britishpathe.com returned HTTP 403 [V].
- Austrian newsreel: *Österreich in Bild und Ton* 42A/35 of 18 Oct 1935 has a story "In den Dolomiten", listed as starting at 00:02:10, between the Carinthian plebiscite anniversary (00:00:21) and "Steirischer Loden" (00:03:43); Public Domain Mark on Europeana; the Filmmuseum page needs JavaScript and served no still to a script — [Europeana record](https://www.europeana.eu/item/08621/13766472067491376647206749) [V]. A Europeana query for "Bild und Ton" and Italien returns 154 issues (Mussolini, the king, Badoglio, Italian manoeuvres); the query with Brenner, Bozen, Meran, Südtirol or Dolomiten returns only this one [V].
- German: *Umsiedlung deutschstämmiger Südtiroler Bevölkerung*, Germany 1944, short documentary, 328 m, 12 min, 35mm — [filmportal.de](https://www.filmportal.de/en/node/1455585) [V]. Wikimedia Commons lists Bundesarchiv photographs titled "Innsbruck, Umsiedler aus Südtirol" and "Innsbruck, Zentralstelle Umsiedlung Südtirol" (Bild 137-055690/-055691/-055693/-057385) [V titles only; licences unread because of rate limiting].
- Trenker's *Der Rebell. Die Feuer rufen* (1932): DFF digitised it in 2021 and filmportal shows the first five minutes; DFF's synopsis sets the revolt against "die bayrisch-französische Besatzung Südtirols" — [Europeana / DFF record](https://www.europeana.eu/item/08604/_filmportal_de_node_2001050) [V]; rights "In Copyright".
- Later reuse: the permanent exhibition *BZ '18–'45* in the monument (2014) ends with "una stanza con audiovisivi" — [cultura.gov.it](https://cultura.gov.it/luogo/monumento-alla-vittoria) [SS]; whether it uses Luce footage was not confirmed.
- Modern talks on the subject on Internet Archive (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0): G. Ferrandi, *Fantasmi e stelle alpine. Mussolini e l'Alto Adige* (13 Feb 2015) and *Al Brennero ci siamo e ci resteremo…* (30 Jan 2017) — [archive.org 2015.02.13-ferrandi](https://archive.org/details/2015.02.13-ferrandi), [2017.01.30-ferrandi](https://archive.org/details/2017.01.30-ferrandi) [V, metadata only].

### Inferences
- [INF] Between 1934 and 1936 Austria depended on Mussolini, and its official newsreel showed him often and South Tyrol, on this sample, not at all. That silence is the Austrian counterpart of Luce's silence on the Option.

### Gaps
- No foreign newsreel still or record could be held. British Pathé, Learning on Screen, the Bundesarchiv filmothek and the OMA portal (Amt für Film und Medien, amateur films) need a hand-opened browser (list below).
- No television history of the Option was checked for reused Luce footage.

## 4. Feature films: what reached Italian audiences, and what was kept from Bolzano

### Takeaway
The Italia Taglia censorship database answers the release question for Trenker and adds a finding the earlier notes could not have: two of his German films were approved for Italy but **banned from public screens in the provinces of Bolzano and Trento** — *Il figliuol prodigo* in September 1935 and *L'imperatore della California* in January 1937. *Condottieri* was registered as a national film. The Ministry's own synopsis of *Der Feuerteufel* sets it in Carinthia, which corrects notes 02.

### Cited Findings
- Seven Trenker films are listed: *Cavalieri della montagna* (Der Sohn der weissen Berge, Itala Film Berlin, visto 26066, 31 Oct 1930, approved), *Figliuol prodigo* (Der verlorene Sohn, Rota Film / Deutsche Universal, distributed by Colosseum Film, visto 28980, 31 Aug 1935), *L'imperatore della California* (Der Kaiser von Kalifornien, distributed by E.N.I.C., visto 29418; the database lists it as "Imperatrice (L') di California"), *Condottieri* (visto 29671, June 1937), *La grande conquista* (Der Berg ruft, visto 30355, Oct 1938), *Lettere d'amore dall'Engadina* (visto 30641, May 1939), *Il ribelle della montagna* (Der Feuerteufel, Bavaria Film, visto 31160, Nov 1940) — [Italia Taglia](https://www.italiataglia.it/search/1913_1943), row 15.01 [V]
- *Figliuol prodigo*: "Approvata con riserva … Le proiezioni in pubblico del film 'Il Figliuol Prodigo' della marca Rota film … sono vietate nelle Provincie di Bolzano e Trento. (settembre 1935)" — row 15.01 [V]
- *L'imperatore della California*: "La proiezione di questa pellicola è vietata nelle Provincie di Trento e Bolzano. (gennaio 1937)" — row 15.01 [V]. Trenker received the Mussolini Cup at Venice in 1936 for this film — notes 02, §2 [S there].

- *Condottieri*: "riconosciuta nazionale a termini di legge, è ammessa a godere del beneficio delle proiezioni obbligatorie stabilito dall'art. 8 del R.D.L. 5 ottobre 1933 – XI, n. 1414 e dell'art. 1 della Legge 13 giugno 1935 – XIII, n. 1083" — row 15.01 [V]
- The nulla osta of *Il ribelle della montagna (Der Feuerteufel)*: "Il film racconta la storia di uno spaccalegna della Carinzia, Valentino Sturmegger il quale, durante la tirannia napoleonica sulla Germania, capeggiava una sommossa popolare nella Carinzia" — [visto 31160 PDF](https://www.italiataglia.it/files/visti/31160.pdf), row 15.24 [V]. - Films titled for the region in the database: *Bellezze italiche: da Bolzano a Cortina* (U.C.I., 1921), *Quarta adunata sciatoria Valligiani al Brennero* (1922), the Austrian *Dramma fra le Dolomiti* (Hintner/Cartellieri, 1921, approved with cuts of a nihilist plot), *Dolomiti italiane* (1923), *S.M. il Re a Bolzano* (1926), and *Fra i monti del Tirolo* (In den Bergen von Tirol, Reichsbahnzentrale für den Deutschen Reiseverkehr, 1942) — row 15.01 [V]
- The ministry's nulla osta registers for June 1927 – January 1936 (files no. 23579 onwards) are missing from Italia Taglia — Carla Mereu Keating, "'100% Italian': The Coming of Hollywood Sound Films in 1930s Italy and State Regulation on Dubbing", *California Italian Studies* — [eScholarship](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7f86023v) [S]. This fits the absence of visa scans for the 1930 and 1935 Trenker records [INF].
- *Montagne in fiamme* (Berge in Flammen) and *Der Rebell* were not found by title or by director (Trenker, Hartl) [V, negative].

### Inferences
- [INF] Censorship worked at two scales. Nationally, Trenker's films were welcome; in the two border provinces, those whose heroes are emigrant mountaineers going home (*Der verlorene Sohn*) or a German-Swiss pioneer (*Der Kaiser von Kalifornien*) were kept off public screens. The ban is the clearest documented case of film being managed specifically for the German-speaking audience.
- [INF] *Der Feuerteufel* (a rising against Napoleon in Carinthia, 1940) was approved without a provincial ban, perhaps because Carinthia was not South Tyrol, perhaps because the Option was already under way.

### Gaps
- The reasons for the two bans are not in the records; the ministry files (ACS, MinCulPop) would hold them.
- No poster or still of a Trenker film under an open licence was found; filmportal's still of *Der Rebell* is "In Copyright".
- Italian films shot in Alto Adige 1930–43 were not identified beyond the titles above.

## 5. Radio and sound: the EIAR station, its reach, and the speeches

### Takeaway
The station is confirmed as opened on 12 July 1928, and its pictures are already in the base (row 2.03, Radiorario 1928 no. 30, PDF pp. 5–6: a full-page "Veduta panoramica della stazione di Bolzano" with two lattice towers, "L'edificio della stazione di Bolzano" and a plan of building and aerial). New: within weeks, listeners in Cagliari, Pisa, Forlì and Portogruaro wrote that they heard Bolzano at night (row 15.06); a 1937–38 Luce/EIAR film lists "la stazione di Bolzano" among the stations it shows (row 15.14). The exact site in Gries, the 1943–45 history and any German-language slot were not found. The 1926 Chamber speech on the Brenner is held as text (row 15.02); no recording exists in what was searched.

### Cited Findings
- 12 July 1928 start: "Su richiesta del Governo abbiamo nel brevissimo spazio di tempo di poco più di un mese impiantata la stazione di Bolzano, che ha cominciato a funzionare il 12 luglio" — row 2.05, notes 02, §3 [V there]. Site: "in località Gries a circa mezzo chilometro fuori dell'abitato di Bolzano" — row 2.03 [V there].
- *Radiorario* no. 37 (9–16 Sept 1928), printed page 4, "Ricezioni della Stazione di Bolzano": Cagliari, 30 Aug: "ho captato l'onda di Bolzano percependo sufficientemente bene una dizione «sulla stretta della mano» un ballabile, le notizie Stefani"; Forlì, 2 Sept: "il giornale dell'Enit dalla Stazione di Bolzano. Forte e chiaro in altoparlante ma disturbato da un sibilo continuo dovuto ad interferenza della stazione di Berna"; Pisa (Calci): "ora udiamo Bolzano (musica, comunicazioni e notizie) tutte le sere" — [worldradiohistory](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/20s/1928/RC-1928-37.pdf), row 15.06 [V]
- 1929 no. 29, PDF page 3: "La piccola stazione di Bolzano infine è di tutte le stazioni italiane quella maggiormente danneggiata dalle interferenze in quanto che, trasmettendo sopra un'onda comune, ha le sue emissioni sommerse nel groviglio di battimenti creati dalle trasmissioni di [10?] stazioni di piccola potenza" — [worldradiohistory](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/20s/1929/RC-1929-29.pdf) [V; the number is unclear in the text layer]
- *Radio italiana anno XVI [EIAR]* (D032902, 1937–38): after "gli impianti di Firenze" and "la stazione radiofonica di Bologna: vedute dell'edificio" comes "la stazione di Bolzano"; later "annunciatori radiofonici al lavoro parlano in inglese e in tedesco" — [catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000083387/1/eiar.html), row 15.14 [V]. The poster frame shows the microphone engraved with six dates, from "IL DUCE: 2 OTT. 1935-XIII" to "IL DUCE: 30 AGOSTO 1936-XIV", among them "S.M. LA REGINA: 18 DIC. 1935-XIV" [V, image], the Giornata della Fede when "la prima a donare la propria fede … fu la regina Elena" — [it.wikipedia, Oro alla Patria](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oro_alla_Patria) [W].
- Language policy: Alpenzeitung full-text searches for 1939–43 ("in deutscher Sprache" with Rundfunk, "deutsche Sendungen", "Sender Bolzano", "Radio Bolzano") found no German-language programme from Bolzano; the paper prints other stations' German news (e.g. Slovak "Deutsche Sendung", 21 Sept 1941) [V, negative] — [Europeana newspapers API](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059910006). Under RAI the German service "vom alten Funkhaus in der Bozner Sparkassenstraße" grew from 1945, and Rai Bozen broadcast from Mazziniplatz from 1960 [SS, search summaries of de/en Wikipedia].
- Mussolini, Chamber of Deputies, 6 Feb 1926 (Atti Parlamentari, pp. 5401–5404): "Veramente del confine del Brennero si può dire che è un confine segnato dalla mano infallibile di Dio"; "I tedeschi nell'Alto Adige non rappresentano una minoranza nazionale, rappresentano una reliquia etnica"; "L'Italia fascista può, se sarà necessario, portare oltre il suo tricolore, abbassarlo mai!", after which the record notes "Si canta l'inno Giovinezza"; and the announcement that in Bolzano, on the foundations of a planned German war memorial, "erigeremo un monumento a Cesare Battisti", which Farinacci turns into a call for a subscription — [storia.camera.it, sed135](https://storia.camera.it/regno/lavori/leg27/sed135.pdf), row 15.02 [V]
- Recordings: the Discoteca di Stato issued "a series of 12 records of speeches delivered by Benito Mussolini in public or before Parliament between October 1935 and May 1936" (DS.002–DS.013) — [REDIRE, Univ. Bonn](https://redire.uni-bonn.de/items/demo_0739.html) [SS]. Internet Archive audio searches for Brennero, Bolzano, "Alto Adige", Trenker and Tirolo found no period recording under an open licence [V, negative].

### Inferences
- [INF] Radio carried Bolzano outward more than notes 02 allowed: a 200-watt station shared a common wave and was drowned by interference, but at night its name and the Enit tourist news reached Sardinia and Tuscany, and listeners wrote in to say so. The station did for the ear what the monument did for the eye: it put Bolzano on the national map.
- [INF] The six dates on the microphone of 1937–38 match the start of the Discoteca di Stato's Mussolini series (October 1935). The 1926 Brenner speech predates both, so it most likely survives only in print.

### Gaps
- The exact site of the 1928 transmitter in Gries and its OpenStreetMap object were not found; no place is proposed.
- What the station did in 1943–45 (Operationszone Alpenvorland) is unknown.
- Whether Radiocorriere listed a German-language slot for Bolzano in 1939–43 was not checked page by page.

## 6. Cinema as an instrument in the city and the valleys

### Takeaway
Film reached the German-speaking valleys from 1927 by travelling cinema: the Opera Nazionale Combattenti's Wanderkino for "landwirtschaftliche und patriotische Films" (1927), Luce's own Wanderkino with the agricultural lecturers through 60 villages (1934, row 15.08), and the fascist farmers' unions' Autokino (1939, 1941). In Bolzano the Edenkino renamed itself "Cinema Luce" on 27 November 1935, and schoolchildren were taken to the Ethiopian-war documentary *Von Adua nach Aksum* (row 15.11). From 1930 a national rule silenced every foreign-language word on Italian screens.

### Cited Findings
- 15 June 1927: "Das Wanderkino für die landwirtschaftliche Propaganda": through the initiative of "On. Angelo Manaresi" the Venezia Tridentina received a Wanderkino of the Opera Nazionale Combattenti; from 15 June to autumn "ganz vorzügliche landwirtschaftliche und patriotische Films", also for "die kleineren Landgemeinden und Bergdörfer" — Alpenzeitung, Europeana page 3 of 6, [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059045013) [V]
- 23 June 1929: a prefectural commissioner (his commune is not named in the OCR I read) announces ONC Wanderkino shows "welche besonders für unsere Landwirte Interesse haben werden", films that "die Tätigkeit der nationalen Regierung … illustrieren", in the open air on the main square on 11 July — Alpenzeitung, Europeana page 5 of 8, [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059044609) [V]. 20 May 1930: "das Wanderkino des Dopolavoro" at Rio di Pusteria — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059045931) [V, OCR snippet].
- 14 June 1934: "Bereits Ende Mai hat die Kinounternehmung 'Luce' eine Rundfahrt durch unsere Provinz unternommen …", teaching films plus "die letzten Weltereignisse", 60 stops from Vandoies to Vadena, Bolzano on 24 June and Gries on 25 June — Alpenzeitung, p. 5, [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046188), row 15.08 [V]. The same page carries "Deutscher Sprachunterricht – Die Errichtung von Privatschulen für den Unterricht der deutschen Sprache" [V].
- 1 July 1939: the Autokino of the fascist provincial union of agricultural workers shows "technisch-agrarische und technisch-soziale Films" at Mezzaselva, Campo di Trens and Vipiteno, 6–8 July — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043427) [V]. 2 Oct 1941: "Das Autolichtspieltheater des fasc. Verbandes der Landwirte gab in der Zone von Merano, Lana und Postal am 27. 28. und 29. September drei Propagandavorstellungen, welchen insgesamt rund 3000 Landwirte beiwohnten" — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059044827), p. 2 [V; the OCR reads "3999", the image "3000"].
- Cinemas: the Alpenzeitung lists the "Edenkino" until 26 Nov 1935 and "Cinema Luce (ex Edenkino)" from 27 Nov 1935, beside the "Central-Kino" — [Europeana, 27 Nov 1935](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046292), row 15.11 [V]. The sanctions, approved on 3 Nov 1935, were to enter into force on 18 Nov — [it.wikipedia, Oro alla Patria](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oro_alla_Patria) [W]. A Cinema Treasures comment gives the reason as "Mussolini's political stance, which opposed that of British Prime Minister Antony Eden" — [cinematreasures, Cinema Eden](https://cinematreasures.org/theaters/54492/comments) [S, unsourced user comment]; Eden was not prime minister but the minister for League of Nations affairs, Foreign Secretary from 22 Dec 1935 — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Eden) [W]. Later listings name "Luce-Kino" (1939, 1941) — Alpenzeitung OCR in `files/03_press_print/` [V].
- The same comment says: sound installed at the Eden in November 1930; Cines sound newsreels added in 1932; "In July 1933, the 50% reduced admission prices imposed by the Fascist regime went into effect"; and earlier, "Following the introduction of a ban on screening many films in German, Bolzano's cinemas equipped themselves with an ingenious system to project German intertitles simultaneously with the Italian intertitles" — [cinematreasures](https://cinematreasures.org/theaters/54492/comments) [S, no source given].
- 27 Nov 1935: "Schülervorstellung im Centralkino. Vorgestern Nachmittag haben sich die Schüler der Kgl. Handelsschule und des Handelsfortbildungskurses in Begleitung ihrer Lehrpersonen im Centralkino einer Vorführung des prächtigen Dokumentarfilms 'Von Adua nach Aksum' beigewohnt" — row 15.11 [V]
- National language rule: on 22 Oct 1930 the Ministry of the Interior ordered that "non venga accordato il nulla osta alla rappresentazione di pellicole cinematografiche che contengano del parlato in lingua straniera sia pure in qualche parte e in misura minima"; between Nov 1929 and Aug 1933, 486 foreign sound productions were released silenced — Mereu Keating, [eScholarship](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7f86023v) [S]
- Ads in the German-language paper give German titles both for German films ("Prinzessin Turandot", "Gruß und Kuß, Veronika") and for Italian ones ("Der Dreispitzhut" with the De Filippo brothers) — row 15.11 and AZ Nov 1935 [V]; in which language each was heard is not stated.
- Already in the base: Cinema Roma (Via dei Cappuccini 30), Central-Kino, Cine Luce, Kino Littorio in the Rione Dux (July 1941, row 3.41), screenings at the GIL house at Ponte Druso (1938) — notes 03, §1B [V there]; the Cinema Druso inside the GIL building — notes 05, §2 [V there].
- For the Cinema Corso / Palazzo del Turismo thread: nothing new found here; the Alpenzeitung has no "Cinema Corso" or "Kino Corso" (notes 03).

### Inferences
- [INF] In the valleys, film arrived as instruction: agriculture first, then "patriotische Films" and world news, explained by an Italian expert. The cinema van was the reach of the state into villages where the newspaper and the radio did not go.
- [INF] The Eden-to-Luce renaming is a change of a single word on a façade that carried the whole sanctions campaign: a private cinema with an English-sounding name took the name of the regime's own film institute. It sits beside the provincial bans of Trenker films as the city-level face of film policy.

### Gaps
- Whether German-language sound films were shown in Bolzano after 1930, dubbed or not, was not settled; the cinema ads and the prefecture's *Fogli Annunzi Legali* (on Teßmann) are the next sources.
- No Dopolavoro hall programme for Bolzano was found.
- The Cinema Treasures history has no named source; the Eden's owners or the Filmclub may hold the original.
