## 1. Ponte Druso / Drususbrücke: construction, authorship, naming, eagles, removal, fate, images

> **Added on 12 November 2026.** A before-and-after pair of the bridge, with and without the eagle columns, was printed in *Tandem*, January 1985 (row 5.33; `13_cinema_corso.md`, section 12). In the provincial council on 6 February 1990 an MSI councillor called the removal "una distruzione inopinata che non ha avuto nessuna motivazione poi con l'allargamento della strada come si voleva contrabbandare a quel tempo": road widening was the reason given at the time. That is his claim, and a lead for the year and the decision.

### Takeaway
Ponte Druso was built in 1930–31 for the state road authority (A.A.S.S.) to a design by engineers Eugenio Miozzi and Gilberto Ricci. It opened on 28 October 1931, the ninth anniversary of the March on Rome. Its four central piers carried more than 8 m of sculpture: fasces holding globes, topped by four giant Roman eagles by the Ancona sculptor Vittorio Morelli. The eagles were **not** removed in 1945. They came down during the bridge's renovation in the **1970s**. The exact year is uncertain: 1972 is the most specific claim, and the press dates it to the "early 1970s". In the 1990s the bridge was widened and given a new parapet in imitation of the old one. The eagles were dumped in the Museo Civico courtyard, moved to the municipal Giardineria in 2004, and on 6 December 2013 the two best-preserved heads were moved into the crypt of the Victory Monument for the new documentation centre.

### Cited Findings
**Construction, engineers, dimensions**
- Work began in March 1930. The client was the A.A.S.S. (Azienda Autonoma Statale della Strada), and chief engineer Eugenio "Mozzi" (sic) was responsible for the design. The bridge opened to traffic on 28 October 1931, the 9th anniversary of the March on Rome. It linked the new residential districts of viale Venezia and via Roma with the station and the centre. [V] — [Comune di Bolzano / Martha Verdorfer, *Bolzano. Percorso tra architettura e fascismo*, 2007 (PDF, downloaded)](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)
- de.wikipedia gives construction as 1929–1931 by the state road administration, "nach Plänen des Architekten Eugenio Miozzi und von Gilberto Ricci". It describes a three-arch reinforced-concrete bridge with stone cladding, **102 m long and 35 m wide** (citing *Alpenzeitung*, 26 Sept 1933, p. 3, "Zwei Jahre Drususbrücke", and K. T. Hoeniger, *Altbozner Bilderbuch*, 3rd ed. 1968, p. 274). The start year conflicts with Verdorfer's March 1930. [W] — [de.wikipedia: Drususbrücke (Bozen)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen))
- The engineers were Eugenio Miozzi, head of the state-roads division for the provinces of Belluno, Bolzano and Trento, and Gilberto Ricci. The bridge has three arches in reinforced concrete faced with **rose porphyry**. It uses "systematic cracks" controlled by compensating joints, a technique Miozzi later reused in Venice. [V, abstract only] — [M. P. Gatti, G. Cacciaguerra, "The story of the transformation of a structure: from arch bridge to…", *ARCH'07, 5th Int. Conf. on Arch Bridges*, Guimarães 2007, IRIS UniTN record](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/77986)
- On 1 March 1931 Miozzi moved to Venice as head of the municipal public works. There he built the Ponte del Littorio (now Ponte della Libertà), the Piazzale Roma garage, and the Scalzi and Accademia bridges. **Translocal note:** the same engineer links Bolzano's "Roman" bridge to Venice's fascist-era causeway. [W] — [it.wikipedia: Eugenio Miozzi](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Miozzi)
- A period presentation portfolio, *Progetto Ponte Druso, Bolzano*, "Omaggio" of the Sezione autonoma del Regio Genio Civile di Bolzano, credited to Miozzi and Ricci (ca. 1930–31), is on sale at an antiquarian bookseller. It contains 4 reproduced drawings showing the eagles on fasces bases. [V, listing] — [Libreria Grandangolo, Modena](https://www.libreriagrandangolo.it/index.php/shop/storialocale2/progettopontedrusobolzanomiozziriccigeniocivilecartellaingegneria8656-detail)

**Inauguration and the press**
- The inauguration on 28 Oct 1931 is documented in the fascist German-language *Alpenzeitung* of 29 Oct 1931, p. 3 (Teßmann scan). [W — I could not open the scan: digital.tessmann.it now shows a JavaScript bot challenge] — [Alpenzeitung 29.10.1931, p. 3](https://digital.tessmann.it/tessmannDigital/Zeitungsarchiv/Seite/Zeitung/26/1/29.10.1931/109184/3); follow-up [Alpenzeitung 26.09.1933, p. 3 "Zwei Jahre Drususbrücke"](https://digital.tessmann.it/tessmannDigital/Zeitungsarchiv/Seite/Zeitung/26/1/26.09.1933/110069/3) (both cited in [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen)))
- The Stadio Druso was inaugurated in 1931 together with the bridge, and its name likewise refers to Drusus, who conquered the Bolzano area in 15 BC. Verdorfer calls this "uno dei molti esempi di strumentalizzazione fascista della storia romana". [V] — [Verdorfer 2007 PDF](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf). en.wikipedia instead says the stadium opened in 1930. [S] — [en.wikipedia: Stadio Druso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Druso)

**Naming and the "Pons Drusi" Romanità narrative**
- The name refers programmatically to Drusus and his Alpine campaign. By invoking the ancient road station *Pons Drusi*, located somewhere in the Bolzano basin, the regime sought to legitimise the Italianisation of South Tyrol. [W] — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen)), citing E. Migliario & H. Obermair, "Roma sulle sponde del Talvera", in Migliario/Santucci (eds), *«Noi figli di Roma». Fascismo e mito della romanità*, Mondadori/Le Monnier 2022, pp. 135–159 (esp. 138–140)
- Fascist propaganda claimed that Bozen had been founded by Drusus and that the area south of the Brenner was only later settled by Germanic "barbarians". Ettore Tolomei demanded that the Walther von der Vogelweide statue be replaced by a **Drusus monument**. Mussolini refused, citing foreign-policy costs, and in 1926 Piazza Walther was only renamed "Piazza Vittorio Emanuele III". [V] — [H. Dunajtschik & G. Steinacher, "Die Architektur für ein italienisches Südtirol 1922–1943", *Geschichte und Region/Storia e regione* 17 (2008) 1, pp. 101–137 (PDF, downloaded)](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)
- The narrative persists in official tourism copy: the Azienda di Soggiorno writes that Bolzano "nasce probabilmente come statio militare romana nel 15 a.C. sotto il nome di 'Pons Drusi'". [V] — [bolzano-bozen.it, "Storia e cultura"](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/storia-e-cultura)

**Iconography: eagles and other decoration**
- The four central piers carried sculptures more than 8 m high: fasces holding globes, on which "troneggiavano gigantesche aquile romane". The end blocks carried imperial shields resting on four helmets and the sides were decorated with Roman swords. There were also Savoy crosses, the arms of the city and of the Azienda della Strada, inscriptions and religious images, which made the bridge "un impressionante strumento di rappresentazione del potere". [V] — [Verdorfer 2007 PDF](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)
- There were **four** "aquile imperiali romane", sculpted in 1931 by **Vittorio Morelli**, "artista anconetano". [V] — [Alto Adige, V. Frangipane, "Le aquile di ponte Druso nel museo del Monumento", 4 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-nel-museo-del-monumento-1.508168); [Alto Adige, V. Frangipane, "Ponte Druso, 40 anni senza aquile", 5 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/ponte-druso-40-anni-senza-aquile-1.300394)
- de.wikipedia also gives four larger-than-life eagles on globes and fasces by Vittorio Morelli of Ancona. [W] — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen))
- **Material:** the statues and parapet were stone. [V, abstract] — [Gatti & Cacciaguerra 2007](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/77986). A blog says the eagles were "Scolpite nel porfido" and stood at "i quattro angoli del ponte" [S/V-blog, unsourced] — [newspastworld, 23 Sept 2016](https://newspastworld.wordpress.com/2016/09/23/dove-sono-finite-le-aquile-imperiali-del-ponte-druso/). This conflicts with Verdorfer, who places them on the "quattro pilastri centrali".
- **Sculptor:** Vittorio Morelli (Ancona 1886–1968) was a leading Ancona sculptor; his best-known work is the 1954 Pinocchio statue in Ancona. [W] — [it.wikipedia: Vittorio Morelli](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Morelli)
- **Conflicting attribution:** an image caption in it.wikipedia credits the bridge's fascist symbolism to **Alcide Ticò**, not Morelli. [W] — [it.wikipedia: Eugenio Miozzi](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Miozzi). Ticò did make the Justitia pediment relief on the Palazzo di Giustizia [W] — [de.wikipedia: Justizpalast (Bozen)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justizpalast_(Bozen)), so the caption may be a mix-up or may refer to the minor decoration.
- Mario Gabinio photographed the bridge in 1932–35. One of his catalogue captions reads "Particolare Di Una Testata Con Ara Votiva Recante La Data Mxmxv", so the bridge-heads carried a votive altar with a date inscription. "Mxmxv" is the catalogue's transcription and looks like a garbled Roman numeral; read the photo itself. [V, Commons metadata] — [Commons: Gabinio 116B24](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gabinio.Bolzano,_Ponte_Druso_-Ponte_In_Pietra_A_Tre_Arcate_Con_Gruppi_Scultorei_Raffiguranti_Aquile_Poggiate_Su_Fasci_Litt_116B24.jpg)

**Removal: when and why (candidate answers)**
- Candidate A, **1972**: "vennero rimosse nel 1972". [V, unsourced blog] — [newspastworld 2016](https://newspastworld.wordpress.com/2016/09/23/dove-sono-finite-le-aquile-imperiali-del-ponte-druso/)
- Candidate B, **early 1970s**: they disappeared after the bridge's "lavatura" in the early 1970s, "quando ponte Druso venne ristrutturato". They never came back because they were "Troppo grandi, troppo fasciste e quindi ingombranti". The headline "40 anni senza aquile" (Sept 2013) implies roughly 1973. [V] — [Alto Adige, 5 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/ponte-druso-40-anni-senza-aquile-1.300394)
- Candidate C, **mid-1970s**: "Alla metà degli anni Settanta del Novecento il ponte fu radicalmente ristrutturato e furono eliminate tutte le parti con riferimenti ideologici." [V] — [Verdorfer 2007 PDF](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)
- Candidate D, **around 1970, during road widening**: "um 1970 im Rahmen einer Straßenverbreiterung entfernt". [W] — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen))
- Engineering account: a 1970s "stylistic washing" renovation changed both design and structural concept and removed the stone parapet and Morelli's statues. Public criticism followed, and in the **1990s** a widening required a new project with a new parapet and ornamental elements resembling the originals. [V, abstract] — [Gatti & Cacciaguerra 2007](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/77986)
- Generic dating: "Le sculture sono state rimosse solo negli anni settanta" / "Erst in den 70er Jahren". [V] — [suedtirol.live IT](https://suedtirol.live/it/attrazione/ponte-druso-bolzano-p8452); [suedtirol.live DE](https://suedtirol.live/de/sehenswuerdigkeit/drususbrucke-bozen-p8452)

**What happened to the eagles**
- After the early-1970s works they were left for years in the inner courtyard of the **Museo Civico** (via Cassa di Risparmio). Museum reconstruction work in **2004** forced their move to the **Giardineria comunale** at the end of via Rio Molino, where they lay "decapitate e mutilate", piled up outdoors. In Sept 2013, PdL commissioner Alessandro Bertoldi staged a press conference on their neglect. Mayor Luigi Spagnolli promised they "Saranno sistemate e messe sotto il Monumento". Archaeologist Umberto Tecchiati (former president of Italia Nostra) had long asked for them to be shown with context. [V] — [Alto Adige 4 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-nel-museo-del-monumento-1.508168); [Alto Adige 5 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/ponte-druso-40-anni-senza-aquile-1.300394)
- On **6 December 2013, at 8 a.m.**, a crane truck took the two best-preserved busts ("Solo due teste, con le ali") from the Giardineria to the crypt of the Victory Monument, under the supervision of **Hannes Obermair** (director of the Archivio Storico) with Uli Prugger (Gruppe Gut). They were only lightly brushed, "Muschio compreso", so visitors could see they were originals. Pedestals, claw blocks and other fragments were not to be displayed, and some pieces are missing. [V] — [Alto Adige, D. Pasquali, 7 Dec 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-in-volo-fino-alla-cripta-del-monumento-alla-vittoria-1.372917); [Alto Adige, 10 Dec 2013 (photo gallery by D. Pasquali)](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/il-volo-delle-aquile-di-ponte-druso-1.436623)
- A 2016 blog says the two heads were on display in the documentation centre from 2014 and were "the only original period objects" there. [S/blog] — [newspastworld 2016](https://newspastworld.wordpress.com/2016/09/23/dove-sono-finite-le-aquile-imperiali-del-ponte-druso/)

### Inferences
- The 1970s "lavatura" was probably an ANAS job (ANAS succeeded the A.A.S.S., and an "Anas magazzino" still stands on viale Druso), with ideology entering through the decision **not to put the eagles back** rather than as the stated reason for the works. This is my inference: no council or ANAS decision has been found.
- The sources disagree on placement (four corners vs four central piers). The Gabinio photos are the best way to settle it visually.
- "Imperial Roman" and "fascist" iconography are not alternatives here. The legionary eagle on the globe and fasces is the regime's standard fusion of the two, and the bridge-head shields, helmets and swords reinforce the Roman reading.

### Gaps
- No primary decision (ANAS, Comune or Provincia) for the 1970s removal was found, and the exact year is unresolved (1972, early 1970s, ~1970 or mid-1970s). Next step: the Alto Adige and Dolomiten archives for 1971–76; Teßmann carries Dolomiten.
- I could not read the 1931 *Alpenzeitung* inauguration report because Teßmann blocks automated access, and the Chrome extension was not connected. Open the URLs above by hand. Also check *La Provincia di Bolzano* (Oct 1931) and *Dolomiten* 29–31 Oct 1931.
- I found no Istituto Luce newsreel of the inauguration. Search archivioluce.com for "Bolzano 1931" / "Ponte Druso".
- I could not verify whether the two eagle heads are still on display in 2026: the 2025 visitor guide of BZ '18–'45 does not mention them ([V] — [2025 Guida ITA PDF](https://www.monumentoallavittoria.com/fileadmin/user_upload/temp/2025_Guida_MAV_ITA.pdf)). Where the other two eagles are is unknown after 2013. **Settled, 14 November 2026:** both heads are on show in the crypt, with the label "Vittorio Morelli, Eagles heads, stone sculptures originally installed along the Druso Bridge, 1931"; photographed that day (notes 17 §1).
- I found no source showing that Tolomei personally chose the name "Ponte Druso". The link to Tolomei rests on the general Drusus myth.
- The text of the inscriptions (votive altar date, other inscriptions) was not found.

## 2. The cinema "on Corso Italia": identification, history, demolition

> **Extended on 12 November 2026.** The Cinema Corso now has its own thread, `13_cinema_corso.md` (rows 13.00–13.26): the 1938 design and model, photographs of 1940, about 1951 and about 1984, the 1940 art show, the Option commissariat of 1941, the German-occupation years, and the demolition as recorded in the provincial council's minutes (permit 15 February 1988; standing on 30 March 1988; demolished by August 1989; developer named). It corrects this section on four points: the "1987" is not the Corso's; the 1942 exhibition was not held there; theatre use began in spring 1944, not December 1944; and "Pietro Tosolini" is confirmed.

### Takeaway
Three lost cinemas fit, and the evidence favours the first.
1. **Cinema Concordia**, on Piazza Cristo Re just off **Corso Italia**. It was the Dominicans' parish cinema-theatre, which grew from the "Sala Veritas" of the Cristo Re parish. Its last screening was around 2005, and it was **demolished in August 2018** and replaced by the "Casa Concordia" condominium. This is the best match for "cinema di corso Italia, now completely destroyed".
2. **Cinema Corso**, the former Palazzo del Turismo by Armando Ronca (designed 1938, built 1938–40, cinema from 1946, 801 seats). It stood at Corso Libertà / via Virgilio, about 150 m from where Corso Italia begins at Piazza Mazzini, and was **demolished in 1988**. This is the architecturally significant fascist-era building, and its name alone could explain the student's "corso".
3. **Cinema Druso**, on viale Druso at the southern end of Corso Italia. It was part of the GIL building and was partly preserved inside today's Eurac.

### Cited Findings
**A. Cinema Concordia (Piazza Cristo Re / Corso Italia): best match for "corso Italia, completely destroyed"**
- Maurizio Ferrandi's 1962 snapshot of Bolzano cinemas lists "Concordia, Corso Italia", playing "Exodus". It was "probably the best known and longest-lasting parish cinema in Bolzano" and hosted Teatro Stabile shows when the Gries theatre was unavailable. It was closed by its owners, the Dominican friars, for a building project that later collapsed, then stood empty "chiuso da anni", and "Ora viene demolito per l'ennesimo progetto della cosiddetta riqualificazione urbanistica". [V] — [salto.bz, M. Ferrandi, "Andavamo al cinema" (Avvenne domani), 18 Aug 2018](https://salto.bz/en/article/16082018/andavamo-al-cinema)
- The demolition was completed on **14 August 2018**, by the article's own "ieri", on **Piazza Cristo Re**, opposite Piazza Tribunale, near Corso Italia. The last screening was 13 years earlier (≈2005). The Dominicans' first plan (a 13,000 m³ building beside the church) was annulled by the regional administrative court (TAR) in **2012** after an appeal by the neighbouring condominium. The project passed to Confcooperative and then **Cooperdolomiti**. The replacement is "Casa Concordia": 22 flats, 40 garages, ground floor plus five storeys, CasaClima A, by the studio **Area (Roberto Pauro, Andrea Fregoni)**, winner of a competition among eight studios. The coop also paid for the redesign of Piazza Cristo Re. Andrea Grata (Cooperdolomiti) is quoted. No heritage debate is reported. [V] — [Alto Adige, "Demolito l'ex «Cinema Concordia»", 15 Aug 2018](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/demolito-l-ex-cinema-concordia-1.1713147)
- Origins: in 1945 the actress Sandra Zizzola recalled the curtain "della Sala Veritas, poi cinema teatro Concordia" in the Cristo Re parish rooms. In 1945–46 workers' amateur drama groups performed there: "Aurora" of the Lancia, "Minerva", and the "Magnesio" group. [V] — [Massimo Bertoldi, "Macerie e passione", in *Dalla liberazione alla ricostruzione. Alto Adige/Südtirol 1945–1948*, Raetia 2013, republished on carloromeo.it, 4 Sept 2024](https://www.carloromeo.it/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=343%3Ail-teatro-della-ricostruzione&catid=10&Itemid=108)
- The Cristo Re church (Guido Pelizzari and Luigi Colombo, 1938–40; Dominicans present from 1938; church opened 24 Dec 1939) forms the fascist "triade" with the Casa Littoria and the courthouse. [W] — [it.wikipedia: Chiesa di Cristo Re (Bolzano)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesa_di_Cristo_Re_(Bolzano)); [V] — [domenicani.it, Casa di Cristo Re](https://www.domenicani.it/monasteri/casa-di-cristo-re-a-bolzano/). A 2013/14 UniTN thesis covers "Il complesso parrocchiale di Cristo Re a Bolzano (1938–1943), un centro per giovani e anziani" (A. Pintimalli, supervisor M. P. Gatti). [V, record only] — [UniTN](https://webapps.unitn.it/Biblioteca/en/Web/TesiDocente/193501)
- The new Piazza Cristo Re followed the "chiusura e abbattimento del cinema Concordia". Architect Roberto Pauro won a competition among six entrants. The design has a new sagrato, porphyry paving and 16 parking spaces, with works by Arredourbano to start 7 June (2021). [V] — [altoadigeinnovazione.it, 30 Apr 2021](https://www.altoadigeinnovazione.it/bolzano-ecco-la-nuova-piazza-cristo-re/)

**B. Cinema Corso, ex Palazzo del Turismo (Corso Libertà / via Virgilio, next to Piazza Mazzini)**
- **Architect Armando Ronca, design 1938.** The building was to house the tourist office (Azienda di Soggiorno), a reception hall and a concert hall, a representative office for the Duca d'Aosta and the ACI. The building commission mocked the slender columns of the via Virgilio front as "fiammiferi", so Ronca added two buttresses. War delayed completion. From December 1944 the concert hall served as a theatre, because the Teatro Verdi in Piazza Verdi had been bombed, and the Palazzo itself was later hit by bombs. It officially became the Azienda di Soggiorno headquarters in April 1946. In 1946 the concert and reception halls were merged: "fu l'inizio della storia del Cinema Corso, che soprattutto negli anni che vanno dal 1960 al 1980 divenne uno dei luoghi di ritrovo più rinomati della città. Nel 1988 l'edificio fu demolito". The site now hosts the Provincial Assessorato alla Sanità e Politiche Sociali. "La demolizione dell'edificio nel 1988 fu deplorata da molti cittadini." [V] — [Verdorfer 2007 PDF](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)
- **Capacity and stage:** 801 seats (730 in the stalls, 71 in 14 boxes on two tiers); stage 16 × 10 × 8.20 m, proscenium 9 × 6.40 m. The hall hosted the Biennale d'Arte della Venezia Tridentina in 1940 and 1942. It was leased for theatre use from 1 Dec 1944 to 31 Dec 1945, and SIRC of Verona then ran it for 16 years with 20 days a year reserved for theatre. Productions included Baseggio (1947), Marika Rökk (1947), and Toti Dal Monte and Elsa Merlini (1948). On **14 Dec 1948 the Innsbruck Exl-Bühne played there**, the first postwar German-language performances, "con significato politico e culturale". [V] — [Bertoldi 2013 / carloromeo.it 2024](https://www.carloromeo.it/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=343%3Ail-teatro-della-ricostruzione&catid=10&Itemid=108)
- "Un piccolo gioiello architettonico perfettamente inserito nell'architettura della strada", with a columned façade, marble staircase, stalls and several tiers of boxes. **C. Cinema (teatro) Druso, viale Druso (southern end of Corso Italia, by Ponte Druso)**

### Inferences
- If "now completely destroyed" refers to something the student saw as an empty site or new build *on Corso Italia*, it is the **Concordia** (2018). If it refers to the lost fascist-era architecture of "the Corso", it is the **Cinema Corso** (1988).
- The Concordia's construction date is unproven. The Sala Veritas existed in 1945 inside the 1938–43 parish complex, so it was probably built with the complex, but I found no direct evidence. That makes the Concordia only possibly a fascist-era structure.
- Of the three, the Cinema Corso has the strongest fascist-period content. It was a regime tourism and representation building, its design was censored by the building commission, it was bombed and then re-used, and it was where German-language theatre returned in 1948. That gives the studio a strong "translocality" story: Veronese architect, Venezia Tridentina Biennale, Innsbruck touring company.
- The 2018 Concordia demolition passed without any heritage debate in the press found. The 1988 Cinema Corso demolition was "deplored" but not stopped. Both fit the Fabbrica del Tempo point that this ensemble has no protection apart from Cristo Re.

A search summary claimed the 1988 demolition was by developer "Pietro Tosolini" and that a protection notice was filed in vain. I could not see the source, so this is **unverified**.

Candidates: the photo gallery of the Concordia demolition on altoadige.it; the book *Lavori in Corso – Die Bozner Freiheitsstraße* (La Fabbrica del Tempo, 2020); Franzmagazine, "Il genio dimenticato di Armando Ronca" (5 Nov 2024), which returned HTTP 429 to me ([link](https://franzmagazine.com/2024/11/05/il-genio-dimenticato-di-armando-ronca)); the Archivio Storico.

## 3. Inventory of fascist-era architecture and monuments in Bolzano, with postwar fate, and street names

### Takeaway
Most of the fascist "new Bolzano" west and south of the Talvera survives and is still in use. It has largely kept its original function: tax office, court, church, insurance blocks, station, school and housing. The main losses are the Cinema Corso (1988), almost all of the Semirurali (1970s–80s), the Lancia, Montecatini and Falck heavy industry (partly), the Ponte Druso sculpture (1970s) and the Concordia (2018). In July 2026 most of the centre of the Palazzo di Giustizia collapsed. Street names were purged of fascist leaders in 1945–48, but many nationalist and military names were kept. In 2002 a referendum restored "Piazza della Vittoria" after the council had renamed it "Piazza della Pace".

### Cited Findings
**Victory Monument (Monumento alla Vittoria / Siegesdenkmal), Piazza della Vittoria**
- Decided on 10 Feb 1926 and designed by Marcello Piacentini. It replaced the unfinished Kaiserjäger monument (Karl Ernstberger, begun 1917), which was demolished. First stone 12 July 1926; inaugurated 12 July 1928 by Vittorio Emanuele III. Arturo Dazzi made the Vittoria; the Latin inscription "HIC PATRIAE FINES SISTE SIGNA / HINC CETEROS EXCOLVIMVS LINGVA LEGIBVS ARTIBVS" was dictated by Pietro Fedele (an earlier draft had "barbaros"). Inside: Wildt's busts of Battisti, Filzi and Chiesa, Andreotti's Risen Christ, Cadorin's crypt frescoes, Canonica's medallions. The south side once read "BEN. MUSSOLINI, ITAL. DUCE A. VI" and was removed after 1945. Later history: the 1 Oct 1978 bomb, fencing, the 1990 and 2009 restorations, and the 2005 explanatory plaques in four languages placed about 50 m away. [W] — [it.wikipedia: Monumento alla Vittoria (Bolzano)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_alla_Vittoria_(Bolzano))
- 14 columns in the form of fasces; the funding subscription raised 3 million lire. The 2014 exhibition covers 13 rooms and 700 m². [V] — [Verdorfer 2007 PDF](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf); [S] — [Comune di Bolzano page](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Vivere-il-Comune/Luoghi/Monumento-alla-Vittoria)
- On 28 Oct 1932 the SS leader Theodor Eicke and 30 SS men took part in the March-on-Rome tenth-anniversary ceremony at the monument. [W] — [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_alla_Vittoria_(Bolzano)); photo on [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Faschos_und_Nazis_am_Siegesdenkmal_1932.tif)
- In the park behind the monument stands a **column for the fallen in Africa (Libya, East Africa) and Spain**, erected "anno XVI" (1938). [V] — [Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf) **14 November 2026:** its donor (the city of Rome), its unveiling on 5 June 1938, its full inscription and what has been done to it since 2020 are in notes 17 §1 and §7.
- **Lupa capitolina and Lion of St Mark** on tall columns by the Talvera bridge are gilded wood sculptures by Ignaz Gabloner, 1938. They were restored by the Comune in 2018 "non cogliendo tuttavia l'occasione per una contestualizzazione storica". [W] — [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_alla_Vittoria_(Bolzano)), citing Kraus & Obermair (eds), *Miti delle dittature*, Castel Tirolo 2019, p. 61

**Piazza della Vittoria and its arcaded buildings; Corso Libertà; Piazza Mazzini; Corso Italia**
- Piazza della Vittoria, the "foro della vittoria", is enclosed on three sides by 5 m-high arcades. The buildings are mostly by the INA and INFPS insurance and social-security institutes. [V] — [Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf). The INA/INPS wings are by Paolo Rossi de Paoli and Michele Busiri Vici. [W] — [it.wikipedia: Corso della Libertà](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corso_della_Libert%C3%A0_(Bolzano)). [V] — [FAU Erlangen excursion report, May 2026](https://www.geschichtsdidaktik.phil.fau.de/files/2026/06/Exkursionsbericht_gesamt.pdf); W. Strobl, *Quaderni di storia* 78 (2013), pp. 87–135 (cited in [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_alla_Vittoria_(Bolzano)))
- **Corso Libertà**: laid out from 1935/36 under Piacentini's plan. Austro-Hungarian buildings (Kuendlhof, Bräuhaus Schwarz, Hotel Badl) were demolished for it. Its names were "Corso dell'Impero" / **"Corso IX Maggio"** (after the 9 May 1936 proclamation of the Empire); in German "9.-Mai-Straße" under the 1943–45 Nazi occupation. It became "Corso della Libertà" only after 1945. [W] — [de.wikipedia: Freiheitsstraße](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiheitsstra%C3%9Fe_(Bozen)). Verdorfer gives "Corso Littorio" and then, from 1936, "Corso 9 Maggio". It opened to traffic in 1938 with arcades "molto più grandi" than the medieval ones and flat roofs throughout. - **Piazza Mazzini** is bounded by "Palazzo Rossi" (1939). From there one turns into **Corso Italia (then viale Giulio Cesare)** and reaches the court, Casa Littoria and Cristo Re "triade di poteri", which functioned as an ideological "porta" of the city, before reaching viale Druso. [V] — [Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)

**Casa del Fascio / Casa Littoria (Piazza Tribunale) and the Piffrader relief**
- Built 1939–42 by Guido Pelizzari, Francesco Rossi and Luis Plattner; convex façade facing the concave courthouse. It has housed the State finance offices since the war. The relief by **Hans Piffrader** (from Klausen/Chiusa) is travertine: 57 panels, 2.75 m high, two rows, 36 m long, 198 m², about 95 t. It was carved by Giovanni Battista Vannucci of Pietrasanta, and the last panels arrived about two months before 25 July 1943. **3 of the 57 panels were installed only in 1957.** After the war the Soprintendenza of Trento proposed removing it, without success. [V] — [bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com, "Il bassorilievo"](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/bassorilievo.html); [W] — [it.wikipedia: Casa del Fascio (Bolzano)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fascio_(Bolzano))
- The relief is dated "EF XX" (1942) and shows the Duce on horseback with the PNF, GIL, GUF, OND and MVSN acronyms and "credere, obbedire, combattere". [V] — [Dunajtschik & Steinacher 2008](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)

**Palazzo di Giustizia (Piazza Tribunale, formerly Piazza Arnaldo Mussolini)**
- By Paolo Rossi de Paoli and Michele Busiri Vici; begun 1939, halted 1942, resumed 1951, inaugurated 15 Sept 1956 by President Gronchi. The architrave inscription reads "PRO ITALICO IMPERIO VIRTVTE IVSTITIA HIERARCHIA VNGVIBVS ET ROSTRIS". The Justitia pediment by **Alcide Ticò** has no blindfold and looks across at the Mussolini relief. It hosted the first postwar Bolzano Fair on 12 Sept 1948. Ownership passed from the State to the Province in December 2017. [W] — [de.wikipedia: Justizpalast (Bozen)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justizpalast_(Bozen))
- **16 July 2026: partial collapse.** The central four-storey courtroom wing behind the colonnade collapsed shortly after 6 a.m. during work on the load-bearing columns. ORF adds that the courthouse inscription "has never been contextualized" and that a ~€10 m renovation plan had been presented in May 2024. [V] — [ORF, "Loch mit Symbolkraft: Mussolini-Bau in Bozen teilweise eingestürzt", 16 July 2026](https://orf.at/stories/3436356/)

[S] — [LaPresse, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.lapresse.it/cronaca/2026/08/11/crollo-tribunale-bolzano-cinque-persone-indagate-per-disastro-colposo/); [Il Dolomiti](https://www.ildolomiti.it/cronaca/2026/crollo-del-palazzo-di-giustizia-di-bolzano-trovate-le-nuove-sedi-ecco-dove-andranno-tribunale-e-procura). [W] — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justizpalast_(Bozen)), citing [salto.bz, M. Hanni, 23 Jul 2026](https://salto.bz/de/article/22072026/mit-augenmass-und-leidenschaft)

The Naiad fountain (Eraldo Fozzer) dates from 1958, and an underground garage and square redesign from 2003. [W] — [de.wikipedia: Gerichtsplatz (Bozen)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerichtsplatz_(Bozen))

**Chiesa di Cristo Re (Piazza Cristo Re / Corso Italia)**
- Guido Pelizzari and Luigi Colombo, 1938–40; the bell tower was added after the war. - Site levelling began in autumn 1935 and the zone was officially opened on **20 Dec 1936**. A **Lancia** foundry started production six months later. **Street names**
- Mass Italian renamings took place, especially in 1929 and 1936 (*Alpenzeitung* 10 Oct 1929 p. 3; 29 Sept 1936 p. 5). Some fascist names were replaced after Mussolini's fall (*Alpenzeitung* 20 Aug 1943 p. 2), and the 1943–45 Nazi authorities added German names. After 1945 some Italians tore down bilingual signs, and the US military government ordered them restored. Commissions sat in 1946 and again in late 1948. ### Inferences
- The fascist regime's built programme in Bolzano is unusually intact because it was never treated as heritage: it stayed infrastructure. Its losses came from speculation (Cinema Corso), housing renewal (Semirurali), deindustrialisation (Alumix) and, in 2026, structural failure (the court). Iconoclasm accounts for very little, with Ponte Druso the main exception.
- For a "translocalities" project the architects and builders are a ready map of mobility. Rome: Piacentini, Rossi de Paoli, Busiri Vici, Calza Bini, Mazzoni. Padua: Mansutti and Miozzo. Verona: Ronca. Milan: Colombo. Ancona: Morelli. Trento: Forlati. Venice/Belluno: Miozzi. Tyrol: Holzmeister, Trenker, Piffrader, Gabloner, Plattner. Pietrasanta: Vannucci, who cut the travertine. Lucca: the marble industrialists who supplied the Victory Monument.

## 4. Postwar fate and "defusing" (storicizzazione / Historisierung): BZ '18–'45, the LED ring, the Arendt quotation, Obermair, reception, what was not defused

### Takeaway
Bolzano's approach is to add rather than remove. After decades of conflict, a 1978 bomb, the 2002 referendum and the 2005 plaques, the January 2011 Bondi–SVP agreement led to two interventions. The first was the documentary exhibition **BZ '18–'45** under the Victory Monument, opened 21 July 2014, with an LED ring around one fasces column. It drew 24,300 visitors in 2015 and over 55,000 by early 2016, and received an EMYA 2016 Special Commendation. The second was an illuminated **Hannah Arendt quotation** in three languages over the Piffrader relief (November 2017), chosen from 486 competition entries (winners Arnold Holzknecht and Michele Bernardi). Hannes Obermair was the main historian-activist behind both. A lot remains un-defused: the courthouse inscription, the colonial column, the Virgil inscription, the Lupa and Lion, the Sparkasse reliefs, the street names, Ponte Druso and the Alpino.

**Path to the exhibition**

[W] — [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_alla_Vittoria_(Bolzano)), citing [Comune di Bolzano](http://www.comune.bolzano.it/cultura_context.jsp?ID_LINK=976&page=5&area=48) and [Alto Adige 22 May 2010](http://ricerca.gelocal.it/altoadige/archivio/altoadige/2010/05/22/AT9PO_AT901.html)

On 2 Feb 2011 the Provincial Government launched an ideas competition for the Casa Littoria façade. [V] — [bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/2011-un-concorso-di-idee.html); [W] — [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_alla_Vittoria_(Bolzano))

[V] — [monumentoallavittoria.com, "Concetto"](https://www.monumentoallavittoria.com/it/mostra/concetto.html)
- The 2014 opening was preceded by an agreement among the three governments, which co-funded it (2012). A non-interference clause shielded the commission. English is placed first on signs to avoid giving primacy to either local language; Ladin was added later. Three "paths": regional macro-history, the monument's micro-history, and "what is a monument". In the crypt, quotes from Arendt ("Nobody has the right to obey"), Brecht and Paine are projected over Cadorin's frescoes. The **LED ring** on one fasces column is a "nose ring for Fascist rhetoric" (Schnapp: "by unbalancing, the ring rebalances"), and it was chosen partly because it was cheaper than the alternatives. ### Inferences
- The 55,000 visitors in about 18 months are modest next to Bolzano's tourism volume, but the per-year figure and the school share (≈4,000 pupils in 2015) suggest the site works mainly as a civic and educational space. This is my reading, not a source claim.
- Defusing so far has concentrated on the two most visible, nationally owned objects (the Monument and the Casa Littoria). The Ponte Druso eagles are a third case: they were removed in the 1970s, before the "add, don't remove" doctrine existed, and then partly re-entered the 2014 exhibition.

## 5. Tourist use today, the Christmas market, guided tours, and current debates (2020–2026)

### Takeaway
Official city tourism mentions the fascist period only in passing. None of its 12 "Bolzano si racconta" audioguides covers a fascist-era site, and the one in the Semirurali park is about a medieval canonry. Its "Storia e cultura" page still opens with the "Pons Drusi" foundation story. The Victory Monument appears mainly as the end point of a summer architecture-and-gelato walk, and as a free museum run by the Comune. Regional portals list BZ '18–'45, the bas-relief and the Casa Semirurale. The Christmas market (since 1991) sits on Piazza Walther in the German-coded old town, not on Piazza Vittoria. The main 2020–2026 events were the 2020–21 closure of the exhibition, a 2025 restoration row at the Victory Monument (with renewed demolition calls), and the 16 July 2026 collapse of the Palazzo di Giustizia, which reopened the question of what to do with the fascist ensemble.

### Inferences
- Official tourism treats the fascist city as a background or architecture topic, and as an optional museum. The "two Bolzanos" tourism geography holds: Christmas, Walther and the Portici on one side, the 1930s city on the other. Meanwhile the Romanità origin myth the regime used survives in official copy as neutral heritage ("Pons Drusi"). For the studio project, this asymmetry is itself a finding.
- The 2026 courthouse collapse turned an un-defused building into the most-reported fascist-heritage story of the decade. It also shifted the debate from symbols to repair, reuse and new function.
