## 1. Postwar branding (1945–2026): from EPT "Alto Adige/Dolomiti" to the "Südtirol" umbrella brand and IDM, and how it relates to fascist-era imagery

### Takeaway
ASTAT's 1950–2025 series shows something the branding literature does not spell out. In 1950, 85% of South Tyrol's overnight stays were by Italians, a direct continuation of the interwar domestic "Alto Adige/Dolomiti" tourism. By 1957 the majority had flipped to foreign, mostly German-speaking, guests. The 1961 bombing campaign cut Italian arrivals by a quarter in a single year. The later "Südtirol" brand therefore sits on a market that turned northward in the 1950s. It was codified in 2005 by the Dachmarke Südtirol, whose credo is "kontrastreiche Symbiose aus alpin und mediterran" (a high-contrast symbiosis of Alpine and Mediterranean), and in 2016 it was absorbed into IDM. I found no scholarship that directly compares fascist-era ENIT/EPT poster imagery with postwar campaigns. The closest work is a Lucerne PhD project (Klee) and the Touriseum's research series.

"Die Bomben verschreckten die südlichen Besucher, während jene aus dem Norden vermehrt buchten" (the bombs scared off the visitors from the south, while those from the north booked more and more). "Einige deutsche Touristen halfen den Separatisten sogar, antiitalienische Transparente zu malen" (some German tourists even helped the separatists paint anti-Italian banners). **[S]** — [Falter, "Fallbeispiel Südtirol"](https://www.falter.at/zeitung/20260407/fallbeispiel-suedtirol). ASTAT partly contradicts the "roughly equal" claim: foreign arrivals already outnumbered Italian ones from 1954. It supports the 1961 shock (see above).

**Fascist-era roots of the tourist infrastructure:**
- Catrina Klee's PhD project at the University of Lucerne (supervisor Valentin Groebner) is titled «Tourismusentwicklung in Südtirol: Utopische, faschistische Architektur: Tourismus als strategische Investition in Südtirol». Its hypothesis: fascist political and military infrastructure investments "are the basis for turning South Tyrol into a region dominated by car and sports tourism between the 1920s and the 1940s". Tourism projects "acted as accelerators of the Italianization". The unrealized "utopias … shape and preformat the later change from the 1950s onward". It also identifies a shift from spa tourism (Bolzano, Merano) to active summer and sports tourism after WWI. **[V]** — [Klee, PhD abstract (PDF)](https://www.unilu.ch/fileadmin/fakultaeten/ksf/institute/gsl/doktorierende/Abstract_PhD_Catrina_Klee.pdf)

Berann. **[SS]** The item record would not load — [Luna Imaging record](https://trial.lunaimaging.com/luna/servlet/detail/lunatrial~18~18~76~1400). **The 2005 umbrella brand and SMG:**
- The Dachmarke Südtirol was introduced "vor fünf Jahren" (five years before), so in 2005, according to an article dated 30 Dec 2009. Its credo: "Südtirol ist die kontrastreiche Symbiose aus alpin und mediterran, Spontaneität und Verlässlichkeit, Natur und Kultur" (South Tyrol is the high-contrast symbiosis of Alpine and Mediterranean, spontaneity and reliability, nature and culture). Brand management sat with the Südtirol Marketing Gesellschaft (SMG). In 2009 there were 1,810 official users. The quality seal in the umbrella-brand design had been awarded 291 times, and the logo "Ein Unternehmen aus Südtirol" about 500 times. The brand was to be used across tourism, food and the wider economy. **[V]** — [stol.it, "Fünf Jahre Dachmarke Südtirol: Neue Maßstäbe", 30 Dec 2009](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/fuenf-jahre-dachmarke-suedtirol-neue-massstaebe)
- Before the brand there were about 90 tourist logos and 50 other brands. Provincial councillor Werner Frick initiated the project, and the Berlin agency MetaDesign was selected. SMG was founded in 2000 as a private-law company receiving €11.2 million a year from public funds. **[SS]** — [markt-kom.com](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=30052)

**IDM (2016–):**
- IDM Südtirol – Alto Adige was set up as a "Sonderbetrieb" (special agency) owned 60% by the Province and 40% by the Chamber of Commerce. It merged SMG, EOS (export), TIS (innovation) and Chamber services, with about 170 staff and a 2016 operating budget of about €40 million. The designated director general was Hansjörg Prast. Mission: "Südtirols Unternehmen wettbewerbsfähiger und das Land zum begehrtesten Lebensraum Europas machen" (make South Tyrol's businesses more competitive and the province the most desirable place to live in Europe). **[V]** — [stol.it, 23 Oct 2015](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/idm-suedtirol-zum-begehrtesten-lebensraum-europas-machen)
- IDM's 2019 image-style rules ("Südtirol Toolbox / Bildstil") say the umbrella brand addresses people interested in "Nachhaltigkeit, Aktivität, Natur und Originalität" (sustainability, activity, nature and originality). Imagery should move "weg von 'Postkartenmotiven' hin zu mehr Echtheit, Natürlichkeit und Authentizität" (away from postcard motifs towards more genuineness, naturalness and authenticity). The rules restate the 2005 brand core ("alpin und mediterran…"). **[V]** — [IDM, Bildstil 2019 (PDF)](https://www.merano-suedtirol.it/media/5e2b4a81-04bd-4220-aa0a-93b712f4de72/bildstil-idm-vorgaben.pdf)

**Dolomites UNESCO (2009) and its marketing effect:**
- Banca d'Italia QEF n. 1027 (June 2026), Cascarano, Fezzi and Rodriguez: after the 2009 listing, Dolomite municipalities in the provinces of Trento, Bolzano and Belluno, "soprattutto quelli del Trentino" (above all those in Trentino), recorded more tourist presences, more markedly among Italian visitors. ### Inferences
- **[INF]** The commonly told "re-Tyrolization" of South Tyrol's tourist image after 1945 (Tracht, farmhouses, Andreas Hofer, onion domes) is better read as following the market than as leading it. The guest base flipped from about 85% Italian (1950) to majority German-speaking (from 1954 for arrivals, 1957 for nights) before the autonomy conflict peaked. Postwar EPT material in English and German (e.g. Berann 1951) still used the fascist-era "Dolomites/Bolzano" framing. Tyrolean folklore imagery became the commercial default for a northern clientele. That is a hypothesis to test against the Touriseum poster archive.
- **[INF]** The 2005 brand credo "alpin und mediterran" is itself a statement of in-betweenness: it markets the border position. That is useful for a translocality framing (Section 5). The German word "Südtirol" in the logo, with "Alto Adige" kept in Italian-language manuals, reproduces the naming dualism inside one brand system.
- **[INF]** Bolzano city never fitted the rural "Südtirol" image. It kept a roughly 50/50 Italian/foreign guest mix for decades, which matches its status as the Italian-majority, fascist-built "Alto Adige" capital.

### Gaps
- I found no scholarship or exhibition that directly compares fascist-era ENIT/EPT/CIT "Dolomiti/Alto Adige" posters with postwar EPT or Landesverkehrsamt posters for conscious echoes or rejections. The archives to check are the Touriseum collection (Merano), the Südtiroler Landesarchiv, and the Museo nazionale Collezione Salce in Treviso (24,000+ advertising graphics) **[SS]** — [cultura.gov.it](https://cultura.gov.it/articolo/nuovo-flash-mob-digitale-per-un-viaggio-in-italia-attraverso-i-manifesti-pubblicitari).

- I could not verify IDM's current campaign slogans for 2023–2026 or any formal brand "relaunch". The suedtirol.info site is JavaScript-rendered, and IDM's brand page returned 404.

## 2. The numbers: tourism pressure in Bolzano city and the province, 2010–2025/26, plus beds, bed cap, short-term rentals, housing and public debate

### Takeaway
Bolzano passed 1 million overnight stays for the first time in 2025 (1,042,903, ASTAT). That is up 81% since 2010 and 45% since 2019, against +34% and +14% for the province. Arrivals reached 457,548, and in 2023 the city already had the most arrivals of any South Tyrolean municipality. In per-capita terms the city is still lightly touristed: about 8 nights per inhabitant (2023) against 67 for the province. The pressure is short-stay (2.3 nights), increasingly international, and shifting into non-hotel accommodation (beds there up 3.5× since 2010). Despite the 2022 bed cap, provincial beds rose from 233,847 (2022) to 262,869 (2025). In April 2026 the cap regulation was loosened for weak-structure municipalities and tightened for private short-term rentals.

- Amendments found: DLH 29 Mar 2023, Nr. 9 (BUR 30 Mar 2023, Nr. 13). **[V]** — [lexbrowser](https://lexbrowser.provinz.bz.it/doc/de/230059/dekret_des_landeshauptmanns_vom_29_m_rz_2023_nr_9.aspx). DLH 13 Apr 2026, Nr. 10 (BUR 16 Apr 2026, Nr. 16) does three things:
  - In municipalities at risk of depopulation or structurally weak (Table A), it extends acquired rights in tourism zones from 4 to 9 years. Municipalities with more than 500,000 nights in 2025 are excluded, which excludes Bolzano. The extension requires a council vote per zone that considers water, energy, traffic and staff housing, a sustainability certification (e.g. the "Nachhaltigkeitslabel Südtirol") within 2 years for new establishments, and the 150-bed maximum.
  - It gives beds for private tourist rentals (LG 12/1995) preferably to applicants who rent at their own residence or registered seat.
  - It makes allocations inadmissible if they would bring a person above **two** rental units. **[V]** — [lexbrowser, DLH 10/2026](https://lexbrowser.provinz.bz.it/doc/de/239379/dekret_des_landeshauptmanns_vom_13_april_2026_nr_10.aspx)

  DLH 23 Sep 2026, Nr. 28 (acquired rights not lost through procedural defects). **[V]** — [lexbrowser](https://lexbrowser.provinz.bz.it/doc/de/240659/dekret_des_landeshauptmanns_vom_23_september_2026_nr_28.aspx)
- The provincial government approved the 5-year extension in a special session on 1 April 2026. Kompatscher: "Die Regelung über die Bettenobergrenze bleibt" (the bed-cap rule stays). Without the extension, rights lapse at the end of September 2026. **[V]** — [stol.it, 2 Apr 2026](https://www.stol.it/artikel/politik/gaestebetten-in-bestimmten-zonen-darf-frist-um-5-jahre-verlaengert-werden). The Heimatpflegeverband says beds exceeded 268,000 by early 2026. **[SS]** — [Heidelberg24, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.heidelberg24.de/welt/suedtirol-weicht-tourismus-regeln-auf-iran-krieg-koennte-urlauber-lage-zuspitzen-zr-94215397.html)

He said: "Die Bettenobergrenze greift" (the bed cap is working). **[V]** — [stol.it, 7 Feb 2025](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/in-einem-jahr-10000-betten-mehr-in-suedtirol-trotz-bettenobergrenze). ### Inferences
- **[INF]** Bolzano's "overtourism" is not a per-capita problem like Val Gardena's (8 vs 67 nights per inhabitant). It is concentrated in time and space: Christmas-market weekends, Piazza Walther and the old town, the A22 exit and the coach terminal, and the cable cars. It is also a housing problem. The city's growth rates since 2019 (+45% nights) are three times the province's, and the short-term-rental component grew fastest. That makes Bolzano a "gateway/hub" overtourism case rather than a resort case.
- **[INF]** The ASTAT 2019 bed figure (224,827) differs from the 229,088 used as the legal 2019 baseline. The law counts permitted or declared beds with late registrations, ASTAT counts registered capacity. Do not compare them directly.
- **[INF]** The April 2026 rules (preference for owner-occupiers and a two-unit maximum for private tourist rentals) are the first provincial instrument aimed squarely at Airbnb-type professionalisation. Bolzano is excluded from the 9-year extension because it exceeds 500,000 nights.

## 3. Bolzano-specific pressure points (Ötzi, Christmas market, Piazza Walther, funiculars, MMM, day trips) and the city's tourism strategy

### Takeaway
The city's pressure concentrates on four nodes. Ötzi has over 310,000 visitors a year and a planned €98 million new museum on the former ENEL site. The Christmas market has grown from 315,000 wristbands in the 2021 Covid edition to an estimated 1 million visitors in 2025/26. The Renon cable car had over 1 million riders in ten months of 2025, which led to a resident priority lane. The fourth node is the city's role as logistics hub for Dolomite day trips and the 2026 Winter Olympics. I found no stand-alone Comune tourism strategy. Policy shows up as ad hoc decisions, such as giving the city's cap beds only to commercial operators.

### Inferences
- **[INF]** Three of the four pressure nodes are being expanded, not capped: the new Ötzi museum, the market extended across the city, and cable-car capacity raised from 550 to 720 per hour. - **[INF]** The proposed Ötzi museum site (Via Dante/Via Marconi) lies on the edge of the 1930s "new Italian" Bolzano west of the Talvera. ## 4. The fascist layer in today's tourist communication: IDM, the Azienda di Soggiorno, guided tours, guidebooks

### Takeaway
Bolzano's official tourism channels no longer hide the fascist layer. The Azienda di Soggiorno's "Tourist attractions" page has substantial entries on BZ '18-'45, the Semirurali House, the Victory Monument's iconography and a full Bolzano Lager memorial itinerary. IDM's portal calls the monument an "apologia for the conquest-hungry fascists" and "a grave insult to German-speaking South Tyroleans". The register stays aestheticizing in the product offer, though. The one regular interwar-architecture tour is sold as "armonia di stili e sapori … e gelato" (harmony of styles and flavours … and gelato). The short history pages give fascism a single sentence. The Piffrader relief does not appear on the tourist-attractions page. The academic literature (Pupella-Noguès; Angelucci & Kerschbamer; Carlà & Mitterhofer) treats Bolzano as a public-history model, with critiques of its didactic, "correct reading" design.

## 5. Translocality theory: annotated bibliography and applications to South Tyrol/Bolzano

### Takeaway
The core translocality literature (Appadurai; Freitag & von Oppen; Brickell & Datta; Greiner & Sakdapolrak) gives the studio a vocabulary for places produced through simultaneous connections across locales: tourists, migrants, regimes, brands. I found **no** published work that explicitly applies "translocality" to Bolzano or South Tyrol. The nearest bodies of work are:
- border and regional-identity history (Grote; Grote & Obermair),
- memory and contested-heritage studies of the Victory Monument and the Option (Pfanzelter; Angelucci & Kerschbamer; Carlà & Mitterhofer; Pupella-Noguès),
- migration and belonging research at Eurac and unibz (Mitterhofer; Marcher),
- tourism-and-fascism history (Klee; Touriseum).

This is a genuine gap the studio can occupy.

### Cited Findings

**A. Core theory (annotations: summaries of the cited abstracts, plus my synthesis where marked)**
1. **Appadurai, Arjun (1995/1996). "The Production of Locality."** In Richard Fardon (ed.), *Counterworks: Managing the Diversity of Knowledge*. London/New York: Routledge (ASA Decennial Conference Series), pp. 204–225. **[SS]** Pages and publisher from search results — [Érudit reference list](https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/as/2001-v25-n3-as375/000261ar/resume/); chapter 10 of the volume — [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Counterworks-Managing-the-Diversity-of-Knowledge/Fardon/p/book/9780415107938). Reprinted in *Modernity at Large: Cultural Dimensions of Globalization*, Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 1996. **[SS]** Same Érudit record. The chapter number in *Modernity at Large* (ch. 9) is from my own knowledge.
   - Annotation **[INF/synthesis]**: Locality is not given but produced, "relational and contextual", through rituals and material practices under conditions of mass migration and media. Appadurai uses "translocalities" for places such as border zones, refugee camps, tourist resorts and migrant hostels, which are tied to the nation-state but inhabited by mobile populations and anchored elsewhere. A tourist-heavy border capital like Bolzano fits the type. Verify the exact wording in Appadurai 1996, ch. 9 and "Sovereignty without territoriality" (1996).
2. **Freitag, Ulrike & Achim von Oppen (eds.)    - Annotation: a historian-led volume (Zentrum Moderner Orient, Berlin). It uses "translocality" to escape Western bias, linear-diffusion models, the local/global dichotomy and shallow historical depth in globalisation studies. Case studies are from the Middle East, Asia and Africa. It is useful because it is historical: translocality as a lens on past regimes of circulation, such as fascist colonial or settler policy in Alto Adige. **[SS]** Abstract per catalogue records (search summary). ### Inferences
- **[INF]** A translocal framing for "translocalities in/on Bolzano" can braid four documented circulations:
  1. Fascist-era settler and worker migration from other Italian regions, which produced the Semirurali, the Rione Battisti and the industrial zone.
  2. A regime-built tourist infrastructure (Klee) that "preformatted" postwar tourism.
  3. A postwar market flip to northern guests and a "Südtirol" brand whose credo is itself a claim of between-ness ("alpin und mediterran").
  4. Today's internationalised, short-stay visitor flows (42.7% of 2025 nights from countries other than Italy and Germany) and short-term rentals that turn resident housing into nodes of global platforms.

  Brickell & Datta's scales (home, neighbourhood, city) and Greiner & Sakdapolrak's "simultaneity" map well onto the Semirurali House museum, BZ '18-'45 and the Christmas market.
- **[INF]** Memory and heritage studies of the Victory Monument are already implicitly translocal. Panels lead with English, and the exhibition addresses an international visitor before either local language group. That can be read as the monument being re-addressed to the translocal tourist rather than to the two "local" communities.
