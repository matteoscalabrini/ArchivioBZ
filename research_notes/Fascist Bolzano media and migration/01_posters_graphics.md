## Q1. Which posters exist for Bolzano, Merano, Alto Adige, Dolomiti, Val Gardena, Carezza, Brennero, the Bolzano fair, etc.? Title, artist, year, printer, commissioner, languages, holding, URLs

### Takeaway
The largest verifiable corpus is the Touriseum (Merano), fully catalogued and online through the provincial OMA portal. A keyword search for "Lenhart" alone returns 462 records, 105 of them posters. About 50 posters from 1919–1943 for Merano, Bolzano-Gries, the Dolomites, Val Gardena, Carezza, Renon and the Brenner line are documented below with printer, commissioning body, language and record and image URLs. The commissioners shift from local spa administrations and local printers (1919–c.1928) to ENIT/FS and the Bolzano-based UTA (Unione Turistica Alberghiera), printed by national lithographers in Milan, Genoa, Florence, Treviso and Naples (1930s). The same image was issued in Italian, German, English and French editions. No interwar poster for a "Fiera di Bolzano" was found: the 1921 *Bozner Messe* is the only fair poster in the period, and the Salce fair posters are post-1948.

### Inferences
- [INF] The same Lenhart image was issued in up to three or four language editions, distributed across collections. Each language edition has its own title formula, while the image stays the same: Italian *L'estate nelle Dolomiti* (Salce), German *Sommer in den Dolomiten* (TOUR) and French *L'été aux Dolomites* (Salce), 1937–38. Likewise English *Bolzano-Gries Dolomites* (TOUR) and German *Bozen-Gries Dolomiten* (Salce), and *Wintersport in den Dolomiten* / *Sport invernale nelle Dolomiti* (1933–34). Italians in the Kingdom saw the Italian edition. The German edition targeted the Reich, Austria and Switzerland: note the Leipzig and Dresden agency stamps on TOUR brochures.
- [INF] Commissioning centralises over time. 1919–c.1928 posters are by local printers (Pötzelberger, Vogelweider, Hauger, Fränzl), carry German or Italian text from the Kurverwaltung/Azienda, and some still use "Südtirol". From c.1930 the large-format posters carry ENIT and FS logos (Savoy crown and knot), "Direzione Generale per il Turismo" approval numbers and fascist-era dates (XIII, XIV, XVII, XVIII). They are printed by national lithographers (Coen, Pizzi & Pizio, Barabino & Graeve/SAIGA, Giaccone, Longo & Zoppelli, Richter).
- [INF] The UTA (Unione Turistica Alberghiera, seat in Bolzano) was the key regional publisher of Dolomites/Merano promotion in 1936–40, alongside ENIT. It has been overlooked compared with ENIT.

## Q2. Who were the artists, and what is known of their South Tyrol commissions?

### Takeaway
Franz Lenhart dominates the corpus. He was a Merano-based Austrian-born naturalised Italian, active for hotels, shops, clubs, the Aziende di soggiorno, UTA, ENIT and FS from 1923 to c.1955. Around him worked Meran/Bozen graphic artists and photographers (Erwin Merlet, Josef Tscholl, Leo Bährendt, Lorenz Fränzl, Otto Zoff) and, from the 1930s, national poster artists commissioned by ENIT/UTA (Mario Puppo, Filippo Romoli, Mario Borgoni, Enrico Grimaldi, Pio Solero, Michele Ortino, Sergio Franciscone, Marcello Dudovich). No South Tyrol work by Boccasile, Riccobaldi or Vittorio Grassi was found in the provincial catalogue.

### Cited Findings
- **Franz (Josef) Lenhart**: born Bad Häring (Tyrol) 7 Jan 1898, died Meran 27 Mar 1992, per the TOUR/OMA authority record [DB✓] (e.g. [Rec](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000481)). Other sources disagree on the dates: the Hungarian National Museum gives 1892–1992 ([Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/1090/MNMMUSEUM1706901)); a Catalogo generale snippet gives 1898/1994 ([catalogo](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/Agent/5e62cf7f8f49ef6e1d54cf3be1a14f2e), [IDX]).
- Lenhart studied art in Vienna and Florence and moved to Florence in 1921. After 1919 he became drawing teacher at the Technical Institute in Merano and took Italian citizenship. In the late 1920s and 1930s he designed posters, brochures and hotel labels for clients in the Northern Italian Alps, especially Merano, Bolzano and Cortina. In 1934 he began working for navigation companies, which took him to Japan. He designed nearly 100 mountain posters between c.1930 and 1955, then turned to painting — [inverso.pt (label-art site)](https://inverso.pt/labels/artists/Lenhard/lenhard.htm). An auction house says he died in his studio in the annex of Schloss Winkel, Meran — [Capitolium Art](https://aste.capitoliumart.it/en/artist/lenhart-franz-1898-1992/xar-10646).
- Lenhart's OMA corpus [DB✓]: 462 records for "Lenhart", of which 105 are posters ("Plakat"), 86 brochures, 26 postcards and 11 luggage labels. Faceted keywords: Frau 116, Hotel 80, Skifahren 51, Tracht 31. Printers recur: Pötzelberger, G. Linke, SED/Levi Cortina, Giaccone e Morelli, Pizzi & Pizio, Barabino & Graeve (OMA results page for "Lenhart", 2026-10-22, https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/results).

His ENIT work: *Bolzano-Gries Dolomites* (1930), *Merano* (1934), *Winter-Sport in Italien*, *Merano* (1938–39), and the ENIT/FS *Monthly Tourist Review … Travel in Italy* cover of Nov 1934 — [Rec](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000330). ### Inferences
- [INF] Lenhart's position was translocal in the literal sense. He was an Austrian-trained, Meran-resident, naturalised Italian designer who rendered the province in Italian, German, English and French for Italian state agencies, while also designing for German-speaking Meran shopkeepers ("Meraner Trachten"). That makes him the hinge figure for a studio reading of "Bolzano seen from elsewhere".
- [INF] The national "names" (Dudovich, Boccasile) barely touched Alto Adige. The province's image was mostly made locally (Lenhart, Merlet, Tscholl, Bährendt, Fränzl) and then nationalised through ENIT/FS logos, approval stamps and national printers.

### Gaps
- I found no archival account, from the ENIT or UTA side, of how Lenhart was commissioned: contracts, fees, briefs. The Festi/Gullo 2001 catalogue is the likely source; it was not consulted.
- Mario Puppo's interwar Alto Adige posters (as opposed to brochures) were not found in OMA. Salce may hold some ([IDX] coverage was incomplete).

## Q3. Where are these held and viewable online?

### Takeaway
The single most productive portal is the provincial *Objekte Museen Archive Südtirol* (OMA). Open-licence images are few: Commons, Europeana (Hungarian National Museum) and Teßmann newspaper crops.

## Q4. Iconography: landscape vs people, Tracht, bourgeois figures, Romanità, the Victory Monument, the "new city", slogans

### Takeaway
In the verified corpus the promotional image of Alto Adige for Italian and foreign audiences is overwhelmingly landscape (Dolomites: Catinaccio/Rosengarten, Sciliar/Schlern, Sassolungo, Tre Cime; Merano: Kurhaus, promenade, palms, blossom, grapes, Texelgruppe, Schloss Tirol). It is peopled by modern leisure figures: women in fashionable dress, golfers, tennis players, motorists, skiers, bathers, jockeys. Tracht is not absent but is selectively folklorised: the Dirndl woman, the Haflinger breeder, the Algund bandsman. Romanità, Drusus, the Victory Monument and the new fascist city do **not** appear on any tourist poster found. The regime enters instead through logos (ENIT and FS with Savoy crown), fascist dating, ministerial approval stamps, an Italian flag on a mountain hut, Italian toponyms on German-language posters, and the Roman-road framing of the Brenner route. The new city surfaces in postcards, not posters.

### Cited Findings
- **Landscape and Dolomites as brand**: the Rosengarten/Catinaccio is the recurring backdrop of Bolzano posters (1921 Essendorfer; Lenhart *Visitate le Dolomiti*, *Bolzano Gries Dolomiti*, *Bolzano-Gries Dolomites*) [DB✓, Q1 #2, 6, 10–12]. Tre Cime appear in the 1935 *Dolomiti, Stazioni climatiche* [DB✓ #27]; Schlern/Seiser Alm in *Sommer in den Dolomiten* 1938 [DB✓ #29].
- **Merano spa iconography**: Kurhaus, promenade, palms and blossom (1919–23 *Größter klimatischer Kurort der Südalpen*; Istituto Italiano d'Arti Grafiche *stazione climatica subalpina*; Tscholl). Grapes and fruit: Lenhart's *Herbst in Merano* (hair of fruit, 1934), *Kennst du das Land…* (Kurhaus and grapes), and the post-war *Conca d'oro* (1947) [DB✓ / DEALER]. Blossom branch: Lenhart *Merano* 1938–39, Borgoni 1935, the Barabino & Graeve Dirndl poster 1935 [DB✓, IMG✓].
- **Modern bourgeois leisure figures** [DB✓]: golfers (1924 *Golf Links, Merano*; Carezza golf), tennis (1927; Franciscone 1936), motorists (couple in cabriolet, *Bolzano-Gries Dolomites* 1930; RACI Merano sports car; SAD bus), skiers (Renon 1930; Val Gardena ski school 1930; *Sport invernale* 1933–34; *Winter-Sport in Italien*), bathers (*Estate nelle Dolomiti* c.1940; Romoli's swimsuit woman; the 1938 two-sheet *Bolzano-Gries* with a diving woman above an Art Deco hotel [DEALER]), horse sport (1935 *Concorso ippico*; *Lotteria di Merano* 1935). The 1934 ENIT *Merano* is a modern female head in a cone of light, with no Tracht [IMG✓].
- **Tracht present but selective** [DB✓]: *SICAM* (man in Tracht with Haflinger and Noriker), *Die Algunder* (trumpeter in Tracht), Lenhart 1935 *Dolomiti, stazioni climatiche* (woman in Dirndl beside two companions), the Barabino & Graeve *Merano* 1935 (blonde woman in Dirndl with a blossom branch). Runggaldier's shop sold "Meraner Trachten" with a Lenhart poster. Post-war, the Fiera di Bolzano posters make the Tyrolean costume parade the main motif (Lenhart 1951, Patsch 1957 [IDX]).
- **Regime signs in the graphics** [DB✓]: ENIT logo "a wreath topped by the Savoy arms and crown … beneath it a figure-of-eight (Savoy) knot", and FS monogram with crown (*Merano* 1938–39). Fascist-era dates "1935/XIII", "anno XIV", "1939-XVII", "1940-XVIII". Approval formulas: "Autorizzazione Direzione Generale per il Turismo No. 136 del 25 Febbraio 1935 – XIII" (PMM 14896); "Direz. Gen. del Turismo Nr. 141 del 23.4.XIII" (#27); "Pubblicazione approvata dall'On. Minist. Stampa e Propaganda – Direz. Gen. Turismo" (UTA book). An Italian flag on the mountain hut in *Visitate le Dolomiti* (CAI Sezione Alto Adige).
- **Romanità**: in the verified corpus Romanità is carried by the ENIT German-language road map *In Italien auf den römischen Konsularstrassen – … von Brennero – Bolzano nach Rom* (1937) [DB✓]. In postcards it appears as **toponymy**: "Ponte nuovo Druso", "Torre Druso" (for the Gscheibter Turm in Gries) [DB✓, Q5]. No poster in OMA, Commons or Europeana shows the Victory Monument, Drusus or Roman remains.
- **Victory Monument as emblem**: it appears in print propaganda (the *Alpenzeitung* front page on the 12 July 1928 inauguration explaining the Latin inscriptions — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AZ_1928_07_13_1_SiD.png)) and in postcards (Fränzl *Bolzano – Monumento alla Vittoria*, 700-64, 1930s, [Rec](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140021403)). The postcard line continues after 1945 (Schlern-Verlag *Südtirol – Bozen – Siegesdenkmal – Nachtbild*, 1960s, [Rec](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140004681)). Not on posters [DB✓].
- **Civic propaganda poster**: a bilingual municipal notice of 19 May 1925, *Municipio di Bolzano – Cittadini! / Bürger!*, by Prefectural Commissioner Steffanini. It welcomes delegations of Italian war invalids, orders every house on their route to fly a tricolour, and calls Bolzano "die Königin des Hochetsch" (the German official rendering of "Alto Adige"). The territory is "designated as ours by natural borders and history … sealed by the long sacrifices and shining victories of our legions". The border is decorated with red fruit [IMG✓] — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Innsbruck-Museum_Zeughaus-Poster_Bozen_1925-01ESD.jpg).
- **Slogans verified** [DB✓ unless marked]: "Visitate Bolzano-Gries centro delle Dolomiti e dei ghiacciai" (1921); "Bolzano Gries Centro delle Dolomiti / Centre of the Dolomites" (1938 [DEALER]); "Visitate le Dolomiti"; "besuchet die Dolomiten"; "Größter klimatischer Kurort der Südalpen" (1919–23); "Stazione climatica alpina di 1° ordine nell'Alto Adige" (1919–24); "La bellissima stazione climatica subalpina dell'Alto Adige"; "la più grande stazione climatica delle Alpi meridionali"; "Alle Kurmittel Traubenkur" (1919–23) and "für Herbst und Traubenkuren" (1930s, German materials); "Herbst in Merano – 50–70% Fahrpreis Ermässigung auf den ital. Staatsbahnen"; "L'estate nelle Dolomiti – 60 stazioni con 40.000 letti" (UTA); "Dolomiti, stazioni climatiche estive – riposo – sport – manifestazioni"; "Passate l'estate a Ortisei … nella più bella valle delle Dolomiti"; "Dobbiaco … Eingang ins Dolomitengebiet"; "Passo delle Palade. La nuova strada diretta"; "Kennst du das Land…" (UTA, quoting Goethe's Mignon).
- The **Dopolavoro as consumer**: photo of a Dopolavoro excursion from Modena to South Tyrol, cars parked on Merano's Freiheitsstraße, 16 Aug 1930 (PMM, [Rec](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80012664)) [DB✓]. Tschofen's review notes the Touriseum ties 1920s–30s mass skiing and mass media to Dopolavoro and KdF, and presents SAD as a tool of nationalisation — [Tschofen 2003](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_296_14691073792923.pdf).

### Inferences
- [INF] **Division of labour between media.** Posters sold a timeless alpine/spa Alto Adige with modern Italian leisure bodies. Postcards and the press carried the new state's monuments and infrastructure (Victory Monument, Ponte Druso, Stazione nuova, Zona Industriale, Lido, Stadio). For the studio: an Italian outside the province most likely met fascist Bolzano on a postcard or in a newspaper, and met "Alto Adige" on a station wall as Dolomites and grapes.
- [INF] *Kennst du das Land…* is a strong translocal device. Goethe's line about the land "wo die Zitronen blühn" is re-routed through Merano (grapes, Kurhaus) to Gardone (lemons) and Venice. The poster sells the province to Anglo-American and German visitors as the threshold of Italy, which echoes the Brenner–Rome "consular roads" map.
- [INF] Tracht is used as a decorative marker of the alpine "type", usually feminised (Dirndl), and never as a sign of a German-speaking national community. This fits the regime's tolerance of folklore while it suppressed the German language.
- [INF] The slogan "Bolzano porta delle Dolomiti" was not found in any 1919–43 object in OMA. The verified period formula is "**centro** delle Dolomiti" (1921, 1938). "Porta/Eingang" is used for Dobbiaco (c.1928).

### Gaps
- No verified period instance of the Italian phrase "cura dell'uva" on a poster; only the German "Traubenkur" was found. Italian-language grape-cure posters may exist in Salce/MART.
- Iconography of Salce items is known only from catalogue titles ([IDX]), not images.
- No poster for the industrial zone (Lancia, Acciaierie, Magnesio) aimed at Italian workers or migrants was found. That kind of material may sit in company archives, outside tourism collections.

## Q5. Postcards: publishers, "Bolzano nuova" series, where digitised

### Takeaway
The dominant Bolzano postcard publishers of 1919–43 were **Lorenz Fränzl** ("Ed. Stab. Art. Fot. Lor. Fränzl – Bolzano", monogram LFB in front of a Rosengarten silhouette, series numbers such as 700-xx and 752-x), whose negatives passed to the **Schlern-Verlag**, and **Johann F. Amonn**. In Merano they were **Leo Bährendt** and Rudolf Stricker. The Pedrotti brothers were Trento-based: Enrico Pedrotti moved to Bolzano only in 1937, and his 1930s Bolzano work in the provincial archive is aerial photography of the new quarters. The "new city" postcards that exist (Ponte nuovo Druso, Stazione nuova, Zona Industriale, Lido, Stadio Druso, Monumento alla Vittoria) are digitised in OMA, mostly in the AFM's Schlern-Verlag holding. Postcards also record Italianisation physically: German names overstamped or struck through.

## Q6. Scholarly literature on fascist-era tourism promotion and poster art in South Tyrol

### Takeaway
There is no dedicated scholarly study of fascist-era tourist posters for Alto Adige that I could verify. The core references are the bilingual Lenhart catalogue (Festi/Gullo 2001), the Touriseum's publications and its review literature in *Geschichte und Region/Storia e regione*, Hans Heiss's tourism-history essays, Catrina Klee's ongoing Lucerne PhD on tourism as fascist strategic investment, and the general Italian literature on ENIT, Touring and fascist tourism (Bosworth). The OMA catalogue records themselves are, at present, the richest primary-source "literature".

### Inferences
- [INF] The gap in the literature is real: tourism history (Heiss, Touriseum) and fascism history (Lechner, Di Michele) in South Tyrol have not been joined at the level of the image. Klee's thesis is the nearest ongoing attempt, and is architecture- and infrastructure-led. A design studio working from the OMA records would be producing new knowledge.
