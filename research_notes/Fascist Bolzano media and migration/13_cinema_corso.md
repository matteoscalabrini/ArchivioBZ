## 1. Commission, programme and design (1937–38)

### Takeaway
The building was commissioned by the Bolzano tourist office (Azienda autonoma di soggiorno, president and later prefectural commissioner Comm. Dr. Guido Broise) from the engineer Armando Ronca, in 1937 by one account. Minister of Popular Culture Dino Alfieri approved it on his visit of 16 June 1938, and on 22 June 1938 Dolomiten printed the final design with a full description. The programme put every tourism body of the province under one roof (tourist office, provincial tourism board, the interprovincial hotel union, the Automobile Club) and added the concert and reception hall that Bolzano lacked. The Comune gave the plot. The press called it the first building of its kind in Italy.

### Cited Findings
- Dolomiten, 22 June 1938, p. 3, "Der Fremdenverkehrspalast in Bolzano": among the buildings of the "Denkmalzone" one of the most imposing will be "der Palast des Tourismus am Corso 9 Maggio". The concert hall, wanted by Broise because the theatre was too small, measures 30 × 13.60 m and will also serve "für große Empfänge". ### Inferences
- The Duca d'Aosta office appears in Schimenti and in Verdorfer (05 §2) but not in the official 1938 list of tenants. It may have been added later or been an unofficial use; the commune's building file (see §2) would settle it. [INF]
- The point of the building was propaganda as much as tourism: one ministerial approval (Popular Culture, not Public Works), one roof for every tourism body, and a hall for the "Monumentalzone". The press line "der erste Palast dieser Art von ganz Italien" made Bolzano a model for the rest of Italy. [INF]
- The 1938 description and the built building differ on cladding: the 1938 text gives St Bernard marble on the Corso arcades, while Schimenti, Franzmagazine and the photographs (rows 13.07, 13.15) show brick facing above the Corso arcade. [INF from the photos]

### Gaps
- No primary record of the 1937 commission or of the building commission's objection to the slender columns ("fiammiferi", 05 §2) was found in the press. The building file is in the Comune's archive (§2).
- The Italian-language *La Provincia di Bolzano* of 21–22 June 1938 did not show the design on the pages I looked at; its other June 1938 issues were not checked.

## 2. Building, 1938–43

### Takeaway
Work began on 10 August 1938. By November 1939 the shell was almost finished. The art exhibition of October 1940 opened in a building still being fitted out. "Bau und Ausstattung" were declared complete only in early November 1941, and Schimenti adds that the building could be formally approved ("collaudato") only in 1943. "Built 1938–40" (05 §2 and Wikipedia) is therefore the date of the shell, not of completion.

### Cited Findings
- Alpenzeitung, 5 November 1939, p. 5, "In der Monumentalzone. Die Errichtung des Palazzo del Turismo": "Der Bau, der vom Ing. Ronca projektiert ist, ist erheblich vorgeschritten. Die Mauerarbeiten sind fast vollendet"; the interior is to be finished over the winter, "so daß der Bau wahrscheinlich im Frühjahre bezogen werden kann". The tourist-office initiative, which "unter der Leitung des Präfekturskommissärs, Comm. Broise, einen neuen Antrieb erhielt", is called one "die in Italien zum ersten Male in die Tat umgesetzt wurde". Besides the offices, the palace will host events for visitors and residents and "einen großen Konzertsaal ... wo nicht nur Konzerte veranstaltet, sondern auch Kunstausstellungen untergebracht werden können". [V, read on the scan] (row 13.04) — [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043542); [Teßmann](https://digital.tessmann.it/tessmannDigital/Zeitungsarchiv/Tagesausgabe/Zeitung/26/05.11.1939)

## 3. The 9th syndical art exhibition of the Venezia Tridentina, 6 October–21 November 1940 (and a correction for 1942)

### Takeaway
The first public use of the building was the IX Mostra Sindacale d'Arte della Venezia Tridentina, opened on Sunday 6 October 1940 by the Minister of Corporations Renato Ricci, in a building whose atrium columns, vestibule and staircase were still unfinished. It showed about 500 works by 85 artists of the two provinces plus a guest group of Umbrian artists, with prize competitions on "spirit and events of the age of Mussolini", and it drew 20,572 visitors. The exhibition of 1942 (the XI) was **not** held here, as 05 §2 says after Bertoldi, but in the Istituto Tecnico "Cesare Battisti" in via Cadorna. By then the palace was the seat of the Option Commissariat (§4).

## 4. Tenants and uses, 1940–43: the Automobile Club, the tourist office, Bonatta's relief, the Option Commissariat

### Takeaway
The Automobile Club moved in on 20 December 1940; the tourist office had its entrance hall by October 1941, with a 15 m² terracotta relief of the Dolomites on the walls. In November 1941 the just-finished palace became the seat of the Commissariat for carrying out the Italo-German agreements for the Alto Adige, headed by Agostino Podestà: the office that administered the Option and resettlement. This use is new to the research base and ties the building to the Option thread.

### Cited Findings
- Alpenzeitung, 19 December 1940, p. 3: "der Provinzialsitz des RACI von Bolzano [wird] mit 20. ds. aus der Via Pietro Micca in den Corso 9 Maggio, Palast des Tourismus, verlegt"; Alpenzeitung, 31 December 1940, p. 5: the RACI offices "in Viale 9 Maggio, Palazzo del Turismo, verlegt worden". How long the Commissariat stayed is not known. [INF]
- The marble Music statue planned for the start of the staircase (competition of 1940) may never have been carved; nothing later mentions it. [INF]

### Gaps
- The Commissariat's stay (1941–43?) and whether it shared the building with the tourist office: look in the Option literature and the Landesarchiv's records of the Commissariato (lead table).

## 5. Under German occupation, 1943–45: the "Fremdenverkehrshaus"

### Takeaway
In the Operationszone Alpenvorland the building was renamed "Fremdenverkehrshaus" ("ex Palazzo Turismo") and the Corso became "9.-Mai-Straße", later "neue Grieser Hauptstraße". Its big hall served the occupiers and the German-speaking population as a stage well before December 1944, the date in 05 §2: rallies of the air-raid wardens (February 1944), concerts and guest plays of the Reichsgautheater Innsbruck (April–September 1944) and an evening of the Deutsche Volksgruppe with the Kreisleiter (June 1944). In January 1945 municipal offices moved in.

## 6. The Cinema-Teatro Corso, 1946–1980s

### Takeaway
In 1946 Ronca himself joined the concert and reception halls into one cinema-theatre, which opened as "Il Corso" (1946 or 1947). Two capacities are reported: 724 seats by Ronca's 1946 plan (Schimenti) and 801 (Bertoldi, 05 §2) or about 800 (Fondazione Teatro). Ronca refitted it for the wide screen in 1962. It was the largest and most modern hall of the region, the stage of the Teatro Stabile di Bolzano's first production in December 1950, and the place of German- and Italian-language theatre and music. A Foto Pedrotti print in the Province's film and media office shows it with the lettering "IL CORSO" and film posters.

## 7. The demolition and what replaced it

### Takeaway
The builder Pietro Tosolini, whose company is Generalbau S.p.A., wanted the building down in summer 1986 to make way for offices. Citizens, shopkeepers and the professional orders petitioned, and a request for protection went to the Soprintendenza, but the demolition went ahead "grazie a un cavillo giuridico". The year is still disputed: 1988 (most sources), 1980 (Schimenti), 1982 (Fondazione Teatro). **Settled since by the provincial council's minutes, read by hand on Teßmann: see section 12** (permit 15 February 1988, standing on 30 March 1988, demolished by August 1989). The "1987 in *Turris Babel* 73" cited in 05 §2 from a search summary is a misreading: that issue gives 1987 for Ronca's cinema at Siusi, not for the Corso. A provincial office building now stands on the site, Corso Libertà 23. Its landlord in 2025 was Generalbau S.p.A., which leases it to the South Tyrol health service; OpenStreetMap places the juvenile court there.

## 8. Armando Ronca

### Takeaway
Born in Verona on 13 September 1901 and died in Bolzano on 19 March 1970 (Wikipedia, citing the 2017 monograph). An engineer trained in Genoa, Turin and Padua, the son of a candle maker. He came to Bolzano through the competitions and state commissions of the mid-1930s: a prize in the April 1935 competition for Bolzano housing types, alongside Piccinato, Muratori and Montuori, then the EIAR radio station above Monticolo (opened October 1935). He opened a studio in Bolzano in 1935 and another in Milan. Before 1945 he worked for the regime's bodies: the Palazzo del Turismo, the building of the fascist dailies by Ponte Druso, and schools, a kindergarten and the town plan of Bressanone. After 1945 he became the leading modernist of the city: the Capitol, Palazzo Vanzo, the INA-Casa tower, the Eurotel chain from Merano, the San Siro extension and San Pio X. San Pio X was placed under protection in June 2022, and more of his buildings were being prepared for protection. A 1960s portrait is printed in *Turris Babel* 73.

### Takeaway

The Teßmann full-text search answers what the web could not. The word-for-word minutes of the South Tyrolean provincial council (Wortprotokolle Landtag, digitised and open) hold the whole legal history of the demolition, read out by the provincial councillor for planning on 30 March 1988, and name the developer. The Province refused to buy the cinema in 1980, annulled the first demolition permit in 1983 on grounds the Consiglio di Stato struck down in 1985, and issued the permit itself on 15 February 1988. The building was still standing when the council debated it in March 1988 and was "abgebrochen" by August 1989. The monthly *Tandem* of January 1985 carries the first two photographs of the building found anywhere in this research, in an essay by the architect Silvano Bassetti that also prints Ponte Druso before and after the eagles. For 1940–46 the newspapers give the building's first public use and its German names.

**How this settles section 7**

- **The year.** Not 1980, 1982 or 1987. The permit is of 15 February 1988, the council debated a standing building on 30 March 1988, and by 28 August 1989 it was "abgebrochen". [V]/[INF]
- **The "cavillo giuridico"** that Franzmagazine mentions has a name and a date: the Consiglio di Stato's decision no. 249 of 8 July 1985, which revived the 1983 permit because the Province had annulled it on architectural grounds. [INF from the two sources]
- **"Summer 1986".** Franzmagazine dates the wish to demolish to summer 1986. The council record has the demolition-and-rebuilding project under appeal on 16 March 1983 and permitted on 5 July 1983, three years earlier. 1986 may be when the petitions began; no source read says so. Both are kept. [V] against [S]
- **The developer's company.** In 1987–88 the applicant was "Co.Mo. Aktiengesellschaft, vertreten durch Geometer Tossolini Pietro"; Generalbau S.p.A. appears only as today's landlord (section 7). Whether Co.Mo. became or belonged to Generalbau is not established. [V]/[INF]
- **The petitions.** The Communists' question of 16 March 1988 says it acts "anche di sollecitazioni provenienti da professionisti, oltre che da altri ambienti sensibili alla questione", which fits Franzmagazine's petitions by citizens and professional orders. [V]
- **The tenant.** Franzmagazine has the provincial health department on the site and the health service leases Corso Libertà 23 in 2025; the council record adds that from 1 November 1990 the Comune paid rent for three floors "in der Freiheitsstraße" for the appeal court. [V]

### Inferences

- The demolition falls between April 1988 (Tribus in May: the affair was "vor ungefähr einem Monat") and 28 August 1989 (Holzmann: "abgebrochen"). 1988, as Verdorfer and Ferrandi give it, fits; 1987 does not, since the permit dates from 15 February 1988 and the council debated a standing building on 30 March 1988. [INF]
- The new building was finished by 1 November 1990, the date from which rent was paid for three floors "in der Freiheitsstraße" for the appeal court. Notes 05 has the site as the provincial health and social department (Verdorfer 2007). Both may be true of different floors or years; the address and present tenants need checking on the spot. [INF]
- In the council the building was defended on architectural grounds by the Communists and by the Greens of the Alternative Liste in 1988, each prefacing the defence with a condemnation of Fascism, and in 1989–90 by the neo-fascist MSI, which called the removal of the Ponte Druso sculpture and the loss of the Corso deliberate erasure of "arte razionalista". The governing SVP answered only with procedure. The same three objects (Ponte Druso, Cinema Corso, ex GIL) are already listed together in 1988–90 as one series of losses. [INF]
- The Province's 1983 annulment was a protection attempt in planning clothes, and the Consiglio di Stato said so: it was struck down for resting on "architektonischen und ästhetischen Gründen". No monument-protection order appears anywhere in the record read. [INF]
- "Landesrat Sfondrini (PSI)", addressed by Tribus on 18 May 1988, carries the surname of the cinema's manager "Giuseppe Sfondrini (socialist)" named by Ferrandi (notes 05). Whether it is the same man is not established. [INF]
- The street's names in sequence, from these pages: Corso IX Maggio / "Viale 9 Maggio" (1940), "9. Mai-Straße" (February 1944), "Neue Grieser Hauptstraße" (April 1946), then Corso Libertà / Freiheitsstraße. The 1946 name is new to the base. [INF]
