## 1. Teßmann digital, Alpenzeitung 29 Oct 1931, p. 3 [V]

URL: https://digital.tessmann.it/tessmannDigital/Zeitungsarchiv/Seite/Zeitung/26/1/29.10.1931/109184/3
Captures: files/07_browser_verifications/1931-10-29_*.

Headline [T]: "Die Feier des 9. Jahrestages der fascistischen Revolution in Bolzano. Eröffnung von Bauwerken des Regimes: Drususbrücke, städt. Badeanstalt, Palast der Krankenkasse, Rundfunksender der „Eiar", Elektrizitätswerk in Val d'Ega, Artilleriekaserne in Oltrisarco, Tramlinie von Vurza nach Laives usw. — Aushebung der Jungen Italienerinnen."

So 28 Oct 1931 was a bundled inauguration day: bridge, municipal baths, health-insurance palace, a new EIAR radio transmitter, the Val d'Ega power station, an artillery barracks in Oltrisarco and the Vurza–Laives tram line, plus the induction of the Giovani Italiane. This links the bridge to the radio thread (02): the EIAR station of 12 July 1928 got its upgraded transmitter here, which matches the 1932 listing at 1 kW in the Radiocorriere.

Ceremony, column 1 [T]: grey sky and rain; at 9 a.m. the Prefect (S. E. Marziali) with the federal secretary and the other authorities arrived at the Drususbrücke, "wo er vom Erbauer Ing. Cav. Albini und dem technischen Stab empfangen wurde". With a scissor cut the tricolour ribbon fell, the passage was opened and the new bridge was handed to traffic. The Bauleiter gave explanations of the new construction. From the bridge the party drove to the new large bridge at Prato all'Isarco, where a symbolic ceremony of opening and consecration of the road works took place; on the way back the electric central of the Merano and Bolzano electricity works in the Eggental was opened.

Note the builder's name: Albini. The secondary sources used in 05 name the engineers Eugenio Miozzi and Gilberto Ricci only. Albini may be the site engineer or the contractor's engineer [INF]; worth a check in the Archivio di Stato di Bolzano Genio Civile files.

"Werke des Regimes — Die Drususbrücke" column [T], the technical sheet:
- Connects the city with the new quarter of San Quirino and is the start of the new road to Val Gardena ("Anfang für die neue Straße nach Val Gardena").
- Reinforced concrete ("Eisenbeton") faced in porphyry, with pier statues of Andriano porphyry.
- Length 102.30 m, three arches: middle span 34.70 m, the two side arches 21.65 m.
- Corner piers 20 x 10 m, founded 6 m deep. Pier height from riverbed to the eagle figures 15.10 m; from road level 8.10 m.
- Iconography: the corner piers carry four helmets with a Roman shield resting on the impost; below them swords, points up and points down, which "mark the beginning and the end of the war". The columns with the Roman eagles rest on globes carried by the fascist lictor's bundle and a base of laurel leaves carved in stone.
- Roadway 14 m, with 2 m sidewalks each side; roadway paved in porphyry cubes, sidewalks asphalted, expansion joints over the piers.
- Railing of Gardena porphyry, 1.20 m high.
- Cost 3,000,000 lire, of which 800,000 contributed by the Comune di Bolzano.
- Works began 9 January 1930 and were interrupted several times by weather.
- Built because the Merano and Mendola roads were being diverted into the city; a new road 10 to 18 m wide toward Castelfirmiano joins the existing road about 2 km from the bridge, avoiding the curves of the Bolzano–Castelfirmiano and Bolzano–Terlano roads.

The regime's own paper thus describes the eagles as explicitly fascist: eagles on globes on fasces, with the war framed as begun and ended by the swords. This settles the "Roman or fascist" question raised in 05: both, by design.

## 2. Teßmann digital, Alpenzeitung 26 Sep 1933, p. 3, "Zwei Jahre Drusus-Brücke" [V]

URL: https://digital.tessmann.it/tessmannDigital/Zeitungsarchiv/Seite/Zeitung/26/1/26.09.1933/110069/3
Captures: files/07_browser_verifications/1933-09-26_*.

- Page title "Aus Bolzano Stadt und Land". The article repeats the figures (102.3 m, 34.7 m middle span, 21.65 m side spans, 14 m roadway, 2 m sidewalks) and calls the bridge one of the monumental works of the last years, needed for the new Bolzano–Castelfirmiano road and the shortening of the Merano road via S. Maurizio.
- Romanità passage [T]: "Die Brücke trägt den Namen des römischen Feldherrn Drusus, der 15 Jahre vor Christi Geburt mit seinen Legionen unsere Alpengebiete eroberte, Straßen zog, Siedlungen gründete, römische Kultur in die von Sümpfen und Wildbächen durchtobten Täler trug. Er schlug auch die erste Brücke bei Castelfirmiano über die Etsch, um den Straßenzug gegen Norden zu führen."
- The halftone photo shows the bridge with the eagle columns in place and a figure on the near column: a usable 1933 press image of the eagles (study copy saved).

## 4. FamilySearch, Rovigo civil registration [V]

URL: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/1482864
Collection: "Italy, Rovigo, Rovigo, Civil Registration (Tribunale), 1871-1937". Births, marriages, deaths held by the Tribunale di Rovigo, with allegati, processetti, pubblicazioni, indici decennali, cittadinanze, atti diversi. Record type: Vital. 826,900 records, 2,761,092 personas, 1,082,856 images, index partly by Computer Aided Indexing. Citation as given by the site: "Italia, Rovigo, Rovigo, Stato Civile (Tribunale), 1871-1937", database with images, FamilySearch, 12 September 2026.

Related collections confirmed on the same page: Treviso (Tribunale) 1871–1941; Venezia (Tribunale) 1871–1930; Pordenone 1871–1911. Padova was not shown on this page and stays at the status given in 04 (search index only).

This confirms the key step of the tracing guide in 04: a family that left the Polesine for the Semirurali in 1937–40 is searchable by name, free, from the Rovigo tribunal registers, then followed in Bolzano through the Comune's historical residence certificate.
