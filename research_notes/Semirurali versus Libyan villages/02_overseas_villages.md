## 1. Libya: the agencies, the 1938 "Ventimila", the 1939 wave, and the full village list with namesakes

### Takeaway
Libyan settlement ran on a three-part machine. A parastatal agency (the ECC of 1932, the ECL from 1935) or the INFPS divided the land, built the farmhouses and managed the families. The colonial public-works office designed and built the "centro rurale". The Commissariato per le Migrazioni e la Colonizzazione Interna selected the families in Italy. Capresi catalogues 23 Italian centres actually built (1933-1939), one 1940 project (Borgo Torelli) and 8 villages for Muslim Libyans. Four of the Italian centres carry irredentist names: Battisti and Oberdan (1938), Filzi and Sauro (1939). None is named after Damiano Chiesa.

### Cited Findings

**Agencies and law**
- The Ente di Colonizzazione della Cirenaica (ECC) was created by RDL 11 June 1932 n.696 (converted by L. 3 April 1933 n.441). It was placed under "la presidenza dello stesso Commissario per le Migrazioni Interne, on. Luigi Razza". Its presidency sat in Rome, a Direzione compartimentale in Barce, and "Uffici di Zona" ran the comprensori. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.51 n.15-16](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The ECC's funders included the Commissariato per le Migrazioni e la Colonizzazione interna, the Banco di Napoli and Banco di Sicilia, INA, the INFPS, the INAIL, the BNL and others. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.51 n.17](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Division of labour: the Government gave tax-free land and built roads, aqueducts, wells and "costruzione dei centri rurali". The Ente reclaimed the land, divided it into lots, built the farmhouses and assisted the families. The price of each podere (advances, lot, house, livestock, works) was to be "riscattato" by the family under the colonial contract. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.52](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- RDL 11 Oct 1934 n.2038 extended the ECC to Tripolitania. RD 26 Sept 1935 n.2283 renamed it "Ente per la Colonizzazione della Libia" (ECL). [S] [Capresi 2007, p.56 n.31](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The INFPS (Istituto Nazionale Fascista della Previdenza Sociale) worked only in Tripolitania, from late 1935 (art. 84, L. 4 Oct 1935 n.1827). It used unemployment funds and took families "dalle regioni dove la disoccupazione era più alta". Its first comprensorio was Bir Terrina: 5,381 ha, later 6,121.5 ha, divided into 167 poderi of about 25 ha around the village "Michele Bianchi". In 1937 it set up an "Ufficio per la colonizzazione demografica" in Rome under Ennio Bartocci, with a Direzione in Tripoli under Alberto Stern. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.53-54 and n.24](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- RDL 7 June 1928 n.1695, art.1: "I terreni del patrimonio demaniale in Tripolitania ed in Cirenaica, sono concessi per l'avvaloramento agricolo diretto al popolamento dei fondi con famiglie di contadini italiani". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.50](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- RDL 17 May 1938 n.701, "Provvedimenti per un piano di colonizzazione demografica intensiva in Libia". From then on the state supplied land and all capital, and ECL and INFPS became "organi ausiliari della Pubblica Amministrazione". The decree of 13 Feb 1939 ("Nuovi provvedimenti per favorire la colonizzazione demografica intensiva") raised the state grant to 33% and added a programme for Muslim villages. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.56, 62](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- RDL 9 Jan 1939 n.70 made the four coastal provinces (Tripoli, Misurata, Bengasi, Derna) part of the metropolitan territory of the Kingdom, the "Quarta Sponda". [S, Wikipedia] [Italian Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya); [Libia italiana](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libia_italiana)

**The 1938 "Ventimila"**
- Balbo announced the programme in May 1938 and fixed the departure for 28 Oct 1938, the 16th anniversary of the March on Rome. In six months 10,000 Italian and 23,000 Libyan workers built 1,800 poderi and their centres. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.58](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Of 70,000 ha, 55,000 went to the ECL (35,000 in Cyrenaica, 20,000 in Tripolitania) for 1,400 families and 15,000 to the INFPS for 400 families. Lots ranged "dai 50 ai 20 ha". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.58](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- On 29 Oct 1938 nine steamers left Genoa. Six more took the southern settlers on board on 30 Oct at Naples, and the last at Syracuse. They arrived at Tripoli on 2 Nov. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.60](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf). Pergher gives the ports as Genoa, Naples and Syracuse, with "a further 20,000 slated to set sail ... every year for the next four years". [S] [Pergher 2018 excerpt, p.7](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/19741/excerpt/9781108419741_excerpt.pdf)

**The 1939 wave and the aborted 1940 wave**
- Ciano's diary, 7 Nov 1938: "Il Duce si è seccato per lo strombazzamento fatto da Balbo ... Per l'avvenire questi coloni partiranno in piccoli scaglioni", citing Littoria peasants who had refused to work. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.61](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The convoys left on 28 and 30 Oct 1939 from Venice, Naples and Palermo and reached Ras Hilal on 2 Nov, then sailed on to Tripoli and Benghazi. There were 1,465 families and 10,907 persons. The plan had been 196,000 ha and 3,000 poderi. In fact 60,000 ha were divided: 31,000 ha to the ECL (898 poderi) and 28,700 ha to the INFPS (540 poderi). [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.61-62](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Luce B1614, 08/11/1939: "Arrivano a Venezia i dieci treni speciali". The settlers are blessed by the Patriarch in Piazza San Marco, with Balbo present, and leave on three Lloyd Triestino steamers. [V] [IL5000023085](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000023085/2/arrivano-venezia-i-dieci-treni-speciali-nuove-famiglie-coloni-venuti-imbarcarsi-libia.html)
- Luce B1616: 165 Sicilian families leave Palermo, "spedizione degli undicimila". [V] [IL5000023134](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000023134/2/partenza-quarta-sponda-165-famiglie-coloni-siciliani-partecipanti-alla-spedizione-degli-undicimila.html)
- Luce B1618: 860 families land at Tripoli for "Crispi", "Marconi", "Pietro Micca", "Giordani", "Garibaldi", "Tazzoli", "Corradini" and "Garabulli". [V] [IL5000023357](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000023357/2/seconda-migrazione-sulla-quarta-sponda-si-e-compiuta-lo-sbarco-tripoli-dai-piroscafi-sardegna-piemonte-e-liguria-860-famiglie.html)
- 1940: the plan shrank to 300 ECL poderi in Cyrenaica plus 100 INFPS poderi, then to 40 near Borgo Torelli. Of these, 24 were put out to contract and 11 houses finished. No family ever arrived. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.63 and n.45; card p.75](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Totals: the 1937 agricultural census counted 2,711 Italian farm families (12,488 persons) in all Libya. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.55](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf). At the end of 1939 the ECL held 2,817 poderi on 88,577 ha for 2,716 families. In 1940 the INFPS held 45,000 ha in 1,029 poderi for about 1,000 families (about 6,000 persons). Both figures come from Cresti, via Ertola. [S] [Ertola, p.76 n.474](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf). Pergher says "close to 30,000 Italians being settled" against a target of 500,000. [S] [Pergher excerpt, p.7](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/19741/excerpt/9781108419741_excerpt.pdf). The 1939 census counted 108,419 Italians in Libya (12.37%), most of them urban. [S, Wikipedia] [Italian Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya)
- The chief engineer Silvio Camilletti reported in Feb 1940 that since the second half of 1938 the OO.PP. had built "ventiquattro nuovi villaggi completi". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.77](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)

### Inferences
- The namesakes fall into recognisable groups [INF]:
  - fascist squad "martyrs" and party founders of 1919-22: Berta, Giordani, Breviglieri, Gioda, Bianchi;
  - aviators: Baracca, Maddalena, Oliveti;
  - Risorgimento and irredentist martyrs: Mameli, Garibaldi, Tazzoli, Micca, Battisti, Filzi, Sauro, Oberdan;
  - colonial ideologues: Crispi, Corradini, D'Annunzio;
  - the colonisation bureaucracy and the dynasty: Razza, Luigi di Savoia;
  - one general (Torelli) and one scientist (Marconi).
- Three of the four irredentist names sit in ECL comprensori in Cyrenaica (Oberdan, Filzi, Sauro in Bengasi province; Battisti in Derna). Filzi lies next to Baracca, and Sauro is an extension of Maddalena. [INF]
- Battisti and Filzi, two of the three busts in the crypt of Bolzano's Victory Monument, both have Libyan villages. Chiesa, the third, does not. [INF]
- The decree naming Filzi and Sauro (11 Aug 1939) falls inside the South Tyrol Option period (agreement June 1939, deadline 31 Dec 1939). That is a timing coincidence, not a documented link. [INF]
- The Libyan names reproduce a national pantheon, not regional identities. They are the opposite of the Ethiopian "Romagna/Puglia/Veneto d'Etiopia" logic (section 4). [INF]

- The text extraction cut the Berta namesake card short. It is presumably Giovanni Berta (Florence 1921), but this is not verified here.
- I found no Libyan village named after Damiano Chiesa in Capresi's 32 cards or elsewhere.
- I did not view the Luce films, only their catalogue sequence lists. Codes and URLs are in `files/02_overseas_villages/luce_records/`.
- I could not download Santoianni's 2008 thesis (Napoli), which has more on the villages. Open by hand: http://www.fedoa.unina.it/1881/1/Santoianni_Progettazione_Architettonica.pdf

## 2. Architects, offices, the village programme, house types, plot sizes, materials, published plans; Casa del Fascio and piazza

### Takeaway
The Libyan "centro rurale" was a non-residential service core, defined by statute (RDL 1938 art.1). The provincial Public Works offices designed and built it, under a chief engineer (Camilletti). Contracted architects signed the perspectives:
- Mario Romano for the four Cyrenaican villages of 1933-34;
- Di Fausto, Di Segni and Pellegrini for 1938;
- Longarini, Di Fausto, Pellegrini and Di Segni for 1939 (Di Segni co-signed Tazzoli).

Farmhouses were single-storey, one- or two-family, and built by the agencies on lots of 10-50 ha. They were clustered before 1938 and placed in isolation on each lot after 1938, explicitly to bind the settler to "his" land. Luigi Piccinato does not appear in any attribution I found.

### Cited Findings

**Offices and procedure**
- The centres were designed and built by the provincial Ufficio Opere Pubbliche. From 1 July 1935 this became the "Ufficio Centrale OO.PP. della Libia" under ing. Silvio Camilletti, with sections at Tripoli, Misurata, Bengasi and Derna. Lotting and farmhouses belonged to ECL or INFPS. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.75-77](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The offices were overloaded, so the Ufficio centrale hired freelance architects. The 1938 perspectives carry the signatures of Pellegrini, Di Fausto and Di Segni. Construction was let to local firms and checked by the technical office. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.78, 81](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The Governor approved "bonifica" projects between L.30,000 and L.5,000,000. Projects above that went to the Ministry, after the opinion of the Consiglio Superiore dei LL.PP. Two contract examples: the Tazzoli capitolato (L.1,100,000, signed by Camilletti) and the Muslim village Fiorita (contractor SAIDA, L.1,425,000). [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.78-79 and n.25](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)

**Architects**
- **Mario Romano** headed OO.PP. Cirenaica from 5.5.1930 to 15.5.1935. His staff file credits him with the "progettazione e costruzione di 4 villaggi sul Gebel". The office had 80 staff: 9 engineers, 30 geometri, 30 assistants and 11 draughtsmen. The only plans are the ones published in *Rassegna* 1935. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.79-80](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- **Giovanni Pellegrini** trained in Milan and was in private practice in Tripoli from around 1930. In 1936 he wrote the "primo manifesto dell'architettura coloniale", presented at the Congresso Nazionale degli architetti and published in *Rassegna di Architettura* 1936. His traces disappear after 1939. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.81-82](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- **Umberto Di Segni** was Jewish, born in Tripoli on 6 Oct 1894 (or in Tunisia) and domiciled in Livorno. Until 1938 the annuari list him "a disposizione dell'ingegnere capo dei lavori pubblici per incarichi speciali", and he is absent from the 1939-40 annuario. He sat on the Tripoli Jewish community's commission from 1937 to 1941 and emigrated to Israel after the war. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.82](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- **Florestano Di Fausto** (Rocca Canterano 1890 - Rome 1965):
  - In 1932 he became head of the Commissione d'Arte e Edilità Coloniale, after Limongelli's death.
  - From 21 Feb 1934 (DG n.81) he advised on every public and private building in Tripoli and the colony. The contract was renewed in 1939 for five years.
  - He never moved his office from Rome.
  - INFPS farmhouse drawings for Bianchi are stamped "visto Di Fausto", which means review, not authorship.

  [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.83-84](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf). Before Libya he worked in the Dodecanese (the Rhodes master plan) and in Nuova Predappio. [S, Wikipedia] [Florestano Di Fausto](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florestano_Di_Fausto)
- **Alfredo Longarini** came from Perugia (Accademia di Belle Arti, Florence, 1930) and was an "architetto a contratto statale". His signatures are on the 1939 axonometries published in *Libia* (Oct 1939). In the early 1950s he reused the Garibaldi church scheme at Pilonico Paterno (Perugia). [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.84-85](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Attributions by village are in the table in section 1. Crispi is "l'unico in Libia nato dalla collaborazione di due architetti" (Di Segni and Pellegrini). [S] [Capresi 2007, card p.50](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Style, per Wikipedia: "linguaggio razionalista declinato in senso mediterraneo, ispirato all'architettura spontanea (volumi semplici, portici, terrazze, scale esterne) oppure con accenti vernacolari padani come nel villaggio 'D'Annunzio'". [S, Wikipedia] [Città di fondazione](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citt%C3%A0_di_fondazione_nel_periodo_fascista)
- Capresi reads the pitched roofs at D'Annunzio as a "ponte semantico" for peasants "provenienti dagli Abruzzi". She also notes that the churches quote Italian models (Crispi recalls Mantua's Sant'Andrea), to make the village "familiare". [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.118, 132-133](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)

**The programme (statutory)**
- RDL 17 May 1938 n.701, art.1, defines the centro rurale as: "un nucleo di edifici destinati ad assicurare servizi pubblici o di pubblica utilità, costituito dalla Chiesa con la Canonica, dalle scuole e dalle abitazioni degli insegnanti, dall'ambulatorio medico con gli accessori e l'abitazione dei sanitari, dalla Casa del fascio e dalle organizzazioni del Regime, dalla ricevitoria postale e dai mercati, nonché, ove necessario, dalle sedi ... dell'autorità di Governo, del Municipio o dei Reali Carabinieri". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.73](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The centres were not inhabited by the farmers. Capresi says it would be more accurate to speak of a "centro logistico o direzionale". The Pontine triad "podere - borgo - città" shrinks in Libya (and on the Aegean islands) to "podere - centro rurale", with informal "borgate rurali" in between. Settlement plans ran year by year ("learning by doing"), but each centre was frozen in a closed form at birth. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.71-74](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Tigrinna set the model. The church stood on a slight rise so its campanile could be seen all around. Around it were a school with teachers' housing, the priest's house, a dopolavoro, a consumer cooperative and the agency's buildings. Everything faced "una piazza chiaramente perimetrata da un muro". "I centri rurali realizzati dal 1934 ... seguirono questo stesso modello". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.69](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Capresi's plan types (ch.5): closed space; arcades; piazza closed on four sides with a perpendicular access road (central or at a corner); piazza closed on three sides; omega piazza; castrum; terrain-led schemes. Borgo Torelli takes the closed piazza to its extreme, "la planimetria ingrandita di un edificio a corte interna ... dilatato alla scala urbana", with a cascade of piazzas from the church down to the market. [S] [Capresi 2007, contents p.2; p.87](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Battisti, worked example (Di Fausto): a rectangle parallel to the road with an off-axis void as the piazza and the church and campanile set back. The buildings are church and canonica, municipio, school with teachers' lodgings, market, post office, Casa del Fascio (external stair, balcony on the road side) and an ECL office. Di Fausto designed the fountain, and Mauro Reggiani frescoed the church of Sant'Antonio. [S] [Capresi 2007, card pp.36-37](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf). Photo (public domain): [Villaggio Battisti](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Villaggio_Battisti_1.jpg) [V, metadata]
- Garibaldi, worked example (Longarini): a rectangular piazza parallel to the coast road, with every building linked by round arches and of one storey. Only the church and the Casa del Fascio/municipio rise higher, and the church alone has a pitched tiled roof. It also has a post office with lodging, a school with teachers' lodgings, an ambulatorio and a covered market. [S] [Capresi 2007, card p.73](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)

**Casa del Fascio and piazza**
- There was no standard plan. The fixed elements were the tower, the arengario and the lictor symbols, with a piazza for rallies in front. In Libya the building served a farming public, "più come un dopolavoro". In villages on the coast road it faced the road, "così da fare pubblicità di sé ... verso l'esterno". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.140](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Di Fausto's range [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.140-142](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf):
  - Oliveti: no separate building; the fascio sits in the one-storey wing left of the church.
  - Mameli: entered through an inner court.
  - Battisti: balcony on the inner piazza.
  - Maddalena and D'Annunzio: balcony and symbols turned outward to the access road.
  - Oberdan: "l'edificio più canonico".
- The Casa del Fascio drawings for Bianchi (INFPS) are in AS INPS, fasc. 35. [S] [Capresi 2007, card p.26 n.34](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)

**House types, plot sizes, materials**
- 1933-34 Cyrenaica: the farmhouses were grouped "in gruppi di 4 fino a 6 case doppie, quindi da 8 fino a 16 famiglie", for security, with a shared spring and well. After the 1938 law the house stood alone on its lot, "con lo specifico scopo di avvicinare anche psicologicamente il colono al proprio terreno". [S] [Capresi 2007, card pp.11-12](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- At Maddalena houses stood in fours on the lot perimeters, "per la prima volta come abitazioni singole". At Marconi the governor ordered them placed at the centre of the lot ("baricentriche al lotto"), each with a 50 m³ rain cistern. [S] [Capresi 2007, card pp.21, 70](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Plans [S] [Capresi 2007, card pp.45, 50, 80](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf):
  - Giordani (INFPS): "tre camere e una cucina, un gabinetto con doccia, un magazzino, stalla e fienile, forno, e una concimaia".
  - Crispi (ECL): "4 stanze e tutti i servizi secondari ... stalla, ... magazzino, il porcile, il forno, una concimaia" and an irrigation network.
  - Muslim Fiorita: three rooms, WC and an enclosed courtyard on 2 ha.
- Wikipedia describes the farmhouses as "unifamiliari o bifamiliari, ad un solo piano, posti direttamente sui singoli appezzamenti". [S, Wikipedia] [Città di fondazione](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citt%C3%A0_di_fondazione_nel_periodo_fascista)
- Model lot layouts [S] [Capresi 2007, card pp.5-6](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf):
  - Cyrenaica, 30 ha: specialised olive 7 ha, almond 2, vine 1, orchard 0.5, simple arable 18.5, unproductive (house, roads) 1.
  - Tripolitania irrigated (Crispi, Gioda), 10-15 ha.
  - Tripolitania semi-irrigated (Bianchi, Giordani, Micca, Corradini...), about 25 ha with 4-5 ha irrigated.
  - Tripolitania dry (Breviglieri, Marconi, Tazzoli), about 50 ha.
- Livestock per Cyrenaican family: 2 Maremma cows, 2 oxen, a horse, a pig, 10 hens and a cock, rabbits, plus plough, harrow, mower, seeder and cart. [S] [Capresi 2007, card p.5](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Materials: the extracted text says almost nothing about construction. Capresi mentions "il bianco uniforme dell'intonaco" unifying the quotations, and roof tiles and coppi only on the Garibaldi church. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.135; card p.73](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)

### Inferences
- Toolkit match with the Semirurali, item by item [INF]:
  - Libya: the agency builds the house and the public office builds the collective core. In Bolzano the IFACP built both, so the split is less clean (sibling to confirm).
  - Libya: single-storey farmhouse on 10-50 ha. Bolzano: two-storey worker house with a garden plot, which is the "semirurale" compromise between the farm-on-its-podere and urban housing.
  - Libya: the post-1938 "house isolated on its own lot" principle has a psychological rationale ("avvicinare il colono al proprio terreno"). It is the overseas version of the ruralist premise behind the orto in Bolzano.
- The Libyan centre is a non-residential civic node: church, Casa del Fascio, school, ambulatorio, market, post office. If the Rione Dux had a civic core (church, Casa del Fascio/GIL, school), it repeats the programme. If it was housing only, it lacks the "centro". Sibling to check. [INF]
- "Borgata" is used in both places: Libyan "borgate rurali" (Tazzoli, Corradini, Micca, Filzi, Sauro) and Roman "borgate". The word may be worth testing against Bolzano usage. [INF]

### Gaps
- Luigi Piccinato: I found no evidence that he designed any Libyan rural centre. He does not appear among Capresi's attributions.
- I found no dimensioned farmhouse plans (m², wall build-up, roof). Capresi points to the AS INPS fasc. 35 farmhouse typologies and a 1939 capitolato for a lot of farmhouses (thesis p.26 description of archives), not reproduced in the text I read. The ONC Ethiopian house was 60 m² (section 4).
- Construction materials and techniques (stone, tufa, concrete frame, roof build-up) are not documented in what I read. Santoianni 2008 and Gresleri, Massaretti and Zagnoni (eds.), *Architettura italiana d'oltremare 1870-1940* (1993) are the next places to look.

## 3. Settlers: numbers, provinces of origin, selection rules, contracts, and the regime's vocabulary

### Takeaway
The settlers were chosen as whole, large peasant families: literate, party members, physically and morally screened. Fascist federations and the agricultural union nominated them, and the Commissariato per le Migrazioni e la Colonizzazione (CMCI) made the final choice. Both mass waves were dominated by the Veneto and the Po delta. In the 1939 list the Veneto provinces alone are about 55% of the families listed, and with Udine about 62%. Ownership was meant to come after about 20-40 years of amortisation, after a salaried phase and a mezzadria phase, with a state grant of 30-33%.

### Cited Findings

**Selection**
- Selection was run by the Commissariato per le Migrazioni Interne, "che aveva già ricoperto un ruolo fondamentale nella colonizzazione della Pianura Pontina", which picked the "fanteria rurale". Balbo (1939, p.17): "La colonizzazione intensiva della Libia è quintessenza di fascismo e deve essere compiuta da fascisti di fede sicura. Selezione fisica e morale adunque: binomio inscindibile". Families had to be large, hardened peasants, literate and party members. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.58](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The 1938 procedure, per *L'Avvenire di Tripoli* of 23 Oct 1938:
  1. The local Unione dei lavoratori dell'agricoltura, on behalf of the Federazione dei Fasci, collected applications (over 500 in one province).
  2. A first selection went to the CMCI.
  3. Each family had a medical examination and "raccolta delle informazioni politiche, professionali e morali", plus criminal-record checks.
  4. A CMCI commission visited each family at home.

  [S] [Capresi 2007, p.58 n.36](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- 1939: families were chosen in regions "che ... presentano un maggiore indice di pressione demografica". The Federazioni fasciste certified them as "più bisognose" and checked "dovuti requisiti tecnici e sociali", and the CMCI made the final selection. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.61 n.41](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)

**Origins**
- 1938 (Segrè, via Grosselli): the commissions examined 6,000 families. "Quasi l'80% veniva dal nord. Il Veneto, e specialmente le zone attorno a Padova, Venezia, Rovigo e Treviso, diede il maggior contributo di famiglie. La Ferrara di Balbo fu al 4° posto con 135 famiglie." *Il Brennero* counted 35 provinces of origin. [S] [Grosselli 2023, p.371 and n.](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- 1938, partial list (*L'Avvenire di Tripoli*, 23 Oct 1938): Ferrara 145; Verona 106; Polesine (Rovigo, Adria) 218; Foggia 30; Bergamo 13; Bolognese 27; Mantova 45; Abruzzi 90 (Pescara 22, Chieti 23, Teramo 8, Aquila 39). Capresi adds that most came from the north, with 350 persons from Mantua. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.133 n.19](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- 1939, full list as quoted (*L'Avvenire di Tripoli*, 26 Oct 1939): Padova 203, Treviso 193, Venezia 166, Udine 102, Vicenza 83, Verona 81, Mantova 32, Brescia 25, Belluno 18, Trento 7, Benevento 48, Aquila 47, Avellino 41, Campobasso 34, Napoli 28, Chieti 26, Frosinone 23, Pescara 21, Teramo 7, Palermo 29, Ragusa 27, Messina 22, Siracusa 21, Caltanissetta 19, Catania 15, Agrigento 13, Enna 13, Trapani 10, Modena 2, Catanzaro 1, Cuneo 1. "TOTALE 1465 famiglie con 10.907 componenti". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.62 n.41](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Earlier settlers:
  - Tigrinna, 1931: 22 Abruzzese families. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.70](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
  - Beda, April 1933: first 7 families. Sept-Oct 1933: 150 heads of family on three months' trial, joined by their families in early 1934 if found "idonei". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.53](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
  - 1932-33 Cyrenaica: 513 families, "in maggioranza pugliesi" (Lazzarini). [S] [Grosselli 2023, p.368](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
  - Luce photos from May 1938 show Beda Littoria colonists "in abiti tradizionali pugliesi" performing for the King. [V, catalogue] [Luce IL3000023964](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000023964/12/nel-villaggio-beda-littoria-gruppo-coloni-abiti-tradizionali-pugliesi-offre-spettacolo-musiche-e-danze-al-re-vittorio-emanuele-e.html)
- Trentino families. On 28 Oct 1938 *Il Brennero* reported 5 Trentino families (32 persons) leaving for Cyrenaica: Brugnara Partel and Pasolli from San Michele all'Adige, Leone and Matteotti from Dro, Valenti from Bondo-Breguzzo. In 1939 more Trentini (from Dro, Roveré della Luna, Giovo, Spormaggiore, Ton) sailed on the Tembien with the Trentino vice-federale Scannagatta. [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.371-375](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)

**Contracts**
- The 1938 regime gave a non-repayable state grant of about 30% of the total podere cost. The rest was to be amortised "dai 30 ai 40 anni circa" at minimal interest. The 1939 decree raised the grant to 33%. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.57, 62](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Settlers in the same comprensorio held different contracts: the 1928 law contracts and the 1938 "contratto unificato". [S] [Capresi 2007, card p.10 n.7](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Crispi and Gioda contract:
  1. two years as wage earners, also used on reclamation work;
  2. three years of mezzadria with the produce split in half;
  3. annual amortisation of the podere cost (survey, roads, house furnishing, 4 cows, a mule, a pig, poultry, clearing, plantations), less the 33% grant.

  The family was expected to own the podere "in 19 anni". [S] [Capresi 2007, card pp.49-50](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Other terms: Tigrinna, ownership after 30 years. In general the transfer of land and house "in media durava 30 anni circa" (ECL monographs on Crispi-Gioda 1939, Beda 1940, Breviglieri 1940). [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.70-71 n.4](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- Arrival kit as reported in the press: "farina, paglia, legna, patate, riso, pomodoro ... petrolio, lampade, fiammiferi, latte". In fact this meant 10 kg of potatoes and of rice, 10 tins of tomatoes, a flask of oil, a flask of kerosene and 5 boxes of matches. [S] [Grosselli 2023, p.371](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)

**Vocabulary** (every term is quoted in the sources)
- "colonizzazione demografica intensiva" is the title of RDL 1938 n.701. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.56](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- "popolamento dei fondi con famiglie di contadini italiani" comes from RDL 1928 n.1695 art.1. [S] [Capresi 2007, p.50](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- "fissare" families to the land (Maugini 1927). Balbo: "masse di braccianti agricoli ... vengano fissate alla terra ... Questo tipo di colonizzazione realizza veramente la famosa formula: la terra ai contadini". [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.48, 71](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- "fanteria rurale" / "fanterie rurali". ## 4. Ethiopia and Eritrea: the regional colonization bodies (1937-1939) and the ONC

### Takeaway
On 6 Dec 1937 three regional colonization bodies were created by decree-law: Romagna d'Etiopia (n.2300), Puglia d'Etiopia (n.2325) and Veneto d'Etiopia (n.2314). Next to them the Opera Nazionale Combattenti, the Agro Pontino agency, ran two veteran-farmer comprensori near Addis Ababa. The regional bodies built little:
- Romagna had fewer than 100 settlers and no families by 1940.
- Puglia's "Bari d'Etiopia" in the Harar region had about 104 settlers including families, out of 276 people employed.
- The Veneto body's programme ("Veneto e Marche") never started.
- The ONC's Olettà and Biscioftù had 92 houses by January 1939 and 425 persons.

Naming African land after an Italian region or city ("Bari d'Etiopia") is the clearest case of transplanted regional identity in the whole programme.

### Cited Findings
- Legal titles [V]:
  - Romagna d'Etiopia: R.D.L. 6 Dec 1937 n.2300, "Ente di colonizzazione di Romagna d'Etiopia", converted by L. 15 Apr 1938 n.683 and amended by L. 4 Dec 1939 n.2107. [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1940/02/02/039U2107/CONSOLIDATED/20091216). Put into liquidation by a 1951 bill presented by De Gasperi as Minister ad interim for Italian Africa. [Senato N.1787, 1951](https://www.parlamento.it/service/PDF/PDFServer/DF/361972.pdf)
  - Puglia d'Etiopia: R.D.L. 6 Dec 1937 n.2325, "costitutivo dell'Ente di colonizzazione di Puglia d'Etiopia". Art.7 was amended by RDL 5 Sep 1938 n.1607, converted by L. 30 Dec 1938 n.2210. [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1939/03/08/038U2210/CONSOLIDATED/20091216)
  - Veneto d'Etiopia: R.D.L. 6 Dec 1937 n.2314, "costitutivo dell'Ente di colonizzazione del Veneto d'Etiopia", art.7 amended by L. 13 May 1940 n.823. [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1940/07/17/040U0823/CONSOLIDATED/20091216)
- The three bodies, publicly financed, were to settle "famiglie contadine provenienti dalle rispettive regioni di pertinenza (Romagna, Puglia, Veneto)". The Veneto programme ("Ente per la colonizzazione demografica 'Veneto e Marche', programma di massima") was "mai avviato". It aimed to transfer "nuclei omogenei di coloni" from "famiglie di autentici contadini, manovali, operai ed artigiani": 1,500 heads of family over six years, re-creating "un ambiente simile a quello lasciato". Natives with land rights were to stay and be "gradualmente assorbiti come manodopera". [S] [Ertola, pp.74-75 and n.465](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- Regime of work: settlers were "inquadrati militarmente" in cohorts of the MVSN Legione Lavoratori with two years' compulsory service. They were salaried for two years (18/20/23 lire a day), then the best were given a podere and joined by their families, working under the Ente until the debt was redeemed (Romagna programme). [S] [Ertola, p.75 n.466-468](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- Numbers [S] [Ertola, p.26 and n.140-149](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf):
  - ONC comprensori near Addis Ababa, 1939: 425 persons.
  - Ente Romagna: fewer than 100 settlers, "nessuno dei quali raggiunto dalla famiglia".
  - Ente Puglia: 104 settlers including families.
  - Ente De Rege: 41 families.
  - Gimma consortium: 14 families.
  - The official 1940 total was about 3,000 "agricoltori", but Larebo estimates no more than 400 real farmer-settlers, about 150 of them with families.
  - Of 55 job applications to Ente Romagna in 1938, only 2 came from real peasants; 41% were carpenters, labourers or brickmakers.
- Ente Romagna obtained 5,600 ha of the 50,000 planned, of which 4,000 were usable. Ente Puglia employed 276 people at Bari d'Etiopia and sowed 650 ha in 1938, 200 in 1939 and about 260 in 1940. [S] [Ertola, p.76 n.474](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- A settler wrote from Bari d'Etiopia in 1940: "pioveva dappertutto nella casa piena di umidità ... Siamo trattati come schiavi". Water was carried in old petrol drums. [S] [Ertola, p.75](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- Luce B1423, 07/12/1938: "Il Viceré visita la zona dell'Harar dove sorgerà il 'centro rurale di Bari d'Etiopia'". [V] [IL5000031676](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000031676/2/il-vicere-visita-zona-harar-dove-sorgera-centro-rurale-bari-d-etiopia.html)
- ONC at Olettà and Biscioftù, 40-50 km from Addis Ababa, on land confiscated from Haile Selassie, from 31 Dec 1936. [S] [Grosselli 2023, p.348](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
  - Plans: 100 families on 10,000 ha in each farm, then 1,000 families on 50-60,000 ha each within five years.
  - Lots of 50-60 ha: a garden, 35-40 ha arable worked with native labour, about 20 ha of pasture. Native dwellings stood 1.5-2 km from the farmhouse.
  - Each house was 60 m² with two bedrooms and a kitchen for up to 6 people, "costruita per avere un aspetto che risultasse familiare ai coloni" (Larebo).
  - The Viceroy complained the houses were too small, which spread conjunctivitis and scabies.

  [S] [Ertola, p.72 and n.444-449](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- ONC president Araldo di Crollalanza to Mussolini, 16 Jan 1939: "Nelle aziende di Olettà e Biscioftù sono ormai pressoché ultimate 92 case". The settlers came "in massima parte dalla Centuria degli Ex Combattenti della 'Sabaudia'". The CMCI found only 56 of 90 families fit. The first group left Brindisi on 20 Feb 1939: 237 settlers, of whom "15 famiglie erano originarie della Sicilia, 8 della Venezia Tridentina, 7 del Veneto". [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.350-351](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- At Olettà in 1940, 6,000 ha were worked by 82 families (452 persons). [S] [Ertola, p.73 n.450](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- For defence, the farms were organised "non attorno ad un nucleo urbano centrale ma in gruppi di case, generalmente otto". Families were to be young, with at most 7-8 members. [S] [Grosselli 2023, p.343](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- Grosselli: "Non era assolutamente casuale che in Etiopia si fossero create colonie di romagnoli (terra del duce) e di pugliesi (terra di Achille Starace)". [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.349-350](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- Comprensorio De Rege (Harar government): the mortgage was redeemable in twenty years from 1943-44. [S] [Ertola, p.74 n.463](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- Luce photos of Olettà, 1936-37, show "Centurie lavoratori" visited by Mons. Castellani. [V, catalogue] [IL0600007013](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL0600007013/8/visita-monsignor-castellani-centurie-lavoratori-ad-oletta.html)
- Eritrea: Tessenei was built in 1924 as the centre of an irrigated colonization district on the Gash. I found nothing for 1937-40. [S, Wikipedia] [Città di fondazione](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citt%C3%A0_di_fondazione_nel_periodo_fascista)

### Inferences
- Ente Romagna's settlement area was probably around Dabat, north of Gondar. The basis is an Ertola footnote citing a 1940 letter from "Dabat" by a settler named Zattoni ("In tutto l'Ente siamo 400"), plus Wikipedia's note on an Italian farm at Dabat. This is unconfirmed. [INF] [Ertola, p.76 n.472](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf); [SS] [Dabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabat)
- The Ethiopian regional bodies mark the opposite pole to Libya. Libya used national-pantheon names, mixed origins in each village and a national agency. Ethiopia used regional agencies, regional recruitment and regional names, with the explicit aim of rebuilding "un ambiente simile a quello lasciato". The Bolzano Semirurali, with their Veneto workers, sit closer to the Ethiopian "homogeneous regional nucleus" logic in recruitment, but closer to the Libyan national logic in naming ("Rione Dux"). [INF]
- Two Agro Pontino institutions recur here: the ONC (Pontine agency, veterans' "Centuria Sabaudia") and the CMCI (family selection). Flag for the institutional-bridge sibling. [INF]

### Gaps
- I found no plans or architect names for Bari d'Etiopia, the Romagna settlement, or the ONC villages at Olettà and Biscioftù. Larebo, *The Building of an Empire* (1994), pp.82-172, and F. Di Lalla, *Un posto al sole* (2012, documentary appendix) are the sources to get.
- The exact locations of the Ente Romagna and Ente Veneto areas are unconfirmed.
- The full text of RDL 6 Dec 1937 nn.2300, 2314 and 2325 (seat, organs, purpose) was not read. Normattiva shows only titles for the repealed acts, and the Gazzetta Ufficiale PDFs should be opened by hand.

## 5. Dodecanese: the agricultural villages on Rhodes and Kos

### Takeaway
On Rhodes, Peveragno Rodio (1929-31, a private company comprensorio) was followed by Campochiaro (1935-36), San Marco (1936) and Savona, renamed San Benedetto (1936-38). On Kos there were Anguillara, renamed Vittorio Egeo in 1939, and Torre in Lambi (1936). An "Ente per l'Assistenza alla Bonifica Agraria nelle isole italiane dell'Egeo" was founded in 1937. Campochiaro is the strongest Alpine link in the whole overseas programme:
- it was built by Governor Mario Lago for woodcutters from the Val di Fiemme (Trentino) and for German-speaking South Tyrolean families;
- it was designed by Armando Bernabiti and Rodolfo Petracco;
- its South Tyrolean families left in 1939 after opting for Germany.

### Cited Findings
- Village list with dates: on Rhodes, "Peveragno Rodio" (1929), "Campochiaro" (1935-36), "San Marco" (1936) and "Savona" (1936-38, called "San Benedetto" from 1938); on Kos, "Anguillara" (1936-38, "Vittorio Egeo" from 1939) and "Torre in Lambi" (1936). Settlers "venivano principalmente dal Meridione, dal Veneto e dal Polesine". In 1936 the islands held 16,711 Italians, and nearly 8,000 had settled by 1940. [S, Wikipedia] [Coloni italiani nel Dodecaneso](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coloni_italiani_nel_Dodecaneso). Present names: San Marco di Cattavia = Kattavia; San Benedetto = Kolymbia; Vittorio Egeo = Linopoti; Campochiaro = Eleousa. [S, Wikipedia] [Città di fondazione](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citt%C3%A0_di_fondazione_nel_periodo_fascista)
- Capresi, citing Martinoli and Perotti 1999: coordination lay with the "Ente per l'Assistenza alla Bonifica Agraria nelle isole italiane dell'Egeo, istituito nel 1937". There were 6 villages (4 on Rhodes, 2 on Kos), each with "una chiesa, una scuola, una casa del fascio, uffici comunali, una caserma dei carabinieri, un ambulatorio medico e alcune botteghe. C'erano anche cinema e osterie". The organisation was a "binomio borgo - podere". [S] [Capresi 2007, p.72](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- **Peveragno Rodio** [Epano Kalamon]. The "Società Agricola Frutticoltura" bought 3,500 ha of uncultivated land at Kalamona from Turkish landowners and planted olives, vines, mulberries and fruit. It was named after Peveragno (Cuneo), described as Lago's native place, and is now a Greek army base. [S, Wikipedia] [Peveragno Rodio](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peveragno_Rodio). Colonisation began in 1929 and the village became a comune on 8 Aug 1930. Settlers came "mainly from Pavia, Romagna and Salerno" (Pavia for field crops, Romagna for orchards, Salerno for livestock). It had schools, a church, a clinic, a Carabinieri barracks, a post office and an aqueduct, and "edifici in stile littorio". [S] [Italia Coloniale 2020](https://italiacoloniale.com/2020/11/11/peveragno-rodio-ieri-e-oggi/)
- **Campochiaro** [Eleousa]:
  - Lago wrote on 14 Oct 1934 to the Trentino forestry engineer Giuseppe Valcanover, proposing "uno o più villaggi di boscaioli delle nostre montagne" and "trenta o quaranta le famiglie di boscaioli che per ora intenderei importare dalle nostre Alpi a Rodi".
  - The village was sited inland at 300 m, at the crossing of three roads. The first seven heads of family from the Fiemme valley (Masi di Cavalese, Capriana) sailed in April 1935. German-speaking South Tyrolean families "originari soprattutto delle zone di confine provinciale con la valle di Fiemme" followed in 1936, along with a few Ladin families from Belluno and a few Friulians.
  - It was "un flusso migratorio organizzato, finanziato ed assistito dallo Stato Italiano ... case pronte".
  - The population peaked at about 280-290 in 1938, "per più della metà trentini".
  - It was a "villaggio nuovo, bianco, mediterraneo ... progettato da Armando Bernabiti e Rodolfo Petracco sotto la supervisione di Mario Lago". It had a school, a priest, nuns and kitchen gardens.
  - In 1939 "Campochiaro ... venne abbandonata dalla maggioranza di boscaioli altoatesini e dalle loro famiglie" because they had opted for Germany. Ludwig Kompatscher of Nova Levante: "Da Rodi noi dovemmo partire per le Opzioni".

  [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.357-364](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- Valcanover was born in Trento in 1885. He worked for the Demanio forestale "in Alto Adige" from 1918 to 1932 and then at Cavalese, and "a Bolzano manteneva l'abitazione della sua famiglia". [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.359-360](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- The Province of Trento's press release on Grosselli's 2012 book: in 1935 "creò a Rodi per le famiglie fiemmesi un nuovo villaggio che fu chiamato Campochiaro". Some families returned in 1939, others in 1943, the last in 1947. The village "cambiò nome e divenne Eleoussa". [S] [Provincia di Trento, comunicato 2849 (2013)](https://www.ufficiostampa.provincia.tn.it/Comunicati/GLI-UOMINI-DEL-LEGNO-SULL-ISOLA-DELLE-ROSE)
- Architects:
  - **Armando Bernabiti** (Crevalcore 1900-1970) worked in 1924 "a Roma all'Istituto Case Popolari". From 1927 he was in the Ufficio Architettura of the OO.PP. Dodecaneso, "in coppia con Rodolfo Petracco", close to Lago. His works include Portolago buildings and Casa del Fascio buildings at Kos and Rhodes. [S, Wikipedia] [Armando Bernabiti](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Bernabiti)
  - **Rodolfo Petracco** (born Trieste 1889) designed Portolago (Leros) with Bernabiti. [S, Wikipedia] [Rodolfo Petracco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Petracco)
- Campochiaro became a German divisional HQ, and most residents were moved to San Benedetto, "in cui operava una grande azienda agricola". [S] [Grosselli 2023, p.365](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)

### Inferences
- Campochiaro reverses the South Tyrol pattern. In Bolzano the state imported Italian workers into a German-speaking province. In Rhodes the state exported Trentino and South Tyrolean families, some of them German-speaking, as "Italian" colonists, and the 1939 Option then pulled the South Tyroleans out. It is a direct, documented crossing of the Alto Adige and colonial settlement files. [INF]
- Bernabiti's 1924 stint at the Roman Istituto Case Popolari puts an IACP-trained designer behind an overseas settler village. The Semirurali were built by the Bolzano IFACP. This is a lineage of institution type only, not a documented personal link. [INF]
- The Aegean villages had a full civic programme (church, Casa del Fascio, school, municipal offices, carabinieri, ambulatorio, cinema), so the "village core" toolkit was already in place before the Libyan mass waves. [INF]

### Gaps
- Founding date of Peveragno Rodio: 1929 (start of colonisation; comune in 1930) or 1931 (Wikipedia lead)?
- Mario Lago's birthplace conflicts. Grosselli says "nato a Savona nel 1878". Wikipedia and Italia Coloniale say Peveragno Rodio was named after his native Peveragno. Perhaps Peveragno was a family origin; this is unresolved.
- I found no architects or plans for San Marco, San Benedetto, Vittorio Egeo or Torre in Lambi. Martinoli and Perotti, *Architettura coloniale italiana nel Dodecaneso* (1999), and V. Colonas, *Italian Architecture in the Dodecanese Islands 1912-1943* (2002), are the sources to get.
- The family and settler counts per Aegean village, and their regions of origin, are not documented beyond the general Wikipedia statement.

## 6. Cross-links to the Alps, and the scholarship (Pergher, Fuller, Cresti, Capresi, McLaren, Segrè, Spiegel)

### Takeaway
There are documented overlaps of people, institutions, media and vocabulary between the overseas villages and South Tyrol:
- **People.** Luigi Razza (Trento union boss, then first CMCI commissioner and first ECC president, with a Libyan village and a Naples pier named after him). Giuseppe Valcanover (Alto Adige forestry, organiser of Campochiaro).
- **Institutions.** The CMCI selected settlers for Libya and Ethiopia. The ONC, the Pontine agency, ran the Ethiopian farms with ex-"Sabaudia" veterans.
- **Settlers.** Trentino and South Tyrolean families were sent to Cyrenaica, Rhodes and Ethiopia.
- **Media.** The same Luce newsreel series and art director (Arturo Gemmiti) covered both the Ventimila and the Bolzano "Rione Popolare Dux".
- **Texts.** Mussolini at Pomezia (April 1938) spoke "dalle Alpi alla Libia", and in 1938 Tolomei explicitly asked for "Ventimila"-style family settlement in Alto Adige.

I found no documented transfer of plans or architects between the colonial offices and the Bolzano IFACP or the Ente Nazionale per le Tre Venezie.

### Cited Findings

**People**
- Luigi Razza, born in Monteleone di Calabria in 1892, was secretary of the Trentino fasci, set up the first fascist union nuclei in Trentino, and from 1921 directed *Il Popolo* and the *Giornale di Trento*. In 1930 he became "primo commissario del neoistituito Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione interna", which promoted agrarian colonisation in the Agro Pontino and in Libya. He was Minister of Public Works from January 1935 and died on 7 Aug 1935 when his plane exploded on the Cairo-Asmara route. [S] [Treccani DBI, "Razza, Luigi"](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/luigi-razza_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- Capresi has Razza as first president and director of the ECC (1932). The Cyrenaican village "Primavera" was renamed Razza after his death. [S] [Capresi 2007, pp.51-52; card p.18](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)
- The Ventimila documentary shows a mass for southern settlers at the "molo Luigi Razza di Napoli". [V] [Luce IL3000052880](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000052880/1/-302.html)
- Grosselli asks whether the Trentino families of 1938 were placed at Razza, Battisti or Filzi because of these Trentino ties, and leaves it unanswered. [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.374-375](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- Giuseppe Valcanover worked for the Demanio forestale in Alto Adige from 1918 to 1932 and had his family home in Bolzano. He recruited and organised the Fiemme and South Tyrolean colonists of Campochiaro (Rhodes) from January 1935. [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.359-360](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)

**Institutions**
- The CMCI selected the Libyan families in 1938 and 1939 (Capresi pp.58, 61) and the ONC's Ethiopian families in 1939 (Grosselli p.350). Pergher's index places the CMCI on pp. 77, 85, 101, 108, 122-124, 145, 225, 227 and 230, which cover both the South Tyrol and Libya chapters. [S] [Capresi 2007](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf); [Grosselli 2023](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186); [Pergher index](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/19741/index/9781108419741_index.pdf). Flag for the institutional-bridge sibling.
- ONC: the Agro Pontino agency also ran Olettà and Biscioftù in Ethiopia, with settlers "dalla Centuria degli Ex Combattenti della 'Sabaudia'". [S] [Grosselli 2023, pp.348-350](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)

### Inferences
- The best documented "same frame" texts are:
  1. Mussolini's "dalle Alpi alla Libia" at Pomezia (April 1938);
  2. Tolomei's 1938 call for Ventimila-style settlement in Alto Adige;
  3. the Luce newsreel series, which treated the Rione Dux (Oct 1939) and the second Libyan wave (Nov 1939) one month apart, with the same art director.

  For the studio, (2) is the clearest proof that a regime insider read Libya and Alto Adige as one sovereignty-by-settlement problem. [INF]
- The Luce Rione Dux record gives 228 casette and 808 apartments. That is a different count from the 327 houses in the brief, perhaps a first lot, or the Luce count is wrong. Flag to the Bolzano sibling. [INF]
- The Bolzano B1599 record shows the same visual grammar as the Libyan newsreels: houses with a plot, kitchen gardens, a religious blessing and party authorities. Only the frame differs, "benessere dei lavoratori" against "colonizzazione demografica". [INF]
- I found no evidence that any of the Libyan architects (Di Fausto, Di Segni, Pellegrini, Longarini, Romano) worked in Bolzano, or that colonial farmhouse plans reached the Bolzano IFACP. The typological similarity (house plus plot plus family self-provisioning) looks like shared ruralist doctrine, not a transfer of drawings. [INF]
