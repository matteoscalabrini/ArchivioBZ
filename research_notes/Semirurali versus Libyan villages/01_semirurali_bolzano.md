## 1. Authorship and office: who planned, designed, financed and approved the Semirurali

### Takeaway
The Semirurali were planned and designed in-house by the provincial public-housing institute. It was founded on 10 April 1934 as the "Istituto Case Economiche e Popolari di Bolzano" and by 1939 used the style "Istituto Fascista Autonomo per le Case Popolari della Provincia di Bolzano" (IFACP).
- **Designer.** The only designer the documents name is the institute's technical director, **Ing. Giulio Garbini**, "chiamato da Venezia". His signature is on the general site plan and on the stamped type drawings.
- **President.** The president in 1938–39 was **"Ing. Rota"**; his first name was not found.
- **Comune.** The Comune built the streets and utilities.
- **State.** The state's role ran through the **Ministero dei Lavori Pubblici**: its contributions to IFACP housing, the presidential appointment, and the "Commissione di vigilanza".

No architect is credited for the Semirurali in any source I read. I found no biography of Garbini and no evidence of colonial or Agro Pontino commissions by him.

### Inferences
- [INF] The Semirurali were an IFACP product end to end. The IFACP planned the site, designed the types, contracted and managed the work, and allocated the dwellings. Around it sat the Comune (streets, sewers, schools), the Ministry of Public Works (subsidy, presidential appointment) and the PNF/Dopolavoro (social organisation, gardens). This is the standard *case popolari* chain, not the ONC/colonization chain. No source I read shows the Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione approving or financing a Semirurali lot.
- [INF] The Venice connection is double:
  - The IACP of Venice built in Bolzano from 1928 (viale Venezia) [S, [Giacomozzi 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf)].
  - Bolzano's technical director came from Venice.
  - The IFACP Bolzano album ended up in the ATER Venezia archive, which suggests administrative or personal continuity worth checking in the ATER/Iuav archives.

## 2. Legal and programme basis: decrees, the "semirurale" programme and other Italian semirurali

### Takeaway
I found **no national law or decree that creates a "casa semirurale" category**. The legal basis was ordinary public-housing law (T.U. 1165/1938, with state contributions via the Ministry of Public Works) plus policy "direttive" on ruralization. In Bolzano these were applied through a local planning loophole ("villino economico raggruppato", art. 19 of the Piacentini plan's regulations).
- The 1934 Bolzano decree-law (R.D.L. 1621) contains only industrial incentives, no housing.
- The 1939 anti-urbanism law (L. 1092) controls residence and labour movement and gives the **Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione** authority over interprovincial labour transfers. It says nothing about house types.
- Semirurali-type quarters were also built by the IFACP Bolzano in Merano, Vipiteno, Bressanone and Brennero, and elsewhere at Testona/Moncalieri, Mestre/Marghera and Rome San Basilio. A Milan-province scheme was designed but largely unbuilt.

### Cited Findings
**The 1934 industrial-zone decree**
- [V] **R.D.L. 28 Sept. 1934, n. 1621**, "Provvedimenti per lo sviluppo industriale del comune di Bolzano" (*G.U.* n. 243, 16 Oct. 1934, p. 4708):
  - Art. 1 applies to "stabilimenti industriali tecnicamente organizzati, che entro il 30 giugno 1944 sorgeranno nel territorio del comune di Bolzano".
  - Art. 2: customs exemption for building materials and machinery.
  - Art. 3: ten-year exemption from "imposta di ricchezza mobile".
  - Arts. 5–6: state contributions of up to 25 million lire (at most 5 million a year), from the budget of the Ministero delle Corporazioni.
  - Art. 7: a granting commission including the Confederazioni fasciste of industrialists and of industrial workers.
  - It was proposed by Mussolini as Head of Government and Minister of the Interior and of Corporations. **It has no housing article.**
  - — [*G.U.* 16 Oct. 1934](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1934/10/16/243/sg/pdf); Normattiva record: converted by "L. 11 aprile 1935, n. 604 (in G.U. 17/05/1935, n. 116)", repealed by D.L. 200/2008 — [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1934-09-28;1621)
- [S] Azzolini says that "viene emanato nel 1934 un decreto legge per l'acquisizione di circa 300 ha necessari alla futura zona industriale". In 1936 plots were assigned: 3 ha to Lancia, 12 to the Acciaierie, 10 to Montecatini, 5 to Masonite and 5 to Magnesio, with initial workforces of 150 to 600. — [Azzolini 1985, p. 18](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline). The text of n. 1621 does not itself provide for expropriation [V]. So the 300-ha instrument is either another act or Azzolini's shorthand [INF].
- [S] The Archivio Storico's fonds "Zona industriale" starts in 1935. It holds the records of the "Ufficio tecnico per la zona industriale, sorto inizialmente presso l'Ufficio provinciale dell'economia corporativa e poi passato … all'amministrazione comunale": expropriations, prices, and lawsuits by landowners. — [ASCBZ inventory](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/ocmultibinary/download/21142/1153451/10/c0bffbb0730db032fa3e3e6031170c45.pdf/file/77be80ccd23153640d6bd928a8b8bc05.pdf)

**The 1939 anti-urbanism law**
- [V] **L. 6 July 1939, n. 1092**, "Provvedimenti contro l'urbanesimo" (*G.U.* n. 185, 9 Aug. 1939, pp. 3754–55):
  - Art. 1: "Nessuno può trasferire la propria residenza in Comuni del Regno capoluoghi di Provincia o in altri Comuni con popolazione superiore a 25.000 abitanti, o in Comuni di notevole importanza industriale … se non dimostri di esservi obbligato dalla carica, dall'impiego, dalla professione o di essersi assicurata una proficua occupazione stabile nel Comune di immigrazione".
  - Art. 2: workers resident elsewhere may be hired only with authorisation from the employment offices "o dal Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione, qualora essi risiedano in provincia diversa".
  - Art. 3: "il Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione può autorizzare lo spostamento temporaneo di gruppi di lavoratori da una Provincia ad un'altra".
  - Art. 4: no registration in the population register without these conditions.
  - Art. 5: ban on renting to unauthorised immigrants.
  - Art. 12 repeals "la legge 24 dicembre 1928-VII, n. 2961, il decreto del Capo del Governo del 22 luglio 1933-XI".
  - Signed at San Rossore by Mussolini, Solmi, Di Revel, Rossoni and Lantini. **No housing or ruralization provisions.**
  - — [*G.U.* 9 Aug. 1939](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1939/08/09/185/sg/pdf); [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1939-07-06;1092)

**The 1938 housing law**
- [V] **R.D. 28 Apr. 1938, n. 1165** (Testo unico sull'edilizia popolare ed economica) is the general frame: IFACP constitution and recognition, mortgages and state contributions, and the Ministry of Public Works' oversight. Its consolidated text has no "semirurale" category. "Rural" appears only in:
  - the tax exemption for "fabbricati rurali" (art. 164);
  - art. 16 n. 12°, which lists as eligible borrowers "gli enti e le società cooperative costituite per la trasformazione fondiaria, irrigazione e colonizzazione, che provvedano alla costruzione di borgate rurali".
  - The 12° item carries an amendment footnote in the consolidated text. Whether it was in the 1938 original could not be confirmed, because the GU OCR is scrambled.
  - — [edizionieuropee consolidated text](https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/15/zn38_04_001.html); [*G.U.* 1938 suppl.](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1938/08/05/177/so/177/sg/pdf)

**The semirurale as a local policy turn**
- [S] Azzolini presents the semirurale as a policy turn, not a statute. After the first 800 dwellings (end of 1937), "Preoccupato dagli effetti negativi dell'urbanesimo, il regime impartisce nuove direttive sulle tipologie edilizie più idonee alla crescita della società fascista. È dominante ormai un'ideologia antiurbana … 'in modo che la vita possa svolgersi senza l'obbligo di fare continuo ricorso alla città vicina, dalla quale si vogliono appunto distaccare le masse lavoratrici e le loro famiglie'". The quoted directive is not referenced. — [Azzolini 1985, p. 24](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline); [Azzolini 2004, p. 119](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2729_Azzolini_ita.pdf)
- [S] The semirurali ran against the town plan. "Nasce così la proposta abitativa della casa 'semirurale' in netta contrapposizione con le indicazioni del Piano Regolatore che prevede solo case popolari a densità elevata (330 ab/ha su una superficie complessiva di 24 ettari)". Garbini "approfitta così dell'art. 19 del Regolamento per l'esecuzione del Piano Regolatore e di Ampliamento, che prevede il tipo 'villino economico raggruppato', ma certo per zone diverse da quelle operaie intensive". — [Azzolini 1985, pp. 25–26](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)
- [S] The Piacentini plan (Piano Regolatore e di Ampliamento):
  - approved by podestà Alteniero degli Azzoni Avogadro on 7 Dec. 1935;
  - rejected by the Consiglio Superiore dei Lavori Pubblici in 1937;
  - approved in 1939 and made law in 1941.
  - Its 1935 zoning table already included a "Borgata mista rurale e popolare" (Rencio, 40 ha, 90 ab/ha, 3,600 inhabitants) and a "Zona e borgata rurale" (S. Maurizio, 135 ha, 40 ab/ha, 5,400 inhabitants), toward a total of 100,000.
  - — [Azzolini 1985, pp. 13–14](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)

**National context (Turin)**
- [S] "Mentre il regime tentava un rilancio della lotta all'urbanesimo, che sfociò in nuove norme restrittive nel 1939, alla politica di espulsione di strati proletari dalle aree centrali si affiancò una politica di «ruralizzazione» della classe operaia che intendeva creare, all'esterno del territorio comunale, villaggi operai con costruzioni di tipo estensivo, il cui modello ideale era la casetta unifamiliare o di pochi alloggi, costruita con materiale autarchico, con annesso un terreno da orto."
  - The stated aims: supplementary income from vegetables and "animali da cortile", work for women, support for the demographic campaign, and food supply and evacuation ("sfollamento") in wartime.
  - From 1938 the Turin IACP acted on the prefect's "autorevole consiglio".
  - — [MuseoTorino digitised volume, pp. 336–337](https://www.museotorino.it/resources/pdf/books/149/files/assets/common/downloads/page0370.pdf); [next page](https://www.museotorino.it/resources/pdf/books/149/files/assets/common/downloads/page0371.pdf). This is a chapter on Turin's industrial society under Fascism; volume title not captured.

### Inferences
- [INF] "Semirurale" was a **typological and ideological label circulating nationally in 1937–40** (Turin, Milan, Rome, Venice and Bolzano IFACPs), not a statutory category. Its legal vehicle was T.U. 1165/1938, with ministerial subsidies. Its political frame was anti-urbanism (L. 1092/1939) and ruralization. Bolzano is a local application of that national type, unusually large and unusually complete.
- [INF] The documentary bridge to colonization runs through **labour mobility, not house types**. L. 1092/1939 (arts. 2–3) puts interprovincial worker transfers under the Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione, and in January 1940 its commissioner chaired a Bolzano meeting on replacing optants (question 5). The sibling researcher should take this up.
- [INF] The 1935 Piacentini plan's "borgata rurale" zones show that rural-suburban thinking was in Bolzano's planning vocabulary before 1937. It was designed for Italian settlement around the city, not necessarily for workers.

## 3. Typology, plots, materials, costs, rents, layout, street names and ideology

### Takeaway
**Houses.**
- Two-storey houses in about eight variants. Most were four-dwelling houses with external stairs to the upper flats (types I, II, IV, V). There were also one- and two-family types with internal stairs for large families (III A/B/C, 74 m²) and shop-houses on piazza Pontinia (VI).
- Dwellings had **2 or 3 rooms of 40–60 m²**, a kitchen niche, a WC and no bathroom.
- Garbini cut the plots at **600 m² per four-dwelling house**.
- Construction was cheap and "autarchic", with masonry walls, stone foundations and tiled pitched roofs.

**Cost and rent.**
- The first 808 dwellings cost **22 million lire** (about 27,000 lire per dwelling [INF]).
- Comparable IFACP Bolzano semirurali in Bressanone rented for about **95–125 lire a month**.

**Layout.** A grid of about 30 blocks between via Milano and the Isarco, centred on **piazza Pontinia**, with streets named after Italian cities.

**Ideology.** Propaganda framed the houses as "between" city and farmhouse, recalling the Duce's birthplace at Predappio, and as a remedy for urbanism. At the inauguration a Mussolini motto on reclaiming land and founding cities hung over the square.

### Inferences
- [INF] Read against a colonization-village checklist, the Bolzano Semirurali share:
  - a single designing institution with a standard-type catalogue;
  - a house-plus-plot unit designed for self-provisioning;
  - Party, OMNI and Dopolavoro facilities, plus a sanitary filter ("disinfezione delle masserizie dei nuovi inquilini");
  - a named central piazza (Pontinia) with a church and shops;
  - inauguration ritual: the "M" arch, a blessing, a hierarch's walk-through, families with many children displayed.
- [INF] They differ in several ways:
  - the occupant was an industrial wage-worker, not a farmer, with plots of about 100–150 m² rather than a farm;
  - there was no ownership path or *podere*;
  - the plan had a dense block grid, and the designer was a housing-institute engineer, not an ONC or colonial architect.
- [INF] "Semi-rural" is exactly the hinge: the type keeps the rural *symbol* (the plot, the Predappio stair) on an urban-industrial *function*.

## 4. Chronology and numbers

### Takeaway
Lot 1 (92 houses, about 340 dwellings) was built from June 1938 to April 1939.
- By the **inauguration of 8 Oct. 1939** (Starace, newsreel Luce B1599) the quarter counted **228 houses with 808 dwellings and 8 shops**, and it took the name **"Dux" on that day**.
- The second lot was handed over on 28 Oct. 1940.
- Lots 4 and 5, including a new six-dwelling type, followed in 1941, and 110 semirural buildings (395 dwellings) were under construction in December 1941.
- Work stopped in 1942–43; the last houses were finished after 1945.

The Comune's total is **327 houses, 1,151 dwellings and about 5,500 inhabitants**. The 2015 booklet's lot breakdown (228/808 added in "estate 1940") conflicts with the period press, which gives 228/808 as the whole quarter in October 1939.

## 5. Allocation, population and the vocabulary of colonization

### Takeaway
No written allocation regulation for the Semirurali was found. Tenancy was tied to **industrial employment** and favoured **large families**, and the institute's stated purpose was to house **workers "provenienti da altre province italiane"**. From 1939 the anti-urbanism law made residence conditional on a job.
- **Who came.** First tenants were overwhelmingly from the rural Veneto, with others from Trentino, Lombardy, the south and the Cadore.
- **The bridge.** In January 1940 the **Commissario per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione** chaired a Bolzano meeting on bringing in workers "per la sostituzione degli allogeni e tedeschi" leaving under the Option. This is the clearest documentary bridge to the colonization apparatus, though it is not specific to the Semirurali.
- **Words.** Period sources call the scheme *rione*, *quartiere*, *villaggio semirurale* and *città operaia*, and in German *Arbeiterstadt*, *Viertel* and *Siedlung/Sied[l]erhäuser*. A reclamation motto, "Land urbar machen und Städte gründen", was hung for the inauguration. **I found no period use of "colonia", "colonizzazione" or "bonifica" for the Semirurali themselves.** "Colonia operaia" appears in a 2007 Comune text.

### Inferences
- [INF] Allocation worked through the **employment–residence link**, with the employer, the employment office and, from 1939, the Commissariato acting as gatekeepers. It did not work through an ethnic clause in the housing rules. The ethnic objective sat one level up, in the industrial-zone and migration policy ("100.000 abitanti", replacing optants). For the studio's test this is the main structural difference from ONC settler selection, and also the point of contact: the same Commissariato vetted labour movements in both cases.
- [INF] The "disinfezione delle masserizie dei nuovi inquilini" facility is a reception or filter function for arriving families. It is closer to a settler-reception routine than to ordinary urban public housing, and deserves documentary follow-up.
- [INF] The German Alpenzeitung's "Siedler/Siedlung" wording reflects German-language usage for small-settlement housing. It should not be read as an Italian "colonizzazione" label without the Italian original of the 1940 ministerial report.

## 6. Afterlife: demolition, replacement, the Casa Semirurale museum and the scholarship

### Takeaway
The quarter's fate was set politically after the 1957 Castelfirmiano protest against new Italian public housing on farmland. Rebuilding densely on land the institute already owned became the compromise.
- **1959.** Replacements began.
- **1964.** The Piccinato plan foresaw total replacement.
- **1976.** A Piano di Attuazione followed (Aymonino, Zoeggeler, Veneri, Unterberger).
- **1978 to the 1990s.** Lots were rebuilt by Aymonino (the "Alcazar"), Darbourne & Darke (the "case inglesi"), Abram and Plattner (the "muro") and Amplatz, Facchini and Clauser.
- **The museum.** A few houses survived, including via Bari 11. Medieval S. Maria in Augia was found in 1986. The Parco opened in 2007 and the **Casa Semirurale museum on 16 May 2015**.

### Inferences
- [INF] The replacement reverses the 1930s logic. The 1930s regime used a low-density *rural* image to settle Italians. In 1957–64 the dense rebuild became the compromise that let Italian public housing grow without new expropriations of South Tyrolean farmland. The land bank Garbini created was thus reused for the same demographic purpose under the opposite typology.
