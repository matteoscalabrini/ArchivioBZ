## 1. Asmara as a planned dual city: the plans of 1908-1939, Cafiero's 1938-39 plan, the racial laws as applied, and the scholarship

### Takeaway
Asmara was zoned by race long before Fascism. Two governor's decrees of 1908 split it into a European-only zone, a "mixed" zone around the market and a native-only zone, plus an industrial periphery, and Cavagnari's 1913-1916 plan kept that frame. The Fascist layer of 1935-1941 had three parts:
- an emergency building boom;
- an aborted 1937 competition, won by a design of the governor himself ("Sannita");
- Vittorio Cafiero's plan, commissioned in April 1938, delivered in February 1939 and approved by the Ministry's planning council in April 1939.

Cafiero used the mixed market zone as a buffer, moved a "Zona indigena" north-west beyond Abba Shawl behind a green belt, and set industry on land cleared of the native village. The 1937-1940 racial laws (madamato, "prestigio di razza", meticci) were imperial statutes, not urban rules. On the street, segregation was enforced by ordinance and practice: Eritreans were kept off Viale Mussolini, movement was restricted at night, and separate entertainment and transport rules applied. Unlike Bolzano, the colonized majority here was legally a different category of person (*suddito*), and the plan said so openly.

### Cited Findings

**Pre-Fascist and early plans**
- 1902: Decreto Governatoriale 3 Aug 1902 n. 136 approved a partial plan for Asmara. [S] [Canali 2016, p. 94 n. 15](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf). The UNESCO dossier says the 1902 plan "was never implemented and of which no illustrated record has been found" and "ignor[ed] the indigenous areas". [S] [Nomination text, §2.a.2.1, printed pp. 38-39](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
- 1908, the racial zoning decrees. Governor Giuseppe Salvago Raggi issued Decreto Governatoriale 23 Oct 1908 n. 798 (*Bullettino Ufficiale della Colonia Eritrea* XIII, 43, 24 Oct 1908) and D.G. 19 Dec 1908 n. 814 (*Bullettino* XIII, 52). They divided Asmara into:
  - a first zone reserved "esclusivamente per le abitazioni degli Italiani e dei Cittadini di Stati stranieri" (art. 1);
  - a second zone for mixed residence of Europeans, Eritreans and "assimilati" (Arabs, Egyptians, Indians, etc.);
  - a third zone "riservata esclusivamente per le abitazioni dei sudditi coloniali e degli assimilati ad essi" (art. 3);
  - a fourth zone for suburban housing and industry.

  [S] [Canali 2016, p. 94 n. 16](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf). The dossier describes the same four quarters, with the mixed one "for Europeans, other foreigners (such as Jews, Greeks and Arab traders) and Eritreans, centred on the market". [S] [Nomination text, pp. 38-39](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
- 1913-1916, Odoardo Cavagnari. The question's "Odorizzi" plan is in fact Cavagnari's.
  - Cavagnari (Busalla 1868 – Asmara 1920), Genio Civile engineer, was director of the Ufficio Centrale del Genio Civile della Colonia Eritrea from 1912. He also drew plans for Cheren (1913), Massaua (1914), Nefasit, Adi Ugri and Addi Caieh. [S] [Canali 2016, pp. 94-95 n. 17](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - In 1913 the Colonial Ministry first published a grid draft. It was superseded the same year by Cavagnari's plan, which "retained important aspects of the 1902 and 1908 plans, including attempts at racial segregation and zoning of the city according to functional requirements". Detailed zone plans followed in 1914, and the "formalisation of the 1913 plans can be seen in the drawings by Cavagnari dated 1916". [S] [Nomination text, pp. 40-41, figs. 2.5-2.8](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
  - The 1914 decree reaffirmed for "gli indigeni e gli assimilati" the ban on living in the first zone. [S] [Canali 2016, p. 95 n. 18](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - The building regulation had stricter, "less decorous" standards for the mixed zone (arts. 33-40) than for zone I (arts. 15-32). [S] [Canali 2016, p. 96 n. 21](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - ICOMOS: the 1908 plan was "based on four zones, in conformity with ethnic segregation and functional programmes"; Cavagnari's 1913 plan "confirmed the racial segregation of the previous plan". [V, ICOMOS document] [ICOMOS 2017, p. 46](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755)
  - The dossier on the plans' purpose: to give Italians a city "reminiscent of home" and to "contain and control the native population, which consistently outnumbered the Italian community until the mid-1930s". Natives "were forced to live to the north of new industrial areas in often unplanned settlements in the northeast", and "Those who had property in the city centre were often forced to sell their land and move out". [S] [Nomination text, p. 41](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
- 1933: "a decree ordered the enlargement of the European zone" and a public-works programme "in anticipation of Italy's invasion of Ethiopia". [S] [Nomination text, p. 41](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)

**1935-1937: boom, Ferrazza, baracche, and the "Sannita" scandal**
- Guido Ferrazza was born at Bocenago (Trento) on 19 March 1887 [S] [Treccani DBI, Talamona 1996](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/guido-ferrazza_(Dizionario-Biografico)/). Canali also calls him "trentino". [S] [Canali 2016, p. 96 n. 24](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - In Trento he won the competition for the Palazzo della Provincia (1920-21) and designed the Banca Cattolica Trentina. He had worked on Benghazi and Tripoli plans under Teruzzi.
  - From 1 Apr 1935 he was hired for three years as consultant to the Asmara municipal technical office (3,000 lire a month for Asmara, 1,200 for Massaua), with 55,000 lire per plan, Asmara and Massaua plans due by 30 June 1938. [S, letter quoted from ACS MAI b. 103] [Canali 2016, pp. 96-98](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - Graziani called him "indesiderabile sotto molti punti di vista" and of "grande venalità" (telegrams of June-July 1936). [S] [ibid., p. 98](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - After the war Ferrazza was urban-planning consultant to the Provveditorato alle Opere Pubbliche per il Trentino-Alto Adige. [S] [Treccani DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/guido-ferrazza_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)
- 1937, shacks. A decree of Governor Guzzoni ordered the shacks between the plan's limits and the municipal boundary demolished within three months, and those inside the city within one month, and banned new shacks (V. Civico, "Notiziario urbanistico", *Urbanistica* 2, Mar-Apr 1937, p. 115). [S] [Canali 2016, pp. 98-99](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
- 1937, Ferrazza's plan. Governor De Feo approved the "Piano Regolatore Generale" drawn up by Ferrazza and the Ufficio Opere Pubbliche, and "verrà accentuato il distacco della Città nazionale dal Quartiere indigeno". "Alla periferia sorgeranno le zone industriali e i quartieri operai. In esse ogni abitazione avrà un giardinetto ed un orticello" (Civico, *Urbanistica* 5, Sep-Oct 1937, p. 344). Zones (via Godio 2008):
  - railway zone for industry and housing for *nazionali*;
  - intensive and villa housing to the NW;
  - "abitazioni economico popolari e ... fabbricati industriali" at Godaif (SW);
  - Ghezza Banda as a "Città giardino" (SE);
  - "il Quartiere indigeno, cui era assegnata come area di sviluppo la zona a Nord, verso il laghetto di Acria".

  [S] [Canali 2016, p. 99](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
- Canali's verdict on the 1937 native-quarter idea: it ignored "che gli Eritrei erano poco meno della maggioranza della popolazione e non sarebbero potuti essere 'chiusi' in un quartiere unico". [S] [Canali 2016, p. 100](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
- The 1937 competition. De Feo called it on 24 July 1937 "per la sistemazione architettonica del centro della città". Seven entries were shown in Nov 1937 at the Casa del Fascio. The first prize (10,000 lire) went to "Sannita", a group that included the governor himself. Undersecretary Teruzzi wrote "annullare il Concorso" on De Feo's report of 15 Dec 1937. De Feo was removed, and Giuseppe Daodiace replaced him. [S, ACS MAI b. 103] [Canali 2016, pp. 100-101](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - The UNESCO dossier tells it differently. It calls it a competition "for the master plan of Asmara" whose "Terms of Reference" "perpetuated the racial segregation" and were written by Marcello Piacentini "acting under the authority of Mussolini". It calls "Sannita" "an overtly racist master plan with a strictly defined ghetto for non-Italians ... in fact designed by De Feo himself", and dates De Feo's governorship April-December 1937. [S] [Nomination text, pp. 48-49](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
  - Canali does not mention Piacentini as author of the terms of reference. The Piacentini attribution is unconfirmed by a second source.

**Cafiero's plan, 1938-1939: commission, content, approval, publication**
- Who Cafiero was: Vittorio Cafiero (Rome 1901-1981), a Roman rationalist (1931 MIAR show), designed Teruzzi's summer villa "Villa Celestina" at Castiglioncello (1930). With Ridolfi and others he worked on the new Ministry of the Colonies building in Rome and on the 1937 Palazzo del Littorio competition. He was a member of the Consiglio Superiore dei Lavori Pubblici (Divisione XXIII, "Piani regolatori") and of the Ministry's "Consulta Coloniale per l'Edilizia e l'Urbanistica". [S] [Canali 2016, p. 101 n. 47](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
- Commission. On 31 Mar 1938 Daodiace proposed that the governorate's Ufficio Opere Pubbliche redo the plan. On 8 Apr 1938 Teruzzi replied that he would send "l'architetto Cafiero, membro della 'Consulta Coloniale per l'Edilizia e l'Urbanistica'", who would take the plan back to Rome "per l'approvazione della competente Consulta". In Oct 1938 Cafiero was also given Assab and Massaua. [S, ACS MAI bb. 103-104] [Canali 2016, p. 102](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - Dating conflict: ICOMOS says "in 1936 Vittorio Cafiero was charged with the task ... His plan, completed in 1938". [V, ICOMOS document] [ICOMOS 2017, p. 46](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755). The dossier says "In 1938 ... Cafiero was sent to Asmara". [S] [Nomination text, pp. 50-51](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767). The archival letters (Apr 1938 commission, Feb 1939 delivery) support 1938-39. [INF]
- Daodiace's brief, as quoted in Cafiero's "Relazione" (ACS MAI b. 106):
  1. study the needs of the city "in funzione strettamente diretta a quella dei vari aggregati urbani e dei loro abitanti";
  2. a "Centro Governativo" and a "Centro di affari";
  3. "frenare il dilagare in periferia";
  4. keep works "entro lo stretto indispensabile";
  5. give Asmara "un volto e un carattere ben chiaro di ordine, di disciplina edilizia e di perfetto funzionamento urbanistico".

  [S] [Canali 2016, p. 103](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
- Zoning of the Cafiero plan, after Godio's summary of the "Relazione". [S] [Canali 2016, pp. 103-104](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - a. Zona governativa around a new "piazza De Cristoforis", with the Governor's palace and two head buildings on viale De Bono for Government offices and the Municipio;
  - b. Zona degli Affari, between corso del Re and viale Mussolini;
  - c. Zona commerciale;
  - d. "Zona ... mista, a contatto a Sud e a Ovest con la zona commerciale e con le aree residenziali";
  - e. "la Zona indigena, spostata a Nord-Ovest dell'insediamento Abba Sciaul";
  - f. inner residential zones (station, viale Crispi);
  - g. "Zone residenziali periferiche, caratterizzate dall'edificazione di casette a schiera con orti e giardini" (Godaif, Gaggiret), with Ghezza Banda semi-intensive villas;
  - h. industrial zones to the E and N "secondo il principio della non individuazione, per scopi di difesa, di un'unica area industriale", plus one in Ghezza Banda;
  - i. railway zone; j. hospital zone; k. sports zone below Forte Galliano.
- The axis and the native quarter: "rafforzamento dell'asse centrale (il viale tra il palazzo del Governatore e la nuova stazione)", "diradamento dell'area più vecchia", and "un nuovo vasto quartiere indigeno al di là delle colline di Abba Shawl ... trasformata in «barriera» verde". The plan "doveva essere attuato partendo proprio da quest'area" (Gresleri 2008). [S] [Canali 2016, pp. 102-103](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf). ICOMOS gives the same content: a "new large indigenous quarter north of Abbashawel, which was intended to be transformed into a green belt". [V, ICOMOS document] [ICOMOS 2017, p. 46](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755)
- The buffer logic, as the UNESCO dossier puts it: "Cafiero exploited the unique 'mixed' quarter as a buffer zone between the strictly Italian district ... and the indigenous areas ... pushed to the extreme north-east corner of the city". The buffer would ensure that "the only contact of the whites with natives will be with the highest category of them, the commercial and industrial one". "The hill of Emba Galliano also provided a 'green' barrier". Planned densities were 380 inhabitants/ha in the native quarter against 140 in the Italian quarter (cited as "Teruzzi, p.391"). "Central to the plan was the separation of the races". [S] [Nomination text, pp. 50-51](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
- What was delivered and how it was approved:
  - On 20 Feb 1939 Cafiero delivered 25 drawings to the Ministry: Piano generale 1:10,000, Zonizzazione 1:10,000, road networks, green zones, the general plan in 15 sheets at 1:2,000, the detailed central plan at 1:500, the "Relazione" and an aerial photogrammetry (letters 20 Feb and 7 Mar 1939). [S, ACS MAI b. 103] [Canali 2016, pp. 104-105](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - In April 1939 the "Consulta Centrale per l'Edilizia e l'Urbanistica" approved it. It asked for detailed architectural studies of six complexes, among them "La nuova zona ospedaliera (nuovo sifilicomio)" and "La nuova zona indigena", and recommended "che, nello studio Particolareggiato della nuova zona indigena, venga ampliata la zona divisoria di Verde" (letter of 7 Apr 1939). On 14 Apr 1939 the Ministry told the governorate that building the new native quarter required clearing the old one "che ... dovrebbe essere trasformata in Zona di verde". [S, ACS MAI b. 103] [Canali 2016, p. 106](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - In May 1939 Alberto Calza Bini reported on the governorate's draft "Regio Decreto" for executing the plan and hoped it might be "un punto di partenza anche per le Leggi urbanistiche metropolitane". [S] [Canali 2016, p. 106](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - Publication: the plan was announced in the Ministry of Public Works' journal *Urbanistica*. "La 'Consulta Centrale per l'Edilizia e l'Urbanistica', presieduta da S.E. Teruzzi, ha approvato il progetto definitivo del Piano regolatore della città, elaborato dall'arch. Vittorio Cafiero" (V. Civico, *Urbanistica* 2, Mar-Apr 1939, p. 127). [S] [Canali 2016, p. 108](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)
  - The Consulta was created by R.D. 12 Nov 1936, "Istituzione di una Consulta centrale e di Comitati locali per l'edilizia e la urbanistica nell'Africa Orientale Italiana e nella Libia" (listed in the GU index of early 1937). [SS] [GU 3 Mar 1937 n. 52](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1937/03/03/52/sg/pdf)
- Resistance from Asmara. Governor Daodiace to Teruzzi, 17 Jul 1939: "Via Tevere è per il quartiere indigeno come via Nazionale o via Veneto a Roma ... Se al quartiere indigeno si dovesse dare la sistemazione trovata da Cafiero tutti gli edifici in via Tevere dovrebbero venire abbattuti ... Io sto attuando la bonifica del quartiere indigeno, abbattendo i tucul peggiori ... la sistemazione del quartiere indigeno ... come studiata e proposta dal Cafiero costituisce una esagerazione urbanistica, che il Cafiero ha proposto per amore del mestiere, ma con scarsa sensibilità di quella che è la realtà". Teruzzi asked for a variant to be filed as "Piano particolareggiato della Zona indigena". [S, ACS MAI b. 103] [Canali 2016, pp. 106-107](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf). Podestà likewise says Daodiace refused the planned forced removal of the African quarter, caravanserai and mosque. [S, read via WebFetch] [Podestà 2015, ¶12](https://journals.openedition.org/diacronie/1919)
- Afterlife of the plan's rules: ICOMOS notes Cafiero's plan was "accompanied by regulations still in existence today" (the Regolamento Edilizio 1938). [V, ICOMOS document] [ICOMOS 2017, p. 46](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755)
- Period description of the dual city, from the CTI guide of 1938 [V] [CTI 1938, pp. 199-206, Commons pages 205-212](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=205):
  - p. 199: "La città italiana, ricca di edifici notevoli e di giardini perennem. fioriti e dominata dal campanile della Cattedrale, è disposta a vie regolari rettilinee a S della città indigena che allinea, fra edifici all'europea numerosi «agdò» dal conico tetto di paglia".
  - p. 199: "Dal 1935 Asmára è tutta un cantiere ... Sorse così alla periferia un anello di sobborghi provvisori di baracche ... sono sorti e vanno sorgendo solidi edifici dalle linee moderne, secondo il piano regolatore modificato e approvato nel 1938".
  - p. 203: "S'apre qui la Zona Industriale, tracciata nella piana già occupata dal Villaggio indigeno, che ora addensa le sue casette sulla collina a N della città".
  - pp. 203/206: "viale Milano, lunga arteria formicolante di indigeni, che separa nettam. la città italiana a S dal villaggio eritreo a N". The "Villaggio Eritreo" is one "la cui visita può essere interessante per rendersi conto delle condizioni di vita degli eritrei".
  - Separate institutions named: "Mercato coperto per gli indigeni" (p. 199), "Tribunale Indigeno", "Villaggio degli Eritrei Cattolici" (p. 206), "Cimitero per gli Europei" (p. 206), "Scuole Eritree Vittorio Emanuele III" (city plan, p. 204).
  - The city plan (1:15,000, pp. 204-205) marks "Quartiere Eritreo" north of viale Milano. Local copies: `period_texts/CTI_1938_Guida_AOI_p204_Pianta_Asmara_W.jpg` and `_p205_..._E.jpg`.

**The racial laws: texts**
- R.D.L. 1 Jun 1936 n. 1019, "Ordinamento e amministrazione dell'Africa Orientale Italiana" (GU n. 136, 13 Jun 1936). This is the constitutional frame of the AOI (citizens vs subjects). [V, title] [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1936-06-01;1019)
- R.D.L. 19 Apr 1937 n. 880, "Sanzioni per i rapporti d'indole coniugale fra cittadini e sudditi" (GU n. 145, 24 Jun 1937; converted by L. 30 Dec 1937 n. 2590). It was proposed by Lessona (Africa Italiana) with Solmi (Justice). Single article: "Il cittadino italiano che nel territorio del Regno o delle Colonie tiene relazione d'indole coniugale con persona suddita dell'Africa Orientale italiana o straniera appartenente a popolazione che abbia tradizioni, costumi e concetti giuridici e sociali analoghi ... è punito con la reclusione da uno a cinque anni." [V] [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1937-04-19;880)
- R.D.L. 17 Nov 1938 n. 1728, "Provvedimenti per la difesa della razza italiana" (GU n. 264, 19 Nov 1938; converted by L. 5 Jan 1939 n. 274). Barrera: it forbade marriage of "an Italian citizen of Aryan race with a person of another race" (art. 1). [V title; S content] [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1938-11-17;1728); [Barrera 1996, p. 38](https://arch.library.northwestern.edu/downloads/kp78gg509?locale=en)

### Inferences
- [INF] The dual-city frame needs no metaphor for Asmara. It is the legal content of the 1908 decrees, which reserve zone I "esclusivamente" for Italians and foreigners and zone III "esclusivamente" for *sudditi*. Bolzano's new city never had a legal residence ban on German-speakers. The contrast is the core of the control case: same planning culture, a different legal subject.
- [INF] The Fascist novelty in Asmara was intensity and enforcement more than the zoning idea. The idea was liberal-era (Bader via Camilleri; ICOMOS). Fascism added three things: a building boom; the use of the mixed market as a buffer with a planned green belt and a relocated native quarter; and street-level exclusion (Viale Mussolini, night curfews) backed after 1939 by the "prestigio di razza" law.
- [INF] Cafiero's "casette a schiera con orti e giardini" peripheral zones and the 1937 promise that "ogni abitazione avrà un giardinetto ed un orticello" are the same semirural vocabulary as Bolzano's Semirurali (1937-39). The ministry's colonial-planning writer Rigotti made the link explicit in 1938 (see Q3 and Q5).
- [INF] Personal overlap with Bolzano's planning chain. Piacentini, who drew Bolzano's 1935 plan (01 notes), is named by the UNESCO dossier as author of the 1937 Asmara terms of reference. Cafiero sat on the Consiglio Superiore dei Lavori Pubblici's planning division, the body that rejected Bolzano's Piacentini plan in 1937 (01 notes; the plan was approved in 1939). I have not verified whether Cafiero handled the Bolzano file.

## 2. The demographic flip: Italians and Eritreans in Asmara and Eritrea, 1905-1952, and the mechanism compared with Bolzano

### Takeaway
Asmara went from about 3,000-4,000 Italians beside 12,000-20,000 Eritreans (1931-35) to an Italian majority by 1938-39. The best-documented figures:
- 98,000 inhabitants "di cui 53 000 Italiani" in the CTI guide of 1938 [V];
- 48,000 Italians and 36,000 Africans in early 1939 (Podestà);
- 41,263 Italian men and 11,177 women in 1939 (Rossetti via Barrera).

The often-repeated "1939 census" label is doubtful. The 98,000/53,000 pair is already printed in the 1938 guide, and Barrera, following Castellano, says "No proper census took place after 1931". The only 1939 census attested is Castellano's count of the native population. Unlike Bolzano, the flip came mostly from a temporary, overwhelmingly male labour and military mass. The colony-wide peak was about 72,000-76,000 Italians in 1939-40, with women at about one-fifth. The flip collapsed after April 1941, though more slowly than often said: Italian officials, judges and police stayed under the British until about 1950.

### Inferences
- [INF] The Asmara flip and the Bolzano flip share a lever: state-financed works pulling unemployed Italian workers through the same Commissariato, from the same north-eastern basin (Veneto first; Q3). They differ in three ways.
  - Duration and gender: Asmara's Italian majority was a male, contract-based, semester-to-semester mass, with women about 21%. Bolzano's industrial zone brought families into the Semirurali (01 notes).
  - Legal hierarchy: Eritreans were *sudditi*; South Tyroleans were citizens, and the Option of 1939 tried to remove them rather than zone them.
  - Scale: Asmara's Italian population went up more than tenfold in five years (3,000-4,000 to about 50,000). Bolzano roughly doubled in the same period: Wikipedia gives 45,505 for 1936 [SS] [Timeline of Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Bolzano).
- [INF] For Asmara, "flipped" means outnumbered by 1938-39, not replaced. The Eritrean population of the city also grew, from about 12,000-20,000 to 36,000-45,000 (Podestà, Canali, dossier), drawn by the labour demand. The same is true of Bolzano, whose German-speaking population did not fall before the Option.
- [INF] Treat "98,000 / 53,000, 1939 census" as "CTI 1938 guide estimate, repeated by Castellano (1948, p. 539)". Barrera's 52,440 (1939, by sex) and Podestà's 48,000 bracket it.

## 3. Workers from the north: the 1935-1939 labour flow to Eritrea and AOI, contracts, regions, the CMCI, the Trentino-Veneto presence, and Luce newsreels

### Takeaway
The Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione did select and channel the workers. Commissioner Sergio Nannini set provincial quotas from firms' requests weighted by unemployment, prefects split them among communes, and local party, union and police screening followed. 201,440 Italian workers left for AOI between Oct 1935 and Jun 1939, 50% of them in 1936. More came home than left: 207,983 repatriated, plus 2,368 deaths. At most about 63,500 were on the road sites at once (June 1937).

By region the Veneto led, with 39,871 of 189,737 workers to Sept 1937. Venezia Tridentina sent only 1,415, about 0.7%, though named Trentino men did go to Asmara. Cobolli Gigli, Minister of Public Works from Sept 1935 to Oct 1939, inspected the imperial roads in 1937-38. He also opened Bolzano's Ponte Littorio and the Ponte Gardena plant on the Isarco. Luce documents embarkations at Naples (1935, 1936) and Asmara's workers' villages (1937).

### Cited Findings

**Numbers and timing**
- Workers were mostly "terrazzieri e manovali (80%) ed autotrasportatori, reclutati in Italia tra i disoccupati iscritti alle liste di collocamento". About 20-25% worked for the Genio or military bodies, the rest for private contractors. [S] [Ertola, pp. 23-24 and n. 124](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)

  - only 2,098 left in the first half of 1939;

**Selection and channelling: the CMCI**
- "La partenza degli operai era concessa solo su richiesta di ditte stabilite – o autorizzate a stabilirsi – in AOI ... La procedura sottostava alle stesse regole previste per la colonizzazione interna, difatti l'invio di operai nell'Impero era gestito da Sergio Nannini, commissario per la colonizzazione e le migrazioni interne". The process:
  - Nannini weighed contractors' requests against regional and provincial unemployment and gave each province a percentage;
  - the prefect split the quota among communes;
  - candidates from the *liste di collocamento* passed medical, occupational and political checks, including police record checks;
  - party secretaries and union officials drew up the final lists;
  - a national check followed at the port.

  [S] [Ertola, pp. 34-35 nn. 209-211](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- Mussolini to Nannini, 16 Nov 1936, on strict selection, to avoid "pochi individui infidi" who could "creare irrequietezza ... anche in masse disciplinatissime e ordinate". The CMCI handled "la selezione degli operai e l'organizzazione e assistenza del viaggio"; once in Africa, workers came under the Ispettorato del Lavoro in AOI. [S, ASDMAE ASMAI Gab. AS b. 18] [Ertola, pp. 35-36 n. 216](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- Practice fell short. A batch from Modena (late 1935) included "un giocatore di calcio, che sa fare solo il giocatore di calcio" and false labourers who were nurses, barbers and waiters. From June 1939 applications went to the Corporations offices, not the placement offices. [S] [Ertola, p. 36; n. 210 on p. 35](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)
- Contracts and discipline:
  - contracts were semestral ("il contratto di impiego a base semestrale") [S] [Ertola, p. 25](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf);
  - worker protection in AOI lay with the PNF, not the fascist unions (Podestà 2004, p. 395);
  - firms delayed wages, withheld hardship allowances and the "fondo di risparmio", and imposed unpaid overtime and arbitrary fines [S] [Bo 2023](https://storicamente.org/bo_atti_lesivi);
  - road workers were often in the MVSN "Legioni Lavoratori" [S] [Bo 2023](https://storicamente.org/bo_atti_lesivi);
  - Luce calls them "operai-soldati" [V] [Luce B099009](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000026560/2/mussolini-passa-rassegna-1000-operai-soldati-partenza-l-africa-orientale.html).
- The minister: Giuseppe Cobolli Gigli (of an Istrian family, deputy for Trieste; his surname was still "Cobol" in the 1920s and was Italianised in 1928 according to Scotti) was Undersecretary of Public Works from 24 Jan 1935 and Minister from 5 Sept 1935 to 31 Oct 1939, succeeding Luigi Razza after Razza's death. On Mussolini's order he spent six months in AOI from late 1936, mainly for the Ethiopian road network, overseeing works led by engineer Giuseppe Pini. He was earlier the Trieste federal secretary (1927-30) and wrote on assimilating the "allogeni" as "Giulio Italico". [S] [it.wikipedia, Giuseppe Cobolli Gigli](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Cobolli_Gigli). The 1938 Asmara office list includes the "Azienda Autonoma Statale della Strada (A.A.S.S.), via A. Oriani" and the "Direzione Strade del Genio Civile". [V] [CTI 1938, p. 198](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=204)

## 4. The earlier settler experiments: Franchetti's colonization (1890-1896), the Tessenei cotton scheme (1924-1941), and whether demographic colonization was planned for Eritrea in 1937-1940

### Takeaway
Eritrea's demographic-colonization experiment was liberal-era. Leopoldo Franchetti was appointed "commissario per la colonizzazione" on 19 June 1890 and set up three government experimental farms at Asmara, Gura and Godofelassi. A snippet dates them 1891-1892; Pagura dates them 1893. He settled roughly 15-30 peasant families from Lombardy, Friuli and Sicily on 16-20 ha lots, on about 4,500 ha of "indemaniated" land. Bahta Hagos's revolt (Dec 1894) and Adwa (1 Mar 1896) ended it; only one or two families were left decades later.

From Martini (1897) onward, policy turned to capitalist concessions in the lowlands. The SIA cotton scheme at Tessenei (dam 1924-28) was worked by Cunama, Beni Amer, Sudanese and Yemeni sharecroppers; in 1938 the place had 1,850 inhabitants, only 50 of them Italian. For 1937-1940 I found no Eritrean counterpart to Ente Romagna, Puglia or Veneto d'Etiopia. Fascist Eritrea was colonized through the city and the war economy, not the farm.

### Cited Findings

**Franchetti, 1890-1896**
- The Crispi government named him "commissario per la colonizzazione" on 19 June 1890. He favoured small peasant property over large concessions worked with native labour. He resigned soon after disputes with Governor Gandolfi but continued the experiments, which let "alcuni gruppi di contadini italiani" settle on the highlands "con buoni risultati" (the DBI's wording). [S, via WebFetch] [Treccani DBI, Franchetti](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leopoldo-franchetti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)
- Melli (1899):
  - Franchetti "impiantò sull'altipiano tre poderi agricoli sperimentali, ad Asmara, Godofelassi e Gura, conducendo seco dei coloni italiani" (p. 64);
  - the government indemaniated land that was "abbandonati o di incerta e mal definita proprietà", footnoted "Furono indemaniati circa 4500 Ettari di terreno produttivo";
  - peasant families "arruolati con atto notarile" got money, food, tools and seed and "la facoltà di diventare proprietari del terreno", to be repaid from produce;
  - "Questi coloni furono stabiliti intorno a Godofelassi, ove fu per loro costituito apposito villaggio";
  - after Franchetti's return to Italy the Ufficio del Demanio ("detto anche Ufficio di Colonizzazione") ran the scheme, but "i gravi avvenimenti guerreschi ... vennero a portare un profondo turbamento ed un ristagno" (pp. 87-90).

  [V] [Melli 1899, IA](https://archive.org/details/lacoloniaeritre00mellgoog); [Wikisource transcription](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Colonia_Eritrea/Parte_II/Capitolo_X)
- Numbers and fate, from Pagura's article on the Friulian settler Luigi Del Mestre (interview in *Il Friuli*, 20 Mar 1901):

  - about 30 families from Lombardy, Friuli, Sicily and elsewhere went to the highlands between 1891 and 1894;
  - Godofelassi had 15 families (7 Lombard, 2 Friulian, 1 Sicilian) when Del Mestre arrived in Oct 1894 with five other Friulian families; a note gives 21 families and about 250 people after that arrival;
  - Del Mestre's 12-person family got 16 ha and three tukul;
  - the family was evacuated to Massawa by 9 Mar 1896 and left Godofelassi for good on 10 Feb 1901;
  - only two families were still on the plateau by the late 1920s (C. Matteoda, *La Voce della Patria*, 30 Sep 1929);
  - "i terreni furono espropriati agli eritrei".

**Tessenei and the SIA, 1905-1941**
- Engineer Nicola Coletta studied the Gash in 1905. Governor Jacopo Gasparini (1923-1928) revived the scheme. The Anglo-Italian Gash water agreement rests on the decree of 28 Apr 1927 n. 1192. The dam was begun in 1924 and finished in 1928 (accounts vary on works and area). Earthworks reshaped "10.000 hectares", and a 1928 report gives 15,000 ha. [S] [Cattaneo 2019](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1109835/1/shsconf_modscapes2018_05002.pdf)
- Labour: "Production activities were mainly based on local workers". By 1929 (TCI) about 1,000 families had settled, mainly Cunama and Beni Amer, on sharecropping contracts of 2-5 feddan. Eight parts were in cotton and two in dura; the cotton was split half and half with the government. Yemeni, Sudanese and Ethiopian workers were also recruited. Farmers lived in government-provided straw huts, grouped by ethnicity in villages such as Sabott, Alighidir and "Villaggio Pozzo n. 10". [S] [Cattaneo 2019, pp. 6-9](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1109835/1/shsconf_modscapes2018_05002.pdf)
- "In 1931 SIA (Società Imprese Africane), founded and run by the former Governor Gasparini, took over the entire area and the production line, adding an oil mill and a soap factory". [S] [Cattaneo 2019, p. 7](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1109835/1/shsconf_modscapes2018_05002.pdf)
- 1938 (TCI): fields let to 320 families; Tessenei "had 1850 inhabitants of whom only 50 were Italian". There were a Residenza, post office, hospital, S.I.A. offices and a Casa del Fascio. The geometric core was "Villaggio Gasparini". "Just south of Villaggio Gasparini, a new town, never built, was designed on a regular grid as often used in colonial planning just to mark the difference between the organic settlements of local population and the rational geometry of the ruler". [S] [Cattaneo 2019, pp. 7-8](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1109835/1/shsconf_modscapes2018_05002.pdf)
- Tessenei is listed among Fascist foundation towns (1924) in the sibling note 02. [S] [02_overseas_villages.md §4](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citt%C3%A0_di_fondazione_nel_periodo_fascista)

**1937-1940: demographic colonization planned for Eritrea?**
- Grosselli: "Dato il territorio (e il clima) in gran parte inadatto alle coltivazioni, l'Eritrea non conobbe significativi esempi di colonizzazione agricola". [S] [Grosselli 2023, p. 334](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)
- The regional bodies (Romagna, Puglia, Veneto d'Etiopia, Dec 1937) and the ONC worked in Ethiopia (Gondar/Dabat, Harar, Olettà and Biscioftù), not Eritrea; see sibling note 02, §4. Podestà says demographic colonization was run by the ONC and regional PNF bodies, with 854 farm families by 1940 per the press against 377 per Sbacchi. [S, via WebFetch] [Podestà 2015, ¶9 n. 9](https://journals.openedition.org/diacronie/1919)
- Within Eritrea the 1930s record shows:
  - the pre-1935 "Italian colonists" farms, with coffee exports booming in the 1930s [S] [Wikipedia, Italian Eritreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritreans);
  - war veterans given free land [S, via WebFetch] [Podestà 2015, ¶22](https://journals.openedition.org/diacronie/1919);
  - the poultry farm on the Asmara-Decamerè road, "un grandioso pollaio (45 000 capi), il più grande dell'Impero e d'Italia" [V] [CTI 1938, p. 197](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=203).
- The 1938 territorial enlargement (Tigray and the Azebo-Galla country added; area from 119,000 to 231,280 km²) produced "Corbetta", a camp named after the Lombard town of its first settlers. [S] [Canali 2016, p. 92 n. 7](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INF] Eritrea inverts the Libyan and Ethiopian pattern. The rural settler experiment came first (1890s) and failed against Eritrean resistance and war. The Fascist phase produced an urban settler majority without agricultural settlement. For the Bolzano reading this is the useful control: a settler-colonial city can be made by state works and labour channelling alone, without farms. That is Bolzano's model too: industrial zone plus Semirurali, not ENTV farms, which came later and stayed small (03 notes).
- [INF] Franchetti's scheme anticipates Fascist instruments: notarial contracts, state advances repaid from produce, and family plots of 16-20 ha in a purpose-built village. The CMCI and ECL "podere" contracts in Libya follow the same pattern (02 notes). The continuity is in instruments, not institutions.

### Gaps
- I found no exact decree for the 1893 land "indemaniamento" (Melli names none). A non-academic source claims 19,000 ha reserved in 1893 and "almost fifteen times" in 1894, unverified [SS] [GlobalSecurity, blocked](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/eritrea/history-2.htm). Tekeste Negash (1987) and Rainero (1960) are the sources to check.
- I found no source for a 1937-1940 plan of demographic colonization in Eritrea proper. A search-result summary of a non-academic source claims Mussolini slowed settlement from 1938; unverified [SS].

## 5. Vocabulary and ritual; newsreels that put Eritrea and Alto Adige in one frame; the afterlife of Italian Asmara (1941-2017) compared with Bolzano's "defusing"

### Takeaway
The regime's words for Eritrea were "colonia primogenita/primigenia" (Luce film title; *Corriere dell'Impero* 1940), "città italiana" (CTI 1938: Asmara "si presenta come una graziosissima città italiana ... tesa verso un avvenire veram. imperiale") and "il centro più progredito dell'Impero". "Piccola Roma" is a post-war or journalistic label with no period source found.

No newsreel puts Asmara and Bolzano in a single item. Luce does show:
- an empire-products exhibition held in Bolzano (Aug 1938);
- Cobolli Gigli opening Bolzano's Ponte Littorio between the industrial zone and the case popolari (July 1939);
- newsreel issues that run a Bolzano story beside Massaua and Asmara items (Dec 1935), or beside the AOI embarkation port of Naples and its stele to Luigi Razza (Oct 1936).

A villaggio "Luigi Razza" in Asmara (1937) honours the CMCI founder who died on the Cairo-Asmara flight (Aug 1935). A South Tyrolean journal compared Asmara's Casa del Fascio with Bolzano's Victory Monument in 2008.

After 1941 the Italian city was neither demolished nor reinterpreted by plaques. It was kept almost intact by the British, by Ethiopia, and after 1991 by the Eritrean state as a symbol of national distinctness. UNESCO inscribed it on 8 July 2017 under criteria (ii) and (iv): 481 ha, buffer zone 1,203 ha. The listing names "racial segregation principles" as part of its value and includes the native quarters Arbate Asmera and Abbashawel inside the property.

**Afterlife, 1941-2017**
- British entry, 1 April 1941. A British Ministry of Information booklet (Dower 1944) described "a European city of broad boulevards, super-cinemas, super-Fascist buildings ... and a first class hotel" and the native area "crowded into a latrineless native quarter". [S] [Nomination text, pp. 54-55](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
- Under the British: "Italian judges still presided in Eritrean courts, Italian tax officers still managed the accounts, Italian law enforcement officers still remained active in the police, the municipality and all public works were largely under Italian influence, and Italian farmers still worked the land and retained the right to shoot trespassers". The British "repealed the racist laws of segregation" and moved industry out of Eritrea. [S] [Nomination text, pp. 282-283, 292-293](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
- Federation with Ethiopia in 1952 under a UN agreement ("without obtaining the people's consent", per the dossier); annexation in November 1962; independence war 1961-1991; liberation of Asmara on 24 May 1991. The dossier says the besieged Ethiopian garrison's appeal for safe passage "contained the guarantee of the preservation of Asmara". [S] [Nomination text, pp. 58-59, 262-263, 268-269](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)
- Preservation by the post-colonial state:
  - "one of the first directives of the Municipality of post-independence Asmara ... No building was to exceed ten storeys" [S] [Nomination text, pp. 262-263](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767);
  - CARP (World Bank loan, 2001, head Naigzy Gebremedhin) produced Denison et al. 2003 and the "historic perimeter"; it was dissolved in 2007; the EU-funded National Heritage Programme resumed in 2009 "with an explicit focus on modern (i.e., colonial) heritage" [S] [Fuller 2011, PDF pp. 9-10](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cisj/article/41166/galley/30790/download);
  - the Asmara Heritage Project was founded in 2014 to prepare the nomination [S] [Nomination text, pp. 298-299](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767).
- Fuller's critique: CARP's "historic perimeter ... corresponds alarmingly to what Italian planners included in the city's European and 'mixed' zones ... Today's delineation of what is valuable in Asmara, in other words, excludes the same area Italian colonizers excluded under the rubric of quartiere indigeno". She notes the regime's use of the Italian past to assert distinctness from Ethiopia, and that "uniquely Eritrean monuments to independence have been added", such as the giant sandals and Denden. [S] [Fuller 2011, PDF pp. 10-11](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cisj/article/41166/galley/30790/download)
- The 2016-17 nomination answered part of that critique. The property "includes the indigenous section of the town (Arbate Asmera and Abbashawel) – area 14 in the nomination dossier – which was incorporated into the plans for colonial Asmara, without enjoying a detailed design of its layout but rather illustrating the exclusion and segregation suffered by the indigenous population during the Italian colonial power, particularly from the advent of Fascism". [V, ICOMOS document] [ICOMOS 2017, p. 48](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755)
- Inscription: 2017, criteria (ii)(iv), property 481 ha, buffer zone 1,203 ha. WHC news item "Three sites in Angola, Eritrea and South Africa added to UNESCO's World Heritage List", 8 July 2017. The brief synthesis: "Asmara's urban-scape outstandingly conveys how colonial planning, based on functional and racial segregation principles, was applied and adapted to the local geographical conditions ... The town has come to be associated with the struggle of the Eritrean people for self-determination, which was pursued whilst embracing the tangible, yet exceptional, evidence of their colonial past". [V, WHC page] [WHC 1550, Wayback Oct 2026](http://web.archive.org/web/20261011124247/https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1550/)
- ICOMOS recommended inscription on (ii) and (iv). It judged criterion (iii) "not ... justified", rejecting the State Party's argument about "Eritrea's response to the physical legacies of this urban heritage". [V, ICOMOS document] [ICOMOS 2017, pp. 49, 55](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755). The dossier's proposed title was "Asmara: Africa's Modernist City"; the inscribed name is "Asmara: A Modernist African City". [V] [ICOMOS 2017, p. 45](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755); [WHC 1550](http://web.archive.org/web/20261011124247/https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1550/)
- Critique of de-ideologised display: the 2006 Berlin exhibition "Asmara – the frozen city" presented Asmara "losgelöst von Ideologien". Christian Welzbacher (*FAZ*, 17 Nov 2006) called this "eine offenkundige Kapitulation vor der Geschichte". [S] [Hanni 2008, p. 199](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957023447.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INF] The two "defusings" run in opposite directions. In Bolzano the heirs of the colonized (German-speaking South Tyroleans) contested the Fascist city for decades, and the response was reinterpretation in place. Examples are the documentation of the Victory Monument and the Mussolini relief; the sibling note 01 cites the city's site [bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com](https://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/). Dates and details of those interventions were not checked in this thread. In Asmara the colonized majority took over the colonizer's city after 1941-52, used it as a national symbol against Ethiopia, and froze it physically. Interpretation was left to UNESCO's statement of value, which names "racial segregation principles" outright.
- [INF] UNESCO's inclusion of Abbashawel and Arbate Asmera (area 14) inside a listing that celebrates the modernist city is the strongest available model for the studio's question: whether to include the "other half" of a dual city in its heritage definition. Bolzano's old town and new town are both protected, but under different narratives.
- [INF] For a "same frame" argument, the Luce record B134904 (an empire exhibition staged in Bolzano, 1938) and Cobolli Gigli's double role are concrete, citable links. The minister of the AOI roads opened Bolzano's bridge between the industrial zone and the workers' housing. The Razza thread (Asmara's "L. Razza" village; Razza's Bolzano industrial-zone decree; his death en route to Asmara) is the strongest single-person link. None of these is an explicit regime text equating Alto Adige with Eritrea. Tolomei 1938 (03 notes) remains the only such text found across the threads.

### Gaps
- No period text was found that explicitly compares Asmara or Eritrea with Alto Adige or the "nuove province".
- The B134904 exhibition in Bolzano needs a date and venue from the local press (*Alpenzeitung* or *La Provincia di Bolzano*, Aug 1938).
- I could not find when or by whom "piccola Roma" was first applied to Asmara.
- The 1952-1991 fate of the Italian street names, the Casa del Fascio and the fasces in Asmara is documented only in passing (fasces kept, "XVI" erased). A systematic list would need Denison et al. 2003 or the dossier annexes (445 MB, not downloaded: [Annexes](https://whc.unesco.org/document/155769)).
