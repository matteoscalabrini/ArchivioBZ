# Bolzano's Semirurali Shared Offices, Not Blueprints

The Bolzano Semirurali were not drawn with the colonization toolkit, but the state machine that peopled the Agro Pontino and Libya also brought their tenants in. On the drawing board every item differs. The provincial public-housing institute (IFACP) planned the quarter in-house under its technical director, **Ing. Giulio Garbini, "chiamato da Venezia"**, and financed it under the ordinary housing code (T.U. 1165/1938) with Public Works Ministry contributions. The result was a local version of a national 1937–40 "semirurale" worker type, also tried in Turin, Milan and Rome: rented flats with garden plots of about **100–150 m² per family**. Pontine and Libyan colonists were meant to redeem farms of **10–60 ha** from agencies such as the ONC and the ECL, and no architect of theirs is documented in Bolzano. Around the quarter, though, the apparatus is shared. The **Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione interna**, which chose the Pontine and Libyan families, policed worker transfers into Bolzano from a local office from 1936–37. The 1939 anti-urbanism law made it the gatekeeper for moves between provinces, and on **21 December 1939** it ruled that settlers replacing departing South Tyroleans must come from **the 56 provinces north of Perugia**. Its founder, Luigi Razza, countersigned the 1935 decree that expropriated land for Bolzano's industrial zone. The same Luce art director filmed the Rione Dux and the Libyan "Ventimila", and the Veneto fed all three flows. The vocabulary splits. No period text the researchers read calls the Semirurali a "colonia" or a "bonifica", yet a reclamation motto framed the inauguration and the central square was named "Pontinia". The regime's colonization words and drawings went instead to South Tyrol's agricultural settlements, above all the ONC's "Borgo Vittoria" at Sinich, whose streets carry the same irredentist names as the Libyan villages. For the studio, the settler-colonial reading holds as a claim about means and structure: settlement filtered by the state, land transfer, and recruitment rules that excluded the local population, all run by Fascism's colonization office. It survives its strongest critics only if framed, with Wolfe, as settlement meant to speed assimilation, with replacement planned only in the Option years. A fourth thread, Asmara, supplies the control case. It was a colonial dual city, zoned by race by governor's decree from 1908 and replanned in 1938–39 with a green belt around a relocated native quarter. Its Italian majority was produced in the same five years by the same Commissariato, but as a rotating mass of men on six-month contracts rather than families in rented flats. Two points in the earlier report need correcting, and they come first.

## Two corrections to the earlier report

### The 1934 decree contains no "regnicola" clause, and no worker formula at all

The earlier report did not put "manodopera regnicola e non allogena" into the decree's text. It attributed the phrase to the awarding commission's 1936 wording as quoted by Carlo Romeo ([Romeo 2022](https://www.novecento.org/sul-confine-lalto-adige-sudtirol-nella-storia-del-novecento/il-fascismo-al-brennero-un-breve-profilo-per-questioni-7361/)). The new notes settle the legal point: the researchers read **R.D.L. 28 settembre 1934 n. 1621** in full, and the phrase is not in it [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1934-09-28;1621); [G.U. 16 Oct 1934](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1934/10/16/243/sg/pdf)).

A re-reading for this report adds a further point: the decree has no rule tying grants to the number or origin of workers [checked]. Art. 5 authorises "la concessione di contributi da parte dello Stato", and art. 6 caps them at 25 million lire, with at most 5 million a year. Art. 7 leaves "le norme per l'assegnazione dei contributi" to decrees of the Minister for Corporations "di concerto con quelli per le finanze e per l'interno". It has each award heard by a commission that included a representative of the Confederazione fascista dei lavoratori dell'industria. The earlier notes spoke of "the grant formula in the 1934 decree". That formula is not in the decree. If it existed, it sits in the Article 7 implementing decrees, which neither thread has found.

The phrase therefore rests on Romeo's account of how the commission worked. It stays tagged **[S]** as reported administrative practice, not as law. It agrees with Dunajtschik and Steinacher, who cite Steininger (pp. 114–115) and Lehmann (pp. 145–146). They write that grants were proportional to the workers hired, "wobei die für die Verteilung zuständige Kommission klarstellte, dass man dabei in erster Linie an Arbeiter aus Altitalien dachte" [S] ([GR/SR 17, 2008, pp. 126–127](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)). The Interior Ministry, which handled the "allogeni", had a statutory say over the allocation rules. That makes the implementing decrees worth hunting for [INF].

### 228 houses and 808 flats was the quarter at its inauguration, not a 1940 addition

The earlier report followed the Comune's 2015 booklet. In that account a first lot of 92 houses with 342 dwellings was inaugurated on 8 October 1939. "Nell'estate del 1940 si aggiungono 228 case con 808 appartamenti", and the final total was **327 houses, 1,151 dwellings and about 5,500 inhabitants** [S] ([Comune booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf)).

Period sources reverse that sequence. In April 1939 *La Provincia di Bolzano* announced that "Duecentoventotto graziose casette contornate di orti e giardini costituiranno il ridente quartiere destinato a ottocentodieci famiglie operaie" [S] ([digest 1939](https://claudiaugusta-bolzano-scomparsa.provincia.bz.it/1939.html)). On inauguration day the *Alpenzeitung* reported: "Der ganze Rayon besteht aus 22[8] Bauten mit insgesamt 808 Wohnungen. Alle Bauten sind fertiggestellt und bis auf 150 Wohnungen, in die nächstens die Familien einziehen werden, sind alle besetzt". It put the cost at 22 million lire [V, checked] ([AZ 8 Oct 1939, p. 5](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043421)). Three days later Luce described "il salubre e ridente Rione Popolare Dux che ultimato consterà di 228 casette con 808 appartamenti e 8 negozi" [V, checked] ([Luce B1599](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022261/2/superba-realizzazione-del-regime-benessere-lavoratori-nella-provincia-del-brennero-inaugurata-dal-ministro-segretario-del-partito.html)). LPB itself rounded up to "trecento graziose villette con un complesso di un migliaio di appartamenti" [V] ([LPB 10 Oct 1939, p. 3](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/media/large/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001/1939/1939_10_10_Nr_240/00000483.jpg)).

The first lot (**92 houses**, built 27 June 1938 to 22 April 1939) is therefore inside the 228/808, not before it. The 228/808 figure counts houses and flats at the inauguration of 8 October 1939, and Luce's "ultimato" points to one unfinished piece. The booklet dates **type VI**, the shop-houses with four flats above four shops, to 1940; two of them formed "gli otto negozi di piazza Pontinia". That plausibly explains the booklet's "estate 1940" [INF].

After that the period sources record further growth. A "zweites Baulos" was handed over on 28 October 1940 [V] ([AZ 25 Oct 1940](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043678)). Eight lots were counted in Rione Dux by May 1941 [V] ([AZ 11 May 1941](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059044952)). In December 1941, 110 semirural buildings with 395 dwellings were under construction [S] ([digest 1941](https://claudiaugusta-bolzano-scomparsa.provincia.bz.it/1941.html)). A sixth lot of 200 dwellings was never contracted [S] ([Azzolini 1985, p. 28](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)).

So the final 327/1,151 total remains plausible: Azzolini's 340 + 400 + 350 gives about 1,090, and the Comune's 2004 text gives 328 houses and 1,147 dwellings. But the booklet reaches its total the wrong way. It adds the first lot to a total that already includes it, and it leaves the 1941 lots out. The two errors roughly cancel, because the 1941 programme (about 395 dwellings) was about the size of the first lot (about 342) [INF].

The safe formulation is: **about 808 dwellings in 228 houses at the inauguration of 8 October 1939, and about 1,100–1,150 dwellings in about 327 houses once the last lots were finished after 1945**. No IFACP table of lots has been found. The IFACP photo album in the Archivio Storico della Città and the IPES records would settle the count.

## The Semirurali came off a housing-institute drawing board

### An in-house engineer used a planning loophole, under ordinary housing law

**Founding and purpose.** The institute that built the quarter was founded on **10 April 1934** as the "Istituto Case Economiche e Popolari di Bolzano". Its brief was a first programme of 500 dwellings "da destinare alla manodopera della nuova zona industriale". It bought land on the right bank of the Isarco that the land registry marked as "paludosi … e quindi facilmente acquistabili" [S] ([Azzolini 1985, p. 19](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)). Giacomozzi states the purpose plainly: "costruire in Alto Adige gli alloggi per i lavoratori dell'industria provenienti da altre province italiane" [S] ([Giacomozzi 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf)).

**The designer.** Garbini is the only designer named in any document. His signature is on the general site plan, "PLANIMETRIA GENERALE – CASE SEMIRURALI – BOLZANO – SCALA 1:2000". It is also on a type drawing stamped "Istituto Fascista Autonomo per le C.P. della Provincia di Bolzano", with the legend "Il Direttore Tecnico" [V] ([*Turris Babel* 1, pp. 25, 31](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)). Period reports confirm both officers: the *Alpenzeitung* names "Ing. Rota" as president and "Ing. Garbini" as technical director [V] ([AZ 14 Dec 1938](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059047939)). No biography of Garbini was found, and no colonial or Agro Pontino commission by him.

**The loophole.** Garbini worked around the town plan. The Piacentini plan foresaw only dense public housing here, at 330 inhabitants per hectare over 24 ha. Garbini instead used art. 19 of the plan's regulations, which allowed a "villino economico raggruppato", "certo per zone diverse da quelle operaie intensive". On about 70 ha he drew some thirty blocks, cut "600 mq per 4 alloggi" [S] ([Azzolini 1985, pp. 25–26](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline); [Azzolini 2004, pp. 119–120](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2729_Azzolini_ita.pdf)).

**Law and money.** The legal frame was the ordinary one. No national law creates a "casa semirurale" category. The *Testo unico sull'edilizia popolare ed economica* (R.D. 28 Apr 1938 n. 1165) governed the IFACP, its state contributions and the Public Works Ministry's oversight. Its art. 27 provides that the president "è nominato con decreto Reale su proposta del Ministro pei lavori pubblici" [V] ([G.U. 5 Aug 1938, suppl.](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1938/08/05/177/so/177/sg/pdf); [consolidated text](https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/15/zn38_04_001.html)). The only rural link in that code points the other way: art. 16 n. 12° lets colonization bodies that build "borgate rurali" borrow under it, and whether that clause was in the 1938 original could not be confirmed [V/S].

The ministry's annual report for 1940 counted 8,790 dwellings built nationwide by the housing institutes "mit staatlichen Beiträgen". It listed Bolzano's "zweites Baulos der halbländlichen Sied[l]erhäuser" among the works handed over on 28 October 1940 [V] ([AZ 25 Oct 1940](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043678)). The Comune paid for streets and sewers, starting with L. 996,400 in 1938 [V] ([AZ 7 Oct 1938](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059048114)).

**What the houses were.** They were two-storey houses, mostly of four dwellings with an external stair to the upper flats (types I, II, IV and V). Flats had 2–3 rooms of 40–60 m², or 74 m² in type III, built "per famiglie numerose". Each had a kitchen niche and a WC, but no bathroom and no cellar or attic [S] ([booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf); [Di Michele 2004, p. 86](https://storiaeregione.eu/attachment/get/up_126_16520955686792.pdf)).

**Cost and rent.** The first 808 dwellings cost about **27,000 lire each** [INF, from 22 million / 808]. The only rents found are for the same institute's semirural houses in Bressanone in June 1938. Three rooms with "100 Quadratmeter Grund für Gemüseanbau" rented for about **125 lire a month**, and two rooms for about 95 lire [V] ([AZ 28 Jun 1938](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059047966)).

### "Semirurale" was a national worker-housing type of 1937–40, not a colonial import

Azzolini traces the semirural turn to "nuove direttive" against urbanism, which he does not source. The stated aim was "in modo che la vita possa svolgersi senza l'obbligo di fare continuo ricorso alla città vicina, dalla quale si vogliono appunto distaccare le masse lavoratrici" [S] ([Azzolini 1985, p. 24](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)).

The same turn shows up across Italy. In Turin the housing institute followed the prefect's "autorevole consiglio" to build "villaggi operai con costruzioni di tipo estensivo". Their model was the "casetta unifamiliare o di pochi alloggi … con annesso un terreno da orto", meant to supply extra income, work for women, support for the demographic campaign and food in wartime; at Testona the garden was about 200 m² [S] ([MuseoTorino, pp. 336–337](https://www.museotorino.it/resources/pdf/books/149/files/assets/common/downloads/page0370.pdf)). In Milan, Bottoni and Pucci drew "borgate semirurali per abitazioni operaie" for the provincial housing institute with 50 million lire of INA money, and little was built [S] ([Archivio Bottoni](https://www.archiviobottoni.polimi.it/docs/archopere/architettura-urbanistica-e-design-1924-1973/op-178-progetto-di-borgate-semirurali-per-abitazioni-operaie-in-provincia-di-milano-1938-39-con-mario-pucci)). In Rome, San Basilio (1940) was a state-subsidised "borgata semi-rurale per famiglie numerose" with gardens of about 500 m² [SS] ([Sapienza handout](https://elearning.uniroma1.it/mod/resource/view.php?id=381183)). In its own province the Bolzano institute built the same type in Merano, Vipiteno, Bressanone and at the Brenner [S] ([Giacomozzi 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf)).

The type's lineage is the national campaign against urbanism, not the colonial village. Bolzano's version is an unusually large and complete local application of it [INF].

### The quarter grew a civic core afterwards; the Libyan village was born with one

The Libyan "centro rurale" was defined in law before anyone lived there. R.D.L. 17 May 1938 n. 701 makes it "un nucleo di edifici destinati ad assicurare servizi pubblici": the "Chiesa con la Canonica", schools, an ambulatorio, the "Casa del fascio e … organizzazioni del Regime", the post office and markets [S] ([Capresi 2007, p. 73](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)).

The Rione Dux gained comparable pieces in stages. The institute's president reported a nursery, an ONMI dispensary, public baths, "locali destinati alla disinfezione delle masserizie dei nuovi inquilini", and "la sede del gruppo rionale fascista e del Dopolavoro" [S] ([Azzolini 1985, pp. 26–27](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)). The eight shops of piazza Pontinia followed in 1940, and the "Scuolette" opened in autumn 1941 [S] ([booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf)). Work on the church in piazza Pontinia began in July 1941 [S] ([digest 1941](https://claudiaugusta-bolzano-scomparsa.provincia.bz.it/1941.html)).

The programme converges with Libya's, but the order is reversed: the Libyan centre was the settlement's starting nucleus, while Bolzano's arrived after the housing [INF]. One item looks more like settler reception than city housing: the room for disinfecting newcomers' belongings. It has no documentary follow-up yet [INF].

## The colonies ran on a three-part machine: agency, public works office and Commissariato

The Agro Pontino set the pattern. The ONC, the veterans' body founded on 10 December 1917, ran the reclamation, and the Commissariato chose the families: "La scelta delle famiglie venete per colonizzare l'Agro Pontino, è fatta esclusivamente da un ente governativo, il C.M.C.I." [S] ([Gaspari 1985, pp. 70–71](https://famigliaperuzzo.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/oscar_gaspari_lemigrazione_veneta_nellag.pdf)). By 1939 there were **2,953 ONC poderi** with 29,300 colonists in five towns and 17–18 borghi, and 59.2% of the families came from the Veneto (counted with Udine), mainly from "Treviso, Udine, Padova, Rovigo, le province più povere del Veneto" [S] (same source). The selection rules quoted from Collari's *La redenzione dell'Agro Pontino* asked for rural families "'fedeli alla terra' … compatta nella sua solida composizione famigliare, unita tutta attorno al suo Capo": ex-combatants from malarial zones of high demographic pressure, and "di pura razza italiana" [S] (same source).

Libya divided the labour three ways, each by statute. The land agency came first: the Ente di Colonizzazione della Cirenaica was created by R.D.L. 11 June 1932 n. 696 under "la presidenza dello stesso Commissario per le Migrazioni Interne, on. Luigi Razza". As the ECL from 1935, and alongside the INFPS in Tripolitania, it divided the land, built the farmhouses and managed the families [S] ([Capresi 2007, pp. 51–56](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)). The Ufficio Centrale OO.PP. under ing. Silvio Camilletti designed and built the centri rurali, with contracted architects signing the drawings: Mario Romano for the four Gebel villages of 1933–34; Florestano Di Fausto, Umberto Di Segni and Giovanni Pellegrini for 1938; Alfredo Longarini and others for 1939 [S] ([Capresi 2007, pp. 75–85](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)). Fascist federations and the agricultural union nominated families, and the Commissariato chose them after medical examinations, the "raccolta delle informazioni politiche, professionali e morali" and a home visit [S] (Capresi, p. 58 n. 36). Balbo summed up the principle: "Selezione fisica e morale adunque: binomio inscindibile" [S] (Capresi, p. 58).

The 1938 "Ventimila" built 1,800 poderi in six months. The 1939 wave carried **1,465 families and 10,907 people**, led by Padova (203), Treviso (193) and Venezia (166) [S] ([Capresi 2007, pp. 58–62](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)). On the listed provinces the Veneto supplied about 55% of families, and with Udine about 62% [INF]. Families earned wages for two years and sharecropped for three, then amortised the podere, net of a state grant of 30–33%. Ownership was expected "in 19 anni", or after about thirty [S] (Capresi, card pp. 49–50).

Ethiopia tried to transplant whole regions. Three decree-laws of **6 December 1937** (nn. 2300, 2314 and 2325) created the *Enti di colonizzazione* of Romagna, Veneto and Puglia d'Etiopia [V] ([Normattiva, Romagna](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1940/02/02/039U2107/CONSOLIDATED/20091216); [Puglia](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1939/03/08/038U2210/CONSOLIDATED/20091216); [Veneto](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/1940/07/17/040U0823/CONSOLIDATED/20091216)). The Veneto programme, never launched, meant to recreate "un ambiente simile a quello lasciato" from "nuclei omogenei di coloni" [S] ([Ertola, pp. 74–75](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)). The ONC, the Pontine agency, ran Olettà and Biscioftù. Its settlers came "in massima parte dalla Centuria degli Ex Combattenti della 'Sabaudia'", and they lived in 60 m² houses built to look "familiare ai coloni". The Commissariato found only 56 of 90 families fit [S] ([Grosselli 2023, pp. 350–351](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186); [Ertola, p. 72](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)).

The Dodecanese adds the Alpine twist. On Rhodes, Governor Mario Lago built **Campochiaro** (1935–36) "per boscaioli delle nostre montagne". The recruiter was Giuseppe Valcanover, a Trentino forester who had worked in Alto Adige from 1918 to 1932 and kept his family home in Bolzano. German-speaking South Tyrolean families followed the Fiemme woodcutters in 1936. The village was designed by Armando Bernabiti, once "a Roma all'Istituto Case Popolari", with Rodolfo Petracco. In 1939 most of the South Tyroleans left it because they had opted for Germany: "Da Rodi noi dovemmo partire per le Opzioni" [S] ([Grosselli 2023, pp. 357–364](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186); [it.wikipedia, Bernabiti](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Bernabiti)).

## Asmara is the control case: a dual city zoned by law

Asmara is where Fascist planning met a colonized majority that was legally a different category of person, so it is the right control for reading Bolzano as a dual city. Three items in the brief need correcting first, as the 04 notes do. The pre-Fascist plan is Odoardo Cavagnari's, not "Odorizzi's". There was no 1939 census of Asmara's Italians. And "piccola Roma" has no period source: the researcher found it only in journalism such as *Il Post* in 2017 [SS] ([Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/2017/07/11/asmara-la-piccola-roma-in-eritrea/)). The regime's own terms were "colonia primogenita" [V] ([Luce M004911](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000051636/1/eritrea-colonia-primogenita-d-italia.html)) and "una graziosissima città italiana … tesa verso un avvenire veram. imperiale" [V] ([CTI 1938, p. 200](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=206)).

### Liberal Asmara was zoned by race in 1908, and Fascism inherited the frame

Asmara's dual city predates Fascism by a generation, and it is written in law. Governor Giuseppe Salvago Raggi's decrees of 23 October and 19 December 1908 (nn. 798 and 814) divided the city into four zones. The first was reserved "esclusivamente per le abitazioni degli Italiani e dei Cittadini di Stati stranieri", and the second was for mixed residence around the market. The third was "riservata esclusivamente per le abitazioni dei sudditi coloniali e degli assimilati ad essi", and the fourth held suburbs and industry [S] ([Canali 2016, p. 94 n. 16](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)). ICOMOS calls the 1908 plan "based on four zones, in conformity with ethnic segregation and functional programmes" [V] ([ICOMOS 2017, p. 46](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755)). Cavagnari directed the colony's Genio Civile from 1912, and his plan of 1913–16 "confirmed the racial segregation of the previous plan" [V] (ICOMOS, same page; [S] Canali, pp. 94–95). A decree of 1914 repeated the ban on "gli indigeni e gli assimilati" living in the first zone [S] (Canali, p. 95 n. 18).

Fascism added a building boom and a sharper line. Guido Ferrazza, born at Bocenago in Trentino in 1887, became the municipality's planning consultant on 1 April 1935. His 1937 plan, approved by Governor De Feo, promised that "verrà accentuato il distacco della Città nazionale dal Quartiere indigeno", and that in the peripheral workers' quarters "ogni abitazione avrà un giardinetto ed un orticello" [S] ([Canali 2016, pp. 96–99](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)). De Feo's 1937 competition was annulled after the first prize went to "Sannita", a group that included the governor himself [S] (Canali, pp. 100–101). The UNESCO dossier adds that the competition's terms "perpetuated the racial segregation" and were written by Marcello Piacentini. Canali does not name him, so that attribution rests on a single source [S] ([Nomination text, pp. 48–49](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)).

### Cafiero's 1938–39 plan used the market as a buffer and a green belt as a wall

Vittorio Cafiero, a Roman rationalist, sat on the Ministry's colonial planning council and on the planning division of the Consiglio Superiore dei Lavori Pubblici. Teruzzi sent him to Asmara on 8 April 1938. He delivered 25 drawings on 20 February 1939, and the Consulta Centrale per l'Edilizia e l'Urbanistica approved the plan in April 1939 [S] ([Canali 2016, pp. 101–106](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)). ICOMOS dates the commission to 1936 and completion to 1938, but the archival letters support 1938–39 [INF].

The plan kept the mixed market zone as a buffer, so that "the only contact of the whites with natives will be with the highest category of them, the commercial and industrial one" [S] ([Nomination text, pp. 50–51](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)). It moved a "Zona indigena" north-west beyond Abba Shawl, behind hills to be "trasformata in «barriera» verde". The Consulta then asked that "la zona divisoria di Verde" be widened, and the Ministry ruled that the new native quarter required clearing the old one [S] (Canali, pp. 102–106). Planned densities were **380 inhabitants per hectare in the native quarter against 140 in the Italian one**. The dossier cites these figures to "Teruzzi, p.391", a source nobody has identified [S] (dossier, pp. 50–51). The 1938 Touring guide shows the result on the ground: a "Zona Industriale, tracciata nella piana già occupata dal Villaggio indigeno", and a viale Milano "che separa nettam. la città italiana a S dal villaggio eritreo a N" [V] ([CTI 1938, pp. 203, 206](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=212)). Governor Daodiace resisted the native-quarter scheme as "una esagerazione urbanistica", while reporting "Io sto attuando la bonifica del quartiere indigeno, abbattendo i tucul peggiori" [S] (Canali, pp. 106–107).

Imperial statutes then made the line personal. R.D.L. 19 April 1937 n. 880 punished a citizen's "relazione d'indole coniugale" with a subject with one to five years in prison [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1937-04-19;880)). L. 29 June 1939 n. 1004, on the "prestigio di razza", defined the citizen as "il cittadino italiano metropolitano di razza ariana" (art. 2). It punished the citizen who "frequenti abitualmente luoghi aperti al pubblico riservati ai nativi" (art. 12) [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1939-06-29;1004)). L. 13 May 1940 n. 822 made the child of a citizen and a native "nativo a tutti gli effetti" [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1940-05-13;822)). On the street, "Eritreans were forbidden from using or crossing Viale Mussolini", and after 1938 their movement "became tightly restricted … especially at night" [S] ([Nomination text, pp. 63, 262–263](http://web.archive.org/web/20200705140326/https://whc.unesco.org/document/155767)). Barrera, following Goglia, reports ordinances on separate residence, entertainment and transport, but their texts were not found [S] ([Barrera 1996, p. 38](https://arch.library.northwestern.edu/downloads/kp78gg509?locale=en)).

### Four figures for the flip, and no census behind any of them

Before 1935 Asmara held about 3,000–4,000 Italians beside 12,000–20,000 Eritreans [S] ([Podestà 2015, ¶11](https://journals.openedition.org/diacronie/1919); [Canali 2016, p. 95](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)). By 1938–39 Italians were the majority. The figures must stay separate, because they measure different things at different dates. The Touring guide of 1938 prints "ab. 98 000 (di cui 53 000 Italiani)" [V] ([CTI 1938, p. 199](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=205)). Podestà gives 48,000 Italians against 36,000 Africans in early 1939 [S] ([Podestà 2015, ¶11](https://journals.openedition.org/diacronie/1919)). Barrera, citing Rossetti, gives 41,263 Italian men and 11,177 women in Asmara in 1939 [S, checked] ([Barrera 1996, p. 42](https://arch.library.northwestern.edu/downloads/kp78gg509?locale=en)).

The "1939 census" label that circulates for the 98,000/53,000 pair is wrong. Barrera states that "No proper census took place after 1931" [S, checked] (Barrera, p. 5 n. 12). The only 1939 census attested is Castellano's count of the native population [S] ([Wikipedia, Castellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Castellano)).

The Italian majority was a labour mass, not a settled population. Women were about a fifth of it, and contracts ran "a base semestrale". Between October 1935 and June 1939 more workers came home from East Africa (**207,983**) than left (**201,440**) [S, checked] ([Ertola, pp. 24–25](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)). The Eritrean population grew too, to 36,000–45,000, so "flip" here means outnumbered, not replaced [INF].

### The Commissariato filled Asmara and Bolzano in the same five years

The labour flow ran through the same office that controlled transfers into Bolzano. "L'invio di operai nell'Impero era gestito da Sergio Nannini, commissario per la colonizzazione e le migrazioni interne", and the procedure "sottostava alle stesse regole previste per la colonizzazione interna" [S, checked] ([Ertola, pp. 34–35](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)). The procedure ran in four steps. Nannini weighed contractors' requests against provincial unemployment and set a quota for each province, and prefects split it among communes. Candidates passed medical, occupational, political and police checks. Finally, party secretaries and union officials drew up the lists (same source).

The Commissariato counted **201,440 Italian workers** sent to East Africa from October 1935 to June 1939 (same source, p. 24). The Veneto led with **39,871 of 189,737** workers up to September 1937, while Venezia Tridentina sent **1,415** [S] ([Tagliacarne 1938 via Montesanto 2023, pp. 15–16](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/c64cf5d2-4a2e-42a3-b6b1-64e3b959e306/Tesi%20Giampaolo%20Montesanto.pdf)). Named Trentino men from Brentonico went to Asmara in 1936. One of them, Italo Giovanazzi, died there in 1957 as a taxi driver [S] ([Grosselli 2023, pp. 335–336](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)). The researcher found no worker from the province of Bolzano. The 1938 guide lists a "Delegazione del Commissariato per le Migrazioni interne e la Colonizzazione, largo M. Camperio" among Asmara's offices [V] ([CTI 1938, p. 198](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=204)). It was the counterpart of the Bolzano desk active from 1937.

Asmara also built the semirural formula. In 1937 four "villaggi operai" were begun, named "A. Mussolini, L. Razza, Col. De Cristoforis, Magg. Toselli" [V] ([CTI 1938, p. 200](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:CTI_-_Guida_dell%27Africa_Orientale_Italiana,_1938.djvu&page=206)), and 300 "casette" each were announced for the first two [S] ([Canali 2016, p. 100](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)). Luce filmed De Feo laying the first stone of the Toselli and Arnaldo Mussolini villages, followed by "la benedizione religiosa dei due villaggi" [V] ([Luce B121507](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000028033/2/l-inizio-lavori-costruzione-due-nuovi-villaggi-operai-alla-presenza-del-governatore-eritrea-de-feo.html)).

The doctrine appeared in the planning journal *Urbanistica*. In "I borghi operai nelle Colonie" (1938), G. Rigotti held that colonial workers' villages should follow the European ones of "Italia e Germania", and that decentralisation "impedisce il fenomeno anti-economico dell'Urbanesimo". Vegetable plots, he argued, cut food costs, and grouping workers' housing served the rule "dividere nettamente, senza falsi scrupoli e senza mezzi termini, le abitazioni dei Nazionali da quelle degli Indigeni" [S] ([Canali 2016, p. 99 n. 37](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/handle/2158/1074524/210456/2016%2A.%20ASUP%204%20ASMARA.pdf)). One village bore the name of Razza, the Commissariato's founder, who had died on the Cairo–Asmara flight in August 1935 [V] ([Luce B073605](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000019726/2/i-funerali-luigi-razza-e-compagni-volo-periti-7-agosto-nell-incidente-aereo-cairo-e-l-asmara.html)).

### Eritrea was colonized through the city, not the farm

The farm route had been tried and had failed. Leopoldo Franchetti, named "commissario per la colonizzazione" in 1890, set up three experimental farms at Asmara, Godofelassi and Gura. They used "circa 4500 Ettari" of land declared state property, worked by peasant families "arruolati con atto notarile" and promised ownership [V] ([Melli 1899, pp. 64, 87–90](https://archive.org/details/lacoloniaeritre00mellgoog)). Some 15–30 families came. Bahta Hagos's revolt of December 1894 and the defeat at Adwa in 1896 ended the scheme [S] ([Pagura 2020](http://www.storiastoriepn.it/le-delizie-delleritrea-ovvero-il-tentativo-fallito-di-colonizzare-laltipiano-eritreo/); [Cattaneo 2019](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1109835/1/shsconf_modscapes2018_05002.pdf)).

The later Tessenei cotton scheme was worked by Cunama and Beni Amer sharecroppers, and in 1938 it had 1,850 inhabitants, 50 of them Italian [S] ([Cattaneo 2019, pp. 7–8](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1109835/1/shsconf_modscapes2018_05002.pdf)). No Eritrean counterpart to the Ethiopian regional colonization bodies was found [S/INF]. Fascist Eritrea gained its Italian majority through state works, building firms and the labour office alone. Bolzano did the same, with the industrial zone and the Semirurali rather than farms [INF].

### The Bolzano links, and two opposite afterlives

No newsreel puts Asmara and Bolzano in one item, and no period text equating Eritrea with Alto Adige was found. The links are men and reels. Luce filmed "la mostra dei prodotti dell'Impero" in Bolzano on 3 August 1938, with "la statua dell'infaticabile lavoratore italiano" [V, checked] ([Luce B134904](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000031251/2/la-mostra-prodotti-impero.html)). Cobolli Gigli inspected the imperial roads in 1937–38 [V] ([Luce B115406](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000024252/2/la-rete-stradale.html)). In July 1939 he opened Bolzano's Ponte Littorio with Prefect Mastromattei: "il ponte unisce la zona industriale con il villaggio delle case popolari" [V, checked] ([Luce B154001](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000021490/2/il-ponte-del-littorio.html)). Piacentini drew Bolzano's plan and, according to the dossier, the 1937 Asmara competition terms [S]. Ferrazza was later planning consultant to the Provveditorato alle Opere Pubbliche per il Trentino-Alto Adige [S] ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/guido-ferrazza_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)). In 2008 Martin Hanni wrote in the Bolzano-based *Geschichte und Region* that the 1940 street front of Asmara's Casa del Fascio, "vergleichbar mit dem ebenfalls dem Monumentalismo zugeschriebenen Monumento della Vittoria in Bozen", was more political demonstration than necessity [S, checked] ([Hanni 2008](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957023447.pdf)).

The afterlives run in opposite directions. Asmara's colonized majority took over the colonizer's city after 1941 and kept it almost intact as a mark of distinctness from Ethiopia. UNESCO inscribed it on 8 July 2017 under criteria (ii) and (iv): 481 ha, with a buffer zone of 1,203 ha [V] ([WHC 1550](http://web.archive.org/web/20261011124247/https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1550/)). The statement of value names the segregation outright: "colonial planning, based on functional and racial segregation principles" (same source). The property includes the native quarters of Arbate Asmera and Abbashawel as "illustrating the exclusion and segregation suffered by the indigenous population" [V] ([ICOMOS 2017, p. 48](http://web.archive.org/web/20230628185943/https://whc.unesco.org/document/159755)). That partly answers Fuller's critique that the earlier heritage perimeter "excludes the same area Italian colonizers excluded under the rubric of quartiere indigeno" [S] ([Fuller 2011](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cisj/article/41166/galley/30790/download)).

Bolzano went the other way. There the heirs of the local population contested the Fascist city for decades, and the answer was reinterpretation in place, the "defusing by addition" described in the earlier report [earlier thread] ([bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com](https://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/)).

## What holds and what fails

### The drawing board fails, and the real twin sits at Sinich

On the drawing board the hypothesis fails in every row. The office was a housing institute, the law the housing code, the money the Public Works Ministry's housing line, the designer a housing engineer with no known colonial work, and the tenure was rent. The 02 researcher found no Libyan architect working in Bolzano and no colonial farmhouse plan reaching the IFACP. They read the typological likeness, a house with a plot for a self-provisioning family, as "shared ruralist doctrine, not a transfer of drawings" [INF].

The shared doctrine is real and can be quoted on both sides. In Libya after 1938 the farmhouse stood alone on its lot "con lo specifico scopo di avvicinare anche psicologicamente il colono al proprio terreno" [S] ([Capresi 2007, card pp. 11–12](https://web.archive.org/web/20211129071917/https://www.ub.tuwien.ac.at/diss/AC05034868.pdf)). In Bolzano, *Atesia Augusta* praised gardens "ove gettare un seme e raccogliere un frutto … per non dimenticare del tutto le origini rurali e attaccarvisi anzi maggiormente" [S] ([Di Michele 2004, p. 86](https://storiaeregione.eu/attachment/get/up_126_16520955686792.pdf)). Both aim to fix families to the soil, but at different scales and for different ends. "Semi-rural" names the hinge: the house keeps the rural symbols (the plot, the Predappio stair) on an urban-industrial function [INF].

The colonization drawing board did reach South Tyrol, but at **Sinich near Merano**, not in Bolzano. The ONC's "Borgo Vittoria" was inaugurated there in 1928 [S, checked] ([GR/SR 17, 2008, pp. 128–131](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)). Its "Piazza Vittoria" gathered the Dopolavoro, the school, the Carabinieri, the church and the Fascio office. The ONC financed houses built "im Stile von Einfamilienhäusern des Veneto", and around the village farmland was brought into cultivation "oder Höfe von Südtirolern enteignet". The settlers were chosen from war veterans, "römisch stark, aber vor allem fruchtbar". The streets carry the names of "Cesare Battisti, Damiano Chiesa, Fabio Filzi und Nazario Sauro", the irredentist pantheon of the Libyan villages and of the crypt of Bolzano's Victory Monument; of the four, only Chiesa has no Libyan village (Capresi's catalogue, 02 notes).

From 1923 the ONC also administered and sold "enemy" properties confiscated under the Treaty of Saint-Germain, which were to go "ausschließlich an Italiener aus den 'alten Provinzen'" (same source). The article says the model for Borgo Vittoria was "die Stadtgründungen in den Pontinischen Sümpfen". That is an anachronism, since Littoria was founded in 1932; the link is the ONC programme, not the Pontine towns [INF].

Sinich also had the other branch. A Montecatini fertiliser plant opened there in 1924–25, and the regime favoured "die ausschließliche Anstellung von Italienern" a decade before Bolzano's industrial zone (same source). The Semirurali are the 1930s scale-up of Sinich's industrial branch, not of Borgo Vittoria [INF]. That is the precise answer to "same toolkit": the regime ran two branches, and the Semirurali belong to the one without colonial drawings.

### The state apparatus and the people hold

At the level of offices, statutes and men, the hypothesis holds better than the earlier report knew.

**The Commissariato's statute.** L. 9 April 1931 n. 358 gave it the "razionale distribuzione della mano d'opera disponibile … in tutto il Regno, e … anche nelle Colonie". Every transfer of "gruppi di lavoratori e di famiglie coloniche" between provinces needed its authorisation [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1931-04-09;358)).

**Its Bolzano office.** It appears in the budget from 1936–37 and was active from April 1937. It controlled "i rapporti di lavoro degli operai che vengono trasferiti in codesta provincia da altre provincie del regno", in "armonia con la Federazione dei Fasci di Combattimento" [S, earlier thread] ([Gallo 2017](https://www.asei.eu/it/2017/05/operazioni-migratorie-belliche-transfrontaliere-e-rimpatri-da-una-parte-allaltra-delle-alpi/)). This overrides the 03 researcher's inference that its Alto Adige role was "minor or late".

**The 1939 anti-urbanism law.** L. 6 July 1939 n. 1092 named the Commissariato as the authority over hiring from other provinces (art. 2) and over "lo spostamento temporaneo di gruppi di lavoratori" (art. 3). It barred residence in provincial capitals and "Comuni di notevole importanza industriale" without "una proficua occupazione stabile" (art. 1) [V] ([G.U. 9 Aug 1939](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1939/08/09/185/sg/pdf)). For a Semirurali tenant, job, residence and flat became one chain [INF].

**The replacement plan.** The Commissariato's circular of 21 December 1939 to the federal secretaries said that settlers "destined to replace the South Tyroleans who would leave for Germany should come from the 56 northern and central provinces, north of Perugia" [V, checked] ([Di Michele, JMIS 2023, n. 22](https://doi.org/10.1080/1354571X.2022.2122369)). It is held at ACS, *Commissariato generale per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione interna*, b. 40G. A month later *La Provincia di Bolzano* reported that "Giuseppe Lombrassa, Commissario per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione" had chaired a meeting on "la immigrazione dei lavoratori necessari per la sostituzione degli allogeni e tedeschi trasferentisi in Germania" [S, digest] ([Bolzano scomparsa 1940](https://claudiaugusta-bolzano-scomparsa.provincia.bz.it/1940.html)). That item also gives a provisional answer to the 03 notes' open question of who headed the Commissariato after Razza, pending the original page.

**Its later life.** The office was not wound up in 1939. From 1 March 1942 it moved under the Ministry of Corporations "per la durata dell'attuale guerra", and its 1931 law was repealed only in 1961 [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1931-04-09;358~art1!vig=1942-06-01)).

The people form a chain across the theatres. **Luigi Razza** was secretary of the Fascio di Trento in 1920, first commissioner of the Commissariato (1930–35) and, from 1932, president of the Cyrenaican agency. As Minister of Public Works he countersigned R.D.L. 7 March 1935 n. 234, which expropriated the industrial-zone land at "puro valore venale del terreno considerato come fondo rustico". The decree excluded any increase in value from "la progettata creazione della zona industriale" and gave tenants nothing for cancelled leases [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1935-03-07;234); [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/luigi-razza_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)). A Libyan village and the Naples pier for southern colonists bear his name [S/V] ([Luce D028106](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000052880/1/-302.html)).

**Giuseppe Cobolli Gigli** had written "Il fascismo e gli allogeni" for *Gerarchia* in 1927, as "Cobol" [S, tertiary]. As Minister of Public Works (5 September 1935 to 31 October 1939) he proposed the 1936 extension of the industrial zone [V] ([Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1936-06-04;1368); [it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Cobolli_Gigli)). His ministry also oversaw and subsidised the IFACPs, so the Semirurali were designed, built and inaugurated under his ministry [INF]. **Count Volpi**, a former Governor of Tripolitania, inaugurated the industrial zone in December 1936; the reel shows "nuove palazzine per i volontari di guerra", housing for Ethiopian-war volunteers [V] ([Luce B1014](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000027681/2/il-ministro-benni-e-conte-volpi-inaugurano-nuovo-centro-industriale-bolzano.html)). **Arturo Gemmiti** was art director of both the Bolzano inauguration reel and the Ventimila reels [V] ([Luce B1599](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022261/2/superba-realizzazione-del-regime-benessere-lavoratori-nella-provincia-del-brennero-inaugurata-dal-ministro-segretario-del-partito.html); [B1406](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000030534/2/l-arrivo-20-000-rurali-italiani-africa.html)).

On screen the ritual grammar is the same: a hierarch, a priest's blessing, families displayed, kitchen gardens filmed. Only the caption changes, from "benessere dei lavoratori" to "colonizzazione demografica" [INF].

The recruiting basin overlaps but was not run as one programme. Pontine families came mostly from Treviso, Udine, Padova and Rovigo; the 1939 Libyan wave from Padova, Treviso and Venezia; Bolzano's worker families "soprattutto dalle province di Padova, Verona e Rovigo". The flows also crossed the other way: Trentino families went to Cyrenaica in 1938–39, eight families "della Venezia Tridentina" were in the first ONC group for Ethiopia, and South Tyroleans settled Campochiaro [S] ([Grosselli 2023](https://archive.org/details/BIBLIOFBK-METS-991022027593606186)).

Selection is where the hypothesis bends without breaking. Pontine and Libyan families were screened as farmers. Bolzano's were filtered as workers by employers, the labour office, the Commissariato and the Fasci, and the industrial-zone commission reportedly favoured labour "regnicola e non allogena" [S]. The criterion differs; the filter is the same kind of machine [INF].

### The vocabulary splits by register

The negative result is solid within its scope. The 01 researcher read in full LPB of 10 October 1939, nine *Alpenzeitung* pages from 1938–41 and the LPB digest for 1937–43 (searched by keyword). None of them applies "colonia", "colonizzazione", "Kolonie" or "bonifica" to the Semirurali [V, negative]. The quarter was a "rione", a "villaggio semirurale", a "città operaia", and in German an "Arbeiterstadt" or the "Viertel der halbländlichen Wohnbauten". "Colonia operaia Rione Dux" appears only in a 2007 municipal text [S] ([Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)). The *Alpenzeitung*'s "Sied[l]erhäuser" of 1940 is ordinary German for small-settlement housing. It should not be read as a translation of "colonizzazione" without the Italian ministerial report [INF].

Reclamation and Pontine words do frame the quarter. At the inauguration a Mussolini motto, "»Land urbar machen und Städte gründen«", hung on a banner "auf der Piazza Littorio des gleichnamigen Stadtviertels". The *Alpenzeitung* said it "paßte für den Tag der Inauguration des Viertels der halbländlichen Wohnbauten, das die Fortsetzung der Arbeiterstadt Littorio bildet" [V, checked] ([AZ 10 Oct 1939, p. 5](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043496)). The banner hung in the neighbouring Littorio quarter, not in the Semirurali themselves. LPB's report says only that banners carried "alcune fra le più significative frasi, tratte da discorsi del Duce", without the wording, so the Italian original remains unknown [V, checked] ([LPB 10 Oct 1939, p. 3](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/media/large/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001/1939/1939_10_10_Nr_240/00000483.jpg)). The square itself was named "piazza Pontinia", after the Agro Pontino [V]. The same LPB page speaks of a "nuovo quartiere della città, che in breve ha visto sorgere e popolarsi trecento graziose villette" [V, checked], though that is the ordinary verb, not the technical "popolamento".

The legal and technical register did reach Alto Adige, through the land programme rather than the workers' quarter. It is in the Commissariato's own name and its December 1939 circular, in the ONC's archival series "Servizio Agrario, Alto Adige, **Coloni**" [S] ([Dalle Mulle and Ambrosino 2023, n. 47](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0018246X23000158)), and in the ENTV law's "opere di bonifica, trasformazione e riordinamento della proprietà fondiaria" [V] ([L. 1780/1939, art. 2](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1939-11-27;1780)).

Tolomei described Sinich in 1939 as an effort that saw "lavoro operoso e fecondo prendere il posto della palude triste e malsana", one of the "conquiste che l'Italia fascista ha realizzato nelle due provincie redente" [S, checked] ([GR/SR 17, 2008, n. 123](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)). In an undated *Archivio* note, quoted in the original by Valente and Ansaloni, he complained: "L'azione statale colonizzatrice sul modello di Roma antica s'è limitata ad una decina di masi dati ai combattenti nel Meranese. Goccia d'acqua in vasta arena … L'Agro Pontino conta interi villaggi di veneti. Non era male per i destini della Patria, se qualche centinaio di famiglie venete s'introduceva nelle valli atesine" [S, checked] (same source, n. 127). It is the clearest Italian-language text in the material that ties Pontine Veneto settlement to Alto Adige.

The terms of the brief sort cleanly. **"Allogeni"** is statutory, from the title of R.D.L. 10 January 1926 n. 16 [S], and recurs in 1939–40 in the press and in the Lombrassa meeting. **"Regnicoli"** survives only in Romeo's quotation of the 1936 commission [S]. **"Popolamento"** belongs to Libyan law (R.D.L. 1695/1928) and to Pontine doctrine. **"Bonifica dei cognomi"** was not found; the regime's term was "restituzione del cognome" [S] ([Giacomelli, Acme](https://www.ledonline.it/acme/allegati/Acme-06-I-07-Giacomelli.pdf)).

The two registers, as the 03 notes frame them [INF], work as follows. Technical words (colonizzazione interna, famiglie coloniche, bonifica, pubblica utilità) named Alto Adige operations exactly as they named the Pontine ones. National words (redenzione, restituzione, allogeni) cast the locals as lapsed Italians to be recovered. That second register is what separates South Tyrol from Libya.

### Asmara sharpens the verdicts, and shows where the analogy breaks

Asmara gives the dual-city reading of Bolzano a documented colonial control case, with something Bolzano never had: zoning by race in law. Asmara's residence zones were reserved "esclusivamente" for Italians and for *sudditi* by the decrees of 1908 and reaffirmed in 1914. Cafiero's buffer and green belt hardened them in 1938–39, and a 1939 statute punished citizens who frequented places reserved to natives.

Bolzano's new city was divided by plan and by class. Dunajtschik and Steinacher write that the regime wanted the two workers' quarters sealed off from the rest of the city "vor allem zur Vermeidung von sozialen Konflikten mit dem Mittelstand in der Città nuova". That separation "verschärfte die ethnische Teilung der Stadt dramatisch", and the German speakers' name for the quarters, "Schanghai", carried an ethnic as well as a social stigma [S, checked] ([GR/SR 17, 2008, p. 128](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)). Bolzano's separation was social in intent and ethnic in effect. No decree in the material barred a German speaker from the città nuova or an Italian from the old town [INF].

The demographic flip is where the two cities meet. In both it was produced in the same five years, 1935–40, by the same Commissariato, with quotas, political screening and a local delegation. Bolzano's Italian speakers rose from 21% in 1921 to 62% in 1939 [S] ([Dalle Mulle and Ambrosino, p. 899](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0018246X23000158)). Asmara's Italians rose from about 4,000 to about 50,000 [S, 04 notes].

Asmara also reverses the direction of the drawing-board hypothesis. The semirural formula of workers' houses with vegetable plots appears in a colony in 1937, the year Garbini drew his first proposal. Rigotti's 1938 doctrine then tied colonial workers' villages to the metropolitan ones of autarkic "Italia e Germania". If a type travelled, it went from the metropole to the colony, not from a colony to Bolzano [INF].

The analogy breaks at four points, and the studio should say so plainly. The first is the legal subject. Eritreans were *sudditi* and South Tyroleans were citizens, so Asmara zoned the native while Bolzano, in 1939, tried to remove him through the Option. The second is the settlers. Asmara's Italians were a rotating male workforce on six-month contracts, and more of them were repatriated than sent. Bolzano's were families in rented flats assigned by a housing institute, and they stayed ("Siamo diventati tutti bolzanini"). The third is scale: Asmara's Italian population rose more than tenfold in five years, while Bolzano's roughly doubled [INF, 04 notes]. The fourth is the outcome: Asmara's Italian majority dissolved after 1941, and Bolzano's did not. Asmara is a control, not a twin [INF].

## Implications for the settler-colonial reading

### The strongest case for the reading: Pergher, Tolomei and Wolfe

Pergher's book is the anchor. It argues that "it was in these territories, and not in Ethiopia, that the signature Fascist policy of extending Italianness through mass population settlement was developed and put into practice". In her account settlement "responded to a major territorial challenge … namely, how best to secure and legitimize rule over contested lands" [V, checked] ([Pergher 2018, pp. 5, 9](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/19741/excerpt/9781108419741_excerpt.pdf)). Her proof text is Tolomei in 1938. He celebrated the "splendid exodus to Libya of twenty thousand settlers" and argued that in Alto Adige "too" a state initiative "placing at least a few dozen families in every municipality" would be "an act of providence". Her comment: "This was no colony but rather a province inside state borders" [V, in Pergher's translation; the Italian is unseen] (same source, p. 10).

Her index shows that she engages the theory directly: "settler colonialism" on pp. 24, 28, 52, 71–72, 108–110, 113, 115, 164–165, 245 and 251; "internal colonization" on pp. 108–113; Wolfe on p. 164; Veracini on pp. 112 and 164 [V, index only] ([index](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/19741/index/9781108419741_index.pdf)). Those pages were not read. Her verdict on Wolfe is therefore unknown and must not be guessed.

Patrick Wolfe's definition is the hinge of the studio's claim [added]. He holds that for settler colonizers "invasion is a structure not an event", driven by a "logic of elimination". He counts assimilation among the forms that logic takes, alongside removal ([Wolfe 2006](https://doi.org/10.1080/14623520601056240)). On that definition, settlement aimed at assimilation is not outside settler colonialism; it is one of its modes.

### The counter-argument: assimilation with distrust, and settlement as a catalyst

Dalle Mulle and Ambrosino argue that "Italian fascists consistently affirmed the inevitable assimilation, and therefore inclusion, of minorities within the Italian nation, but they also deeply distrusted them". On their account, land colonization "was never meant as a total replacement for the assimilation of the local population, but rather as a catalyst to accelerate this". They criticise works that "over-emphasize the Italian government's determination to move the entire German-speaking population", and they note that "resource constraints consistently frustrated the regime's ambitions" [V, checked] ([Historical Journal 66, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0018246X23000158)). Their numbers bite. By 30 September 1938 the land agency had moved **325 farms and 8,933 ha**, "only 0.6 per cent of the productive agricultural surface of the new provinces" (same source, p. 898). Even Pergher, as they paraphrase her, concedes that colonization before 1939 was "piecemeal at best".

Di Michele tests the colonial-subject analogy and largely rejects it. "Severe discriminatory measures … regarding citizenship rights were not in fact taken": the 1926 decree-law on revoking the citizenship of allogeni was limited in scope and never applied. South Tyrolean peasants were represented as "a condensate of virtues that largely coincided with those that the regime promised to instil in all Italians", unlike the contempt shown to Slovenes and Croats [V, checked] ([JMIS 28:1, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/1354571X.2022.2122369)).

The steelman is strong. The Semirurali tenants held no Tyrolean land title. The masi largely stayed with their owners, and the locals kept their citizenship. The demographic shift that took Bolzano's Italian speakers from **21% in 1921 to 62% in 1939** came through factories and rented flats, not through dispossessed farms [S] ([Dalle Mulle and Ambrosino, p. 899](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0018246X23000158)).

### What survives the clash

The counter-argument attacks "replacement" and "colonial subject". It does not attack "colonization". Di Michele's own project describes "le politiche italiane di **colonizzazione** dell'Alto Adige" and asks about the "aree geografiche di provenienza dei coloni" [V] ([Di Michele, unibz](https://www.unibz.it/assets/Documents/Competence-Centres/unibz-storiaregionale-DiMichele-Conquista-del-suolo.pdf)). Dalle Mulle and Ambrosino's "catalyst" is, in Wolfe's terms, a description of how elimination by assimilation was meant to work. Pergher's phrase, as they quote it, is "a kind of beguiling encirclement that would surround the allogeni and eventually absorb them" [S] (Dalle Mulle and Ambrosino, p. 900). Surnames were "restituiti". The Tyrolean farmhouse was to give way to an Italian family house through the 1934 competition "La casa per l'Alto Adige", judged by Piacentini and Prefect Mastromattei [S] ([GR/SR 17, 2008](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)). Both measures work on the collective, not on persons.

The "structure, not event" test also finds evidence after the regime fell. As the ENTV, the land agency blocked sales of optants' property to allogeni until 1954 [S] ([Taccetti 2024](https://aro-isig.fbk.eu/issues/2024/2/terra-italiana-giulio-taccetti/pdf/)). In 1949 it still leased 52 farm units in the Adige valley to tenants "provenienti da diverse zone" on a promise of ownership [V] ([Senate bill 328/1949](https://www.parlamento.it/service/PDF/PDFServer/DF/353447.pdf)). In 1957 the South Tyrolean People's Party's central committee accused Rome of changing "il carattere popolare del nostro paese" and wrote that "Come mezzo principale … l'Italia si serve dell'edilizia popolare" [S] ([Azzolini 1985, pp. 8, 32](https://oappcbz.learnhub24.com/bprofile/public_download/6KRGH_L-NCZFpy6zueXNybCj0rYuoWThPdqpfW0oRVE/?mode=inline)). The dense rebuilding of the Semirurali from 1959 then reused Garbini's land for the same demographic end, with the opposite typology [INF].

The limits are equally clear. Fascist settlement in South Tyrol was a structure with an assimilatory aim and thin means. Replacement was openly planned only in 1939–43, through the Option, the Commissariato's circular and the ENTV. Even then about two-thirds of optants never left [S] ([Dalle Mulle and Ambrosino, pp. 907–908](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0018246X23000158)).

### What Asmara adds to the claim

Asmara strengthens one claim and limits another. The strengthened claim is that the Italian majorities of Bolzano and Asmara were both made in 1935–40 by the regime's colonization office, through a procedure that, in Ertola's words, "sottostava alle stesse regole previste per la colonizzazione interna" [S] ([Ertola, pp. 34–35](https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c378-f054-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/tesi%20dottorato.pdf)). The same men framed both cities. Razza's name was given to an Asmara workers' village. Cobolli Gigli built the imperial roads and opened Bolzano's bridge to the case popolari. Piacentini planned Bolzano and, per one source, set the terms of the Asmara competition. That makes the settler-colonial reading of Bolzano a comparison with documented colonial practice, not a metaphor [INF].

The claim Asmara limits is "dual city". Read against Asmara, Bolzano was a city divided by plan, class and language among citizens, not a city zoned by race. The studio should use Asmara to calibrate the word rather than to borrow it.

Asmara also offers a heritage model the studio can test in Bolzano. UNESCO's listing puts the native quarters inside the protected property, and its statement of value names "racial segregation principles" as part of what the city shows. In Bolzano the old town and the Fascist new town are protected under separate narratives, and the Semirurali were largely demolished [INF].

For the translocalities theme, Asmara adds three more crossings: men from Brentonico building Asmara, an exhibition of the empire's products staged in Bolzano, and a Trentino-born architect, Ferrazza, who planned Asmara and later advised the public-works office for Trentino-Alto Adige.

For the theme "translocalities in Bolzano", the toolkit question gives the studio its best material [INF]. The Semirurali are an object assembled from elsewhere: a national worker-housing type tested in Turin, Milan and Rome; a square named after the Agro Pontino; streets named for Palermo, Cagliari and Bari; the stair of Mussolini's birthplace at Predappio; an engineer from Venice; tenants from Padova, Verona and Rovigo; and a migration office in Rome.

The same toolkit ran the other way. South Tyroleans went to Rhodes as "Italian" woodcutters and were pulled back by the Option. Battisti and Filzi named villages in Cyrenaica and streets in Sinich. Via Bari 11, the surviving house, shares a city name with "Bari d'Etiopia".

The settler-colonial frame explains why these flows were organised. The translocal frame records what the people in them made of it. A family of returning optants called their 1949 flat in via Bari "eine große Errungenschaft". A resident remembered: "Siamo diventati tutti bolzanini" [S] ([booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf)). The two frames complement each other, and the studio needs both.

## Conclusion

The question "same toolkit?" sorts Fascist settlement into two branches that met in Bolzano. The agricultural branch (ONC, land agency, poderi, martyrs' names, colonial architects) reached South Tyrol only at Sinich, in a dozen Meranese masi and in 0.6% of the farmland: Tolomei's "goccia d'acqua in vasta arena". The urban-industrial branch did the demographic work without any colonial drawing. What joined the two was not architecture but paperwork: who could transfer, register as a resident, be hired or replace an optant. The colonization office governed those steps, by statute and from its own Bolzano desk. So the fingerprints of colonization are on the labour and residence files, the grant decrees and the Commissariato's circulars, not on Garbini's sheets. That shifts where the studio should look and what it should draw.

For a settler-colonial reading the consequence is a sharper claim, not a weaker one. Read through Wolfe, assimilation with distrust is not an alternative to settler colonialism but one of its modes. The defensible thesis is that Fascism ran a settler-colonial structure in South Tyrol, aimed at absorbing the local collective and run by the office that peopled the Agro Pontino and Libya. Its instruments, the land agency and public housing used as demographic policy, outlived the regime. The Semirurali were its most visible housing and its least colonial design. Asmara marks the limit of the claim. There the same office fed a city zoned by race; in Bolzano it fed a city divided by plan, class and language among citizens.
