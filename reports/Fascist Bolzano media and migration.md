# Fascism Showed Italians Two Different Bolzanos

Under Fascism, Italians outside South Tyrol came to know two different places, and the media split the work between them. Tourist posters commissioned by ENIT, the State Railways and the Bolzano-based Unione Turistica Alberghiera, most of them drawn by the Merano designer Franz Lenhart, sold a timeless "Alto Adige" of Dolomites, spa promenades and grape cures. They came in Italian, German, English and French editions, and none of those the researchers found shows the Victory Monument, Drusus or the new fascist city. That Italian Bolzano reached the nation through other channels. Luce made at least **163 newsreel stories (1928–1943)**. An **EIAR radio station went on air on 12 July 1928**, the day the monument was inaugurated. *La Stampa* ran headlines from "Tutta l'Italia oggi in Bolzano italiana" to "LA GRANDE BOLZANO", and postcards showed the "Ponte nuovo Druso". Above all it travelled through people: **24,056 Italians moved into the comune in 1937–1940**, mostly from Veneto and Trentino. They came for factory jobs whose state grants rewarded hiring "manodopera regnicola e non allogena", that is, workers from the old provinces rather than local German speakers. In the same years the 1939 Option sent about 75,000 South Tyroleans north. Film was almost silent about it, and the Italian press framed it as the "rimpatrio" of "allogeni". These flows can be traced only by working backwards from Bolzano, because Antenati has nothing for the city and only Napoleonic-era registers for the Polesine. FamilySearch fills the Veneto gap, and OptionDigital and the Lager lists are the name-searchable records of 1940s Bolzano. Ponte Druso lost its four Roman eagles in the 1970s, not in 1945. The "cinema on Corso Italia" is most likely the Concordia, demolished in 2018, while Armando Ronca's Cinema Corso (demolished 1988) is the larger architectural loss. For the studio, the key finding is that today's tourist copy repeats the posters' silence about the fascist city, in a city that passed one million overnight stays in 2025. No published work yet applies "translocality" to Bolzano.

## Posters sold Dolomites and grapes, and none showed the fascist city

The largest body of interwar tourist promotion that can be checked online is the collection of the Touriseum (Merano) and the Palais Mamming museum. It is published through the provincial portal *Objekte Museen Archive Südtirol* (OMA), where a search for Franz Lenhart alone returns **462 records, 105 of them posters** ([OMA portal](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/search/simple)). The researchers documented about **50 posters from 1919–1943**, with printer, commissioner and language. They also exported **414 dated records** to `files/01_posters_graphics/oma_records_1919-1943.csv`: 77 posters, 195 postcards, 97 brochures and leaflets, plus maps and programmes.

The corpus shows who commissioned the images, and how that changed. Early posters came from local printers and spa boards and still used Habsburg spa language. Examples are *Meran, Südtirol / Italien, Größter klimatischer Kurort der Südalpen* (Pötzelberger, 1919–23) ([OMA 140000352](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000352)) and J. Essendorfer's Italian linocut *Visitate Bolzano-Gries centro delle Dolomiti e dei ghiacciai* (Vogelweider, 1921) ([OMA 140001915](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140001915)). From about 1930 the large posters carry the **ENIT and State Railways (FS) logos under the Savoy crown**, ministerial approval numbers and fascist-era year numbers. They were printed by national lithographers in Milan, Genoa, Florence, Treviso and Naples [INF].

Lenhart's *Bolzano-Gries Dolomites* (1930) shows a couple in an open car driving towards the city, and names **ENIT as "Auftraggeber"**, the commissioning client ([OMA 140000481](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000481)). His 1938–39 *Merano* carries UTA, ENIT and FS marks together with the line "Esente da Bollo – Provv. N. 254 del 30/12/38 – XVII" ([OMA 140000485](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000485)). The researchers single out the Bolzano-based **Unione Turistica Alberghiera (UTA)**, alongside ENIT, as the overlooked regional publisher of 1936–40 [INF].

Language editions show who the audience was. The same Lenhart image appeared in Italian as *L'estate nelle Dolomiti* (ENIT and FS, 1937–38) and in French as *L'été aux Dolomites*. Both are known only from Collezione Salce index entries [IDX] ([Salce 0500662577](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500662577), [Salce 0500658987](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0500658987)). The German edition, *Sommer in den Dolomiten* (1938), names **U.T.A. Bolzano** as publisher ([OMA 140002078](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140002078)). The researchers infer that Italians in the Kingdom saw the Italian edition, while the German one targeted the Reich, Austria and Switzerland [INF].

The objects support this reading. UTA's brochure *Wintersport in den Dolomiten* (1937–38) carries a **Leipzig travel-agency stamp** ([OMA 140006230](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140006230)). Rudolf Parsch's ENIT/UTA tourist map (1938-XVI) carries a stamp from a Dresden agency ([OMA 140006398](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140006398)). Merano's spa board printed a leaflet **in Polish** in 1934 ([OMA 80025594](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80025594)), and another advertised the crossing from New York on the liners *Saturnia* and *Vulcania* ([OMA 80023859](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80023859)). German-language copy promised rail discounts, as in *Herbst in Merano – 50–70% Fahrpreis Ermässigung auf den ital. Staatsbahnen* (Lenhart, 1934) ([OMA 140002076](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140002076)). Even the state's notice *Disposizioni per il soggiorno degli stranieri* (1940), which hotels had to display, was printed in **German, Italian, English and French** ([OMA 140000252](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000252)).

What the posters sold was landscape and leisure. The Rosengarten/Catinaccio is the recurring backdrop of Bolzano posters. Merano offers its Kurhaus, palms, blossom and grapes. The people are modern holidaymakers: golfers, motorists, skiers, bathers and jockeys.

Traditional dress (Tracht) appears too, but as folklore, and usually on women. One example is the blonde woman in a Dirndl on the 1935 Barabino & Graeve *Merano*, stamped "Autorizzazione Direzione Generale per il Turismo No. 136 del 25 Febbraio 1935 – XIII" ([OMA 80036119](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80036119)). The researchers read such figures as a decorative Alpine "type", never as a sign of a German-speaking national community [INF].

The regime appears through signs rather than scenes. Beyond the crowned logos, the year numbers and the approval formulas, there are two telling cases. Lenhart's *Visitate le Dolomiti*, made for the CAI Sezione Alto Adige and the Bolzano Azienda di cura, flies an **Italian flag over the mountain hut** ([OMA 140000486](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000486)). The ENIT road map *In Italien auf den römischen Konsularstrassen – Im Automobil von Passo di Resia – Bolzano und von Brennero – Bolzano nach Rom* (De Agostini, 1937) presents the Brenner route as a Roman road ([OMA 140009622](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140009622)).

Across OMA, Commons and Europeana, **no tourist poster shows the Victory Monument, Drusus or the new fascist city**. In a corpus this size that absence is meaningful, though it does not prove no such poster existed. Even the slogan differs from later habit: the period formula is "centro delle Dolomiti" (1921, 1938), not "porta delle Dolomiti" [INF].

Two slogans read sharply today. UTA's *L'estate nelle Dolomiti – 60 stazioni con 40.000 letti* (c. 1936–37) sold bed capacity as an attraction ([OMA 140018192](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140018192)). *Kennst du das Land…* (c. 1930) took Goethe's Mignon and routed it through Merano's grapes to Gardone's lemons and Venice, selling the province as Italy's threshold ([OMA 140005642](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140005642)) [INF].

The new city travelled on postcards instead. Lorenz Fränzl's studio issued real-photo cards with the imprint "Edit. e fotogr. L. Fränzl, Bolzano" ([OMA 140021402](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140021402)), among them *Bolzano – Monumento alla Vittoria* (series 700-64, 1930s) ([OMA 140021403](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140021403)). The Schlern-Verlag postcard holding of the provincial Office for Film and Media (AFM) adds more. It holds *Bolzano: Ponte nuovo Druso verso le Dolomiti* ([OMA 120072449](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072449)) and a card of the bridge's eagle statue ([OMA 120072451](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072451)). There are also cards titled *Stazione nuova*, *Zona Industriale* and *Sportanlage Drususstadion*, and one of the medieval Gscheibter Turm renamed *Torre Druso* ([OMA 120072443](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120072443)).

Cards also record Italianisation physically. On a 1933 *Oltrisarco* card the German name is blacked out and overprinted ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Postcard_-_bolzano_-_oltrisarco_-_1933_(53850205879).jpg)). On a 1920s card of the Karerseehotel, the German caption was stamped over with "Alto Adige, Italia" ([OMA 140004432](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140004432)).

The most telling item is photographic. Enrico Pedrotti's aerial views of the new *Rione Littorio*, and of the *Semirurali* among orchards, are filed in the AFM collection "009-Südtirol Tourismuswerbung (Foto)", which is a tourism-advertising holding ([OMA 120016156](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120016156), [OMA 120016154](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/120016154)). This suggests the regime's new quarters were at least considered promotional material [INF].

Two hinge figures emerge from the corpus [INF]. One is Fränzl, a German-born photographer who produced the regime's Italian visual toponymy. The other is **Franz Lenhart** (born Bad Häring, Tyrol, 1898; died Meran, 1992). Austrian-trained and later an Italian citizen, he drew the province in four languages for ENIT while also advertising "Meraner Trachten" for German-speaking shopkeepers ([OMA 140000481](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/140000481); [inverso.pt](https://inverso.pt/labels/artists/Lenhard/lenhard.htm)).

Inside the province, the regime's posters spoke with a civic voice rather than a tourist one. A bilingual municipal notice of 19 May 1925, *Municipio di Bolzano – Cittadini! / Bürger!*, signed by Prefectural Commissioner Steffanini, ordered every house on the route of visiting Italian war invalids to fly the tricolour. It called Bolzano "die Königin des Hochetsch" ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Innsbruck-Museum_Zeughaus-Poster_Bozen_1925-01ESD.jpg)).

Mass leisure also travelled in the other direction. A Palais Mamming photograph shows a **Dopolavoro excursion from Modena**, its cars parked on Merano's Freiheitsstraße on 16 August 1930 ([OMA 80012664](https://objekte-museen-archive.provinz.bz.it/Details/bz/80012664)).

No dedicated study of fascist-era tourist posters for Alto Adige exists. The closest is Catrina Klee's Lucerne PhD project, whose hypothesis is that 1920s–40s tourism projects "acted as accelerators of the Italianization" ([Klee abstract](https://www.unilu.ch/fileadmin/fakultaeten/ksf/institute/gsl/doktorierende/Abstract_PhD_Catrina_Klee.pdf)).

## Newsreels, radio and the press staged Bolzano as a national rite

### Luce filed at least 163 stories, but none on the Option

The researchers ran 47 search terms through the Istituto Luce catalogue. They found **at least 163 newsreel stories (1928–1943) and about 45 period documentaries** on South Tyrol and the Brenner; 152 of the newsreels have YouTube links ([Luce catalogue](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/search/result.html)). Coverage is thin until 1931 and peaks in **1935 (25 stories) and 1938 (26)**. Those peaks match the regime's two pushes on the region [I]. The first was Mussolini's August 1935 visit, during the manoeuvres of the Ethiopian mobilisation. The second was 1938: the Anschluss, Hitler's journey and the princes' tour of the new industrial zone.

The founding image is *A Bolzano inaugurazione del monumento alla Vittoria d'Italia* (Giornale Luce A0137, IL5000048984, July 1928, 4'43"). Priests bless the monument, Giuriati speaks before the king, Balbo stands beside him, a "banda musicale altoatesina" plays, and Balilla march past ([Luce](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048984/2/a-bolzano-inaugurazione-del-monumento-alla-vittoria-d-italia.html)). The same reel carried *Visita altoatesina a Roma*, in which South Tyroleans in costume climb the Campidoglio and meet Mussolini ([Luce](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000048647/2/visita-altoatesina-roma.html)). That reel began a series of South Tyrolean youth brought to Rome, culminating in the thousand avanguardisti of the "Legione Marco Druso" whom the Duce reviewed in March 1935 ([Luce](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000015232/2/duce-passa-rivista-i-mille-avanguardisti-della-legione-marco-druso-bolzano-ospiti-roma.html)).

The researchers sort the coverage into four pictures [I]. The first is **a staged Italian Bolzano**. In 1935 Mussolini honours "i busti marmorei dedicati a Battisti, Filzi e Chiesa" inside the monument ([Luce B0741](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020037/2/la-visita-mussolini-alla-citta-bolzano.html)). In *Il ministro Benni e il conte Volpi inaugurano il nuovo centro industriale di Bolzano* (23/12/1936), officials enter "uno stabilimento 'Lancia e C.'" ([Luce B1014](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000027681/2/il-ministro-benni-e-conte-volpi-inaugurano-nuovo-centro-industriale-bolzano.html)). *"Una superba realizzazione del Regime per il benessere dei lavoratori nella provincia del Brennero…"* (11/10/1939) presents "il nuovo Rione Popolare Dux, case a due piani con un piccolo terreno" ([Luce B1599](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022261/2/superba-realizzazione-del-regime-benessere-lavoratori-nella-provincia-del-brennero-inaugurata-dal-ministro-segretario-del-partito.html)).

The second is **a cosmopolitan, folkloric Merano and Dolomites** of horse races tied to the national lottery, flower parades, costumes and ski championships. Even the 1932 *Bolzano. La festa dell'uva* belongs here ([Luce B0147](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000008599/2/-2011.html)).

The third is **the Brenner, first as sacred border and then as Axis gateway**. In 1935 "il Duce arriva sul cippo del confine" ([Luce B0742](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000020066/2/mussolini-visita-confine-italo-austriaco-presso-passo-del-brennero.html)). In 1938 Hitler's train is greeted at "la stazione di Bolzano – una grande folla accoglie acclamante il passaggio del treno" ([Luce documentary](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL3000084593/1/dal-brennero-roma-arrivo-alla-stazione-ostiense-prima-giornata-romana-piazza-siena-manovra-guerra-aerea-furbara-esercitazione.html)). The same year brings *Il passaggio dei treni rurali con migliaia di agricoltori italiani diretti in Germania* ([Luce B1275](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000033074/2/il-passaggio-treni-rurali-migliaia-agricoltori-italiani-diretti-germania.html)), and the Brenner meeting of 18 March 1940 follows ([Luce B1692](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000024850/2/l-incontro-duce-e-fuehrer.html)).

The fourth is the flow of youth groups to Rome described above. German speakers appear almost only as costume. The one exception is a 1938 rally of former Austro-Hungarian veterans, framed as "riconoscenza" towards the regime [I].

What Luce left out is just as instructive. Keyword searches found **no Luce item on the 1939 Option**, although Luce did cover *Il rimpatrio dei tedeschi dal Baltico* (13/12/1939) ([Luce B1635](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000022891/2/il-rimpatrio-tedeschi-dal-baltico-croce-rossa-tedesca-e-organizzazioni-assistenziali-naziste-hanno-preso-cura-questi-rimpatriati.html)). A keyword search cannot prove absence [I]. Nothing turned up on Ponte Druso or the Stadio Druso before 1945. The closest item is *Il ponte del Littorio* (1939), a different bridge that "unisce la zona industriale con il villaggio delle case popolari e la via Roma" ([Luce B1540](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000021490/2/il-ponte-del-littorio.html)). Nothing turned up either on the Semirurali by that name, the Fiera di Bolzano, the Vallo Alpino or a Giro d'Italia stage.

The city's name, meanwhile, travelled without the place. The heavy cruiser *Bolzano*, launched at Sestri Ponente on 31 August 1932, appeared in cinemas nationwide ([Luce A1003](https://patrimonio.archivioluce.com/luce-web/detail/IL5000034894/2/il-varo-della-nave-bolzano-31-agosto-1932.html)) [I].

In fiction film, the South Tyrolean face on Italian screens was Luis Trenker. His *Condottieri* (1937) received 9.6 million lire of Italian state funding [S] ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condottieri_(film))). German Wikipedia says he opted for the Reich in March 1940 "nach langem Zögern". Yet Goebbels wrote on 5 March 1940: "Dieses Schweinestück hat in Südtirol nicht für uns optiert" [S] ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Trenker)). The two sources contradict each other, and the notes leave the contradiction open.

### A radio station built for the monument's inauguration

EIAR's Bolzano station went on air on **12 July 1928, the day the Victory Monument was inaugurated**, and its first programme was the ceremony itself. According to the EIAR weekly, the station was built "aderendo prontamente a un desiderio espresso da S. E. il Ministro delle Comunicazioni" (Costanzo Ciano). Work began on 10 June, and the transmission "ha costituito … il battesimo di una nuova stazione dell'E.I.A.R., installata di sana pianta in poco più di un mese" ([Radiorario 1928 n. 30](https://worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/20s/1928/RC-1928-30.pdf)). The station stood in **Gries** and ran at **200 W** on about **400 m**, using a Standard Elettrica Italiana transmitter. It fed "tre potenti altoparlanti" in piazza Walter and had telephone lines to the Casa del Fascio and the Teatro Civico. The same issue describes the special trains and overnight cars that brought "frotte di camicie nere da tutta l'Italia" to the ceremony.

EIAR's report on 1928 says the station "non era prevista nel nostro capitolato d'oneri" and was "sentita bene in tutto il Trentino" ([Radiocorriere 1929 n. 15](https://worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/20s/1929/RC-1929-15.pdf)). It was rated at 1 kW by 1932 ([Radiocorriere 1932 n. 42](https://worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/30s/1932/RC-1932-42.pdf)), and by 1939 it relayed the national programme ([Radiocorriere 1939 n. 10](https://worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Radiocorriere/30s/1939/RC-1939-10.pdf)). German-language broadcasting began only under RAI in 1945 [S] ([de.wikipedia, Rai Südtirol](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_S%C3%BCdtirol)).

The researchers judge that a small, Italian-only transmitter sharing its wavelength with foreign stations could not compete with Vienna's 15 kW for German-speaking listeners. Its real job was to serve the incoming Italian population and to signal the state's presence. Radio therefore carried the nation into Bolzano far more than it carried Bolzano to the nation [I].

Turin was Lancia's home city. On 17 February 1937 the paper reported "QUARANTAMILA PERSONE seguono la salma di Vincenzo Lancia" ([La Stampa](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,7/articleid,1132_01_1937_0041_0007_24920881/)). The Bolzano plant was being narrated to the city whose skilled workers staffed it, a direct translocal axis [I].

The Touring Club Italiano's *Le Vie d'Italia* is almost complete for 1919–43 on the Internet Archive, and over those years it moves from tourism economics to regime works [I]. In March 1922 it printed Bolzano's visitor statistics: **77,143 visitors in 1921, against 123,191 in 1913** ([Vie d'Italia, Mar 1922](https://archive.org/details/2023-02-27-02-08?q=Bolzano)). In February 1937 it ran Emilio Emmer's "Opere del Regime – Bolzano e la nuova Zona Industriale" with photographs by the Giacomelli studio of Venice ([Vie d'Italia, Feb 1937](https://archive.org/details/2022-12-06-02-43?q=%22zona+industriale%22)). Those photographs belong to the 1936 series the TCI now publishes on Digitouring, including "Veduta della zona industriale di Bolzano da Castel Flavone" ([Digitouring](https://www.digitouring.it/it/cerca/33298-veduta-della-zona-industriale-di-bolzano-da-castel-flavone)). The researchers read the article and the prints as one visual campaign [I].

The regime's glossy *Rivista Illustrata del Popolo d'Italia* is on the Internet Archive for 1923–43. It moved from the monument to aluminium and was blunt about the population it meant to displace: "Saltiamo a Bolzano … questi tedeschi che popolano abusivamente il territorio italiano" (1932) ([Rivista Illustrata 1932](https://archive.org/details/larivista-1932-vol2?q=Bolzano)). By 1937 it wrote: "Per chi ha conosciuto Bolzano e l'Alto Adige nell'immediato dopoguerra e li rivede oggi, parlare di prodigi non è …" ([Rivista Illustrata 1937](https://archive.org/details/larivista-1937-vol1?q=Bolzano)).

The other national papers are harder to reach. The Corriere della Sera archive is metered and paywalled ([Corriere archive](https://archivio.corriere.it/Archivio/interface/landing.html)). No free run of *Il Popolo d'Italia* was found, nor of *L'Illustrazione Italiana* after 1923.

Inside the province, the fascist Italian daily *La Provincia di Bolzano* (LPB) and the regime's German-language *Alpenzeitung* (AZ) ran the same stories for different readers. On 21 February 1935 the LPB headline read "LE DIRETTIVE DEL CAPO PER L'IMMEDIATA REALIZZAZIONE DELLA ZONA INDUSTRIALE – Il Duce per la più grande Bolzano" ([LPB](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001-1935-1935_02_21_Nr_45.html)). The AZ rendered it word for word as "Mussolini für die Schaffung von Groß-Bolzano" ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046469)). La Stampa wrote "Una zona industriale a Bolzano sorgerà per volontà del Duce" ([La Stampa](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,2/articleid,1137_01_1935_0045_0002_24911363/)).

A German Wikipedia mirror describes the AZ as consisting largely of translated LPB articles, with about 7,000 copies in 1937 [U]. That makes the AZ a full-text-searchable stand-in for the LPB, which has no OCR.

The AZ also covered migration as a story in its own right. In 1935 it explained population growth by the Army Corps headquarters and "aus der Schaffung der Industriezone" ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046326)). In 1936 it counted "die viertausend Arbeiter, die gegenwärtig in unserer Stadt beschäftigt sind" ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046931)). In 1941 it described the Rione Dux and Rione Littorio as quarters "aus dem Nichts entstanden" ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059044975)), and in July of that year it reported the opening of the **Kino Littorio in the Rione Dux** ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059044700)).

The AZ also fills a gap left by the newsreel search, which found no *treno popolare* (cheap excursion train) to Bolzano. The AZ reported a "Volkszug Torino-Bolzano" on 1 September 1931 ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046780)). It also announced "mehrere Volkszüge aus den alten Provinzen nach Bolzano" for that year's grape festival ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046705)). The national scheme carried 8,774,451 passengers between 1931 and 1939 [S] ([it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treni_popolari)).

## Workers, soldiers and optants carried Bolzano further than any screen

### Industrial policy worked as population policy

The legal basis was the **R.D.L. 28 settembre 1934 n. 1621**. It gave plants founded in Bolzano by 30 June 1944 customs exemptions, ten years' exemption from the *ricchezza mobile* tax on business income, and up to five years of operating grants [SECONDARY] ([normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto.legge:1934-09-28;1621)).

Carlo Romeo shows how the grants were tied to workers' origins. They were calculated partly on the number of employees from outside the province: "manodopera regnicola e non allogena", in the awarding commission's 1936 phrase. In Romeo's words, "I tecnici e gli operai specializzati provenivano in maggioranza dalle case madri lombarde e piemontesi, mentre la manovalanza soprattutto dal Veneto e dal Trentino" ([Romeo 2022](https://www.novecento.org/sul-confine-lalto-adige-sudtirol-nella-storia-del-novecento/il-fascismo-al-brennero-un-breve-profilo-per-questioni-7361/)).

From 1936–37 a Bolzano office of the Commissariato per le migrazioni e la colonizzazione interna policed these transfers "in armonia con la Federazione dei Fasci di Combattimento". Once the plants opened, "si fece massiccio l'ingresso di famiglie provenienti soprattutto dalle province di Padova, Verona e Rovigo" ([Gallo 2017](https://www.asei.eu/it/2017/05/operazioni-migratorie-belliche-transfrontaliere-e-rimpatri-da-una-parte-allaltra-delle-alpi/)). By tying the subsidy to the ethnic make-up of the workforce, the grant formula turned industrial policy into population policy [INFERENCE].

### The Semirurali gave the newcomers a "piccola città giardino"

Most newcomers were housed by the **IFACP della Provincia di Bolzano**, the provincial public-housing institute, founded in 1934 ([Giacomozzi 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf)). Its Rione Dux, known as the Semirurali, began in spring 1938. The first lot, between via Palermo, via Milano and via Cagliari, had **92 buildings with 342 dwellings** and was inaugurated on 8 October 1939. Another 228 houses with 808 flats followed in summer 1940, for a final total of **327 houses, 1,151 dwellings and about 5,500 inhabitants** ([Comune booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf)). Other counts run from 1,094 to 1,147 dwellings, and the start is dated 1937 or 1938. The houses followed "casa minima" standards, each with a vegetable plot "al fine di integrare gli svalutati salari … secondo l'ideologia ruralista del fascismo". Propaganda compared them to "la piccola casa di Predappio, dove il Duce ebbe i suoi natali". Residents of the piazza Matteotti blocks called the quarter "Shanghai" (same source).

The rest of Italy saw the quarter through the media described above. La Stampa reported "l'inaugurazione di un rione di 300 casette rurali" on 9 October 1939 ([La Stampa](http://www.archiviolastampa.it/component/option,com_lastampa/task,search/mod,libera/Itemid,3,/action,viewer/page,1/articleid,0030_01_1939_0239A_0001_16426902/)). LPB's front page showed "l'ingresso al rione Dux dopo il rito inaugurale (Foto Walsa)" ([LPB](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001-1939-1939_10_10_Nr_240.html)).

Residents recorded for the Casa Semirurale museum describe the move from inside ([Comune booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf)). One remembers "Mio padre è venuto in bicicletta dal Cadore … Ha trovato lavoro alla Lancia e in poco tempo ha ricevuto anche l'appartamento". Another concludes "Siamo diventati tutti bolzanini … il nostro dialetto non era un dialetto veneto o un dialetto calabrese ma un dialetto bolzanino".

### Soldiers and ritual crowds

Military service brought ordinary families into contact with the place. Bolzano was headquarters of the 11th Infantry Division "Brennero" [S] ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Infantry_Division_%22Brennero%22)), and Merano of the Alpine division "Tridentina". The Vallo Alpino fortification line was built at "breakneck speed" in 1939–40, by Italian contractors only: more than 1,000 bunkers were planned and 306 completed [S] ([south-tirol.com](https://www.south-tirol.com/blog/south-tyrols-bunker-landscape)). A single local source says the contractors' workforces came mainly from southern Italy [S].

One item shows how this channel worked. On 22 April 1940 a soldier at the "Deposito Munizioni – Stilves – Campo di Trens (Bolzano)", dating his cards "Stilves 22-4-40-XVIII", wrote two postcards to Signora Ricci Maddalena and Signor Ricci Ferdinando at S. Ippolito di Vernio (Firenze). One of them is a Fränzl card of Maria Trens ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/006_20240119_20240119_1643); [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/110_20231218)). The researchers judge that garrison, frontier guard and Vallo labour were probably the largest non-media channel through which families came to "know" Bolzano, and that this channel peaked during the Option years [I].

### The Option ran the other way and was framed as "rimpatrio"

The Option had no formal treaty behind it. It grew out of a Berlin conference on **23 June 1939**; the implementing *Richtlinien* (guidelines) are dated **21 October 1939**, and declarations were due by **31 December 1939** ([Tiroler Landesarchiv, glossary "O"](https://www.tirol.gv.at/kunst-kultur/landesarchiv/archivglossar/archivglossar-o/)). LPB's front page of 11 January 1940 gave the official count: "166.488 hanno optato per la cittadinanza germanica … 27.712 … cittadinanza italiana e 35.300, non avendo sottoscritto alcuna [dichiarazione], restano … cittadini italiani" ([LPB](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001-1940-1940_01_11_Nr_9.html)).

The headline numbers depend on what is counted. The official result was 69.4% for Germany, out of about 267,000 eligible people. The familiar **86%** is a modern recalculation for today's provincial territory, where about 246,000 were eligible ([TLA glossary "O"](https://www.tirol.gv.at/kunst-kultur/landesarchiv/archivglossar/archivglossar-o/)).

About 75,000 people left by 1943 [SECONDARY] ([ReMIGRA](https://www.uibk.ac.at/de/zeitgeschichte/regionalgeschichte/remigra/projekt/)). The Tiroler Landesarchiv counts 81,500 including men drafted into German service, which leaves 60,000–70,000 civilians ([TLA glossary "U"](https://www.tirol.gv.at/kunst-kultur/landesarchiv/archivglossar/archivglossar-u/)). Legal re-options in 1949–55 numbered about 9,000, while estimates of all returns run to 15,000–25,000 (same source; [Heiss 1990](https://journals.univie.ac.at/index.php/oezg/article/download/5441/5369/11321)). No figure was found for Bolzano city alone.

The results headline is identical in LPB and La Stampa, so a single Stefani agency text was syndicated to both [I].

The framing split by language. LPB put the implementing rules, signed by Prefect Mastromattei and the German consul-general Otto Bene, on its front page. It presented them as rules for "il rimpatrio dei cittadini germanici e … l'emigrazione di allogeni tedeschi dall'Alto Adige in Germania" ([LPB](https://webaleph.bpi.claudiaugusta.it/La_Provincia_di_Bolzano_001-1939-1939_10_26_Nr_254.html)). The German-language papers used the legal vocabulary of "Option der Staatsbürgerschaft" ([AZ](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043498)). Dolomiten printed the prefect's warning that "nach zwanzig Jahren hat niemand mehr das Recht, am Fenster zu stehen" ([DOL 30 Oct 1939](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000115205581)). The AZ ran a recurring box, "Die Option der Atesiner", promising that anyone who chose Italian citizenship "und dadurch beweist, daß er sich als Italiener fühlt … wird in seiner Heimat bleiben können" ([AZ 23 Nov 1939](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059043441)).

The **Ente nazionale per le Tre Venezie (ENTV)** was created from the older ERA by L. 27 novembre 1939 n. 1780, with the task of taking over optants' property and reselling it ([Di Michele, unibz](https://www.unibz.it/assets/Documents/Competence-Centres/unibz-storiaregionale-DiMichele-Conquista-del-suolo.pdf)). In 1949 it still administered 52 farm units of 12–20 ha in the Adige valley, leased to tenants "provenienti da diverse zone" ([Senato DDL 328/1949](https://www.parlamento.it/service/PDF/PDFServer/DF/353447.pdf)).

The two flows mirror each other: Italians arrived at roughly 4,000–7,700 a year while about 75,000 South Tyroleans left [INFERENCE]. One quarter holds both. A Casa Semirurale testimony reads: "1949, im Oktober, sind wir von Graz als Rückoptanten nach Bozen gekommen. Es war eine große Errungenschaft die Wohnung in der via Bari" ([Comune booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf)). The 1944–45 Nazi transit camp (the Lager) stood next door. That makes the Semirurali the one site where state-organised worker migration, the Option and its reversal, and deportation all meet [INFERENCE].

For a Polesine family that reached the Semirurali in 1938, the researchers' tested route starts in Bolzano. First come the Comune's historical certificates of residence and household composition, which should give the registration date and the comune of origin [INFERENCE]. Next come civil-status extracts for any births, marriages or deaths in Bolzano. The Semirurali streets were vie Palermo, Milano, Cagliari, Brescia, Genova, Vercelli, Belluno, Mantova, Alessandria and Udine ([Giacomozzi](https://web.archive.org/web/20071008090923id_/http://www.comune.bolzano.it/UploadDocs/2746_Giacomozzi2_ita.pdf)), so the family's street places them in the quarter. Then come the Bolzano-side name lists: the police register, the Lager lists, the 3 May 1945 record, the IMI/CAR lists and the war-dead database.

Only then does the trace cross to the Polesine. FamilySearch's Rovigo Tribunale images cover 1871–1937, and the comune of birth can fill the missing years. Parish registers and the Diocese of Adria-Rovigo cover 1816–1871, and Antenati's Napoleonic registers cover 1806–1815 ([AS Rovigo guide](https://archiviodistatorovigo.cultura.gov.it/fileadmin/risorse/Moduli_e_risorse/Risorse/00_NoteRicercaGenealogica.pdf)).

Military records split by generation. Fathers called up in the Polesine are in Rovigo's conscription registers, some of which are held in Padova. Sons born around 1918–1926 most likely appear in the **Bolzano** conscription registers, because these are compiled where the family lives when the conscript is 18–20 [INFERENCE].

The one 1940s Bolzano population that can be traced name by name online is the Lager's. Dario Venegoni's book lists **7,982 people** with birthplace, occupation, arrest, transport and fate ([Venegoni 2005](https://deportati.it/static/pdf/libri/venegoni_sec.pdf)). One entry reads: "Trevisan Romeo. Nato a Padova (PD) il 17/6/1915, meccanico. Arrestato a Bolzano … Deceduto a Gusen il 29/3/1945". Its note adds: "Operaio della Lancia, capocellula del CLN in fabbrica col nome di 'Trevi'".

Filtering the book for "Arrestato a Bolzano" plus a Veneto birthplace would give a reproducible list of industrial-zone migrants caught in the 1944 repression [INFERENCE]. The totals still disagree: the Comune lists 8,294 names, Venegoni 7,982, and estimates of the number of prisoners run from about 9,500 to about 11,000 ([Comune booklet 2015](https://dev.comune.bolzano.it/content/download/22494/1276894/file/BOOKLET_SEMIRURALI-2015-it.pdf)). The unidentified remainder is itself a finding [INFERENCE].

## Ponte Druso lost its eagles in the 1970s, and developers took the Corso Italia cinemas

### The eagles stood for four decades and left in a 1970s renovation

Ponte Druso was built in 1930–31 for the state road authority (A.A.S.S.), with work starting in March 1930. It opened to traffic on **28 October 1931, the ninth anniversary of the March on Rome**, linking the new districts of viale Venezia and via Roma with the station and the centre ([Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)). German Wikipedia gives 1929–31 and dimensions of 102 m by 35 m [W] ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen))).

The engineers were **Eugenio Miozzi and Gilberto Ricci**, who built three reinforced-concrete arches clad in rose porphyry ([Gatti & Cacciaguerra 2007](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/77986)). Miozzi left for Venice in March 1931 and built the Ponte del Littorio causeway there. One engineer thus links Bolzano's "Roman" bridge to Venice's fascist-era causeway [W] ([it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Miozzi)).

The name was programmatic. It invoked the Roman road station *Pons Drusi* to claim a Roman foundation and so legitimise Italianisation [W] ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen))). Ettore Tolomei even demanded that the Walther statue be replaced by a Drusus monument, which Mussolini refused ([Dunajtschik & Steinacher 2008](https://geschichteundregion.eu/attachment/get/up_90_16520957026012.pdf)).

On the four central piers stood sculptures more than 8 m high: fasces holding globes, on which "troneggiavano gigantesche aquile romane". Shields, helmets and swords completed "un impressionante strumento di rappresentazione del potere" ([Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)). There were **four eagles, carved in 1931 by Vittorio Morelli of Ancona** ([Alto Adige, 4 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-nel-museo-del-monumento-1.508168)).

Two details are disputed. A Wikipedia caption credits the symbolism to Alcide Ticò [W]. An unsourced blog places the eagles on the four corners rather than the central piers [S]. Mario Gabinio's photographs of 1932–35 can settle the placement ([Commons, Gabinio](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gabinio.Bolzano,_Ponte_Druso-Ponte_In_Pietra_A_Tre_Arcate_Con_Gruppi_Scultorei_Raffiguranti_Aquile_Poggiate_Su_Fasci_Littori_116B17.jpg)).

Contemporaries admired the bridge. The AZ praised "der geradezu zyklopische Neubau der Drususbrücke und deren einwandfreier Stil" ([AZ 13 Nov 1931](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059046820)), and *Le Vie d'Italia* printed "Il Ponte Druso a Bolzano" in July 1932 ([Vie d'Italia](https://archive.org/details/2021-11-03-22-15?q=Druso)).

**The eagles were not removed in 1945.** A 1960s postcard still shows them in place ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Postcard_-_bolzano_-_ponte_druso_-_anni_%2760_(53851921130).jpg)). Four candidate dates exist, all pointing to a 1970s renovation:

| Candidate date | Wording in the source | Source |
|---|---|---|
| About 1970 | Removed "um 1970 im Rahmen einer Straßenverbreiterung" | [W] ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drususbr%C3%BCcke_(Bozen))) |
| 1972 | "vennero rimosse nel 1972" | Unsourced blog [S] ([newspastworld](https://newspastworld.wordpress.com/2016/09/23/dove-sono-finite-le-aquile-imperiali-del-ponte-druso/)) |
| Early 1970s | Gone after the bridge's "lavatura" (clean-up) and never returned, because they were "Troppo grandi, troppo fasciste e quindi ingombranti"; the headline "40 anni senza aquile" (Sept 2013) implies about 1973 | [Alto Adige, 5 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/ponte-druso-40-anni-senza-aquile-1.300394) |
| Mid-1970s | "Alla metà degli anni Settanta … furono eliminate tutte le parti con riferimenti ideologici" | [Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf) |

The engineering account describes a 1970s "stylistic washing" that removed the stone parapet and Morelli's statues. Public criticism followed, and in the 1990s a widening gave the bridge a new parapet imitating the old one ([Gatti & Cacciaguerra 2007](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/77986)). The researchers read ideology as entering through the decision **not to put the eagles back**, rather than as the stated reason for the works. No decision by the council or by ANAS (the state roads agency) has been found [INF].

The local press documents what happened to the eagles next ([Alto Adige, 4 Sept 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-nel-museo-del-monumento-1.508168); [Alto Adige, 7 Dec 2013](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/le-aquile-di-ponte-druso-in-volo-fino-alla-cripta-del-monumento-alla-vittoria-1.372917)). For years they lay in the courtyard of the **Museo Civico**. In **2004** building work there forced a move to the municipal **Giardineria** on via Rio Molino, where they lay outdoors "decapitate e mutilate". After a press conference on their neglect in September 2013, Mayor Luigi Spagnolli promised they would be "sistemate e messe sotto il Monumento". On **6 December 2013** a crane truck moved the two best-preserved heads ("Solo due teste, con le ali") into the **crypt of the Victory Monument**, under Hannes Obermair's supervision. They were brushed only lightly, "Muschio compreso", so that visitors could see they were originals.

The 2025 visitor guide does not mention them ([MAV guide 2025](https://www.monumentoallavittoria.com/fileadmin/user_upload/temp/2025_Guida_MAV_ITA.pdf)). Whether they are still on display, and where the other two eagles are, remains open.

### The "cinema on Corso Italia" is the Concordia, but the Cinema Corso is the bigger loss

**The Concordia** best matches a cinema on Corso Italia that has been completely destroyed. It stood on Piazza Cristo Re, just off Corso Italia, which was called viale Giulio Cesare under Fascism. In 1962 it was listed as "Concordia, Corso Italia" and described as "probably the best known and longest-lasting parish cinema in Bolzano" ([salto.bz, Ferrandi](https://salto.bz/en/article/16082018/andavamo-al-cinema)). It grew out of the "Sala Veritas" in the Cristo Re parish rooms, where Lancia's amateur drama group "Aurora" performed in 1945–46 ([Bertoldi / carloromeo.it](https://www.carloromeo.it/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=343%3Ail-teatro-della-ricostruzione&catid=10&Itemid=108)). Its last screening was around 2005. In 2012 the regional administrative court annulled the Dominicans' first building plan for the site. Demolition was completed on **14 August 2018**, and the site became "Casa Concordia", a block of 22 flats designed by the Area studio (Roberto Pauro, Andrea Fregoni) for Cooperdolomiti. The press reported no heritage debate ([Alto Adige, 15 Aug 2018](https://www.altoadige.it/cronaca/bolzano/demolito-l-ex-cinema-concordia-1.1713147)). Its construction date and architect are unproven, so it is only possibly a fascist-era building.

**The Cinema Corso** is the architecturally weightier loss. It stood on Corso Libertà at via Virgilio, about 150 m from where Corso Italia begins. It started as the **Palazzo del Turismo**, designed in 1938 by the Veronese architect **Armando Ronca** for the tourist office, a concert hall and offices for the Duca d'Aosta and the Automobile Club. The building commission mocked its slender columns as "fiammiferi". It became a cinema in 1946: "fu l'inizio della storia del Cinema Corso … Nel 1988 l'edificio fu demolito", and the demolition "fu deplorata da molti cittadini" ([Verdorfer 2007](https://www.basrelief-bolzano.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/Gedenkorte/Bolzano.Percorso_tra_architettura_e_fascismo.pdf)).

The hall had **801 seats**. It hosted the Biennale d'Arte della Venezia Tridentina in 1940 and 1942, and on **14 December 1948** Innsbruck's Exl-Bühne gave the first postwar German-language performances there ([Bertoldi / carloromeo.it](https://www.carloromeo.it/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=343%3Ail-teatro-della-ricostruzione&catid=10&Itemid=108)). German Wikipedia blames building speculation for its loss [W] ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiheitsstra%C3%9Fe_(Bozen))). La Fabbrica del Tempo notes that it went "per mano dello stesso imprenditore che oggi propone il nuovo museo". It adds that "nessuno degli edifici del complesso corso Libertà – piazza Vittoria – piazza Mazzini – corso Italia gode di tutela, salvo la chiesa di Cristo Re" ([Fabbrica del Tempo 2022](http://www.fabbricadeltempo.it/J3/index.php/it/il-percorso/interventi.html)).

**The Cinema Druso**, in the former GIL (Fascist youth organisation) building at the southern end of the axis, was partly preserved inside today's Eurac building ([salto.bz](https://salto.bz/en/article/16082018/andavamo-al-cinema)).

The researchers' resolution: an empty site or new building *on* Corso Italia points to the Concordia, while lost fascist-era architecture on "the Corso" points to the Cinema Corso [INF]. One detail supports reading the Corso as a postwar cinema. The AZ's OCR returns no hits for "Kino Corso" before 1943, although it lists the Cinema Roma (via Cappuccini 30), the Central-Kino, Cine Luce and the Rione Dux's Kino Littorio ([AZ 22 July 1941](https://www.europeana.eu/item/9200333/BibliographicResource_3000059044700)).

### Fascist Bolzano survived as infrastructure and is being defused by addition

Most of the fascist "new Bolzano" survives and still does its original job: tax office, court, church, insurance blocks, station, schools and housing. Its losses came from causes other than iconoclasm [INF]. Speculation took the Cinema Corso in 1988. Housing renewal took the Semirurali from the 1970s–80s, leaving only via Bari 11, a museum since 16 May 2015 ([Comune](https://www.comune.bolzano.bz.it/Vivere-il-Comune/Luoghi/Casa-Semirurale)). Deindustrialisation emptied the Montecatini/Alumix aluminium plant, which was listed in 2004 and reused as NOI Techpark from 2017 [S] ([NOI](https://noi.bz.it/en/about-us/history-architecture)).

Structural failure followed on **16 July 2026**, when most of the Palazzo di Giustizia's central wing collapsed during work on its columns. Historian Günther Pallaver said the fascist monuments were never really de-fascistised after 1945, and "das rächt sich jetzt" ([ORF](https://orf.at/stories/3436356/)). Ponte Druso is the main exception, the one case where iconoclasm removed something.

Street names were purged of fascist leaders in 1945–48, but nationalist and war names were kept [W] ([de.wikipedia list](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_der_Stra%C3%9Fen_und_Pl%C3%A4tze_in_Bozen)):

| Fascist-era name | Postwar name |
|---|---|
| Corso Littorio / Corso dell'Impero / Corso IX Maggio | Corso della Libertà |
| Viale Giulio Cesare | Corso Italia |
| Piazza Arnaldo Mussolini | Piazza del Tribunale |
| Piazza Vittorio Emanuele III (1926) | Piazza Walther |

In 2002 a consultative referendum restored "Piazza della Vittoria" with **61.94%**, after the council had renamed the square "Piazza della Pace" [W] ([it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_alla_Vittoria_(Bolzano))).

The city's doctrine is to add rather than remove. Culture minister Sandro Bondi's letter of January 2011 led to two interventions. The first was **BZ '18–'45**, the documentation centre under the Victory Monument, which opened on **21 July 2014**. Its LED ring around one fasces column is, in Jeffrey Schnapp's phrase, a "nose ring for Fascist rhetoric" ([Carlà & Mitterhofer 2017](https://karolinum.cz/data/clanek/6122/ST_17_2_0011.pdf)). In 2015 "oltre 24.300 persone" visited, about 4,000 of them school pupils, bringing the total since opening to over 55,000 ([MAV press](https://www.monumentoallavittoria.com/it/info/stampa.html)). It received a European Museum of the Year (EMYA) Special Commendation in 2016.

The second was **the Hannah Arendt quotation** mounted over Hans Piffrader's relief on the former Casa del Fascio in November 2017 (on 5 November, according to Pupella-Noguès). It reads "Kein Mensch hat das Recht zu gehorchen – Nessuno ha il diritto di obbedire – Degnu n'a l dërt de ulghè", set against the relief's "credere, obbedire, combattere" ([bassorilievo, Arendt](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/arendt.html)). It was chosen from **486** competition entries ([bassorilievo, concorso](http://www.bassorilievomonumentale-bolzano.com/it/i-temi/2011-un-concorso-di-idee.html)).

Much remains undefused. The courthouse inscription "PRO ITALICO IMPERIO VIRTVTE IVSTITIA HIERARCHIA VNGVIBVS ET ROSTRIS" "has never been contextualized" ([ORF](https://orf.at/stories/3436356/)). The 1938 column to the fallen in Africa and Spain and the Virgil inscription on the INA building are both "nicht erklärt" ([FAU 2026](https://www.geschichtsdidaktik.phil.fau.de/files/2026/06/Exkursionsbericht_gesamt.pdf)). The Piffrader reliefs on the Sparkasse headquarters were restored in 2017–18 without contextualisation ([Sparkasse](https://www.sparkasse.it/wp-content/uploads/Fassade-Sitz-Sparkasse.pdf)). The bridge and the stadium still bear the name Druso.

The builders of this layer form a map of mobility of their own [INF]. Piacentini, Rossi de Paoli and Busiri Vici came from Rome, Ronca from Verona, and Mansutti and Miozzo from Padua. Morelli came from Ancona and Miozzi from Belluno by way of Venice. Piffrader, Gabloner and Holzmeister came from Tyrol, and Vannucci, who cut the travertine, from Pietrasanta.

### After 1950 the market turned north, and the Italian tourist of the fascist era faded

ASTAT's long series shows what the branding literature leaves out ([ASTAT Gemeindetabellen](https://assets-eu-01.kc-usercontent.com:443/b5376750-8076-01cf-17d2-d343e29778a7/17a9c673-9799-4497-967f-ad52d963008f/astat%20data%2006_2026%20Gemeindetabellen.xlsx)). In **1950** Italians accounted for **85.1%** of the province's overnight stays, a direct continuation of interwar domestic tourism. Foreign arrivals overtook Italian ones in **1954**, and by **1957** the Italian share of nights had fallen to 45.8%. In **1961**, the year of the separatist bombing campaign, Italian arrivals fell by 24.8%. Bolzano city kept roughly half Italian guests until 1990 (55.8%), then fell to 31.7% by 2025.

The "Südtirol" brand therefore sits on a market that had already turned north. Its 2005 credo, "Südtirol ist die kontrastreiche Symbiose aus alpin und mediterran" ([stol.it 2009](https://www.stol.it/artikel/wirtschaft/fuenf-jahre-dachmarke-suedtirol-neue-massstaebe)), markets the border position itself. IDM's 2019 image rules ask for photography "weg von 'Postkartenmotiven' hin zu mehr Echtheit" ([IDM Bildstil](https://www.merano-suedtirol.it/media/5e2b4a81-04bd-4220-aa0a-93b712f4de72/bildstil-idm-vorgaben.pdf)).

Klee's thesis links the two eras: the regime's "utopian" tourism infrastructure "shape[s] and preformat[s] the later change from the 1950s onward" ([Klee abstract](https://www.unilu.ch/fileadmin/fakultaeten/ksf/institute/gsl/doktorierende/Abstract_PhD_Catrina_Klee.pdf)). No scholarship directly compares fascist-era ENIT or EPT posters with postwar campaigns.

### Tourist copy repeats the posters' silence

Official tourism communication today speaks in two registers [INF].

The information pages are frank. IDM's portal calls the Victory Monument an "apologia for the conquest-hungry fascists" and "a grave insult to German-speaking South Tyroleans" ([suedtirol.info](https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/experiences-and-events/plp-experiences/experiences-south-tyrol/pdp-experience.smgpoiarchapp97.victory-monument.bolzano-centro-bozen-zentrum)). The tourist office's attractions page covers BZ '18–'45, the Casa Semirurale and a full Lager memorial itinerary, though a search of it for "Piffrader", "Casa del Fascio" or "Mussolini" returns nothing ([bolzano-bozen.it](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/en/tourist-attractions.htm)).

The sales copy repeats the posters' silence. The history page gives fascism a single sentence, about the 1935 urban plan ([bolzano-bozen.it](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/bolzano-storia-cultura.htm)). Another page still says the city "nasce probabilmente come statio militare romana nel 15 a.C. sotto il nome di 'Pons Drusi'", which is the founding myth the regime used ([bolzano-bozen.it](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/storia-e-cultura)). None of the twelve official audioguides covers a fascist-era site ([audioguide](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/audioguide/)). The one regular tour of interwar architecture is sold as "Bolzano, armonia di stili e sapori: storia, architettura e gelato". It ends at the Victory Monument and then at a gelateria ([visite guidate](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/it/visite-guidate-bolzano.htm)).

The geography follows the same split. The Christmas market has occupied Piazza Walther, in the German-coded old town, since 1991 [S] ([italia.it](https://www.italia.it/it/alto-adige/bolzano/cosa-fare/mercatini-di-natale-di-bolzano)). The fascist authorities removed the Walther statue from that square in 1935, and it returned only in 1985 ([bolzano-bozen.it](https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/en/tourist-attractions.htm)). The interwar posters sold Alto Adige without its fascist city. Today's brand sells the fascist city as one more "contrast", folded into "armonia".

Academic critics are sharper. Angelucci and Kerschbamer liken the red LED ring to "the 'open' sign of a cheap restaurant", which leaves the monument "once again 'open for business'" ([Public History Review](https://doi.org/10.5130/phrj.v24i0.5776)). The exhibition's panels put English first, addressing the international visitor before either local language group [INF].

### Translocality is an open field for Bolzano

The theory supplies a vocabulary. Appadurai's "production of locality" treats places as produced through ritual and media under conditions of mass migration. The researcher's annotation says he counts border zones and tourist resorts among "translocalities", a wording that still needs checking against the 1996 text [INF] ([Érudit reference](https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/as/2001-v25-n3-as375/000261ar/resume/)). Brickell and Datta offer "simultaneous situatedness across different locales" at the scales of home, neighbourhood and city; this is the researcher's synthesis from the abstract [INF] ([LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/37558)). Greiner and Sakdapolrak give the standard definition, dynamics "of simultaneity and identity formation that transcend boundaries" [SS] ([u:cris](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/en/publications/translocality-concepts-applications-and-emerging-research-perspec/)). Freitag and von Oppen use the concept historically, which suits a study of past regimes of circulation [SS] ([ZMO](https://zmo.de/en/publications/publication-search/translocality)).

The researchers found **no published work that applies "translocality" to Bolzano or South Tyrol**. The closest work is Grote and Obermair's *A Land on the Threshold* [SS] ([Peter Lang](https://www.peterlang.com/view/title/36911)), Pfanzelter's work on memory of the Option [SS] ([GR/SR](https://storiaeregione.eu/en/our-journal/read/optioin-und-erinnerung-la-memoria-delle-opzioni)), the heritage studies of the Victory Monument ([Pupella-Noguès 2021](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-543-8/978-88-6969-543-8-ch-04_9UmATvM.pdf)), and Klee's thesis. The researchers propose braiding four documented circulations [INF]. The first is the worker and settler migration that produced the Semirurali and the industrial zone. The second is the regime-built tourist infrastructure that "preformatted" postwar tourism. The third is the postwar turn of the market to the north, sold through a brand built on in-betweenness. The fourth is today's internationalised short-stay flows and platform rentals.

## Conclusion

The regime's media divided the work, and the present has kept that division. Posters made Alto Adige desirable by leaving the fascist city out. Newsreels, *La Stampa* and the illustrated magazines made Bolzano Italian by putting it at the centre. Migrants, soldiers and optants made both real. Ninety years later, information pages tell the fascist history frankly, while the product, the slogans and the visitor flows still follow the posters: Dolomites, Christmas, gelato, "contrast". The strongest move open to the studio is to make this division visible site by site. Each fascist building can be paired with three things: the poster that omitted it, the newsreel or headline that staged it, and a named person who arrived or left because of it. The material cited here already allows that for the Monument, Ponte Druso, the industrial zone and the Semirurali.

The evidence itself is translocal. Bolzano's interwar image now survives in Merano (the Touriseum) and Treviso (the Collezione Salce), and in Budapest, where the Hungarian National Museum holds a Lenhart *Merano*. It survives in Turin (Gabinio's photographs and *La Stampa*), in Rome (Luce) and in Innsbruck (the Option card indexes). A Tuscan village library kept a soldier's Stilves postcards, and the scanned copy of *La Provincia di Bolzano* appears to be Trento's. The map of the archives is the map of the flows. The one site where all three flows physically meet, via Bari 11, opens on Saturday afternoons. No one has yet written a translocal history of Bolzano, and the material collected here is enough to begin one.

## Addendum: hand-opened browser verifications (2026-10-22)

After the six researchers reported, the two platforms that block scripted clients were opened by hand in Chrome. Full notes and captures: `07_browser_verifications.md` and `files/07_browser_verifications/`. What changed:

- **Ponte Druso, from the regime's own paper.** The *Alpenzeitung* of 29 Oct 1931, p. 3, reports the opening of 28 Oct 1931 under the headline "Die Feier des 9. Jahrestages der fascistischen Revolution in Bolzano" and lists the works opened that day: "Drususbrücke, städt. Badeanstalt, Palast der Krankenkasse, Rundfunksender der „Eiar", Elektrizitätswerk in Val d'Ega, Artilleriekaserne in Oltrisarco, Tramlinie von Vurza nach Laives". The Prefect Marziali cut the ribbon and was received by the builder "Ing. Cav. Albini", a name absent from the secondary sources used in section 5. The "Werke des Regimes" column gives the technical sheet: 102.30 m, three arches of 34.70 m and 21.65 m, reinforced concrete faced in porphyry, 14 m roadway, cost 3,000,000 lire of which 800,000 from the Comune, works begun 9 January 1930. It describes the corner piers as carrying four helmets with Roman shields, swords pointing up and down "marking the beginning and the end of the war", and columns with Roman eagles resting on globes carried by the fascist lictor's bundle over carved laurel. The eagles were therefore fascist by explicit design, not Roman décor later read as fascist. [V, transcription from Fraktur]
- **The Drusus justification in print.** The *Alpenzeitung* of 26 Sep 1933, p. 3 ("Zwei Jahre Drusus-Brücke"), explains the name: Drusus conquered "unsere Alpengebiete" fifteen years before Christ, built roads and settlements, carried Roman culture into the swampy valleys and "schlug auch die erste Brücke bei Castelfirmiano über die Etsch". The page carries a halftone photo of the bridge with the eagle columns in place, saved as a study copy. [V]
- **Radio.** The same 28 Oct 1931 bundle included a new EIAR transmitter for Bolzano, which sits between the 12 July 1928 opening and the 1 kW listing of 1932 in section 2. [V]
